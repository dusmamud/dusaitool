A poultry farm does not fail in one day. It fails in small daily leaks: feed given a little too much or a little too little, a few birds dying without anyone noticing the pattern, eggs counted on paper that never matches the money received, and a buyer who pays late because there was no proper bill. The tools in this article are chosen for those four daily jobs: flocks, feed, health, and sales. They work in India, on an ordinary phone, without a VPN, and most of them can be started free or on a trial before any money is paid. Nothing here replaces a veterinary doctor, a feed supplier's advice on your own flock, or your own daily visit to the shed.

The short answer for India is this: for a broiler farm, start with [Poultrix India](https://poultrixindia.com/) for flock, feed, mortality, medicine and finance records in one place, with its AI features for feed conversion, mortality prediction and harvest advice. For a farm that wants to work mainly through WhatsApp, look at [Flockify](https://www.flockify.in/) for WhatsApp-based farm management, sensor data, AI forecasting and veterinary access. For a layer farm, a broiler farm or a feed mill that wants one cloud dashboard, look at [CloudPoultry](https://cloudpoultry.com/) for egg collection, flock tracking, feed mill stock and accounting reports. For a larger or multi-unit operation, compare [PoultryCare](https://www.poultry.care/) for breeder, broiler, layer, hatchery, feed mill and trading management in one system. For daily records that cost nothing to start, use Google Sheets. For selling, use WhatsApp Business for your catalogue and customer messages, and [Vyapar](https://vyaparapp.in/) for GST-ready billing in India. For egg prices, check the National Egg Co-ordination Committee, NECC, suggested prices before you bargain, and remember that NECC itself says those prices are suggestive, not mandatory.

This article is general information only, not veterinary, financial or legal advice. A sick flock, a new medicine, a feed change or a large sale should be checked with a qualified veterinary doctor, your feed supplier or a professional adviser, and with the official source for that rule or price. Prices, free limits and trial terms change. Before you pay for anything, or promise a buyer a date, a quantity or a price based on an app, re-check the official pricing and help page of that tool. A vendor claim about profit, feed saving or mortality is marketing until it is proven on your own flock, with your own numbers, over a full batch.

## Poultrix India: broiler flock, feed and harvest advice in one place

Poultrix India describes itself as a smart poultry farm management platform built specifically for the Indian broiler poultry industry. Its site lists chick batch management, daily farm data entry, feed and feed conversion ratio tracking, mortality monitoring, medicine management, a farm performance dashboard, and financial and expense tracking, with role-based access for company admins, supervisors, dealers and farmers. That combination matters because a poultry business is not one job. The person feeding the birds, the supervisor checking mortality, and the owner checking payment are often three different people, and a system that gives each of them only their own data is safer than a shared notebook.

Its AI layer is the reason it leads this article. The site describes seven AI features: a seven-day forecast of feed conversion ratio and mortality trends, a smart harvest advisor that compares profit today with profit after three or five more days and factors in feed conversion deterioration, a daily feed recommendation based on batch history that flags overfeeding and underfeeding and estimates cost saving per bird, a disease risk alert that ranks active batches by risk and flags mortality spikes, low water intake and weight stagnation, a growing-contract scheme advisory that compares schemes and predicts the final payable amount, an AI farm assistant that answers questions about your own farm data in plain Hindi or English, and an automated weekly performance summary that can be shared on WhatsApp.

Use those features the right way. A seven-day mortality forecast is useful only if daily mortality, feed in kilograms, water intake and body weight are entered honestly every day. If mortality is entered late, or feed is guessed, the forecast will look confident and still be wrong. Enter data at the same time every day, from the shed register, not from memory at night. Check the harvest advisor against your own contract terms, transport availability and buyer readiness, because the most profitable date on a screen is not profitable if the buyer cannot lift birds that day. Treat a disease risk alert as an early warning to visit the shed and call a vet, not as a diagnosis. A spike in mortality, a drop in water intake or a flat weight curve can have many causes, and only a vet who sees the flock can separate them.

Be careful with the name. There are similarly named poultry sites for other countries, including a Poultrix site based in Israel and a Poultrix site describing itself as a layer farm software for Pakistan. The Indian broiler platform in this article is Poultrix India, at poultrixindia.com, which describes itself as Poultrix India Pvt Ltd and gives an Indian contact email. Do not sign up, pay or share farm data on a similarly named site without checking that it is the Indian company you meant. On price, its site says a free trial is available with no credit card required, and enterprise plans are by contact. This article therefore does not quote a monthly price for Poultrix India, because the site does not publish one in the material checked. Ask for the price in writing, including how many farms, users and batches it covers, before you move a full season onto it. Its site also says it was established in 2026 while also claiming more than ten years of experience in the poultry industry. Those two statements can both be true if the experience belongs to the people behind the company, but the wording is ambiguous, so judge the product by a trial on your own farm, not by the experience claim alone.

## Flockify: poultry management through WhatsApp, with sensors and forecasting

Flockify takes a different approach, and for many Indian farm owners it may be the more practical one. Its site describes intelligent poultry management using WhatsApp-based automation, so the whole farm can be managed directly through WhatsApp, with no complex training required. From sensors to a smartphone, in its own words, it gives complete farm control, real-time insights from daily live data, AI forecasting for mortality, feed conversion and harvest timing, sensor integration for climate, feed and water monitoring, and instant access to veterinary experts.

The WhatsApp point deserves attention. Many farm workers and supervisors already use WhatsApp every day, and they do not need to learn a new app interface to send a daily entry. If your team already reports mortality and feed on WhatsApp, a system that collects that same message into a structured record is much more likely to be used than a system that asks for a separate login and a long form. The risk is the opposite one: WhatsApp messages are easy to send and easy to lose. Make one person responsible for checking that every shed's daily message has actually arrived and been recorded, and keep a paper shed register as backup for at least the first batch. A sensor reading is also only useful if the sensor is working. Check water and climate sensors against a manual reading once a week, because a faulty sensor that reports normal temperature while the shed is overheating is worse than no sensor at all.

Flockify's modules cover breeder, broiler and layer management, with fertility and hatchability analytics for breeders, growth curve and weight analytics for broilers, and feed conversion and efficiency intelligence across the business. Its site also promises proactive health surveillance and veterinary access. That access is valuable, but read it carefully. Instant access to a veterinary expert through a platform is not the same as a vet visiting your shed, examining birds and prescribing treatment. Use the platform vet for a first opinion and for follow-up questions, and call a local vet for a serious outbreak, a sudden large mortality, or any treatment that involves medicine, withdrawal periods or a large cost. Flockify's site does not publish a clear monthly price in the material checked, so this article does not invent one. Ask for the price, the sensor cost if any, and what happens to your data if you stop paying, before you commit.

## CloudPoultry: one dashboard for layers, broilers and feed mills

CloudPoultry is the clearest option in this article for a farm that wants one price on the page and one dashboard for several jobs. Its site describes poultry ERP software for India, Canada and the United States, trusted by more than 50 poultry farms across India, including Telangana, Andhra Pradesh and Tamil Nadu, and it states plans from ₹4,200 per month, with no credit card required and cancellation anytime. That price is quoted from its own site, and it must be re-checked on its official pricing page before paying, because plans and limits change, and the starting plan may not include every module or every farm.

What it covers is broad. Daily egg types, batch-wise mortality and health logs for egg collection and flock tracking. More than ten reports for layer analytics, covering production, feed cost and profitability. Raw material batches, transfers and cost per kilogram for feed mill and inventory management. Structured financials for accounting, sales and purchase ledgers, with Tally ERP export. That Tally export matters in India, because many accountants already work in Tally, and a poultry system that cannot hand clean numbers to an accountant creates double work at the end of the month. Its site also claims a 12 percent reduction in feed waste. Treat that as a vendor claim, not a guaranteed result. Feed waste depends on feeder design, feed quality, bird age, shed management and how honestly waste is measured, and no software alone can promise the same percentage on every farm. Test it on one batch: measure feed issued, feed consumed and feed wasted before and after, using the same method both times.

CloudPoultry suits a layer farm first, because egg collection, egg types and layer profitability reports are its most specific features. A broiler-only farm may find Poultrix India or Flockify more specific to broiler contracts and harvest timing. A farm with its own feed mill, however, should look closely at CloudPoultry, because feed is usually the largest cost in poultry, and a system that tracks raw material batches and cost per kilogram, rather than only total feed bags, gives a truer cost per egg or per kilogram of live bird. Before paying, ask whether the ₹4,200 starting plan covers your number of farms, sheds and users, whether egg, feed mill and accounting modules are all included, and whether support is available in your language and time zone.

## PoultryCare: the multi-unit system for breeders, hatcheries and trading

PoultryCare is the largest system in this article, and it should be judged as a business system, not as a simple farm app. Its site describes an all-in-one poultry management platform covering breeder management, broiler management, layer management, contract broiler farming, hatchery management, feed mill management, processing plant management and trading management, with real-time data insights and streamlined operations, accessible from any device. It says it is trusted by more than 250 poultry businesses across more than 15 countries, and it gives Indian customer examples from Namakkal, Salem and Palladam in Tamil Nadu.

For a single small farm, that breadth can be a disadvantage. A system built for breeders, hatcheries, processing plants and trading will have screens and settings a small layer unit never needs, and the setup time can be longer than the time it saves in the first month. For a company with several farms, a hatchery, a feed mill and traders, that breadth is the point: one set of numbers for birds, feed, eggs, sales and money, instead of five spreadsheets that never agree. Its broiler features cover growth tracking, health checks and feed efficiency. Its layer features cover production tracking, quality control and performance data. Its breeder features cover health monitoring, fertility insights and daily tracking. Its hatchery features cover incubation control, chick monitoring and process efficiency.

PoultryCare's site also makes strong outcome claims, including that an average customer reduces feed waste by 12 percent and doubles profitability within one year. Those are vendor claims from its own marketing page, not independent results, and this article does not repeat them as facts about your farm. Profitability in poultry depends on chick price, feed price, egg and broiler price, mortality, disease, weather and contract terms, and no software can double it on its own. Judge PoultryCare by a demo with your own data: enter one real batch, one real feed purchase and one real sale, and see whether the reports match your own notebook. On price, its site does not publish a clear monthly price in the material checked, so ask for a written quotation, including implementation, training and support, before comparing it with CloudPoultry or Poultrix India.

## Google Sheets and general AI chatbots: the free record and the free helper

Not every poultry job needs a poultry system. For a small farm, a well-kept Google Sheet is often better than an expensive system that nobody fills in. Google Sheets is free for individuals as part of Google's free Docs Editors suite, it works on a phone and a computer, and it can be shared with a supervisor, an accountant or a buyer without buying a licence. Build one sheet with five tabs, and keep it simple: birds in and birds out, daily mortality, feed issued in kilograms, water and medicine notes, eggs collected by type, and sales with date, buyer, quantity, rate and amount received. Add one formula for feed conversion ratio only when you understand what it means for your flock type, because a wrong formula, applied confidently for a whole batch, is worse than no formula at all.

General AI chatbots, such as ChatGPT and Gemini, have free tiers in India, and paid plans exist with higher limits. They are useful helpers, not poultry systems. Use them to draft a message to a buyer in clear Hindi or English, to explain a poultry term, to turn a rough shed note into a clean daily report, or to prepare questions for a vet. Do not use them to diagnose a disease, choose a medicine, set a dose, or decide a feed formula on their own. A chatbot does not know your flock age, your shed temperature, your last vaccination or your local disease situation, and it answers in confident language even when it is guessing. If a chatbot suggests a medicine, a dose or a feed change, check it with a vet or with the official product information before acting, and never follow a dose from a chatbot alone. Also, do not paste customer phone numbers, bank details or other personal data into a free chatbot account without checking its privacy settings, because a free consumer plan is not the same as a business plan with a data agreement.

One more caution applies to old free offers. Some widely shared free AI offers in India in 2025 and 2026, including telecom-linked free periods for AI plans, have closed to new claims, and some free periods convert to paid subscriptions at renewal. This article therefore does not promise any telecom free AI offer as current. If a chatbot or AI plan is offered free, check the official offer page for the start date, the end date, the renewal price and the cancellation steps, and set a reminder before renewal. A free tool that silently becomes a paid subscription is not free.

## WhatsApp Business and Vyapar: selling eggs and birds without losing the bill

A poultry business lives or dies on sales, and sales in India often happen on WhatsApp. WhatsApp Business is the free app from Meta for small businesses, with a business profile, a catalogue, labels for organising chats, quick replies, greeting and away messages, and broadcast lists. Use the catalogue for your real products: table eggs by tray, country eggs if you sell them, live broilers by kilogram, dressed chicken if that is your business, and chicks if you sell them. Add a clear photo, the unit, and the price basis, so a buyer does not have to ask the same three questions every time. Use labels for regular buyers, new buyers, feed suppliers and transporters, so a payment reminder does not go to the wrong person.

Know the limits. A WhatsApp Business broadcast list is capped at 256 contacts per list, and a broadcast only reaches a recipient who has saved your business number. That means a broadcast to 1,000 past buyers needs several lists, and it will still miss buyers who never saved your number. Do not buy bulk WhatsApp marketing tools that promise to bypass those limits without checking WhatsApp's own rules and India's spam and consent rules, because an account blocked for spam loses every customer conversation with it. For daily selling, a short personal message to a regular buyer, with today's quantity, rate and delivery time, works better than a large broadcast, and it keeps the relationship that brings repeat orders.

Vyapar is the billing tool that turns those sales into proper records. Its site describes GST billing software for small businesses in India, with GST-ready billing, tax compliance, payment tracking, inventory control, e-invoice and e-way bill creation, and support for cash, UPI, card and bank transfer, with local language support and offline-first reliability for small business owners. Start with a free trial from its official site, enter your business details, add your items with prices and GST rates, and create one real bill for one real sale before you decide. Check the paid price on its official pricing page, because a billing tool is only useful if its price fits a small farm's margin, and because GST rules and e-invoice limits depend on your turnover and registration status. Vyapar does not replace an accountant or a tax adviser. For GST registration, e-invoicing thresholds, input tax credit or a dispute with a buyer, check with a qualified professional and with the official GST source, not with an app screen alone.

Keep sales, feed and mortality in the same weekly review. Every Sunday, check four numbers: birds alive, feed consumed, eggs or live weight sold, and money received versus money billed. If feed consumed rises but eggs or weight do not, look at the shed before you blame the feed. If sales rise but money received does not, look at credit given to buyers before you expand. That weekly habit, even in a free sheet, is worth more than any AI forecast that nobody reviews.

## Egg and broiler prices in India: NECC, local buyers and the price on the screen

No poultry tool can set your selling price. It can only help you check it. For eggs, the National Egg Co-ordination Committee, NECC, publishes daily and monthly suggested egg prices for production centres and consumption centres across India, on its e2necc site. Its own notice is important and must be repeated: the daily egg prices suggested by NECC are merely suggestive and not mandatory, they are published solely for the reference and information of the trade and industry, and NECC does not enforce compliance with them. Use the NECC suggested price for your zone as a bargaining reference, then check your local buyer's actual offer, your transport cost, egg size and breakage, and your payment terms. A suggested price in another zone, or a price from last week, is not the price you will receive today.

For broilers, there is no single national suggested price in the same form, so your price comes from your contract, your integrator if you are in contract farming, or your local live-bird market. Record every sale with date, buyer, quantity, live weight, rate and payment date, and compare the rate across buyers over a month. A buyer who pays a slightly lower rate on time is often better than a buyer who promises a higher rate and pays late, because feed and chick suppliers do not wait. Do not promise a buyer a future quantity or rate based on an AI harvest forecast alone. Confirm bird weight, mortality to date, feed stock and transport before you promise, and put the quantity, rate and payment terms in a written message or bill, even for a regular buyer.

## Using AI tools in India: language, data, payment and verification

Language is the first test. Poultrix India's AI farm assistant answers questions about farm data in plain Hindi or English, which is a real advantage for a team that works in Hindi. Flockify's WhatsApp approach also reduces typing and English, because workers already use WhatsApp. CloudPoultry and PoultryCare are business systems, so check their language support, mobile usability and training before paying, especially if the person entering daily data is not comfortable in English. A system that needs long English typing every day will be abandoned, however good its reports look in a demo.

Data is the second test. Poultry systems hold sensitive business data: flock numbers, mortality, feed cost, medicine use, buyer names and sale prices. Before you enter that data, check who can see it, whether it is shared with other farms, how it can be exported, and what happens if you stop paying. Role-based access, as Poultrix India describes, is a good sign, because a dealer, a supervisor and a farm worker should not all see the same financial data. Keep a weekly export in Excel, PDF or CSV, so your farm's history does not live only inside one company's system. Never share OTPs, bank passwords or full bank details with anyone who offers to set up a poultry app for a fee.

Payment is the third test. CloudPoultry publishes a starting plan, Poultrix India offers a free trial with enterprise plans by contact, and Flockify and PoultryCare require a check with the vendor for price. That difference is itself information: a published starting price is easier to compare, while a price by contact may depend on farm size, but it can also hide extra charges for users, farms, sensors or support. Always ask for the full price in writing, including GST if applicable, the number of farms and users covered, sensor or implementation charges, and the cancellation terms. Never pay a middleman to register you on a free government service, and never buy feed, medicine or chicks from inside an advice app without comparing the price with your local supplier.

Verification is the habit that makes all the others safe. Check an egg price in NECC and with a local buyer before you bargain. Check a disease alert with a shed visit and a vet before you treat a whole flock. Check a feed recommendation against your own feed stock and bird weight before you change a ration. Check a harvest date against buyer readiness and transport before you promise it. Keep one poultry system, one free record in Sheets, and one sales record in Vyapar or a bill book, so that a single app's outage, price change or account problem does not leave you without your farm's history.

## What to avoid

Avoid any tool that promises a guaranteed egg price, a guaranteed broiler weight, a guaranteed feed conversion ratio, or a cure for a flock disease from one photo or one message. No app can promise those things, and a promise like that is a sales pitch, not advice. Avoid treating a chatbot, an AI forecast or a WhatsApp forward as a veterinary diagnosis, and avoid giving medicine, changing feed or vaccinating on the basis of a single app answer without a vet's check. Avoid tools that need a VPN to work in India, because a tool that is blocked here will also have no local support when you need it. Avoid similarly named poultry sites from other countries when you meant the Indian company, and avoid sharing farm, buyer or bank data on a site whose company, country and contact details you have not checked. Avoid depending on one app alone for a whole batch: keep a paper shed register, a free sheet and a proper bill, so that a phone loss, an app outage or a subscription ending does not erase your flock's record. Avoid bulk messaging tools that promise to bypass WhatsApp limits without checking the rules, because a blocked business number loses every customer conversation. And avoid paying for a large multi-unit system before you have tested a small system on one real batch, with real feed, real mortality and real sales.

## Frequently asked questions

**Which AI tool should a small poultry farm in India start with?** Start with Google Sheets for daily records and WhatsApp Business for sales, because both are free to start and your team already knows them. Add Poultrix India on its free trial for a broiler farm, or Flockify if your team works mainly on WhatsApp, and only pay after one batch proves the system saves time or money.

**Can AI predict mortality or feed conversion for my flock?** It can forecast trends from your own daily data, as Poultrix India and Flockify describe, but a forecast is only as good as the daily entries behind it. Enter mortality, feed, water and weight honestly every day, and treat any spike or warning as a reason to visit the shed and call a vet, not as a diagnosis on its own.

**Do these poultry tools work in Hindi?** Poultrix India's AI farm assistant is described as answering in plain Hindi or English, and Flockify's WhatsApp approach reduces English typing. For other systems, check language support, mobile usability and training inside the product before paying, because a system that needs long English typing daily is often abandoned.

**Is CloudPoultry really ₹4,200 per month?** Its own site states plans from ₹4,200 per month, with no credit card required and cancellation anytime. That is a starting plan, not a promise that every module, farm and user is included, so re-check the official pricing page and ask what the plan covers before paying.

**Can I use NECC egg prices as my selling price?** No, not directly. NECC itself says its daily egg prices are merely suggestive and not mandatory, published for reference only. Use your zone's suggested price as a bargaining reference, then agree the actual rate, quantity, transport and payment terms with your buyer.

**Should I trust a vendor claim that feed waste falls by 12 percent?** No, not as a guarantee. Both CloudPoultry and PoultryCare use a 12 percent feed waste figure on their own sites, but feed waste depends on feeders, feed quality, bird age and shed management. Measure feed issued, consumed and wasted with the same method before and after, on your own farm, before you believe any percentage.

**Can a chatbot diagnose a sick flock or choose medicine?** No. A general chatbot can explain a term or draft a message, but it does not know your flock age, shed conditions, vaccination history or local disease situation. For a sick flock, a medicine choice or a dose, consult a qualified veterinary doctor and the official product information.

**Do I need a poultry ERP if I have only a few hundred birds?** Usually not at first. A free sheet, a paper shed register, WhatsApp Business for sales and Vyapar for billing cover most small farms. Move to a poultry ERP when daily entries, multiple sheds or multiple buyers make a sheet hard to trust, and test it on one batch first.

**What should I check before paying for any poultry tool?** Check the full price in writing, including GST, farms, users, sensors and support. Check data export, cancellation terms and who can see your data. Test the free trial or free tier for a full batch, and confirm any costly decision, such as treatment, feed change or harvest date, with a vet, a supplier or a buyer before acting on an app alone.

## The final verdict, and a checklist before you pay

Start with Poultrix or Flockify for flock records, Sheets for daily data, and WhatsApp plus Vyapar for sales and billing; get a vet for a sick flock. That combination covers the four jobs that decide a poultry business: how many birds you have, how much feed they eat, how healthy they are, and how much money actually reaches you. Add CloudPoultry when layers or a feed mill make one dashboard worth paying for, and consider PoultryCare only when several farms, a hatchery or a trading business make a multi-unit system necessary.

Before you pay, run this checklist. Have you entered one real batch, with real mortality, feed and sales, in the free trial or free tier. Is the full price in writing, with farms, users and support included. Can you export your data in Excel, PDF or CSV. Have you checked the egg or broiler price with a second source before bargaining. Have you confirmed any treatment, feed change or harvest promise with a vet, supplier or buyer. If any answer is no, wait. The right tool for a poultry farm is not the newest AI system, it is the system your team actually fills in every day, whose numbers match your shed register, and whose sales records match the money in your account.
