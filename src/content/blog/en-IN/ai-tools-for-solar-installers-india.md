If you install rooftop solar in India, your customer is making one of the biggest purchases of their life, and they are nervous. They have heard about government subsidy, they have seen a neighbour's panels, and they have three installers quoting three different prices for what sounds like the same system. The sale takes weeks, not hours: a site visit, a roof measurement, a shadow check, a quote, a subsidy application, and then installation. The daily trouble is keeping track of all of it — which house was visited, which quote was sent, which subsidy form is pending, and which customer is waiting for a call back.

The direct answer is simple: start with WhatsApp Business for site-visit bookings, roof photos and quotes, Google Business Profile so nearby homeowners find you when they search for solar, Canva for simple posters that explain savings and subsidy in plain language, ChatGPT to write polite quotes and follow-up messages, and Google Sheets to track every lead, visit, quote and installation. All five are free to begin with, and together they turn slow, nervous enquiries into signed installations.

## 1. WhatsApp Business: bookings and reminders that bring customers back

WhatsApp Business is the free business version of the WhatsApp your customers already use every day. It adds a business profile with your hours, services and location, quick replies for the questions you answer again and again, labels to sort customers, and a catalogue that lists your services with prices.

For a solar installer, this means booking the site visit on WhatsApp, receiving roof photos before travelling, and sending a written, itemised quote after the visit. The customer can compare the quote with family and other installers, and the installer has proof of what was promised.

How to start: Set up your catalogue with three to five services and prices, write quick replies for price and timing, and send reminders only to customers who agreed to messages.

Watch out for: Never spam customers with daily offers, and never share a customer's phone number or personal details with anyone else.

## 2. Google Business Profile: be found when nearby customers search

Google Business Profile is the free listing that decides whether a nearby customer finds you or your competitor. When someone searches for your service near them, Google shows your profile with photos, hours, phone number, directions and reviews.

For a solar installer, this means appearing when a nearby homeowner searches for rooftop solar, with photos of real installations, correct hours and honest reviews. A family considering solar will study reviews carefully, so three genuine reviews beat thirty bought ones.

How to start: Claim your profile, add ten real photos of your work, correct hours and phone number, and reply to your last five reviews.

Watch out for: Fake reviews or wrong hours damage trust fast, so keep the details honest and reply politely even to unhappy customers.

## 3. Canva: offer posters and photos that sell the next visit

Canva helps a small business look professional without hiring a designer. Its free plan has templates for offer posters, price cards and festival discounts, and its photo tools can brighten a real before-and-after photo so the difference is obvious on a phone screen.

For a solar installer, this means one clear poster showing how a rooftop system works, what subsidy support exists, and a real photo of a finished installation. A poster that explains instead of shouting builds trust with a nervous buyer far better than a poster full of discounts.

How to start: Make one festival offer poster with your real prices and phone number, and share it on WhatsApp Status this week.

Watch out for: Do not use stock photos of work you did not do, or a heavily edited photo that hides what you did not fix.

## 4. ChatGPT: messages, replies and offers in minutes

ChatGPT is an AI assistant that handles the writing a small business owner does every day. Give it the facts — service, price and date — and it writes a short, polite message in Hindi or English, replies to a customer asking about price, drafts a festival offer, or answers a complaint calmly.

For a solar installer, this means a polite written quote with panels, inverter and structure listed separately, a calm reply when a customer compares your price with a cheaper quote, and a gentle follow-up a week after the site visit. The owner gives the facts, and the tool writes the message in Hindi or English.

How to start: Ask it to write a short Hindi reminder for customers whose last visit was three weeks ago, then read it and send it yourself.

Watch out for: AI can invent a price or a service you do not offer, so check every number and every promise before sending a message.

## 5. Google Sheets: customers, jobs and cash in one sheet

Google Sheets is a free spreadsheet that works on any phone and becomes the record book of a small business. One sheet can list every customer, the service they took, the date, the amount paid and the next due date, so you can greet a regular by name and suggest the right service.

For a solar installer, this means one row per lead: name, address, roof size, visit date, quote amount, subsidy status, and installation date. No lead is lost in a chat thread, and the installer knows every morning which customer needs a follow-up call.

How to start: Make one sheet with this month's customers, services and dates, and use it to send reminders next week.

Watch out for: Do not store a customer's full address, ID or payment details in a shared sheet; keep only what the business actually needs.

## Using AI in India: solar installers

The pattern that works for Indian solar installers is patient, written and honest about savings. A rooftop system costs a lot, and the customer will compare you with two other installers, so your quote must be written, itemised and comparable: panels, inverter, structure, wiring and subsidy shown separately. Never promise an exact monthly saving, because sunshine, usage and tariff change; show a range and explain it in Hindi or the customer's language. Subsidy rules change, so check the official government portal before promising any amount or date, and help the customer with the paperwork instead of leaving them to figure it out alone.

## What to avoid

Avoid promising an exact subsidy amount or approval date, quoting one price and charging another after installation, and installing panels on a weak roof without a proper structure check. Avoid using low-quality panels while charging for a premium brand, and never start work without a written agreement and an advance receipt.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the best AI tool for solar installers in India?**
There is no single best tool, because a solar installer needs bookings, records, reminders and customer trust. Start with WhatsApp Business, Google Business Profile, Canva, ChatGPT and Google Sheets, and add paid software only when the free tools start losing jobs.


**Can AI replace the skilled work in this business?**
No. AI can organise bookings, write messages and keep records, but it cannot do the skilled work itself or judge quality. Every AI message must be checked by a human before it reaches a customer.


**How much do AI tools for solar installers cost in India?**
The starting set is free: the tools in this article all have free plans that cover daily work for a small business. Check current pricing on the official page before paying for anything.


**How do I remind customers without spamming them?**
Send one polite reminder at the right time, only to customers who agreed to messages, and stop if they ask you to. One useful reminder beats ten daily offers that get your number blocked.


**How do I explain subsidy to a customer without misleading them?**
Explain that subsidy is given by the government under current rules, that the amount and timing can change, and that you will help with the application but cannot guarantee approval. Show the official portal or notice, and put the subsidy figure separately in the quote so the customer sees the system cost with and without it.


**How long should I wait before following up on a quote?**
Wait three days, then send one polite message asking whether the customer has questions about the quote. Follow up once more after a week, and then stop unless the customer replies. A customer who needs time will come back to the installer who was helpful and patient, not the one who messaged every day.
