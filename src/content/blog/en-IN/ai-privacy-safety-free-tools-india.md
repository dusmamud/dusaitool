## Bitwarden Free: passwords, breaches, and the k-anonymity trick

[Bitwarden](https://bitwarden.com/) is a free password manager that secures account passwords on Windows, macOS, and Linux, and on mobile with downloadable versions for Android and iOS, preventing unauthorised access, with its free version covering everything a regular user needs with no silly limits, and its full feature set worth paying for if you want it. Its vault health reports help you evaluate the security of your vault, with reports like Reused Passwords and Weak Passwords run locally on your client, identifying offending items without Bitwarden ever having access to unencrypted versions of your data, but most vault health reports are only available for premium users, including members of paid organisations, families, teams, or enterprise, while the Data Breach report is free for all users.

The **Data Breach report** identifies compromised data, like email addresses, passwords, credit cards, and date of birth, in known breaches, using [Have I Been Pwned](https://haveibeenpwned.com/), and when you create a Bitwarden account, you have the option to run this report on your master password before deciding to use it. Its privacy trick is **k-anonymity**: to run the report, only the first five digits of a hash of your master password are sent to Have I Been Pwned and compared to stored exposed hashes, with your master password itself never exposed by Bitwarden, and the returned matching list of hashes then locally compared with the full hash of your passwords, with that comparison only done locally to preserve k-anonymity. If the report was performed with your actual passwords, it would not matter whether they were exposed or not, because you would be voluntarily leaking them to the service, which is why the first-five-digits method matters, and why Bitwarden's breach report is safe to run.

A breach result does not always mean your account has been compromised, rather that you are using a password that has been found in databases of exposed passwords, but Bitwarden highly recommends not using leaked and non-unique passwords, and once identified, you should create a new password for affected accounts or services. Bitwarden data breach scanning, included in all plans, alerts you when your email or credentials appear in known breaches, while premium users also receive vault health reports that identify weak, reused, and compromised passwords, with actionable recommendations, so run these reports regularly to find vulnerabilities before attackers do, and update exposed credentials immediately. For Indian users, Bitwarden free's breach report is the most valuable free security check in this article, because a leaked password reused across Gmail, bank, and social accounts is the most common way Indian accounts get hacked, and a free report that finds it, without exposing your password, is worth more than any paid VPN.

**Use Bitwarden free in a specific way:** store every password in Bitwarden, never reuse a password across accounts, let Bitwarden generate strong passwords, and run the Data Breach report monthly, changing every leaked or reused password it finds, because a password manager with reused passwords is only slightly better than no manager, while a manager with unique, strong passwords is the single biggest security upgrade most users can make. For your master password, pick a long, memorable phrase you have never used elsewhere, and never share it, because your master password protects every other password, and a leaked master password compromises your whole vault.

**Fourth**, browse privately on public Wi-Fi with Proton VPN free, turning it on before you join the network, because public Wi-Fi is the easiest place for criminals to intercept unencrypted traffic, and a VPN encrypts it. **Fifth**, search privately with DuckDuckGo free as your default, because a private default used every day reduces your search profile more than a private search used once a week. **Sixth**, do not share personal information on forms, quizzes, or giveaways that do not need it, because a phone number, address, or date of birth shared for a free gift becomes data broker inventory, and DuckDuckGo's removal service, which is paid, cannot remove what you keep sharing for free. **Seventh**, check every link before you click it, because phishing links, fake bank messages, and fake job offers are the most common Indian cybercrime, and no VPN, password manager, or search engine blocks a link you click yourself.

For families in India, teach children and elders these habits, because children click links without checking, and elders trust messages that look official, and a family that shares one Bitwarden account, or teaches each member to use Bitwarden free, Proton VPN free, and DuckDuckGo free, is safer than a family where one member pays for security and the rest use nothing.
