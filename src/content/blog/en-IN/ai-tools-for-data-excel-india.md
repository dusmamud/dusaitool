## Using data AI in India: messy data, formulas, and verification

Messy data is the normal state of Indian spreadsheets, because data comes from WhatsApp, from paper, from multiple people, and from different formats, with inconsistent date formats, mixed Hindi and English, and missing values. Inconsistent date formats are a classic cause of failed comparisons, and ChatGPT spreadsheets caught them across 1,200 rows in testing, which is the kind of error a human misses for hours. Clean data before you analyse it: standardise dates, standardise names, fill or flag missing values, and remove duplicates, because AI analysis on dirty data will give you confident, wrong answers, and a clean sheet is worth more than a clever prompt.

Formulas are where AI helps most and hurts most, because a generated formula can look right and be wrong, especially with references, assumptions, and multi-tab files. Always review a generated formula cell by cell, check its references, and test it on a small sample before you trust it on a full sheet, because a wrong formula in a salary sheet, a tax sheet, or a marks sheet is worse than no formula. For VBA and macros, note that ChatGPT spreadsheets may not fully support them, so a macro-heavy Excel file will need human review, and a macro that AI generates should be tested on a copy, not on the original, because a bad macro can delete data.

Verification is the habit that makes data AI safe in India: duplicate the file first, so you can revert if needed, and review formulas, calculations, citations, and changed cells before sharing or relying on them. For a business, verify totals by hand on a sample, because AI can sum a column wrong if it misreads a format, and for a student, verify a formula by tracing it, because a formula you do not understand will fail in an exam. For Hindi data, test AI on a small Hindi sample first, because Hindi and Hinglish data can be misread, and a classification that works in English may fail in Hindi, so check the first 20 classified rows by hand before you trust the rest.
