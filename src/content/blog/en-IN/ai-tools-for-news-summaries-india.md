## How this list was checked, and where sources disagree

A tool is on this list only if it works in India without a VPN, has a genuinely free tier, and summarises news rather than just listing headlines. Each free limit below comes from the tool's own site, app listing, or help page, checked in November 2026. Where a source did not state an exact number, this article says so instead of guessing one.

Three disagreements and gaps are worth stating plainly. First, Perplexity's official pricing page says its free plan has "limited daily usage" but does not publish an exact number of free searches, so any article that quotes a precise daily number for Perplexity free is guessing. Second, Particle's India App Store listing shows the app as free with in-app purchases but does not list the premium price on that page, so check inside the app before paying. Third, Ground News has a free plan and a paid Vantage tier, but the help page checked for this article describes the tiers without stating the Vantage price, so no price is quoted here. If you see a different price or limit elsewhere, trust the official page over this article.

One more warning before the tools: do not build your news habit on a dead app. Artifact, the AI news app from Instagram's co-founders, wound down its standalone app in April 2024 and its technology moved into Yahoo News. Old "best AI news app" lists still recommend it. It is gone as a standalone app, so skip it.

## Inshorts: 60-word news, in English and Hindi

Inshorts is the simplest answer to "what happened today" in India. Its own site describes the product in one line: it cuts the clutter and delivers news in 60-word shorts, aimed at people who do not have time to read long articles every day. Its AI engine personalises a "My Feed" from what you read, and you can browse categories or read the full article inside the app when a short interests you.

Why it wins for most readers in India: it is free, it is made for India, and it publishes in both English and Hindi, as its own homepage title states. The App Store listing for India shows it as a free app. The 60-word format is a real discipline: you get the core fact, and you tap through only when you want depth. That makes it the best default daily habit in this article.

The limit is the format itself. A 60-word short cannot carry nuance, context, or a dissenting view. Treat Inshorts as a briefing, not as the whole story. For court cases, budgets, elections, and anything where the detail changes the meaning, open the full article or check a second source.

## Dailyhunt: news in 14 Indian languages

Dailyhunt is the pick if you, or someone in your family, prefers news in an Indian language other than Hindi or English. Its own site describes it as personalised news in 14 Indian languages, and it is available on Android and iOS in India. It is a free app, and it covers national news, state news, cricket, entertainment, and local updates in one feed.

The strength here is language reach, not AI summarising in the narrow sense. Dailyhunt personalises what you see, but its stories are largely full articles from publishers rather than 60-word shorts. Use it alongside Inshorts: Inshorts for the quick briefing, Dailyhunt for reading in your own language and for regional news that national apps under-cover.

One honest note: Dailyhunt's feed mixes publisher news with entertainment and viral content, so your feed quality depends heavily on the topics and sources you follow. Spend five minutes unfollowing noisy categories on day one, or the personalisation will learn the wrong lesson.

## Google News: the free daily briefing you already have

Google News needs no new account and no payment. Its India edition, at news.google.com with the India region, gives a daily briefing of top stories from Indian and international publishers, plus topics you follow. It is free, it works in India without a VPN, and it is the easiest way to check whether a story is being covered by more than one outlet.

Google News is not an AI summariser in the way Particle is. It groups coverage and shows headlines and short snippets, and its "Your briefing" is a personalised front page rather than a rewritten summary. Its value is breadth and verification: if a claim appears in only one place, Google News will usually show you that quickly. Use it as your cross-checking tool, and as the backup when a dedicated app is having a bad day.

If you read news in Hindi, set the India edition's language to Hindi as well as English. The same account can carry both editions, which is useful in a house where people read in different languages.

## NewsBytes: short India news stories

NewsBytes is an Indian news site and app that publishes short, readable stories on India, cricket, politics, Bollywood, and gadgets. Its homepage shows dated stories across sections, and its stories are written to be read quickly rather than as long analysis pieces. It is free to read.

NewsBytes fits a reader who wants Indian news in short form but finds 60 words too short. Its stories are longer than Inshorts shorts and shorter than a newspaper article, which is a comfortable middle length for a morning read. It is mostly English, so it is not the pick for Hindi-first readers; pair it with Inshorts or Dailyhunt for that.

Because NewsBytes is a publisher, not an aggregator, its view of the day is its own editorial view. That is fine for a briefing, but for a contested story, compare it with Google News or Ground News below before forming an opinion.

## Finshots: money news in plain English, in under 3 minutes

Finshots is a free Indian newsletter that explains the most important financial developments in plain English, in less than 3 minutes a day, according to its own site. Its daily edition covers one or two business stories properly, and its Sunday "Sunny Side Up" edition covers interesting things beyond finance. It is free to join by email, and its site lists no paid plan.

This is the most useful single newsletter in this article if you follow markets, startups, or your own money. A typical Finshots story explains not just what happened but why it matters, which is exactly what a short news app cannot do. The limit is scope: Finshots is finance and business, not general news, so it replaces no other tool here. It complements Inshorts.

Finshots also runs limited-edition personal finance series, such as its "Money Milestones" series, which are worth reading if you are setting up your own finances. Do not confuse a Finshots explainer with financial advice. It explains the news; it does not tell you what to buy.

## TLDR: the daily tech digest

TLDR is a free, byte-sized daily tech newsletter. Each edition links to the day's important technology stories with a one- or two-line summary and a reading time, so you can scan the whole tech day in a few minutes and open only what matters to you. It is free to subscribe by email.

TLDR is the pick for readers who follow technology, AI, startups, and programming. It is US-centric in its story selection, so Indian tech stories appear when they are globally significant, not every day. Pair it with an Indian general source rather than relying on it alone for Indian news. If you work in tech, TLDR plus Finshots is a strong two-newsletter morning.

One caution: TLDR's summaries are short by design, and its links go to the original publishers. If a story affects your work or money, open the original article before acting on the summary.

## Perplexity: ask for a cited summary of a breaking story

Perplexity is not a news app, but it is the best free way to get a cited summary of a specific story on demand. You ask a question, such as what happened in a particular event and what different sources say, and it answers with citations to the sources it used. Its official pricing page lists a free plan at 0 dollars a month, with search, accurate answers, and citations, and limited daily usage. Its paid Pro plan is listed at 17 dollars a month when billed annually, with raised limits and more models.

Why it belongs here: when a big story breaks, a short news app gives you the fact and a newsletter gives you the explanation tomorrow. Perplexity gives you a sourced summary now, and its citations let you check the answer. That makes it the right tool for "what exactly happened, and who reported it" questions.

The limits matter. The free plan's daily usage is limited, and the official page does not state an exact number, so do not plan a whole research workflow around the free tier. More importantly, an AI answer can still misstate a detail even with citations, so for anything serious, open at least one cited source yourself. Perplexity is a research starting point, not a source you quote blindly. ## Particle: an AI-personalised news feed with smart summaries

Particle is a personalised news app built around AI summaries. Its India App Store listing describes it as a personalised news app with smart summaries built for you, listed as free with in-app purchases, and rated 4.9 from its reviewers there. It supports English and 18 more languages on that listing. It builds a feed around your interests and summarises stories so you can scan many topics quickly, then read full coverage where you want depth.

Particle is the most "AI news" product on this list, and it is the one to try if Inshorts feels too short and Google News feels too manual. Its summaries are designed to give you the shape of a story, including follow-ups and context, rather than a single headline. Because it is a newer, smaller app than Google News, its Indian coverage is worth testing for a week against your existing sources before you trust it as your only feed.

The honest gap: the App Store listing does not state the premium price, and in-app purchases mean some features cost money. Try the free tier first, and check the price inside the app before upgrading. Particle is also English-first in practice, so Hindi-first readers should keep Inshorts or Dailyhunt alongside it.

## Ground News: compare how different outlets cover one story

Ground News is different from every other tool here. Instead of summarising a story once, it shows you how many outlets cover it, where those outlets sit on the political spectrum, and how their framing differs. Its help page describes a free plan alongside its paid Vantage tier, which adds deeper ownership data, personal bias insights, and alternative media coverage.

This is the tool for the question "is this story being told differently elsewhere?" For elections, protests, court judgments, and any story where framing is the story, Ground News is more useful than another summary. Its free plan is enough to compare coverage of a major story. The paid Vantage tier is for readers who want the full bias and ownership analysis, and its price is not stated on the help page checked for this article, so check the pricing page before paying.

Ground News is US- and world-focused in its coverage, so Indian stories appear when they are internationally covered. It is a verification tool for your news diet, not a replacement for an Indian daily briefing.

## Using a chatbot to summarise any article

A method rather than a tool, and one worth knowing: paste any article link or text into a chatbot and ask for a summary in your language. This works with the general chatbots covered in our article on [chatbots beyond ChatGPT and Gemini](/blog/ai-chatbots-beyond-chatgpt-gemini-india/), and it is useful for Hindi summaries of English articles, or for turning a long government press release into plain language.

The rule for this method is simple: never trust a chatbot summary of a story you have not at least skimmed yourself. Chatbots can invent quotes, dates, and numbers, and a summary of a long article is exactly where that risk is highest. Use the chatbot to understand the shape of an article, then verify the key facts in the original. ## Using news summary tools in India: language, payment, and no VPN

Language first. If you read in Hindi, Inshorts and Dailyhunt are the natural defaults, and Google News has a Hindi India edition. If you read in English, every tool here works, with Finshots, TLDR, and Ground News being English-only in practice. In a mixed-language household, set up one Hindi feed and one English feed rather than forcing one app to do both badly.

Payment: every tool in the short-answer table has a free tier that works in India, and none of them requires a VPN. Paid tiers, where they exist, are Perplexity Pro, Particle's in-app purchases, and Ground News Vantage. Prices are quoted in dollars on some official pages, and the rupee amount you actually pay can differ with taxes and exchange rates, so check the checkout price in rupees before paying. Do not pay for a news tool until you have used its free tier for at least a week.

No VPN is needed for any tool here, and that matters. A news tool that needs a VPN is a tool you will stop using, and a VPN can also change which regional edition you see. If a tool ever asks you to change your region to use it, treat that as a reason to drop it, not as a setup step.

A final India-specific point: be careful with news you receive on social media and messaging apps. A short summary forwarded on WhatsApp is not a news source, and AI summaries make convincing fakes easy. If a forwarded summary matters, check it in Inshorts, Google News, or the original publisher before forwarding it further. ## What to avoid

First, avoid paying for a news summary tool on day one. Every tool here has a free tier, and a week of free use tells you more than any review, including this one. Second, avoid treating any single summary as the whole story, especially on elections, courts, health, and money. A summary is a briefing; the full article, or a second outlet, is the check.

Third, avoid dead or zombie apps. Artifact is the famous example: wound down in April 2024, its technology folded into Yahoo News. If an old list recommends an app you cannot find in the Indian Play Store or App Store, that is usually why. Fourth, avoid AI summaries of health, legal, and financial decisions without reading the original source. A wrong summary of a medicine, a court order, or a tax rule can cost real money or health.

Fifth, avoid building your whole news diet inside one personalised feed. Personalisation is convenient, but it narrows what you see over time. Keep at least one source that shows you stories you did not ask for, whether that is Google News, a newspaper homepage, or Ground News. ## Frequently asked questions

**What is the best free AI news summary app in India?** For most readers, Inshorts, because it is free, Indian, and publishes 60-word shorts in English and Hindi. Add Google News for breadth and Finshots for money news.

**Can I get Hindi news summaries for free?** Yes. Inshorts publishes Hindi shorts, Dailyhunt covers 14 Indian languages including Hindi, and Google News has a Hindi India edition. All three are free.

**Is Perplexity free for news summaries?** Yes, with limits. Its official pricing page lists a free plan at 0 dollars a month with limited daily usage and citations in every answer. The exact daily number is not published, so test it before relying on it.

**Is Particle available in India?** Yes. Its App Store listing for India shows it as free with in-app purchases, in English and 18 more languages. Check the premium price inside the app before paying.

**Are AI news summaries reliable?** They are reliable as briefings and unreliable as final sources. Use them to know what happened, then open the original article or a second outlet for anything that affects your health, money, legal position, or vote.

**Should I pay for Ground News Vantage?** Only if you regularly compare coverage of contested stories and want its bias and ownership analysis. Its free plan already lets you compare how outlets cover a major story, so try that first.

**What happened to Artifact?** Artifact, the AI news app from Instagram's co-founders, wound down its standalone app in April 2024. Its personalisation technology was acquired by Yahoo and folded into Yahoo News. Do not install lookalike apps claiming to be Artifact.

**Can AI summarise a long article in Hindi for me?** Yes. Paste the article into a chatbot and ask for a Hindi summary, or use a translation tool. Always verify names, numbers, and dates against the original, because AI summaries can invent details.

## The final verdict, and a checklist before you pay

Start with Inshorts for 60-word Hindi and English news, add Finshots for money news, and check Perplexity answers against its citations. That is the whole system for most readers, and it costs nothing. Add Dailyhunt if you read in another Indian language, TLDR if you follow tech, and Ground News when a story's framing matters more than its facts.

Before you pay for any news tool, run this checklist: have you used the free tier for a full week, does it cover Indian news as well as world news, does it work in your language without a VPN, and have you checked the rupee price on the official pricing page? If any answer is no, stay free for now. Prices and limits change, so re-check the official page before paying or promising a deadline based on any tool in this article.
