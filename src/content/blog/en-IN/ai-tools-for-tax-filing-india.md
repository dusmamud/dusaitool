> **Important disclaimer:** This article is general information only, not professional tax, legal, or financial advice. Tax rules, forms, due dates, and section numbers change, and the right choice depends on your income, residential status, and documents. Before filing, paying, or promising yourself a deadline, check the official Income Tax Department portal and, for anything complex, a qualified chartered accountant. Nothing here guarantees a correct return, a refund, or freedom from a notice.

The direct answer is simple: for most salaried Indians, the best starting point is the official Income Tax Department e-filing portal itself, because it is free and it already pre-fills much of your return from AIS and TIS. If you want guided questions, document uploads, and error checks on top of that, ClearTax and Quicko are the strongest paid-and-free options this comparison found, with Tax2Win and myITreturn as cheaper alternatives for simpler returns. The word "AI" on these platforms mostly means document reading, guided questionnaires, and pre-submission checks — not a machine that understands your tax life. No tool in this article computes tax with genuine artificial intelligence, files the return for you without your review, or removes your responsibility for what is submitted. The final return is yours, and the tax department holds you, not the app, responsible for it.

That distinction matters because marketing hides it. One careful 2026 comparison found that ClearTax's AI copilot, called Neha, answers questions but cannot read or write your return; Tax2Win's tips follow a decision tree; Quicko's strength is broker imports through an API, not AI reasoning; and the government's own Kar Saathi chatbot guides but cannot file. So judge every tool below on workflow fit — does it support your income type, import your documents, and flag mismatches — rather than on how loudly it says "AI". Prices in this article come from third-party comparisons and the providers' own pages, and they disagree with each other in places, which this article says openly. Prices and free limits change often, so re-check the official pricing and help page of any tool before paying or promising a deadline based on a paid feature.

## The official e-filing portal and Kar Saathi: start here, pay nothing

The official portal at [incometaxindia.gov.in](https://www.incometaxindia.gov.in/) is the one tool every other tool ultimately feeds into, and for a straightforward salary return it is often enough on its own. You log in with your PAN as user ID, pick the correct return form, review the pre-filled data, preview the computation, validate with zero errors, and submit. Verification is a separate step that many first-time filers miss: you can e-verify at once with an OTP on your Aadhaar-linked mobile number, through net banking, with an electronic verification code from your bank or demat account, or with a digital signature certificate. You can also e-verify later from the portal, or send a signed ITR-V by speed post to the Centralized Processing Center in Bengaluru. The department's own guidance is blunt that a return is not complete until it is verified, and an unverified return can be treated as invalid, so filing without verifying is the single most common self-inflicted failure in this whole process.

Kar Saathi, the government's AI assistant launched in April 2026 under the PRARAMBH 2026 campaign, sits inside the official portal and answers questions around the clock in Hindi and English. It helps with form selection, deductions, deadlines, e-verification, refund tracking, and notice responses, and it explains provisions of the new Income-tax Act, 2025 in simpler words. What it cannot do is file your return. Treat it as a free tutor, not a filer: ask it what a term means, which form fits your income, or what a notice is asking for, and then do the filing yourself on the portal. Because it is free, official, and works in India without any VPN, it should be the first place you ask a question — before you hand your PAN and salary details to any private platform.

One honest complication for 2026: the tax law itself changed labels. Income earned in FY 2025-26 is filed for AY 2026-27 under the older wording, while from 1 April 2026 the Income-tax Act, 2025 applies, with a "tax year" replacing the old previous-year and assessment-year pair, and with renumbered sections. One 2026 source reports that the old Section 80C becomes Section 123 and Section 80D becomes Section 126 under the new Act, while most guides, tools, and older articles still say 80C and 80D. Sources disagree on how quickly the new numbering shows up in tools and forms, so this article uses the familiar 80C and 80D names and flags the new numbers alongside them. Check the portal's current form and help pages for the labels your return actually uses this year, and do not let a section-number mismatch delay your filing.

## ClearTax: the most guided option for salary plus investments

ClearTax is the best-documented guided option in the sources this article checked. Its workflow starts with account access and PAN linking through OTP, pre-fills details from the Income Tax portal, and lets you upload Form 16 or enter salary details manually. For investors it adds capital gains handling, including importing stock or mutual fund transactions through a supported broker or an uploaded Excel statement, plus fields for foreign investments, dividends, savings interest, fixed deposits, deductions, brought-forward losses, and tax paid. Its own AY 2026-27 ITR-2 guide lists the documents it expects: Form 16, Form 16A, Form 26AS, rent receipts, capital-gains statements, bank and fixed-deposit records, and deduction proofs.

Its AI feature, the Neha copilot, is genuinely AI in the chatbot sense — one comparison cites a Microsoft case study describing Azure OpenAI GPT-4o mini with retrieval-augmented generation, which cut support latency sharply — but it is guidance only. It answers tax questions in natural language; it does not fill in, check, or submit your return. The tax optimiser feature is more practically useful: it compares the new and old regimes for you and reminds you of investments you may have missed. On price, one 2026 comparison lists self-filing ITR-1 as free, ITR-2 at ₹1,499, and CA-assisted plans from ₹1,999 to ₹4,999. Those are third-party figures, not ClearTax's own current price list, so re-check before paying. Choose ClearTax if your return mixes salary with capital gains, foreign income, or multiple sources and you want the most hand-holding; skip the paid tier if your return is salary-only, because the free portal already covers that.

## Tax2Win: the chatbot-style filer with a price warning

Tax2Win describes both do-it-yourself and expert-assisted filing, and its chatbot asks friendly questions and builds the return from your answers, warning immediately about wrong-looking information. That conversational style suits first-time filers who freeze in front of a bare form. Its own site describes support for capital gains, futures and options, NRI filing, updated returns (ITR-U), Form 16 upload, broker and Form 26AS imports, and tax-notice assistance. One useful clarification from its own pages: futures and options income is treated as business income and must be reported even when it is a loss, which matters because many traders wrongly assume a loss year needs no filing.

Its pricing is where sources disagree, and the disagreement itself is worth reporting. One comparison lists ITR-1 basic at ₹49 and ITR-2 at ₹999 to ₹1,499, with assisted plans from ₹1,499 to ₹3,999. Tax2Win's own pricing page, meanwhile, lists expert-assisted standard plans at a discounted ₹2,314 plus taxes, with higher tiers for multiple Form 16s, business income, capital gains, NRI, and foreign income, and notes that the discounted prices are a limited-period welcome offer. Both can be true at different times or for different plan bundles, which is exactly why this article refuses to quote one number as settled. Check the live pricing page on the day you file. Choose Tax2Win if you like being questioned step by step and want assisted help available; do not choose it on price alone without that live check.

## Quicko: the trader's tool, built around broker imports

Quicko's official site presents an online service for saving, paying, filing, and tracking taxes, with guided flows and support for complex income categories. Its real strength, confirmed across sources, is broker integration: it connects to brokers such as Zerodha, Groww, and Upstox, pulls the full year's profit-and-loss statement, and computes capital gains from it. It also fetches AIS and TIS from the portal and matches them against your records, which is the single best defence against a notice triggered by a mismatch between what the department already knows and what you declared.

That matching feature deserves emphasis, because AIS is now the department's memory. AIS shows interest, dividends, securities transactions, and other reported activity; TIS summarises it category-wise; Form 26AS now shows mainly TDS and TCS data. A tool that reconciles all three against your own statements before submission catches the errors a human eye skips. One comparison prices Quicko's ITR-1 as free and ITR-2 at around ₹749, with CA-assisted plans from ₹1,499 to ₹2,999 — again, third-party figures to re-check. Its "AI" label is mostly API-based data transfer and rule-based classification, not reasoning, but for a trader that distinction barely matters: accurate imports beat clever chat. Choose Quicko if you trade or invest actively; a salary-only filer gains little from it over the free portal.

## myITreturn: the value pick with a free claim to verify

myITreturn is described in the sources as offering free self-filing alongside paid assisted plans, guided questions, data imports, and error checks. One comparison prices it at around ₹99 for ITR-1 and ₹699 for ITR-2, with assisted plans from ₹1,299 to ₹2,499. Note the tension: "free self-filing" and "₹99 for ITR-1" appear in different sources, which probably reflects different plan years or different definitions of self-filing. Verify on its own site what is actually free for your form before you start, because starting a return on a free promise and hitting a paywall at submission is a miserable experience.

Its pitch is straightforward rather than flashy: answer guided questions, import what you can, run error checks, and file. That is enough for a large middle group — salaried filers with a house property, some interest income, and standard deductions — who want more guidance than the bare portal but do not want to pay ClearTax-level prices. Its weakness, on the evidence available, is simply less documentation: the sources describe its features in general terms, while ClearTax and Quicko have detailed workflow pages to check. Choose myITreturn as a value experiment for a simple return, and keep the free portal as your fallback if the free tier turns out narrower than advertised.

## TaxBuddy and EZTax: assisted help and a pre-submission audit

TaxBuddy positions itself on expert access: its AI auto-reads Form 16 and 26AS, pre-fills fields, and analyses investment statements for optimisation, while a human CA network sits behind the assisted plans. One comparison lists assisted filing from around ₹699 for ITR-1 and ₹799 for ITR-2, with plans up to about ₹2,999. Its marketing claim of filing in three minutes should be read as the time for the automated part, not the whole job: gathering documents, reconciling AIS, and reviewing the computation still take real time, and anyone promising a three-minute complete filing for a complex return is selling speed you should not trust. Choose TaxBuddy if you want a human to review a return you are unsure about, especially with multiple income sources.

EZTax is the budget mobile option with one genuinely distinctive feature: a "Creative Audit" that runs pre-submission validation to flag mismatches and potential notice triggers, plus an AI tax optimiser and one-click auto-fill via Aadhaar OTP. One comparison lists it as free for income under ₹5 lakh and from ₹199 otherwise, with ITR-2 at around ₹499 and assisted plans from ₹999. The pre-submission audit is the most useful AI-adjacent idea on this list, because most filing pain comes not from calculation but from a mismatch discovered months later. Choose EZTax if you file from your phone and want that final mismatch check; verify the under-₹5-lakh free claim on its own site first, since eligibility conditions for free tiers change without notice.

## ChatGPT and Gemini: excellent tutors, dangerous filers

General chatbots deserve a section precisely because so many people now paste financial documents into them. Used well, ChatGPT or Gemini can explain the difference between the old and new regimes in plain Hindi or English, turn your document pile into a checklist, draft questions for your CA, and explain a notice sentence by sentence. Used badly, they are the riskiest tool on this list. Never paste your PAN, Aadhaar number, passwords, OTPs, bank login details, or full bank statements into a general chatbot. A chatbot can also hallucinate a section number, a slab, or a deadline with total confidence, and its answer may describe last year's law. Use it for learning and drafting, with numbers you already know and no identifiers, and verify every figure against the official portal or a CA. If you want a chatbot that is actually tax-specific, use the government's Kar Saathi or a filing tool's own copilot, and remember even those only guide — they do not file.

## Using these tools in India: regime, deductions, and documents

India's first real decision is not which tool to buy but which regime to choose, and every tool above will ask you this. Under the new regime, which is the default, income up to ₹12 lakh is effectively tax-free through a rebate, or ₹12.75 lakh for salaried taxpayers after the ₹75,000 standard deduction, according to 2026 sources. The catch is that most deductions disappear under the new regime. Under the old regime, you keep the deductions but face higher slabs, with a ₹50,000 standard deduction for salaried taxpayers. A salaried person without business income can compare the two regimes every year and pick the cheaper one, so run both computations in your tool before locking in — that single comparison is worth more than any AI feature.

The deductions that matter most under the old regime are the familiar ones. Section 80C covers up to ₹1.5 lakh across life insurance premiums, PPF, EPF, ELSS, and similar items, all sharing one ceiling, so an EPF contribution that already fills the limit leaves no extra benefit for a new policy bought only for tax. Section 80D covers health insurance premiums up to ₹25,000, or ₹50,000 for senior citizens, with a separate limit for parents and a ₹5,000 preventive check-up amount inside those limits. House rent allowance, home loan interest and principal, and donations under Section 80G fill out the usual list. Under the new Act's renumbering reported by one 2026 source, 80C becomes Section 123 and 80D becomes Section 126, but the limits and the old-regime-only condition are the same; check which labels your form uses. Never buy an insurance policy or an investment only for a deduction: a deduction saves a fraction of the premium, while the premium itself is the full amount out of your pocket.

Documents decide whether any tool helps or just produces a confident-looking wrong return. Gather these before you open any app: PAN and Aadhaar; Form 16 from every employer you worked for during the year; Form 16A for TDS on interest or other payments; bank statements and interest certificates; rent receipts and the rent agreement if you claim HRA; investment and premium receipts for every deduction you will claim; capital-gains statements from every broker; home loan interest and principal certificates; and medical bills and premium receipts for health claims. Then, before submitting anything, open AIS, TIS, and Form 26AS on the portal and reconcile them line by line against your documents. If AIS shows interest you forgot, a dividend you missed, or a transaction you do not recognise, fix it in the return or use the AIS feedback option — do not submit and hope the department missed it too, because AIS exists precisely so it does not miss it. For freelancers and small businesses, the same discipline applies to invoices, expense records, and advance tax challans; a tool that organises those papers is worth more than one with a fancier chatbot. ## What to avoid

Avoid any tool or person who guarantees a refund amount, a zero-tax outcome, or freedom from notices before seeing your documents — no honest tool can promise that, and the guarantee is itself the warning sign. Avoid sharing OTPs, passwords, or bank logins with any tool, agent, or chatbot, including one that claims it needs them to "auto-file faster"; the official routes use Aadhaar OTP and net banking verification through the portal itself, not through a stranger's login form. Avoid filing from screenshots or forwarded messages alone: reconcile against AIS, TIS, and Form 26AS, and keep the filed computation and acknowledgement for at least the years the department can reopen. Avoid the trap of buying deductions in March without checking whether the new regime already makes them worthless for you. Avoid trusting a chatbot's slab, section number, or due date without checking the portal, especially in a year when the Act itself has been renumbered. And avoid the most human trap of all: filing on the last day, discovering a mismatch, and submitting anyway to beat the clock. A late, correct return with a fee is better than an on-time return you know is wrong, but the only good plan is starting early enough that you never have to choose.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can AI file my ITR completely on its own?**
No. The tools in this article read documents, ask guided questions, import data, and run checks, but the official submission and verification happen on the e-filing portal, and you remain responsible for every figure. Even the government's Kar Saathi chatbot only guides; it does not file. Treat AI as a fast assistant and a careful checker, never as the filer.

**Which tool is best for a salary-only return?**
The free official portal is usually enough, because your Form 16, AIS, and TIS already cover most of the data. Add ClearTax's free tier or myITreturn only if you want guided questions and extra error checks. Paying for assisted filing on a simple salary return buys convenience, not a different tax outcome.

**Which tool is best for capital gains and trading income?**
Quicko, because of its broker imports from platforms such as Zerodha, Groww, and Upstox and its AIS and TIS matching. ClearTax is the alternative if your return also mixes foreign income or multiple sources. Whichever you pick, reconcile every broker statement against AIS yourself before submitting.

**Do I need the old regime to claim 80C and 80D deductions?**
Yes, on the sources checked: the popular deductions, including 80C up to ₹1.5 lakh and 80D health premiums up to ₹25,000 or ₹50,000 for senior citizens, apply under the old regime, not the default new regime. Salaried taxpayers without business income can compare both regimes each year, so run both computations in your tool before choosing.

**What documents do I need before I start filing?**
PAN and Aadhaar, Form 16 from each employer, Form 16A where applicable, bank statements and interest certificates, rent receipts and agreement for HRA, investment and premium proofs for deductions, capital-gains statements, home loan certificates, and medical bills. Then reconcile AIS, TIS, and Form 26AS against those documents before submitting.

**What happens if I file but forget to e-verify?**
Your filing is incomplete, and the department can treat an unverified return as invalid. E-verify at once with Aadhaar OTP, net banking, an electronic verification code, or a digital signature, or send the signed ITR-V to the Centralized Processing Center in Bengaluru within the allowed window. Check the portal for the current verification window, since it is time-limited.

**Is it safe to upload Form 16 and PAN details to a private tax tool?**
Only on a reputable platform with bank-level security, and only the documents that tool needs for your return. Never share passwords, OTPs, or bank logins with any tool or chatbot, and never paste full statements into a general chatbot. If a platform asks for more access than the filing requires, stop and use the official portal instead.

**Are the prices in this article final?**
No. Prices and free limits in this article come from third-party comparisons and provider pages that disagree with each other in places, and they change often. Re-check the official pricing and help page of any tool before paying, and never promise yourself a deadline based on a paid feature until you have confirmed it is still included at that price.

## Final verdict

Start free on the official e-filing portal, use ClearTax or Quicko for guided filing, and always reconcile AIS and Form 16 before you submit.
