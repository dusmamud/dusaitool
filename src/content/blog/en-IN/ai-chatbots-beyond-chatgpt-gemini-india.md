Most Indian users know two chatbots: ChatGPT and Gemini. That is not a bad starting point, but it misses the tools that are better for specific jobs: Claude for long documents and code, Perplexity for answers with citations, DeepSeek for free chat with no published cap, and Copilot for work that already lives in Microsoft apps. Using the right chatbot for the job saves time, and it saves money, because you do not need to pay for a tool whose free tier already covers your job.

The short answer for India is this: keep ChatGPT for everyday chat, because its free plan states unlimited everyday text chats, and keep Gemini for Google work, because its free tier is inside any Google account and its cheapest paid step is only 4.99 dollars a month. Add Claude free for long writing, code, and documents, and add Perplexity free for sourced answers, knowing its advanced searches are only three to five a day. Try DeepSeek free for long, cost-free chat, with a privacy warning. Everything else is for a specific need, not for everyday use.

This article is written for India on purpose: Hindi, Hinglish, and English use, a normal phone and laptop, prices in dollars and rupees where Indian checks give them, and no assumption that you have a paid plan or a US account. It also carries a warning that matters for chatbots more than for image tools: a chatbot can invent a plausible-sounding fact, statistic, or citation, and the more confident it sounds, the more you must check it. Perplexity is the exception that helps here, because it cites sources, but even its citations must be opened and checked for the exact claim.

If you read nothing else, remember three rules. First, a five-hour clock is not a message count. Gemini and Claude both refresh limits every five hours up to a weekly cap, which means a long session can stop suddenly and you must wait. Plan long work in the morning, not at midnight before a deadline. Second, citations are not proof. ## ChatGPT and Gemini: the baseline, and why you already have them

ChatGPT free is the baseline for everyday chat in November 2026, because OpenAI lists unlimited text chats on its free plan. The caps sit elsewhere: uploads, image creation, voice, deep research, and memory are each limited, the GPT Instant context is 27,000 tokens, and ads may show in some countries. ChatGPT Go is 8 dollars a month and Plus is 20 dollars a month in the checks. For everyday writing, questions, study mode, and short drafts, ChatGPT free is enough, and it is the tool to keep for tasks where you do not need citations or a very long document.

Gemini free is the most complete free alternative in November 2026 in one comparison, and the cheapest paid step in another, because it lives inside any Google account and gives Flash-Lite model chat, Deep Research, image generation and editing with Nano Banana 2, music generation, and audio overviews on free. Its limit refreshes every five hours until you reach a weekly cap, and its context window is 32,000 tokens without a paid plan. Google AI Plus is 4.99 dollars a month, or 399 rupees in India checks, for twice the limits and a 128,000-token context, and AI Pro is 19.99 dollars a month, or 1,950 rupees in India checks, for four times the limits. Deep Research may be unavailable at peak demand on the no-plan tier, so do not promise it on a busy day.

For Indian users, Gemini has a practical advantage: it is already inside your Google account, your Gmail, your Docs, and your YouTube history, if you choose to connect them. That makes it easy for Google Workspace work, for summarizing your own Docs, and for image editing with Nano Banana. ChatGPT has a practical advantage too: its free text chats are unlimited in the stated plan, which makes it better for long, back-and-forth writing sessions where Gemini's five-hour clock might stop you. Use both free, and use each for what it does best, instead of paying for one to do both jobs badly.

## Claude: the best free writer, for long documents and code

Claude free is the best free writer in the November 2026 comparison, and the reason is its feature list, not its model name alone. Free includes Claude Sonnet and Haiku, web search, file creation, code execution, Artifacts, and up to five Projects, after SMS phone verification. Its context window is listed up to one million tokens, varying by model, which is why you open Claude for a long file. Opus, Research, and Claude Code stay paid, and there is no image generation on free. Claude Pro is 20 dollars a month, or 17 dollars a month billed annually, and gives at least five times more usage.

Claude's cap is a session-based usage limit that resets every five hours, with no fixed message count, and it varies with demand. That means a long document session can stop without warning, and you must wait out the reset. For a student writing a long report, a developer debugging code, or a teacher drafting a long lesson plan, that is still worth it, because Claude's long-document handling and code explanations are cleaner and less hyped than ChatGPT's in the tests. Claude's Artifacts feature, which shows code, documents, and interactive previews in a side panel, is also genuinely useful for building and checking work, not just chatting about it.

Use Claude free in a specific way. Break long work into Projects, one per subject or assignment, and keep files inside the Project so Claude can refer to them. Ask for an outline first, then for a draft section by section, and check each section before you ask for the next one. That way, a five-hour reset costs you one section, not a whole document. For code, ask Claude to explain each function, then test the code yourself, because AI code can look correct and still fail on an edge case. Claude is a writing and coding partner, not a compiler or a teacher, and its output must be tested like any other draft.

## Perplexity: sourced answers, when a few searches a day cover the job

Perplexity is different from ChatGPT, Gemini, and Claude, because it is built to answer with cited sources, with Perplexity picking the model for you. Its free tier gives answers with cited sources, but Pro Searches are only three to five a day by Perplexity's own help pages, with one Research a month in one India check, and no image generation. File uploads are limited. Perplexity Pro is 20 dollars a month, or 17 dollars a month billed annually, and 1,999 rupees on the App Store in one India check.

Perplexity is best when you need to know whether a claim is true, and where it came from, not when you need a long creative draft. For news, for a product price, for a government rule, or for a scientific fact, Perplexity's citations save you the step of searching separately and then checking. For a story, an essay, or a script, ChatGPT or Claude free will give you a better draft. Do not waste your three to five Pro Searches a day on questions that a normal web search or a ChatGPT free chat can answer. Save them for claims that matter: a deadline, a price, a rule, or a fact you will publish or submit.

Open every citation that matters. Perplexity cites sources, but a citation can support a different claim than the one you asked about, or it can be from an old page, a low-quality source, or a page that does not actually contain the number. Check the date, the source, and the exact sentence. If the source does not support the claim, the answer is wrong, even if it has a citation. That habit, opening the source, is what turns Perplexity from a fast answer machine into a reliable research tool.

## DeepSeek, Copilot, and Meta AI: free chat, Microsoft work, and social chat

DeepSeek free is the most generous free chat in one sense: the web chat and apps, with DeepThink and Search modes, have no published message cap, and there is no chat subscription at all. The API is priced per token for builders, but the chat itself is free. That makes DeepSeek useful for long, cost-free chat, for reasoning problems, and for search-mode questions, especially for students who cannot pay for any chatbot. The warning is privacy: chats are stored on DeepSeek's servers, and DeepSeek is a Chinese company, which matters if you are typing sensitive personal, business, or government information. Do not type passwords, private documents, or sensitive personal data into any free chatbot, and be extra careful with DeepSeek for sensitive work. For general learning, coding questions, and maths problems, DeepSeek free is a strong option.

Microsoft Copilot free gives web-grounded chat with no sign-in, plus chat history, image creation, longer chats, and voice once you sign in with a Microsoft account. Its depth limit is inside Office: Copilot inside Word, Excel, and Outlook needs a Microsoft 365 plan, with Microsoft 365 Personal listed at 689 rupees in one India check and 9.99 dollars a month in another. If your work already lives in Microsoft 365, Copilot is convenient for summarizing emails, drafting in Word, and searching the web without leaving Microsoft apps. If your work lives in Google Workspace, Gemini free is the equivalent choice. Do not pay for both.

Meta AI free gives a daily limit that resets, with no numbers published, and Meta is testing limits on heavier features. Meta AI lives inside WhatsApp, Instagram, and Facebook, which makes it the chatbot many Indian users already have without installing anything. That convenience is real for quick questions, for image generation inside a chat, and for asking about a post you just saw. It is not a replacement for Claude for long writing, or for Perplexity for sourced research, because its daily limit is unpublished and its answers are designed for social apps, not for documents. Use Meta AI for quick, low-stakes questions, and use a dedicated chatbot for work you will submit or publish.

## Using chatbots in India: language, privacy, and verification

Language in chatbots works like language in study tools: write in the language you think in, and check technical terms in the language you will submit in. You can ask ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, and DeepSeek to answer in Hindi, Hinglish, or English, and for many Indian users, learning in Hindi first and then writing the answer in English technical terms works well. For Hindi answers, check spelling and technical terms carefully, because AI Hindi can mix formal and informal terms in the same answer. For English answers, learn the exact technical terms your teacher, examiner, or client uses, because an AI answer that uses a generic term instead of the exact term can still lose marks or confuse a client.

Privacy is the most important rule for chatbots, and it is simple: do not type anything into a free chatbot that you would not want stored on a company's servers. That includes passwords, bank details, private documents, exam papers you should not share, and sensitive personal information. Claude requires SMS phone verification, which ties your account to your phone number. DeepSeek stores chats on its servers. Duck.ai needs no account, but it still processes your chat. For sensitive work, use a paid plan with clear data terms, or do not use a chatbot at all for that document. For general learning, coding questions, and public information, free chatbots are safe and useful.

Verification is the second rule, and it applies to every chatbot, including Perplexity. Check facts, dates, prices, and rules against an official source before you publish, submit, or act on them. Check code by running it. Check maths by solving it yourself. Check a citation by opening it. A chatbot that sounds confident is not a source, and a chatbot with a citation is only as good as that citation. The students and creators who use chatbots well are not the ones who ask the most questions. They are the ones who check the most answers.

## What to avoid

Do not trust a chatbot answer without checking it, especially for facts, dates, prices, and rules. Do not use a chatbot to write an assignment you are supposed to write yourself, without checking your course rules, and do not submit AI text as your own work without disclosure when your course requires it. Do not type sensitive personal, financial, or private documents into any free chatbot. Do not waste Perplexity Pro Searches on questions a normal search can answer, and do not plan a deadline around Gemini Deep Research at peak demand, when it may be unavailable on free. Do not pay for five chatbots at once. Pay for one chatbot, for one reason, after free use has proven that reason over a month. And do not assume a free plan is permanent. Chatbot free limits change frequently, with five-hour clocks, weekly caps, and daily limits all moving, so check the current plan page before you rely on a free tier for a deadline.

A final trap is chatbot hopping. Beginners often ask the same question to five chatbots, get five different answers, and then trust the answer they like most, not the answer that is true. Ask one chatbot, check its answer against a source, and only then ask a second chatbot if the first answer was unclear or contradictory. More chatbots do not make an answer truer. Sources do.
