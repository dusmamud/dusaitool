A podcast in India is not one job. Recording guests, cleaning noisy audio, cutting filler words, editing by transcript, and publishing with an RSS feed are five different jobs, and no single free tool does all five well. The tool that records best does not always edit best, and the tool that cleans best does not host your show. The free stack that actually works is **a combination, not a single app**.

The short answer for India is this: record free in [Spotify for Creators](https://creators.spotify.com/), which gives free hosting and a basic recorder with Spotify distribution included. Clean free in [Adobe Podcast](https://podcast.adobe.com/en), whose *Enhance Speech* takes free files up to 30 minutes and 500 MB, with only an hour a day, and whose free Studio projects top out at 30 minutes with two downloads a day. Edit free in [Audacity](https://www.audacityteam.org/), which is free and open source with no caps, editing on a waveform, or in [Descript](https://www.descript.com/) free, which gives 60 media minutes a month with transcript editing and a watermark on video. For remote guests, [Riverside](https://riverside.com/)'s free plan gives two hours one-off at 720p with a watermark, while Pro costs 24 dollars a month billed yearly.

This article is written for India on purpose: podcasters, YouTubers, teachers, freelancers, and small businesses, Hindi, Hinglish, and English audio, a normal phone and laptop, and no assumption that you have a paid plan, a studio, or a US account. It also carries a warning that matters for audio creators more than for any other group in this series: no editor can fix a bad call after the fact, so **recording quality matters more than editing tools**, and a remote guest recorded badly will sound bad no matter which AI cleanup you use.

## Descript: the transcript editor, with an hour meter

Descript is the editor: Hobbyist costs 16 dollars per person per month billed annually, Creator 24 dollars, Business 50 dollars, with a free plan at 60 media minutes a month. Transcript editing is still the fastest way to cut a talky episode: you edit the text, and the audio edits itself, cutting filler words, mistakes, and long pauses without touching a waveform. *Studio Sound* and filler-word removal are included on paid plans rather than bolted on, and Descript's editor is deeper, with AI voice clones and DAW timeline export on Creator.

Descript free gives 60 media minutes a month, 100 AI credits one time, and watermarked video, which is enough to learn the editor before you import a real archive, but not enough to run a show. Its Hobbyist plan includes 10 media hours and 400 AI credits a month, and cannot buy top-ups when either runs out, which means a busy month can stop you mid-episode. Media hours count imported and recorded files, so importing a backlog of old episodes burns the cap before you edit a single new episode. Use Descript free in a specific way: import one short episode, learn transcript editing, cut filler words, and export, then decide whether transcript editing saves you enough time to justify Hobbyist, because if you prefer waveform editing, Audacity free does it for nothing.

For Indian podcasters, Descript's [transcription](/blog/free-ai-voice-tools-india/) languages matter: it transcribes in 25 languages, but Hindi and Hinglish transcription quality varies, and a Hinglish episode may transcribe partly in Hindi script and partly in English, which makes transcript editing messy. Test Descript free on a five-minute Hinglish clip first, and check whether the transcript is clean enough to edit by text, or whether you are better off editing by waveform in Audacity and using Descript only for cleanup.

## Adobe Podcast: free cleanup, with a 30-minute wall

Adobe Podcast's free plan includes transcript-based editing in Studio, but free projects are capped at 30 minutes and two downloads a day. You cut, copy, and paste audio like a text document in Studio, which Adobe still labels beta, and free Studio recording is unlimited, capturing each person as a separate 16-bit, 48 kHz WAV track. Since a March 2026 update, you can also upload separate tracks from Zoom, Riverside, or StreamYard and edit them in sync, which is useful for Indian podcasters who record guests on Zoom and then need to edit the tracks together.

The limit is length. Free Studio projects top out at 30 minutes, with two downloads a day and Podcast-branded audiograms. Enhance Speech, Adobe's answer to Studio Sound, takes free files up to 30 minutes and 500 MB, and only an hour a day. A 45-minute interview does not fit, and a daily show with two episodes will hit the two-download limit before lunch. Paying lifts that, with Premium coming with Adobe Firefly Pro at 19.99 dollars a month in the US. Adobe Podcast free is therefore best for a single messy voice file that needs noise reduction and nothing else, or for a short episode under 30 minutes, not for running a weekly hour-long show free.

Use Adobe Podcast free in a specific way: record your episode, split it into 30-minute parts if needed, clean each part with Enhance Speech, and download within the two-a-day limit, planning downloads the day before you publish. For Hindi audio, test Enhance Speech on a noisy Hindi clip first, because noise reduction can distort Hindi consonants and make speech sound robotic if over-applied, and a lightly cleaned clip often sounds more natural than a heavily cleaned one.

## Audacity, Riverside, and Spotify for Creators

Audacity is free and open source on Windows, Mac, and Linux, and it edits on a waveform, not a transcript, with no caps and no paid plan, because it stays free. For solo audio that never needs a transcript or a guest on another continent, Audacity is all you need: record, cut, normalize, compress, and export, with no meter, no watermark, and no subscription. Its limit is that it does not edit by transcript, does not clean with AI, and does not host your show, so a talky episode with many filler words will take longer to edit in Audacity than in Descript, but it will cost nothing, and for a beginner podcaster in India, nothing is the right price to start.

Riverside is the recorder: each guest is a local file, and the paid plan is what lets you download those files. Riverside Pro costs 24 dollars a month billed yearly, 288 dollars up front, with month-to-month at 35 dollars on one check, although the FAQ on the same page still says 29 dollars, so check current pricing before you pay. Pro includes unlimited text-based editing, silence and filler-word removal, *Magic Audio* cleanup, and clips and show notes, with 15 hours of separate-track downloads a month, and core AI tools like *Magic Clips* and *Magic Audio* need no credits, while credits are spent on extras like AI translation and AI B-roll, with Pro starting at 2,000 credits. Pick Riverside when remote guests are why your episodes sound rough, because each person is recorded locally and no editor can fix a bad call after the fact, and Riverside's separate tracks are the product, not an add-on.

Spotify for Creators, *formerly Spotify for Podcasters*, is free hosting and a basic recorder, with Spotify distribution included, for shows that need an RSS feed and a place to land, not a production suite. For an Indian podcaster starting out, Spotify for Creators free is the publishing step: record in Riverside or on your phone, clean in Adobe Podcast, edit in Audacity or Descript, and publish free on Spotify for Creators with an RSS feed that also distributes to other platforms. Do not expect Spotify for Creators to edit your audio: it hosts and distributes, and editing happens elsewhere.

## Using podcast AI in India: guests, language, and publishing

Remote guests are the hardest part of Indian podcasting, because internet quality varies, and a guest on a weak connection will sound bad no matter which tool you use.

- **Record each guest locally**, with Riverside or with a local recorder on the guest's phone, because a local file survives a bad call, while a call recording does not.
- **Ask every guest to record on their phone as a backup**, even if you record on Riverside, because a backup file has saved more episodes than any AI cleanup ever will.
- **For solo episodes, record in a quiet room**, close to the microphone, with no fan noise, because a clean recording needs less cleanup, and less cleanup sounds more natural.

Language for Indian podcasts is Hindi, Hinglish, English, and regional languages, and AI transcription and cleanup quality varies by language. English transcription is best, Hindi transcription is good but variable, and Hinglish transcription is the weakest, because tools must guess the script and the language for every sentence. Test your transcription tool on a five-minute clip in your actual language before you commit to transcript editing, and if the transcript is messy, edit by waveform in Audacity instead, using AI only for noise cleanup. For publishing, write show notes in the language your audience searches in, because a Hindi podcast with English show notes will be found by English searchers, not Hindi searchers, and vice versa.

Publishing in India also means distribution: Spotify is strong in India, YouTube is strong for video podcasts, and Apple Podcasts matters for iPhone listeners. Spotify for Creators free gives you an RSS feed and Spotify distribution, but you should also distribute to YouTube, with a static image or a video version, because many Indian listeners discover podcasts on [YouTube](/blog/ai-tools-for-youtube-creators-india/) first. For video podcasts, Descript's watermarked video on free is a problem, so export audio from Descript and make the video with [free AI video editing tools](/blog/free-ai-video-editing-captions-india/), or use Riverside's video recording with a paid plan when video matters.

## What to avoid

- Do not import a **backlog** of old episodes into Descript free, because media hours count imported files, and your backlog will burn the 60-minute cap before you edit anything new.
- Do not plan a **45-minute episode** around Adobe Podcast free, because free projects top out at 30 minutes with two downloads a day, and you will be stuck mid-episode.
- Do not rely on **Riverside free** as a plan, because it has no free plan, only a 14-day trial, with a reported two hours one-off at 720p with a watermark, and Pro is the real product.
- Do not use Alitu's call recorder for multi-guest episodes, because it puts every voice on a **single track**, and you cannot fix one guest's audio without affecting the others.
- Do not pay for **three podcast seats** when you have one host, because Descript Creator and Business seats are billed separately, and a three-person show is three seats.

A final trap is buying tools before you have ten episodes. Beginners often pay for Riverside Pro, Descript Creator, and Alitu at once, feel professional, and then stop after five episodes, because the tools did not make the show, the habit did. Record ten episodes free first, with Audacity, Adobe Podcast cleanup, and Spotify for Creators hosting, and pay only when a specific free limit, like Descript's 60 minutes or Adobe's 30 minutes, repeatedly blocks episodes you are actually publishing, because a tool you pay for but do not use is worse than a free tool you use every week.
