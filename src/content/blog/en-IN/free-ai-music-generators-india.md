AI music generators can make a full song with vocals, or a background track for a video, from a text prompt in minutes. That does not mean you can publish that song, monetise it, or release it on Spotify. The difference between a fun AI song and a usable AI track is not sound quality. It is **downloads and rights**. Many free plans let you generate music for free and then keep downloads, commercial use, or streaming release for paid plans, and discovering that after you have made your favourite song wastes the whole session.

The short answer for India is this: use *Suno* free to test full songs with vocals, knowing its free downloads are only **seven lifetime trial downloads** for personal, non-commercial use. Use *Mubert* free for [background tracks for videos](/blog/free-ai-video-generators-india/), knowing its free downloads are five a month for non-commercial use and that no Mubert plan allows **streaming release**. Use *AIVA* free for instrumental composition with three downloads a month, non-commercial, with credit required. Treat *Udio* free as on-platform creation only, because its downloads are disabled. Everything else is for a specific job, not for releasing music.

This article is written for India on purpose: creators, students, small shop owners, and musicians testing ideas, a normal phone and laptop, Hindi, Hinglish, and English songs, and no assumption that you have a paid plan, a record label, or a US account. It also carries a warning that matters for music more than for images: a song that sounds good is not a song you own. Ownership, download rights, and streaming rights are three different things, and a free plan can give you one without giving you the others.

If you read nothing else, remember three rules.

1. **Generation is not ownership.** A free plan that lets you generate 50 songs a day does not let you download or sell them.
2. **A lifetime download cap is not a monthly cap.** Suno free gives seven downloads in total, not seven a month, so spending them on test songs wastes your only downloads.

Check all three rights, generation, download, and release, before you make a song you plan to publish.

Use Suno free in a specific way:

For Indian creators, Udio's lesson is general: check download before you invest time in a song. A tool that disables downloads after a label partnership can change its value overnight, and a song you love on-platform is worthless for your channel if you cannot export it. Always test download with a short test song before you write lyrics, extend a track, or build a whole project in a new music tool.

## Using AI music in India: language, copyright, and Indian music

Language in AI music is a special problem. Suno and Udio can generate Hindi and Hinglish vocals, but AI vocals in Hindi can mispronounce words, mix formal and informal Hindi, or sing a word with the wrong stress, which native listeners notice immediately.

- For a Hindi song, generate a test verse first, listen with headphones, and check every word, especially names, places, and emotional words that carry the song's meaning.
- For a Hinglish song, write lyrics in the Hinglish you actually speak, not in formal Hindi that you would never sing.

AI songs often sound written, not sung, so edit lyrics for rhythm and for singability, not just for meaning.

Copyright in India works like copyright elsewhere for AI music: you cannot claim a song that copies a famous artist's voice, lyrics, or melody, even if an AI tool generated it. AI tools can imitate styles, and some tools block artist names, but imitation is not ownership, and a song that sounds like a famous artist can still be claimed. For Indian film songs, classical music, and folk songs, be extra careful, because those works have living rights holders and active enforcement. Use AI music for original compositions, not for recreating a famous song with a different singer. If you want to cover a famous song, get the proper licence, or do not publish it.

For Indian creators, the safest use of AI music is background music for videos, podcasts, and social posts, with a clear licence from a tool like Mubert or Loudly, or a paid Suno download with commercial rights. For original songs, use AI as a demo tool: generate a demo, write and sing the final version yourself, or pay for a download with commercial rights and keep the licence record. A demo that becomes a hit without a licence becomes a legal problem, not a success story.

## What to avoid

- Do not spend Suno's seven lifetime downloads on test songs, because they are personal and non-commercial only and they do not come back.
- Do not build a main song in Udio and expect to download it, because downloads are disabled.
- Do not assume a free track can be released on Spotify, because Mubert forbids streaming release on every plan and Suno requires a paid download first.
- Do not use an AI song that imitates a famous artist's voice or melody as if it were your own original, and do not publish a cover without a licence.
- Do not pay for five music tools at once. Pay for one tool, for one reason, after free use has proven that reason over a month.
- And do not trust a tool that calls itself free but keeps downloads for paid plans, without checking the download cap first.

A final trap is generating too many songs. Beginners often generate 100 songs in a week and then never finish any of them. Generate fewer songs, **finish one**, get feedback, and improve it. A finished song with a clear licence is worth more than 100 generated songs you cannot download, publish, or release.
