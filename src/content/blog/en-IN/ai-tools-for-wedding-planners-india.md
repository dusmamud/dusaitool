## The direct answer

If you run a wedding planning business in India, you do not need one magic AI tool that does everything. You need a small stack that covers four jobs: finding and tracking vendors, keeping the budget honest, building a timeline that every vendor actually follows, and keeping clients informed without answering the same question twenty times.

The most practical starting stack is this: Aisle Planner for wedding-specific planning, HoneyBook for proposals, contracts and payments, Timeline Genius for the final day-of timeline, Social Tables for seating and floor plans, Canva for invitations and mood boards, and a general chatbot such as ChatGPT or Gemini only for first drafts. For finding vendors in India, WedMeGood and WeddingWire.in are the directories couples already use, so a planner who is not visible and responsive there is missing enquiries. Notion can hold the whole system together if you prefer building your own trackers instead of paying for a second platform.

That stack works in India without a VPN. All of these tools are web-based, work in a normal browser, and are used by planners internationally. What changes in India is not the software, but the wedding itself: multi-day functions, large guest lists, family decision-makers rather than only the couple, venues with strict access and curfew windows, and vendors who often prefer phone calls and WhatsApp over email. Any tool that cannot handle a three-day wedding, a 500-guest list, and a vendor who confirms on WhatsApp will frustrate you, no matter how good its marketing looks.

Prices and free limits change often, and different comparison sources disagree about the exact monthly price of the same tool, because billing period, plan, and promotional offers differ. Treat every price in this article as a starting point from late 2026 research, not as a quote. Before you pay, or before you promise a client a deadline that depends on a paid tool, re-check the official pricing and help page of that tool. This article is general information only, not professional financial, legal, or tax advice, and you should check contracts, payment terms, and tax treatment with a qualified professional before relying on them.

## Aisle Planner: the wedding-native core

Aisle Planner is built specifically for wedding and event professionals, and that specialisation is its main advantage. It covers client management, checklists, timelines, budgets, guest lists, seating, vendor tracking, and a client portal where couples can see progress, complete tasks, and upload inspiration. It also covers the sales side on its sales plans: leads, proposals, contracts, e-signatures, invoices, and payments.

For an Indian planner, the project template matters more than any AI feature. Build one template for a typical multi-day wedding, with separate checklists for mehndi, sangeet, ceremony, and reception, and with tasks assigned to the couple, to family members, and to vendors. Reuse that template for every wedding, and change only the dates, venue, and vendor names. That single habit saves more time than any chatbot.

On pricing, sources agree on the shape but differ on the exact starting figure depending on the plan described. Its sales-only plan has been reported at around 49.99 dollars per month, while planning plans that include timelines and the client portal start higher, at around 69.99 dollars per month for up to 15 projects, rising with project count. A 30-day free trial has been reported. Because Aisle Planner prices by active projects, a planner handling many weddings at once should calculate the annual cost at their real project count, not at the cheapest headline price. Re-check the official pricing page before paying, and confirm whether yearly billing still offers a discount.

The honest limitation is the learning curve. New users report that the system feels deep at first, and a planner who tries to set up every feature in week one will stall. Start with three things only: the client portal, the budget tracker, and the timeline. Add seating and design tools only after those three are working on a live wedding.

## HoneyBook: proposals, contracts, and getting paid

HoneyBook is not wedding-specific. It is a client-flow tool for service businesses, and its strength is the business side: polished proposals, contracts with e-signatures, invoices, online payments, scheduling, automations, and a client portal. For a planner whose bottleneck is sales administration and cash flow, rather than timeline design, HoneyBook is often the better first purchase.

Its AI value is practical rather than spectacular. HoneyBook AI can help draft client communications, and its templates and automations move an enquiry from first message to signed contract to paid deposit without the planner retyping the same details. Clients can pay by card, bank transfer, and common wallets depending on the region and plan, with payment plans and reminders on supported plans.

Pricing sources disagree slightly, which is itself a useful warning. Some sources list HoneyBook from 29 dollars per month billed yearly, with higher plans at 49 and 109 dollars per month, while others list monthly billing figures of 36 dollars and above for the same tier. A 30-day trial has been reported. The disagreement is not a mystery: yearly and monthly billing give different monthly figures, and time-limited offers change the effective price. Do not quote a client a figure from this article. Open the official pricing page, choose yearly or monthly billing, and note the exact figure for the plan you need, including how many team members it allows.

The limitation to understand is that HoneyBook is weaker on wedding timelines than Aisle Planner. A planner who buys HoneyBook alone will still need a timeline tool for the wedding day. That is not a flaw in HoneyBook, it is a reason to pair it with Timeline Genius or Aisle Planner rather than expecting one tool to do both jobs perfectly.

## Dubsado: workflows for planners who like control

Dubsado covers forms, proposals, contracts, invoicing, scheduling, and automated workflows, and it appeals to planners who want deep customisation. You can build a workflow that sends a questionnaire after an enquiry, follows up if the couple does not reply, sends a contract when they confirm, and sends an invoice when the contract is signed, all without manual chasing.

Reported pricing is around 35 dollars per month, or around 335 dollars per year for its Starter plan, with a trial that allows a limited number of clients. Team seats are included on some plans, which matters for a small studio. As with every tool here, confirm the current trial length and seat limit on the official page before committing.

The warning from experienced users is consistent: setup takes careful planning. A workflow built in a hurry will send the wrong form at the wrong time, and fixing that during a live wedding season is painful. Build Dubsado workflows in the off-season, test them with a fake couple, and only then use them with real clients. If you do not enjoy building systems, Aisle Planner or HoneyBook will feel easier, because more of the wedding structure is already built for you.

## Timeline Genius: the day-of document everyone trusts

Timeline Genius does one job, and it does it well: it builds day-of timelines with vendor schedules, contact details, and minute-by-minute plans. On the wedding day, this is the document that matters most, because the photographer, the caterer, the decorator, the DJ, and the venue manager all work from it. If they work from different versions, the ceremony starts late, the food service collides with the speeches, and the planner spends the day correcting confusion instead of managing the event.

For Indian weddings, build the timeline backwards from fixed points that cannot move: the muhurat or ceremony start time, the venue access time, the meal service window, and the venue curfew. Then place vendor arrival, setup, sound check, and photo slots around those anchors. Give every vendor only their own relevant slice plus the full timeline link, and state clearly that the shared link is the only current version. Do not send five different PDF attachments to five vendors, because that creates five versions that drift apart.

Timeline Genius is deliberately narrow. It is not a budget tracker, a guest-list manager, or a contract tool. Use it for the final timeline, and keep the long-term plan in Aisle Planner or Notion. Check its official page for current pricing and trial terms, because comparison sources describe it as a dedicated paid tool rather than a free one, and terms change.

## Social Tables: seating and floor plans that prevent chaos

Social Tables is the industry-standard floor-planning and seating tool, used to draw venue layouts, place tables, assign guests, and share diagrams with venues and caterers. Its pricing page reports over 96,000 planner users and millions of diagrams created, which is a sign of how widely it is trusted for this specific job.

Its free plan is genuinely useful for trying the tool: one user account, a small number of events and diagrams per event, and an attendee list limit. Paid plans rise from around 49 dollars per month for occasional use to higher tiers for frequent planners, and the pricing page states that paid plans carry a 12-month commitment. That commitment is the detail to notice before upgrading. A planner who does only a few weddings a year may be better served by the free plan plus a wedding platform's built-in seating tool, rather than a year-long subscription.

For Indian weddings, seating is not a minor detail. Large guest lists, family groupings, dietary requirements, and VIP tables all interact, and a seating error becomes visible to hundreds of guests at once. Export the final seating chart as a caterer-ready document, share it from one source, and freeze changes after a clear cut-off time, so the venue team is not working from yesterday's version on the morning of the wedding.

## WedMeGood and WeddingWire.in: where Indian couples find vendors

No AI feature replaces being found. WedMeGood is India's large wedding vendor directory, covering venues, photographers, makeup artists, decorators, caterers, and planners, with city-wise listings and reviews. WeddingWire.in, the Indian version of the international WeddingWire platform, lists thousands of vendors by category, including over 15,000 photography and video vendors, over 7,500 wedding planners, and thousands of caterers and card vendors according to its own category pages.

For a planner, these directories serve three purposes. First, they are research tools: you can shortlist photographers, decorators, and venues in a new city, read reviews, and compare styles before contacting anyone. Second, they are lead sources: couples searching for a planner in their city will find your profile, so keep photos current, answer enquiries quickly, and collect honest reviews after each wedding. Third, they are a reality check on pricing and availability, because vendor listings reveal what competitors charge and which dates are already busy.

Do not rely on a directory listing alone. Verify every vendor yourself before recommending them to a client: visit a live event if possible, check recent work rather than only portfolio highlights, confirm backup equipment and backup team members, and get cancellation and payment terms in writing. A directory review is a starting point, not a guarantee, and the planner's reputation is what the client trusts when something goes wrong.

## Canva: invitations, mood boards, and social content

Canva is the design tool most planners and couples already know, and its wedding template library is large, including Indian styles when you prompt and search specifically for them. Use it for invitations, save-the-date cards, mood boards, day-of stationery such as welcome signs and seating charts, and social media posts that market your business.

Its AI features, grouped under Magic Studio, include auto-layout from a prompt, text generation for copy, and image tools. The free plan covers templates and some AI features permanently, while Pro unlocks the fuller AI set and the video-clip tool. Reported Pro pricing in India is around 499 rupees per month or around 4,500 rupees per year, plus GST, though other sources list slightly different annual figures, so re-check the official Canva India pricing page. The difference between sources is small, but it proves the general rule of this article: never copy a price from a blog into a client quote.

Two practical warnings matter for wedding work. First, AI image generation defaults to generic or Western aesthetics unless you prompt explicitly for Indian detail, such as marigold and saffron palettes, jaali borders, or a Rajasthani palace setting. Second, print specifications must be checked with your printer before ordering in bulk: colours, bleed, paper size, and font embedding all affect the final card, and a design that looks perfect on screen can print badly. Always order one proof print before a full run.

## ChatGPT and Gemini: drafting, not deciding

General chatbots are the admin workhorses of a planning business. Use ChatGPT or Gemini to turn a rough vendor brief into a clear email, draft a timeline note in plain language, rewrite a confusing budget update so a client can understand it, brainstorm ceremony ideas from a couple's preferences, or draft invitation wording in English, Hindi, or another Indian language. Starting from a structured draft instead of a blank page is where the hours are saved.

Both tools offer free tiers with limits, and paid plans in India have been reported at around 399 rupees per month for entry-level plans and around 1,999 rupees per month for fuller plans, depending on the provider and plan. Those figures change, and the free tier's limits change too, so check the official page for the plan you actually use. Gemini integrates closely with Google Docs, which suits planners who already live in Google Workspace, while ChatGPT is widely used for drafting and brainstorming.

The safety rules are non-negotiable. Never paste a couple's full names, venue contracts, payment details, or identity documents into a general chatbot without understanding your privacy obligations. Use anonymised examples for drafting, keep contracts and payments in your secure planning system, and never let an AI tool send a vendor commitment in your name without your review. A chatbot will happily invent a muhurat time, a venue address, or a price if you forget to pin the facts, and a polished email that confirms the wrong time is still a wrong confirmation. Every date, amount, and name in an AI draft must be checked by a human before it leaves your business.

## Notion: build your own system if you prefer control

Notion is not wedding-specific, but it is the most flexible hub in this list. You can build client portals, vendor trackers, master checklists, budget sheets, and note summaries in one place, and share a clean portal with each couple. Its free plan exists, and its AI features sit on paid plans, so a new planner can start free and upgrade only when the AI summaries and automations are genuinely needed.

The risk with Notion is overbuilding. It is easy to spend a month designing a perfect system with ten linked databases and never use it on a live wedding. Start with one template: a vendor tracker with columns for vendor type, contact, quoted price, advance paid, balance due, and confirmation status, plus a checklist and a budget total. Use that template for three weddings, note what breaks, and only then add a second database. A simple system used every day beats a perfect system used never.

Notion pairs well with the specialist tools above. Use Aisle Planner or HoneyBook for money and contracts, Timeline Genius for the day, Social Tables for seating, and Notion as the internal knowledge base where you store vendor notes, venue quirks, and lessons from each wedding. That division keeps each tool doing the job it does best.

## How to use AI in India: vendors, budgets, and timelines

Indian weddings add complexity that generic wedding advice often misses, so adapt the tools rather than copying a foreign workflow. Start vendor management with a single tracker, whether in Aisle Planner or Notion, and record not just the vendor's name and price, but their city, language, backup contact, advance and balance schedule, and cancellation terms. When a vendor confirms on WhatsApp, copy the confirmed facts into the tracker the same day, because a chat message buried under fifty others is not a record.

For budgets, build the budget with the couple before booking anything, and separate fixed costs such as venue and catering from flexible costs such as decor upgrades and entertainment. Track advances and balances separately from quoted prices, because a wedding that looks within budget on quotes can still fail on cash flow if too many balances fall due in the same week. Share a simple budget summary with the couple at fixed intervals, such as after every major booking, rather than sending a spreadsheet they never open. When AI drafts a budget update, check every number against the tracker, because a single wrong figure destroys trust faster than a late reply.

For timelines, work in three layers. The planning timeline covers months: venue booking, vendor booking, outfit trials, invitation dispatch, and final guest-count confirmation. The week-of timeline covers rehearsals, deliveries, setup days, and vendor arrival windows. The day-of timeline, built in Timeline Genius, covers minutes. AI can draft all three layers from your constraints, but you must correct them with venue knowledge that no chatbot has: how long decor setup really takes in that banquet hall, whether the venue allows early access, and how monsoon traffic affects guest arrival. Keep one master timeline per wedding, date every version, and tell vendors explicitly which version is current.

Client communication is where AI saves the most time in India, because decisions often involve parents and extended family, not only the couple. Draft a weekly update in a chatbot, then edit it into your own voice, with clear decisions needed and deadlines attached. Use a client portal so family members can see the same plan, rather than running five parallel WhatsApp threads with five different versions of the truth. When a decision changes, update the portal and the timeline together, and send one message pointing to the update, rather than scattering corrections across chats.

## What to avoid

Avoid letting AI send commitments, confirm prices, or promise dates without human review. A chatbot draft is a first draft, and the planner's check is what makes it professional. Avoid pasting private client data, contracts, or payment details into general AI tools. Avoid buying every tool at once: start with one planning platform and one design tool, prove them on two or three weddings, and add tools only when a specific pain remains. Avoid trusting a price, a free limit, or a feature list from any article, including this one, without re-checking the official page on the day you pay. Avoid building a system so complex that your team stops using it during wedding season, because an unused system is worse than a simple spreadsheet that everyone updates. Avoid recommending a vendor from a directory listing alone without your own verification, and avoid promising a client a vendor, a date, or a price that you have not confirmed in writing with that vendor.

Also avoid the trap of AI-generated visuals that misrepresent the wedding. An AI mood board is inspiration, not a promise of what the decorator will deliver, and an AI invitation mock-up is not a print-ready file until the printer confirms it. Show clients the difference clearly, label AI drafts as drafts, and get written approval on final designs, final guest counts, and final timelines before production begins.

## FAQ

**Which single AI tool should an Indian wedding planner buy first?**

A wedding-native planner such as Aisle Planner is the best first purchase if you want one system for timelines, budgets, vendors, and client portals. If your bigger problem is proposals and payments, buy HoneyBook first and add a timeline tool alongside it. There is no single tool that does both jobs perfectly, so choose by your biggest bottleneck, not by the longest feature list.

**Do these tools work in India without a VPN?**

Yes, the tools in this article are web-based and work in India in a normal browser without a VPN, based on their public availability and use by Indian planners and businesses. If a specific tool ever shows a regional restriction, check its official help page rather than assuming a workaround, and do not route client data through an untrusted VPN to bypass a restriction.

**Are there genuinely free tools for wedding planners?**

Yes, but free means limited, not unlimited. Canva has a permanent free plan, Notion has a free plan, Social Tables has a free plan with event and attendee limits, and ChatGPT and Gemini have free tiers with usage limits. Wedding platforms such as Aisle Planner and HoneyBook offer trials rather than permanent free plans, according to the sources reviewed. A free plan is enough to learn a tool, but a busy planner should budget for at least one paid platform.

**How should a planner use AI for vendor management?**

Keep one vendor tracker with contact, price, advance, balance, backup contact, and cancellation terms, and update it the same day a vendor confirms, even if the confirmation arrived on WhatsApp or by phone. Use AI to draft vendor briefs and follow-up emails, but check every date and price before sending, and keep the signed agreement and payment record in your planning or contract system, not only in a chat thread.

**How should a planner build a wedding budget with AI?**

Start with the couple's total budget, split it into venue, catering, decor, photography, outfits, entertainment, and contingency, and track quoted prices separately from advances and balances due. Use AI to draft budget summaries and to explain trade-offs in plain language, but verify every figure against your tracker, because an AI summary can repeat a wrong number very confidently. Review the budget after every major booking, not only at the end.

**Can AI build the wedding day timeline?**

AI can draft a timeline from your constraints, including ceremony time, venue access, meal service, and vendor arrival windows, and that draft saves hours. It cannot replace your venue knowledge, so correct the draft for real setup times, curfew rules, and travel delays, then publish one dated version from Timeline Genius or your planning platform and tell every vendor that version is the only current one.

**Should planners trust AI prices and free limits in articles?**

No, not without re-checking. Prices, trial lengths, and free limits change, and sources disagree because billing periods and plans differ. Before you pay, or before you quote a price or a deadline to a client, open the tool's official pricing and help page and note the exact plan, billing period, and limits. That five-minute check prevents the most common and most embarrassing planner error.

**Is it safe to put client details into a chatbot?**

No, not without care. Do not paste full names, contracts, payment details, or identity documents into a general chatbot unless you understand your privacy obligations and the tool's data handling. Use anonymised drafts for wording help, keep contracts and payments in your secure system, and never let AI send a commitment in your name without your review.

**What is the cheapest sensible stack for a new planner?**

Start free: Notion for trackers, Canva free for designs, a chatbot free tier for drafts, and WedMeGood and WeddingWire.in for vendor research and leads. Add one paid platform, either Aisle Planner or HoneyBook, when you have steady enquiries and the free system starts costing you time. Add Timeline Genius and Social Tables paid plans only when seating and day-of timelines become regular paid work rather than occasional favours.

**Do Indian wedding planners need a separate tool for seating?**

Not always. Aisle Planner includes seating and layouts, and small weddings can be seated from a spreadsheet. Social Tables becomes worth paying for when guest lists are large, family groupings are complex, and the venue and caterer need a professional diagram. Try its free plan on a real wedding before committing to a year-long paid plan.

## Final verdict

Start with Aisle Planner for weddings, HoneyBook for money, and Canva plus a chatbot for drafts — check prices before you pay.

That verdict is deliberately boring, because wedding planning rewards boring reliability over novelty. The planner who keeps one vendor tracker, one budget, and one dated timeline, and who checks every AI draft before it reaches a client or a vendor, will deliver calmer weddings than the planner with ten subscriptions and no single source of truth. Choose the stack you will actually update during wedding season, verify vendors yourself, re-check prices on the official page before paying, and let AI do the drafting while you keep the decisions, the relationships, and the final check.
