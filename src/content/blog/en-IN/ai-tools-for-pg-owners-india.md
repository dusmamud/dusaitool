If you own a paying guest house in India, your business runs on two things that never stop: enquiries from students and working professionals looking for a room, and rent that must be collected on time every month. The daily trouble is small but constant. A room is vacant but the listing still says it is full, a tenant paid by UPI but nobody recorded which month it was for, a food complaint was mentioned in passing and forgotten, and a notice about water timing never reached the third floor. Good PGs fill up by word of mouth, but they empty just as fast when tenants feel ignored.

The direct answer is simple: start with WhatsApp Business for enquiries, room photos and notices, Google Forms so every enquiry arrives with the details you actually need, Google Sheets to track rooms, tenants, rent and deposits, ChatGPT to write polite rent reminders and house rules, and Google Calendar for rent due dates, inspections and maintenance. All five are free to begin with, and together they keep rooms full, rent on time, and tenants informed without a pile of paper registers.

## 1. WhatsApp Business: bookings and reminders that bring customers back

WhatsApp Business is the free business version of the WhatsApp your customers already use every day. It adds a business profile with your hours, services and location, quick replies for the questions you answer again and again, labels to sort customers, and a catalogue that lists your services with prices.

For a PG owner, this means a catalogue with room types, rent and photos, quick replies for the questions every enquirer asks, and a broadcast-style notice that reaches every tenant at once. A student who gets photos and the real rent within minutes is far more likely to visit than one who waits two days for a reply.

How to start: Set up your catalogue with three to five services and prices, write quick replies for price and timing, and send reminders only to customers who agreed to messages.

Watch out for: Never spam customers with daily offers, and never share a customer's phone number or personal details with anyone else.

## 2. Google Forms: enquiries and records without phone calls and paper slips

Google Forms is a free way to collect information without phone calls and paper slips. You can make a short form for enquiries, bookings or complaints, share its link on WhatsApp, and every answer lands in a Google Sheet automatically.

For a PG owner, this means one short form asking for name, joining date, budget, food preference and workplace or college. Every enquiry arrives complete, so the owner can answer with a straight yes or no instead of five rounds of questions on the phone.

How to start: Make a five-question enquiry form, share its link on WhatsApp, and check the answers in the linked Sheet every evening.

Watch out for: Keep forms short; a form with twenty questions will be ignored, while five clear questions will be answered.

## 3. Google Sheets: customers, jobs and cash in one sheet

Google Sheets is a free spreadsheet that works on any phone and becomes the record book of a small business. One sheet can list every customer, the service they took, the date, the amount paid and the next due date, so you can greet a regular by name and suggest the right service.

For a PG owner, this means one row per tenant: room number, name, joining date, monthly rent, deposit paid, and each month's payment status. At a glance the owner sees who has paid, who is late, and which room will be free next month.

How to start: Make one sheet with this month's customers, services and dates, and use it to send reminders next week.

Watch out for: Do not store a customer's full address, ID or payment details in a shared sheet; keep only what the business actually needs.

## 4. ChatGPT: messages, replies and offers in minutes

ChatGPT is an AI assistant that handles the writing a small business owner does every day. Give it the facts — service, price and date — and it writes a short, polite message in Hindi or English, replies to a customer asking about price, drafts a festival offer, or answers a complaint calmly.

For a PG owner, this means a polite rent reminder three days before the due date, a calm written reply when a tenant asks for more time, and house rules written in plain Hindi or English that every tenant receives on joining. Written rules end most arguments before they start.

How to start: Ask it to write a short Hindi reminder for customers whose last visit was three weeks ago, then read it and send it yourself.

Watch out for: AI can invent a price or a service you do not offer, so check every number and every promise before sending a message.

## 5. Google Calendar: bookings, visits and follow-ups without double-booking

Google Calendar is a free calendar that keeps bookings, visits and follow-ups in one place and reminds you before each one. You can create separate calendars for different staff or services, share them with your team, and set repeat reminders for regular customers.

For a PG owner, this means every rent due date, room inspection and maintenance job sitting in one calendar with reminders. When a geyser breaks or a fan needs repair, the job is booked the same day instead of being forgotten until a tenant complains twice.

How to start: Create one shared calendar for this week, add every booking to it, and turn on a reminder one hour before each visit.

Watch out for: A calendar only works if every booking goes into it, so do not keep some bookings only in your head or in chat messages.

## Using AI in India: PG owners

The pattern that works for Indian PGs is clear, written and respectful. Most tenants are students or young professionals who live on their phones, pay by UPI, and expect a reply within hours, not days. Put the real room photos, the real rent, and the real rules in writing before anyone moves in, because most disputes come from something that was said but never written down. Food, water timing, visitors and notice period are the four topics that cause most arguments, so write them down once and share them with every new tenant on WhatsApp.

## What to avoid

Avoid taking a deposit without a written receipt, promising facilities you do not provide, and entering a tenant's room without notice. Avoid keeping rent records only in your head, and never share a tenant's phone number, ID or personal details with other tenants or strangers.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the best AI tool for PG owners in India?**
There is no single best tool, because a PG owner needs bookings, records, reminders and customer trust. Start with WhatsApp Business, Google Forms, Google Sheets, ChatGPT and Google Calendar, and add paid software only when the free tools start losing jobs.


**Can AI replace the skilled work in this business?**
No. AI can organise bookings, write messages and keep records, but it cannot do the skilled work itself or judge quality. Every AI message must be checked by a human before it reaches a customer.


**How much do AI tools for PG owners cost in India?**
The starting set is free: the tools in this article all have free plans that cover daily work for a small business. Check current pricing on the official page before paying for anything.


**How do I remind customers without spamming them?**
Send one polite reminder at the right time, only to customers who agreed to messages, and stop if they ask you to. One useful reminder beats ten daily offers that get your number blocked.


**How do I handle a tenant who is late with rent?**
Send one polite written reminder three days before the due date and one on the day itself, with the UPI details included. If the tenant asks for more time, agree a new date in writing and record it in your sheet. Stay calm and written: a polite record protects both sides, while shouting loses a tenant and solves nothing.


**Should I show the room on a video call before a tenant visits?**
Yes, a short live video call builds trust and saves wasted visits, especially for students coming from another city. Show the actual room, the bathroom, the kitchen and the entry, and answer questions honestly. Never send old photos of a different room, because the tenant will discover the truth on arrival and leave.
