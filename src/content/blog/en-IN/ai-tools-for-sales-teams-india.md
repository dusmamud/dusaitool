A small sales team in India does not need an enterprise sales stack. It needs four things: a list of people who might buy, a way to contact them, one place to track every conversation, and a follow-up system that does not depend on anyone's memory. Most teams fail at the last two, not the first two. They buy a lead list, send one round of emails, and then lose track of who replied, who asked for a quote, and who said "call next month."

The short answer for India is this: use [Zoho CRM](https://www.zoho.com/crm/) free for your pipeline, because it is free forever for up to 3 users and it is built by an Indian company. Use [Apollo](https://www.apollo.io/pricing) free for finding leads, because its free plan gives 75 credits per seat per month for email and phone reveals. Use [Hunter](https://hunter.io/pricing) free for finding an email address when you already know the company and the person. Add [Snov.io](https://snov.io/pricing) trial for follow-up email sequences, because its trial renews every month with a small allowance. Everything else in this article is for a specific need, not for every team.

This article is written for India on purpose: small teams of two to ten people, selling to Indian businesses or to clients abroad from India, working in English, Hindi, or Hinglish, on normal phones and laptops, with no assumption of a paid plan, a US account, or a dedicated sales-ops person. It also carries a warning that matters more in sales than in most tool categories: prices and free limits change often, and some of the numbers below already disagree across sources. Before you pay for any plan, or promise a client or a manager a deadline based on a tool, re-check the official pricing and help page yourself.

## Zoho CRM: the free pipeline for a tiny Indian team

[Zoho CRM](https://www.zoho.com/crm/) is the obvious starting point for a small Indian sales team, and not only because it is free. Zoho is an Indian company, its support and billing understand Indian businesses, and its free edition is genuinely usable rather than a demo. The free edition is free forever for up to 3 users, which covers a founder plus two salespeople, or a founder, a salesperson, and an operations person.

What the free edition includes, per Zoho's own pricing page, is contact management, follow-up reminders, workflow automation, up to 10 custom email templates, tasks, meetings and calls tracking, standard reports, data import and export, and 5,000 API calls per day, plus mobile apps. That is enough to run a real pipeline: every lead gets a stage, every deal gets a value and a next step, and nobody has to remember follow-ups in their head. The workflow automation on the free plan is the feature most teams underuse. A simple rule like "when a deal moves to quotation sent, create a follow-up task in three days" prevents more lost deals than any AI writing feature.

The limits to know are specific. Data export is free only up to 10 times on the free edition, so do not treat Zoho free as a place you casually dump data in and out of. The free edition does not include built-in calling, mass email, or multiple pipelines; those arrive on the paid Standard plan and above. If your team grows past three users, everyone needs a paid seat, so plan for that cost before you build your whole process around the free tier. Still, for a team of three or fewer, Zoho CRM free is the best free pipeline available in India in these checks, and the Indian billing and support make it easier to pay later than most foreign tools.

## HubSpot CRM: the simple free pipeline for global clients

[HubSpot CRM](https://www.hubspot.com/products/crm) is the best-known free CRM in the world, and its free plan is a good fit for Indian teams that sell to clients abroad or want the cleanest, simplest interface. The free plan includes contact management, deal pipelines, CRM import, a reporting dashboard, and its Breeze AI assistant, with no credit card required and no time limit. It connects to Gmail and Outlook, which matters because most Indian sales teams live in Gmail.

The free limits need careful reading, because sources disagree here. HubSpot's current pricing FAQ says a free account allows 1,000 contacts and up to 2 users, and that free accounts are subject to a limit of 1 million records across all other standard object types. Older guides and older HubSpot pages quoted much larger free contact allowances, and that older number still circulates in blog posts. The current figure to plan around is 1,000 contacts and 2 users. If your list is bigger than that, or your team is bigger than two, you will need a paid plan, and HubSpot's paid Starter plan starts at 15 dollars per month per seat in the checks. That is not expensive by global standards, but for a small Indian team it adds up in rupees, and HubSpot bills in dollars, so check the exchange rate and any bank charges before committing.

HubSpot's strength is not its free limits, which are tighter than Zoho's for a three-person team. Its strength is simplicity and the ecosystem: forms, email tracking, meeting scheduling, and integrations that all work without setup pain. If your team sells to the US, UK, or EU and wants a CRM that foreign clients and hires already know, HubSpot free is a strong choice for two people. If your team is three people selling mostly in India, Zoho free usually fits better.

## Freshsales: the Indian-origin CRM with calls and chat

[Freshsales](https://www.freshworks.com/crm/pricing-compare/) comes from Freshworks, a company founded in Chennai, and it shows in the product: it is built for small teams that sell by phone and chat as much as by email. Its free plan costs 0 dollars for up to 3 users and includes contact and account management, contact lifecycle stages, an activity timeline, tasks, appointments and notes, a sales dashboard, and a Kanban view of records. Like Zoho, it covers the three-user small team for free.

Freshsales is worth considering over Zoho if your team sells by phone. Its paid plans add built-in phone, chat, and AI insights from Freddy AI, and the product feels more like a phone-first sales tool than a form-first database. The free plan, though, does not include the phone or chat features that make Freshsales distinctive; those sit on paid plans, with the Growth plan at 9 dollars per user per month billed annually in the checks. So the honest way to use Freshsales free is as a clean, simple pipeline for three users, and to pay only when your call volume justifies it.

For most small Indian teams, the choice between Zoho free and Freshsales free comes down to habit: Zoho if you want workflow automation and Indian billing from day one, Freshsales if you like its interface and expect to add calling later. Either is better than tracking deals in a spreadsheet, which is where most small teams start and where most deals get lost.

## Apollo: finding leads and their contact details

[Apollo](https://www.apollo.io/pricing) is the tool that answers the hardest question for a small sales team: who do I contact? It is a database of companies and people with filters for industry, company size, location, job title, and technology used, plus email sequencing built in. For an Indian team selling software, services, or equipment to other businesses, Apollo replaces hours of LinkedIn searching and guessing email addresses.

Apollo's free plan is a permanent tier, not a trial. Per Apollo's own guide, the free plan gives 75 credits per seat per month, granted monthly, which works out to 900 credits per user per year but not 900 on day one. An email reveal costs 1 credit and a phone reveal costs 8 credits, so 75 credits buy 75 email addresses or about 9 phone numbers a month. Credits are pooled across the team and unused credits expire at the end of each cycle. Saving a new contact to a list costs 1 credit, but reusing an already-saved contact across lists costs nothing, which makes list hygiene a real money-saver on the free plan.

A note on disagreement: Apollo's free credit number has changed over time, and older articles quote different figures. The figure to trust is the one on Apollo's own pricing and guide pages at the time you sign up, because Apollo changes this number. Also note that Apollo's database is strongest for US and global companies; for purely Indian small businesses, coverage is thinner, and you will still need Google, JustDial, IndiaMART, and trade directories for many local leads. Apollo is a prospecting tool, not a magic list of every Indian business.

## Hunter: finding one email address, quickly

[Hunter](https://hunter.io/pricing) does one job well: given a company domain, it finds email addresses, and given a name, it guesses and verifies the most likely email pattern. For a small team that already knows which companies to target, Hunter is faster and cheaper than a full database tool.

Hunter's free plan costs 0 dollars and gives 50 credits per month for Email Finder, Email Verifier, and Domain Search, with credits that do not roll over. It also includes 30 AI searches per month and 1 connected email account, plus a basic outreach sequences tool. Older guides quoted 25 free searches per month for Hunter; the current pricing page shows 50 credits per month, so again, trust the current page over old blog posts. Fifty credits a month is roughly two verified email addresses per working day, which is enough for a team doing careful, targeted outreach and not enough for bulk blasting. That is a feature, not a bug: targeted outreach converts better, and it keeps your domain safe.

Hunter also verifies addresses before you send, which protects your sender reputation. Sending to dead addresses gets your domain flagged as spam, and recovering a flagged domain takes weeks. For a small Indian team using Gmail or a company domain, that protection alone is worth the habit of verifying every address.

## Snov.io: follow-up sequences on a renewing trial

[Snov.io](https://snov.io/pricing) combines lead finding, email verification, and outreach campaigns with a small built-in CRM, and its free arrangement is unusual: the Trial plan does not end, but renews every month. On trial you get 50 credits, 100 recipients, 25 enrichment tokens, and 1 email warm-up slot, and you can search for leads, verify emails, enrich data, find phone numbers, warm up your sender account, send campaigns, and manage deals. One recipient is charged when you first contact a lead; follow-ups to that lead in the same billing period are free and unlimited.

The trial's limits are honest and important. Premium features like integrations, bulk search, bulk verification, teamwork, email A/B testing, API and webhook access, and export are not available on trial. Export matters: you cannot easily take your list out of Snov.io on the free plan, so treat it as a sending tool, not your permanent database. Keep your master list in Zoho or HubSpot and use Snov.io for sequences. The warm-up slot is genuinely useful for a small team starting cold email, because warming a new sending domain slowly is what keeps your emails out of spam.

Snov.io also offers a free 7-day trial of its LinkedIn Automation add-on. That is a trial inside a trial, so note the dates if you switch it on, or you will be surprised by a charge or a cutoff.

## Instantly: cold email at volume, trial only

[Instantly](https://help.instantly.ai/en/articles/13941298-instantly-free-trial) is the tool small teams graduate to when they want to send cold email at real volume with warm-up, inbox placement testing, and analytics. It is powerful, and its free arrangement is the least generous in this article, so read it carefully.

Instantly offers a 14-day free trial that starts automatically on sign-up, with no credit card required. During the trial you can connect up to 2 email accounts, upload up to 250 leads to campaigns, send up to 1,000 campaign emails, and you get 1,000 welcome credits for limited Lead Finder results plus 2 free inbox placement tests. There is no permanent free plan. Once the 14-day trial ends, you lose access to the dashboard, your warm-up and accounts are paused automatically, all campaigns are paused, and a trial account that is not upgraded is automatically deleted after expiration.

That makes Instantly a tool to try with a plan, not a tool to park leads in. Prepare your lead list, your email copy, and your sending domains before you start the trial, so the 14 days produce real replies instead of setup work. For most small Indian teams, the honest order is: prove that cold email works for you with Hunter and Snov.io free allowances first, and only then pay for Instantly. Paying for volume before you know your reply rate is how small teams burn money.

## Lusha: contact details straight from LinkedIn

[Lusha](https://www.lusha.com/pricing/) is a browser extension that shows contact details while you browse LinkedIn and company websites. For a salesperson who lives on LinkedIn, that workflow matters: you find a prospect, click Lusha, and get the email or phone without copying names into another tool.

Lusha's free plan provides up to 40 credits per month, with basic prospecting features, the browser extension, and CRM integrations. Revealing a verified email address costs 1 credit and revealing a phone number costs 5 credits, so 40 credits buy 40 emails or 8 phone numbers a month, or a mix. That is a small allowance, but for a team doing high-value, one-by-one outreach to decision makers, it is enough to test the tool properly. Lusha's data quality for Indian contacts is decent for larger companies and thinner for small local firms, the same pattern as Apollo, so verify important numbers before calling.

Lusha is best used as a complement, not a base: Apollo or Hunter for building lists, Lusha for the few prospects where you need a direct number and LinkedIn is your starting point.

## Using sales AI in India: language, payment, and no VPN

All eight tools in this article work in India without a VPN. They are standard global software services with no India block, and Zoho and Freshworks are Indian companies. That is the easy part. The harder parts are language, payment, and how Indian buyers actually respond.

Language first. These tools are English-first. Their interfaces, templates, and AI suggestions are written for English outreach. If you sell in Hindi or Hinglish, which most Indian small teams do at least some of the time, you will write the actual messages yourself, and the AI is a drafting helper, not the voice of your team. A template that sounds natural in American English can sound stiff or pushy in an Indian business email, so edit every AI draft for tone before sending. The same care applies to follow-ups: a polite Hinglish follow-up from a human beats a perfect English sequence from a robot.

Payment second. Zoho and Freshworks bill in rupees and understand Indian payment methods. HubSpot, Apollo, Hunter, Snov.io, Instantly, and Lusha bill in dollars, usually by international card, so check your card's international transactions, the exchange rate, and whether tax is added on your invoice before you commit to an annual plan. Annual plans look cheaper per month but lock you in for a year; for a small team, monthly billing for the first three months is usually the smarter test, even at a higher monthly price.

Third, and most important for India: most Indian buyers do not live in their email inbox the way American buyers do. A decision maker at a small Indian firm is far more likely to respond on WhatsApp or a phone call than to a cold email. These tools are email-first, so use them for what they do well — finding contacts, tracking deals, running follow-ups — and pair them with phone and WhatsApp follow-up done by a human. A team that only sends email in India will conclude that "cold outreach does not work," when the real lesson is that the channel was wrong. Also note that WhatsApp Business messaging has its own rules and templates; do not bulk-message strangers on WhatsApp from a personal number, or the number gets banned.

On data and consent, keep it simple and safe: contact business addresses for genuine business purposes, give every recipient a clear way to opt out, stop contacting people who ask you to stop, and do not buy scraped personal phone numbers. India now has a data protection law, and even leaving the law aside, spamming destroys the domain and phone number your whole team depends on. This article is not legal advice; if your outreach is large-scale, get proper advice.

For the working habits of a small team, two tools you already have elsewhere on this site are worth pairing with this stack: [email writing help](/blog/ai-tools-for-email-writing-india/) for outreach copy, and [freelancer workflows](/blog/ai-tools-for-freelancers-india/) if you are a one-person team rather than a team. Lead lists also get messy fast, so the [data and Excel guidance](/blog/ai-tools-for-data-excel-india/) helps when you clean and track a list, and [social media tools](/blog/ai-tools-for-social-media-india/) help when your prospecting starts on LinkedIn rather than email.

## What to avoid

Avoid buying lead lists. A bought list is usually old, full of dead addresses, and shared with every competitor who bought the same file. It also gets your domain flagged, which hurts the good outreach you do later. Build a smaller, verified list instead; fifty real prospects beat five thousand stale ones.

Avoid sending cold email from your main company domain. Use a secondary domain for outreach, warm it up slowly, and keep your main domain clean for customers, invoices, and support. Teams that skip this step learn it the hard way when their invoices start landing in spam.

Avoid fake personalization. Writing "I loved your recent post about growth" to a thousand people is worse than no personalization at all, because the first prospect who spots it tells the rest. Personalize a small number of high-value prospects properly, and send a clean, honest generic message to the rest.

Avoid paying annually before you have replies. A tool's trial proves the interface works; only replies prove the channel works for your market. Get ten real conversations first, then commit to a year.

Avoid tracking deals in chat apps and notebooks once you pass about twenty active conversations. That is the point where a free CRM stops being admin work and starts being the difference between a follow-up and a lost deal.

Avoid promising a client, a partner, or a manager a result based on a free limit you read in an article, including this one. Limits change, as the HubSpot, Hunter, and Apollo disagreements above show. Re-check the official pricing page on the day you commit.

## Frequently asked questions

**Which free CRM is best for a 3-person sales team in India?** Zoho CRM free, because it is free forever for up to 3 users, includes workflow automation and mobile apps, and bills in rupees when you upgrade. Freshsales free is the closest alternative for the same team size, and HubSpot free fits better if your team is two people selling abroad.

**Is Apollo really free?** Yes, Apollo has a permanent free plan, not just a trial. It gives 75 credits per seat per month, with emails at 1 credit and phone numbers at 8 credits, and unused credits expire each cycle. It is enough for careful prospecting and not enough for bulk outreach, which is the right shape for a small team.

**Can I do cold email from India without getting blocked?** Yes, if you warm up your sending domain slowly, verify every address, send in small daily volumes, and stop mailing people who do not engage. What gets teams blocked is volume without warm-up, dead addresses, and spam complaints. Use a secondary domain for outreach, never your main company domain.

**Do these tools work on a phone?** Zoho CRM, HubSpot, and Freshsales all have mobile apps for checking deals and logging calls on the go. Apollo, Hunter, Snov.io, Instantly, and Lusha are mainly desktop tools, with Lusha working as a browser extension. Expect to do list-building at a desk and deal-tracking anywhere.

**Should a small team pay for Instantly or use free tools?** Use the free tools first. Prove that cold email gets replies in your market with Hunter and Snov.io allowances, then pay for Instantly when volume, warm-up, and inbox placement become your bottleneck. Paying for volume before you have a reply rate is the most common waste in this category.

**Is buying a lead list a good shortcut?** No. Bought lists are usually stale, shared with competitors, and full of dead addresses that damage your sender reputation. A smaller list you build and verify yourself converts better and keeps your domain safe.

**Do I need all eight tools?** No. Most small teams need three: a CRM, a lead-finding tool, and an email verification or sequencing tool. Start with Zoho CRM free, Apollo free, and Hunter free, add Snov.io trial for sequences, and add the rest only when a specific bottleneck appears.

## The final verdict, and a checklist before you pay

Start with Zoho CRM free for your pipeline and Apollo free for leads. Add Hunter free for email lookup and Snov.io trial for follow-ups. That stack costs nothing, covers finding, contacting, tracking, and following up, and it is the honest minimum for a small Indian sales team. Pay for Instantly only after cold email has produced real replies, pay for a CRM seat only when your team grows past the free user count, and re-check every official pricing page before you commit, because the limits in this article will change. If you remember one rule from this article, make it this: a small verified list, followed up properly, beats a huge list that nobody follows up at all.
