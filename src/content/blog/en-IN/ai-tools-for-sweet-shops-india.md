## The short answer

If you run a mithai shop in India, you do not need an expensive system to start using AI tools well. Start with three free tools: WhatsApp Business for orders and customer messages, Google Business Profile for being found on Google Search and Google Maps, and Canva for festival posters and price lists. Use Google Sheets to track orders, stock and gift-box bookings in one place. Use ChatGPT or Gemini to draft messages, captions and menu descriptions in Hindi, English or Hinglish, and then edit every draft yourself before sending it. Add a paid billing tool such as Vyapar only when your daily billing, stock and GST work grow beyond what a sheet can handle comfortably.

That order matters. A sweet shop lives or dies on trust, freshness and timing. Customers order laddoo for a wedding, kaju katli for Diwali, or a mixed gift box for Raksha Bandhan, and they expect the shop to remember the order, confirm the price, and deliver on the promised day. No AI tool can taste your sweets or replace that promise. The tools in this guide only help you answer faster, write more clearly, keep better records, and look more professional online. They do not replace your recipes, your staff, or your own checking of every order before it leaves the counter.

This guide is general information only, not professional business, tax or legal advice. Prices and free limits change often, so re-check the official pricing and help page of any tool before you pay for it or promise a customer a deadline based on it. Food licensing and tax rules also depend on your city, your turnover and the kind of food you sell, so check the official government sources for your situation, or speak to a qualified professional, before acting on anything here.

## WhatsApp Business: your order counter on the phone

WhatsApp Business is the single most useful tool for most Indian mithai shops, because customers already use WhatsApp every day. According to its official pages, the app is free to download and use, with a mix of free and paid features, and it adds business tools on top of ordinary WhatsApp: a business profile, a product catalogue, greeting messages, away messages, quick replies and labels for organising chats.

For a sweet shop, the catalogue is the practical starting point. Put your best-selling items in it with a clear name, a real photo, the weight or piece count, and the price. A customer who can see "Motichur Laddoo, 1 kg" with a photo is far less likely to ask the same price question five times, and far more likely to place an order directly. Use labels to sort chats into groups such as "Diwali orders", "Wedding orders", "Paid", "To deliver" and "Enquiry only". During a festival week, when messages arrive faster than anyone can read them, labels are what stop an order from being lost between two chat screens.

Quick replies save even more time. Write short, checked replies for the questions you answer every day: the price per kilogram, the minimum order for gift boxes, the delivery areas you serve, the last date for festival bookings, and the payment options you accept. A quick reply is not a robot pretending to be you. It is your own checked wording, sent in two taps instead of typed out for the twentieth time that day. Greeting messages welcome new customers automatically, and away messages tell people honestly when the shop is closed and when you will reply, which is far better than silence.

There are limits to be honest about. The official Play Store listing notes that in-app orders and payments are not available in all markets, and some features, such as broadcasts, are available only to eligible users in select countries. Do not build your whole festival plan on a feature until you have tested it on your own phone. Also, WhatsApp Business works best when one phone number is dedicated to the shop. The official listing confirms that a personal account and a business account can live on the same device only if they use two different phone numbers. Mixing family chats with customer orders on one number is how orders get missed and prices get misquoted.

Most importantly, never treat a WhatsApp message as a confirmed order until you have checked it yourself. Confirm the item, the quantity, the weight, the price, the delivery date and the address, and then send a short written confirmation back to the customer. If a customer changes an order, ask them to confirm the change in writing too. AI cannot do this checking for you, and a polite written confirmation protects both the shop and the customer.

## Google Business Profile: being found when someone searches "mithai near me"

Many customers find a sweet shop the same way they find a restaurant: they search on Google or open Google Maps. Google Business Profile is the free listing that controls how your shop appears there. Google's own India page describes it plainly as a free Business Profile, and its help pages confirm that adding or claiming a profile costs nothing.

Claim and verify your profile, then fill it in completely. Add the correct shop name, address, phone number and opening hours, and update the hours before every major festival, because nothing frustrates a customer more than arriving at a closed shop that Google said was open. Add real photos of your counter, your sweets and your gift boxes, not stock photos from the internet. Customers trust a shop whose photos look like the shop they will actually visit. Post updates about festival specials, new items and last booking dates, and reply to reviews politely, including the unhappy ones. A calm, honest reply to a complaint does more for trust than ten five-star reviews that nobody answered.

Verification is the step many small shops skip, and it matters. Until the profile is verified, you cannot fully control how it appears. Follow the verification option Google offers you, and do not pay any third party who claims Google charges for a basic listing, because Google's own help pages say adding or claiming a profile is free. If someone has already created an unverified profile for your shop, claim it through Google Maps rather than creating a duplicate, since two listings for one shop split your reviews and confuse customers.

## Canva: festival posters without a designer on staff

Festivals are the busiest and most visual time for a mithai shop. Diwali, Holi, Raksha Bandhan, Eid and wedding season all need posters, gift-box inserts, price lists and social media posts, often at short notice. Canva is a design tool that lets a shop owner or a staff member make these from templates, without hiring a designer for every small job.

Canva's free plan costs nothing for one person and includes a large template library, basic AI features and limited cloud storage, according to recent reporting on its India pricing. That is enough for most small shops: a Diwali poster, a price list, a gift-box card and a few social media posts each month. The paid plans add the full AI toolkit, background removal, resizing for different platforms, more storage and brand controls, but you should only pay for them when the free plan is genuinely holding you back, for example when you need the same poster resized for WhatsApp, Instagram and a printed banner in one afternoon.

Be careful with two things. First, sources disagree on some paid prices. One recent report puts Canva Pro at 4,000 rupees per year for one person, while another comparison lists a Pro Lite tier and slightly different Business pricing. That disagreement is itself the lesson: do not quote a Canva price to anyone, and do not buy a plan, until you have checked Canva's own pricing page for India on the day you plan to pay. Second, use your own photos of your own sweets. A poster that shows sweets you do not sell will bring customers in once and disappoint them forever. Take clear photos in daylight, use one template style for the whole festival so your posters look like they come from one shop, and always check the price, weight and last booking date on the poster before printing or posting it.

## ChatGPT and Gemini: drafting help, not a shop manager

ChatGPT and Gemini are general AI chat tools, and for a sweet shop their best job is drafting: messages, captions, menu descriptions, festival greetings and replies to common questions. Both offer free plans in India, with limits, and both work without a VPN on an ordinary internet connection.

ChatGPT's free plan, as described in recent reporting, includes access to its current model with web search, limited file and image uploads, restricted image generation and voice mode. Gemini's free plan, as shown on Google's own India subscriptions page, costs nothing with a Google account and includes its lighter model, image generation and editing, Deep Research, Gemini Live, Canvas and Gems, plus 15 GB of shared storage across Gmail, Drive and Photos. The exact models and limits change regularly, so treat any description of a free plan as a snapshot, not a promise, and check the official page before relying on a limit for a festival campaign.

Use these tools for first drafts, and then do the part only you can do: correct them. Ask for five caption options for a Diwali gift box, pick the best one, fix the price, and add your shop's real details. Ask for a polite reply to a customer who wants a bulk discount, then adjust it to the discount you actually offer. Ask for a menu description of your speciality, then remove any claim the tool invented, such as an ingredient you do not use or a "since 1950" history you do not have. AI tools are confident writers and unreliable fact-checkers, so every price, weight, date and ingredient in a draft must be checked against your own price list before it reaches a customer.

One more caution matters for a food business: never paste customer phone numbers, addresses or payment details into a public AI chat tool, and never ask an AI tool to decide a food safety, allergy or labelling question for you. If a customer asks whether a sweet contains nuts, milk or gluten, answer from your own recipe and your own labels, not from an AI guess. A wrong allergy answer is not a small mistake, and no chatbot disclaimer protects a customer who eats something unsafe.

## Google Sheets: one honest order book for festival season

Before buying any software, try running festival orders in Google Sheets. It is free with a Google account, it works on a phone as well as a computer, and more than one staff member can view it, which matters when one person takes orders at the counter and another packs them in the back.

Make one sheet with columns that match your real workflow: order date, customer name, phone number, item, quantity or weight, price, amount paid, amount due, delivery or pickup date, delivery address, and status. Status should use only a few clear words, such as "Booked", "Paid", "Packing" and "Delivered", so anyone can read the sheet at a glance. Add a second tab for stock, with each sweet, the quantity made that morning, the quantity sold, and the quantity left. During Diwali week, that stock tab is what tells you at noon whether you need to make more laddoo or whether you can still accept orders for the evening.

Sheets has real limits. Two people editing the same cell at the same time can overwrite each other, and a sheet with thousands of festival rows becomes slow and easy to break. Give one person ownership of the sheet, agree on who may change prices, and take a copy of the sheet at the end of each festival so you can look back next year and see what actually sold, in what quantity, and on which days. That history is more valuable than any AI forecast, because it is your shop's own record, not a generic prediction.

## Vyapar: when billing and stock outgrow a sheet

Vyapar is an Indian billing, inventory and accounting app built for small businesses. Its own pricing page describes invoice creation, sales and purchase orders, payment reminders, expense recording, live stock status with low-stock alerts and expiry or batch tracking, and GST invoice formats and reports where GST applies. Those are exactly the jobs that become painful in a sheet once a shop sells many items every day, offers gift boxes in several sizes, and needs proper bills and tax reports.

Vyapar is not a free tool, and this guide will not pretend otherwise. Its pricing page shows paid plans starting at around 242 rupees per month, with a 15-day free trial. That is a modest cost for a busy shop, but it is still a cost, and the right time to pay it is when you can point to a concrete problem it solves, such as bills taking too long at the counter, stock counts disagreeing with the shelf, or tax reports taking a whole day to prepare. Use the trial honestly: enter your real items, make real bills, and check the stock report against your actual counter before paying. If the trial does not make a busy day easier, do not buy it.

Whatever billing tool you choose, keep the basics the same: one price list that everyone in the shop uses, bills that match what the customer actually paid for, and a daily check that the money in hand matches the bills for the day. Software can speed up billing, but it cannot fix a price list that two staff members remember differently.

## Using these tools in India: festivals, weddings and delivery

Indian sweet shops do not have a flat year. They have peaks, and the tools must be planned around those peaks. About three weeks before a major festival, update your Google Business Profile hours and festival items, put the festival gift boxes in your WhatsApp catalogue, and make your Canva posters in one sitting so you are not designing posters on the busiest day of the season. Set a last booking date for gift boxes and announce it everywhere at once: WhatsApp status, Google profile post and in the shop itself. A single last date, stated clearly, prevents the painful conversation in which a customer expects a Diwali box on Diwali morning that was never booked.

Wedding and bulk orders need a different workflow from walk-in sales. Take a small advance, record the full order in Sheets with the delivery date and address, and confirm the final quantity and price a few days before the event, because wedding guest counts change. For delivery, be honest about your range. If you deliver only within your neighbourhood, say so in your WhatsApp profile and your Google listing. If you use a delivery partner or a local courier for gift boxes, confirm the delivery date, the handling instructions and who pays for damaged boxes before the festival rush, not during it. Sweets are fragile and perishable, and a box that arrives melted or broken costs you the customer, not just the box.

Language matters too. Many customers are most comfortable in Hindi, or in Hinglish, and a stiff English-only message can feel distant. Use ChatGPT or Gemini to draft the same message in Hindi and English, then read both aloud and fix anything that sounds unnatural. A short Hindi message that says the price, the weight and the pickup date clearly will outsell a long English paragraph that hides the price at the end. Keep the tone warm and specific, and never let an AI tool invent a festival offer, a discount or a delivery promise that you have not approved.

Finally, plan for the day after the festival as well. Thank customers on WhatsApp status, reply to new Google reviews, update your stock sheet, and note which gift boxes sold out first and which sweets were left over. That short review, done while the festival is fresh in your mind, is what makes next year's planning easier, and it costs nothing but twenty minutes.

## What to avoid

Avoid buying a big, expensive system before you have a simple one that works. A shop that cannot keep a clean order sheet will not be saved by costly software; it will only make the same mistakes faster. Avoid posting AI-generated photos of sweets you do not sell, because customers will notice the difference at the counter. Avoid letting an AI tool write your prices, weights or festival dates without checking them, because one wrong price on a poster can cost you a whole day of arguments at the counter.

Avoid sharing customer data carelessly. Do not paste customer lists, phone numbers or addresses into tools you do not need to share them with, and do not forward order screenshots to groups that do not need them. Avoid promising delivery dates, discounts or bulk rates in an AI-drafted message until you have checked that you can actually deliver them. And avoid ignoring reviews and messages during a festival, because a customer who hears nothing for two days will simply order from the next shop on the map.

## Frequently asked questions

**Which single AI tool should a small mithai shop start with?**

Start with WhatsApp Business, because that is where your customers already message you. Add Google Business Profile in the same week, since both are free to start and both bring orders in directly, while Canva and a chat tool help you look professional once the basics are in place.

**Are these tools really free for a sweet shop in India?**

WhatsApp Business is free to download with a mix of free and paid features, Google Business Profile is free to create and manage, Canva has a free plan, and ChatGPT and Gemini have free plans with limits. Paid features exist in all of them, so the honest answer is that starting is free, but growing, advertising or unlocking premium features may cost money.

**Do I need a VPN to use these tools in India?**

No. WhatsApp Business, Google Business Profile, Canva, ChatGPT, Gemini, Google Sheets and Vyapar are all normally available in India on an ordinary internet connection, without a VPN. If a tool ever asks you to use a VPN, treat that as a warning sign and check its official help page first.

**How do I handle a sudden rush of Diwali orders on WhatsApp?**

Use labels for "Booked", "Paid" and "To deliver", use quick replies for prices and last booking dates, and confirm every order in writing with the item, weight, price and date. When you reach the quantity you can actually make, stop taking orders for that item and say so honestly, rather than accepting orders you cannot fulfil.

**Can AI write my festival posters and captions for me?**

AI can draft captions and Canva can help you design posters from templates, but you must check every price, weight, date and offer yourself, and you should use photos of your own sweets. An AI draft is a starting point, not a finished poster, and a poster with a wrong price does more harm than no poster at all.

**Should a small shop buy Vyapar or keep using Google Sheets?**

Keep using Sheets while your daily orders fit comfortably in one sheet and one person can own it. Move to Vyapar, or a similar billing tool, when billing, stock counts or tax reports regularly take too long, and test it with your real items during its trial before paying.

**What about food licences and GST for a mithai shop?**

This guide gives general information only, not legal or tax advice. Food licensing and GST depend on your business type, turnover and location, so check the official government sources for your situation, or speak to a qualified professional, before opening, expanding or changing how you sell food.

**How do I protect customer privacy while using these tools?**

Share only what each tool needs, keep customer phone numbers and addresses out of public AI chats, use one business number for the shop, and do not forward order details to groups that do not need them. If you are unsure whether a tool needs a piece of customer data, leave it out and check the tool's official privacy help page.

## Final verdict

WhatsApp Business plus Google Business Profile and Canva cover most mithai shops free; add Sheets for orders and Vyapar only when billing grows. Start small, confirm every order yourself, use your own photos and prices, and let the tools handle the repetitive work while you keep the trust, freshness and timing that make customers come back for every festival.
