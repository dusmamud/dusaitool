## The direct answer

If you run a construction business in India — one site or ten, houses or small commercial work — the most useful starting stack is simple: Zoho Projects or Trello for running the project day by day, Fieldwire for site drawings, tasks and punch lists when your field team grows, Zoho Books or Vyapar for GST billing, and a general AI assistant such as ChatGPT, Gemini or Claude only for drafting, summarising and checking your own lists. Procore and Buildertrend are serious platforms for larger contractors, but both work on custom quotes rather than a published monthly price, so they only make sense once your project volume clearly justifies a sales conversation and an annual contract.

No tool in this article replaces a qualified engineer, a chartered accountant, or your own site visit. An AI assistant can draft a scope of work, turn a messy site note into a checklist, or reformat a bill of quantities, but it can also invent a rate, misread a drawing dimension, or quote a rule that does not apply in your state. Prices and free limits also change often, so before you pay for anything, or before you promise a client a deadline that depends on a tool, re-check the official pricing and help page of that tool. Everything below states the free limit as verified at the time of research, and flags clearly where sources disagree.

This article is general information only, not professional engineering, accounting, tax or legal advice. For structural decisions, GST filing, contract wording and safety compliance, check official sources and a qualified professional. That warning is not decoration: in construction, a wrong quantity, a wrong tax split or an unchecked drawing costs real money and can create real liability.

## Zoho Projects: the best free starting point for most small builders

Zoho Projects is the tool to try first if you run two or three sites at once and need more than a to-do list. Its free plan covers 3 projects, 5 users and 5 GB of storage, with subtasks, an iOS and Android app, and integration with Google apps and Office 365. Paid plans add unlimited projects, project budgets, earned value tracking, time logs and timesheets, and the Zia AI features for summaries, translation and task creation. For a builder, the practical meaning is this: one project per site, a task list per stage of work — excavation, footing, columns, slab, brickwork, plaster, finishing — an owner for each task, and a due date the whole team can see.

Use it for the weekly rhythm, not just for storing files. Every Monday, the site engineer updates what is done, what is blocked, and what material is needed that week. The office checks the budget view and the timesheet view before ordering. That single habit — one update per site per week, with photos attached — prevents the most common builder problem, which is discovering a delay two weeks after it happened. Zoho Projects works in India without a VPN, and because Zoho is an Indian company with GST-aware sister products, pairing it with Zoho Books feels natural for a small firm. The honest limit is scale: the free plan ends at 3 projects, so a builder running five sites at once will hit the wall quickly and must price the paid plan before promising a client that everything lives in one system.

## Trello: the simplest board your mistri and supervisor will actually use

Trello is the right choice when your team will not open a complex tool. Work is organised as boards, lists and cards: one board per site, lists such as To Do, Doing, Blocked and Done, and one card per job — for example, "Second-floor slab concrete" with a photo, a due date and the name of the person responsible. The free plan allows up to 10 collaborators per workspace and up to 10 boards per workspace, with unlimited cards, unlimited storage at 10 MB per file, and 250 workspace command runs per month for automation. The Standard paid plan, listed at 5 US dollars per user per month billed annually, adds AI features, unlimited boards and larger file limits.

Trello wins on adoption and loses on depth. It does not give you a real project budget, earned value, or a bill of quantities. It is excellent for daily site coordination — who is doing what today, what is blocked, what was finished with a photo as proof — and weak for cost control. A good pattern is Trello for the field team and a spreadsheet or Zoho Books for money, with the site engineer posting one photo per completed card. Do not store your only copy of a drawing or a signed measurement sheet in a free Trello board with a 10 MB per-file limit; keep originals in a proper drive folder and link to them. Trello works in India without a VPN and has iOS and Android apps, which matters when the supervisor's only device is a phone.

## ClickUp and Asana: powerful, but read the free limit before you commit

ClickUp's Free Forever plan allows unlimited members and unlimited tasks, with docs and list, board and calendar views. Its trade-off is storage: the free plan provides only 60 MB of total workspace storage, which a photo-heavy construction team can exhaust in a single week of site photos. Its entry paid plan is listed at 7 US dollars per user per month billed annually and adds unlimited storage and spaces. ClickUp suits a builder who wants tasks, documents, chat and dashboards in one workspace and is willing to spend a day setting it up properly. It does not suit a team that just wants a board and will never open a dashboard.

Asana's Personal plan, by contrast, covers only 2 users for new accounts, with unlimited storage subject to a 100 MB limit per file. Its Starter paid plan is listed at 10.99 US dollars per user per month billed annually. Asana is clean and reliable, but a 2-user free plan cannot carry a site engineer, a supervisor, an office accountant and a client at the same time. If your team is genuinely two people in an office, Asana's free plan is fine. If your team includes field staff, ClickUp's unlimited members or Zoho Projects' 5 free users fit better. In all three cases, re-check the official pricing page before paying: comparison pages disagree on small details, and the vendors change seat rules, as Asana did for accounts created after November 2025.

## Fieldwire: the field-first tool for drawings, tasks and punch lists

Fieldwire, now part of Hilti, is built for the exact moment a builder outgrows WhatsApp photos: the site team needs the latest drawing, a task list tied to locations on that drawing, and a punch list that does not get lost in a chat group. It works on mobile and desktop, including without an internet connection, which is a genuine advantage on sites with weak signal. Its free basic plan is reported for small teams, with third-party comparisons describing a limit of 5 team members and 3 projects, and a basic plan limited to 100 sheets. Advanced features such as RFIs, submittals, change orders and budget management sit on higher tiers, and the BIM viewer is gated to the Business tier and above.

Treat those third-party numbers as a starting point, not gospel, and confirm them on Fieldwire's own pricing page during your trial. The practical test is simple: load one real drawing set, mark ten punch items on it from the site, and see whether your supervisor can do it on a phone, in sunlight, with gloves off, in under five minutes per item. If yes, Fieldwire earns its place. If your supervisor still prefers a printed drawing and a notebook, no software will fix that, and a simpler Trello board plus printed drawings is the honest choice until the team is ready. Fieldwire is available in India without a VPN, but confirm support hours and data hosting expectations with the vendor before putting client drawings on it.

## Procore: powerful for large contractors, priced on volume, not seats

Procore is the construction platform large owners, general contractors and specialty contractors use as a single system of record: documents, drawings, RFIs, submittals, budgets, schedules and field reports in one place, with unlimited users and unlimited data storage included in the contract. Its pricing page does not publish a monthly rate. Instead, it asks for a custom quote and charges an upfront annual fee based on the products selected and on Annual Construction Volume — the total value of construction work managed on the platform. Unlimited users is a real advantage for a large project with many collaborators, because clients, subcontractors and vendors can all access the platform without per-seat charges.

For a small Indian builder, Procore is usually the wrong first purchase. An annual, volume-based contract is hard to justify for one or two houses, and the platform's depth becomes overhead when the team is five people. Procore makes sense when you run multiple concurrent projects, coordinate many subcontractors, and already feel pain from version confusion — different teams working from different drawing revisions. If that is you, take the demo, ask for the total annual cost including every module you will actually use, ask what renewal rate protection means in writing, and run a paid pilot on one live project before rolling it out. Never budget from a blog's guess at a Procore price, because the vendor itself does not publish one.

## Buildertrend: one platform for home builders, also on a custom quote

Buildertrend is a cloud platform for home builders, remodelers and specialty contractors, covering leads, scheduling, project management, financial tools and client communication, with unlimited users and unlimited projects in the core subscription. Its pricing page likewise asks for a custom quote based on the business, and its own description says it is built for builders who oversee five or more projects a year or handle complex, multi-phase builds. Secondary guides report that Buildertrend does not currently offer a free trial and instead offers personalised demos, so verify that on the official pricing page before planning a trial week around it.

Buildertrend's pitch is client experience as much as site control: clients see selections, change orders, photos and payment schedules in one portal, which reduces the daily "what is happening at my site" phone calls. That matters for residential builders whose clients are emotionally invested and quick to worry. The honest question is fit: if you build two houses a year, a custom-quoted annual platform built for five-plus projects a year is likely oversized. If you build ten houses a year and lose hours every week to client updates and change-order paperwork, a demo is worth your time. As with Procore, get the quote in writing, confirm what "unlimited" covers, and test client onboarding with one real client before paying for a year.

## FalconBrick and ProjectBase: India-built platforms worth a demo

Two India-focused platforms deserve attention precisely because they are designed around how Indian sites actually run. FalconBrick describes itself as a mobile-first, end-to-end platform for construction and real estate, with a mobile app for site staff — allocating daily work, updating progress with live photos, performing quality inspections — and a web-based live project monitoring system for senior management, plus analytics on contractor performance and quality issues. It reports more than 300 customers across about 12 cities and more than 600 projects, including Hyderabad, Bengaluru, Mumbai, Pune, Delhi-NCR, Kolkata and Chennai. That footprint is a useful signal, but it is the vendor's own claim, so judge it by a demo on your own project, not by the headline number.

ProjectBase, also built for Indian contractors, connects project teams and back-office teams in one cloud system, with particular strength in procurement and inventory: online material requests, RFQs sent to suppliers with quotes entered directly in a portal, automated quote comparison, purchase order approvals, and instant reports of material available at each site and warehouse, separated between project stock and common stock. It also offers project financial dashboards showing costs incurred, billing done, unbilled value, cost overruns and estimated versus actual margins. For a contractor whose biggest leak is material — ordered twice, issued without record, or sitting unused at a closed site — that procurement chain is more valuable than a prettier task board. Both platforms work in India without a VPN. Neither publishes a simple monthly price you can safely quote here, so treat any number from a third-party directory as unverified until the vendor confirms it for your project count and user count.

## Zoho Books: GST billing that fits a small construction firm

Billing is where many builders lose money quietly: work done but not invoiced, invoices without the right tax split, or a running total that only the accountant understands. Zoho Books is built around Indian GST, with GST-compliant invoicing, HSN and SAC codes, place-of-supply logic, and GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B report preparation. Multiple guides report that its free plan covers businesses below Rs. 25 lakh in annual revenue, with 1 user plus 1 accountant, and that its Standard plan costs Rs. 899 per month, or Rs. 749 per month billed annually, plus GST. The difference between those two Standard figures is billing period, not a contradiction: monthly billing costs more per month than annual billing. Confirm the current revenue threshold and user limit on Zoho's own pricing page before relying on the free plan, because thresholds change.

For a construction firm, the valuable habit is milestone billing tied to site progress. Create the project in Zoho Books, raise a tax invoice at each certified stage — footing, slab, brickwork, finishing — attach the measurement sheet or client approval, and send automatic payment reminders for overdue invoices. Keep retention money, advances and GST input credit visible in the same system, and let your chartered accountant work directly in the books rather than from forwarded PDFs. Zoho Books alone does not make you GST-compliant: e-invoicing and e-way bill requirements depend on your turnover and on the goods you move, and those features are often paid-only. Check with your accountant which requirements apply to you, and never promise a client a tax treatment the software suggested without that check.

## Vyapar: fast bills from a phone, built for Indian small business habits

Vyapar is the mobile-first alternative when the billing moment happens on site, not at a desk. Its basic billing, GST invoice generation and inventory features on the Android app are free to use, and it supports billing in Hindi and several regional languages, plus WhatsApp sharing of invoices — a workflow many Indian customers and small suppliers already expect. That combination makes it a strong fit for a small contractor, a material supplier, or a builder who raises running bills from the site office and wants the client to receive the invoice on WhatsApp within minutes.

Sources disagree on Vyapar's paid pricing, and that disagreement is worth stating plainly. One guide reports premium tiers near Rs. 599 to Rs. 799 per year, while another reports a Silver plan for desktop and mobile at Rs. 4,399 per year. Those figures describe different plan combinations and different check dates, so neither should be used for budgeting. What is consistent across sources is the structure: the free app is Android-based and single-device, invoices may carry Vyapar branding, and desktop use, multi-device sync, e-invoicing and e-way bills need a paid plan. Before you choose Vyapar over Zoho Books, test the exact flow you will use daily — create a GST invoice with your items, share it on WhatsApp, and print or export it for your accountant — and confirm the current paid price on Vyapar's own pricing page.

## General AI assistants: drafting power, with strict limits

ChatGPT, Gemini and Claude are not construction software, but for a builder they are the highest-value free AI available right now, because almost every office task is drafting, summarising or checking. Use them to draft a scope of work, write a subcontractor exclusion list, phrase an RFI politely but firmly, turn site-meeting notes into an action list, prepare a pre-pour checklist or a toolbox-talk outline, convert units, and sanity-check quantities you computed yourself. All three offer free plans with usage limits that reset over hours or days, and all three support file uploads on free plans within limits. Entry paid plans are widely reported around 20 US dollars per month per provider, but confirm the current figure and model on the provider's own pricing page.

The limits are serious and must be respected. These models will confidently produce wrong load calculations, wrong code citations, wrong material rates and wrong legal wording. They are useful for reading and structuring — for example, extracting room names, dimensions and a door-window schedule from a drawing page into a table, while explicitly listing every dimension they could not read — and dangerous for pricing, structural decisions and contract advice. Never paste confidential client drawings, tender documents or personal worker data into a consumer free plan without checking the provider's data-use settings, because free-tier data handling varies by provider and setting. The rule for the whole office should be written down: AI drafts, humans decide, and every rate, dimension and deadline is checked against a current source before it reaches a client or a supplier.

## AI takeoff and estimating tools: promising, but verify every rate in India

A newer category uses AI to read floor plans and produce measurements and estimates: detecting rooms, returning areas and perimeters, and building a takeoff faster than manual measurement. Third-party guides report free options such as Vega AI with an AI Measure plan and STACK with a free version of its takeoff tool, while noting that their AI takeoff features sit on paid plans, and that some tools quote prices on a United States basis. That last point matters enormously in India: a quantity in square feet is portable, but a rate per square foot is not. Cement, steel, labour and transport rates vary by city and by month, and a tool trained on another market's costs will produce a confident, wrong estimate.

Use takeoff AI only for quantities, never for final prices. Let it measure, then apply your own current local rates, checked against recent supplier quotations, and have a qualified estimator sign off before the figure becomes a client quote or a tender bid. No free tool produces a tender-ready price on its own. If a vendor claims otherwise, ask for a worked example on your own drawing, compare its quantities against a manual measurement, and check the variance before paying. For most small Indian builders, this category is a watch-and-test area, not a day-one purchase: the free general assistants plus Zoho Projects plus Zoho Books solve more of the daily problem, at lower cost, than an imported estimating tool with foreign rates.

## India-use section: making the tools work on a real Indian site

Indian sites impose conditions that software marketing rarely mentions, and your choice should be tested against them, not against a feature list. First, the phone is the site office. The supervisor, the mistri and the material supplier all work from phones, often Android, often on patchy mobile data. Any tool that needs a laptop, a stable connection or a long form will be abandoned within a week. Prefer tools with a real Android app, offline or low-bandwidth behaviour, and photo capture built in — Fieldwire's offline working and FalconBrick's mobile-first design are advantages here, while a desktop-heavy platform will frustrate a field team no matter how good its reports look in the head office.

Second, language and literacy vary across the team. The office may work in English, the supervisor in Hindi, and workers in a regional language. Tools that support Hindi billing, like Vyapar, or that allow photo-based proof instead of long written updates, reduce friction. Set a simple rule: every completed task gets one photo, every material delivery gets one photo of the challan, and every blocked task gets one line in the local language the supervisor actually speaks. Third, money runs on milestones, advances, retention and GST, not on a simple monthly subscription model. Your billing tool must handle stage-wise invoices, advances adjusted against later bills, and retention deducted and released on schedule. Test those exact flows in a trial, with your accountant watching, before migrating live billing.

Fourth, materials are the leak. Cement bags, steel tonnage, sand and aggregate, tiles and fittings move between sites, get damaged, or sit unused. A system that tracks material requests, purchase orders, site stock and issues to subcontractors — ProjectBase's strength — pays for itself faster than a prettier dashboard. Even on a free stack, enforce the discipline manually: one indent per requirement, one approval, one goods-received note with a photo, and a weekly stock count on the two highest-value items. Fifth, weather, festivals, labour availability and approval delays will break any schedule, so build buffer into every plan and record the reason for every delay in the tool. A delay log with reasons — rain, material shortage, drawing revision, labour shortage — is what turns next year's estimate from a guess into a plan. Finally, keep a paper and PDF backup of every signed measurement, client approval and payment receipt. Software accounts close, trials expire and phones break; your legal record must not depend on a single app.

## What to avoid

Avoid buying a volume-priced annual platform before you have a free-stack habit. If your team does not update Trello or Zoho Projects weekly, Procore or Buildertrend will not create that habit; it will only make the unused system more expensive. Avoid trusting any AI-generated rate, drawing dimension, structural note or legal clause without a human check. Avoid uploading full tender sets, client identity documents or worker personal data to free consumer AI plans without checking data-use settings. Avoid storing your only copy of drawings, contracts or measurement sheets inside a single free tool with a small storage limit, such as ClickUp's 60 MB free storage. Avoid choosing a tool from a single blog's price table: prices, free limits and plan names change, and secondary sources already disagree on Vyapar's paid price and on small details of other plans. Avoid promising a client a completion date, a final price or a tax treatment based on what a tool suggested; promise only what your engineer, your supplier quotation and your accountant have confirmed. Avoid running billing and site progress in completely separate worlds — if the site says a slab is done and billing says it is not invoiced, that gap is lost money. And avoid paying for five tools when two will do: a project tool, a billing tool and a drafting assistant cover most small builders, and every extra tool is another login the site team will forget.

## Frequently asked questions

**Which free tool should a small Indian builder start with?**
Start with Zoho Projects for project tracking and Zoho Books for GST billing if you run a few sites and want proper accounts, or Trello plus Vyapar if your team needs the simplest possible board and phone-first billing. Run both free plans on one live site for two weeks before paying for anything, and keep the tool your supervisor actually opens every day.

**Do Procore and Buildertrend work in India without a VPN?**
Yes, both are cloud platforms accessible from India without a VPN, but access is not the same as fit. Both use custom quotes rather than published monthly prices, and both are designed for builders handling several projects at a time. Take a demo, get a written annual quote, and pilot on one project before committing.

**Can AI prepare a bill of quantities or an estimate I can send to a client?**
AI can draft the structure of a bill of quantities and help extract quantities from drawings, but it cannot produce a client-ready or tender-ready price on its own. Apply your own current local rates, check every quantity against a manual measurement or a recent supplier quotation, and have a qualified estimator review the result before it reaches a client.

**Is Zoho Books really free for small businesses?**
Multiple guides report that Zoho Books is free for businesses below Rs. 25 lakh in annual revenue, with 1 user plus 1 accountant on the free plan. That threshold and user limit must be confirmed on Zoho's own pricing page at signup, because free-plan rules change, and e-invoicing and advanced automation generally need a paid plan.

**What is the best tool for tracking workers and attendance on site?**
None of the tools in this article is a dedicated attendance system, and you should not buy a project tool expecting it to replace one. In practice, builders use the project tool's task assignments and time logs — Zoho Projects timesheets, for example — plus a daily site photo and a simple attendance register, whether paper or a dedicated attendance app chosen separately. Keep wage and personal data out of shared project boards.

**How do I track materials so nothing is ordered twice or goes missing?**
Use one indent flow for every requirement: material request, approval, purchase order, delivery photo with challan, site stock entry, and issue to a subcontractor. ProjectBase is built for exactly this chain, and FalconBrick covers site execution with photo updates. On a free stack, enforce the same flow manually in a spreadsheet linked from your project tool, and count the two highest-value materials weekly.

**Should I put drawings and client documents into a free AI tool?**
No, not without checking the tool's data-use settings first. Free consumer AI plans may handle uploaded data differently from paid business plans, and a drawing set can contain client details, site addresses and commercial terms. Use AI on non-sensitive excerpts for drafting and structuring, and keep full drawings in your project platform or drive with proper access control.

**What should I check before paying for any tool in this article?**
Re-check the official pricing and help page for the current free limit, paid price, user limit and storage limit; confirm whether GST or taxes are included in the quoted price; test the exact daily flow — task update, photo upload, invoice, WhatsApp share — on your own phone and your supervisor's phone; and confirm the cancellation and data-export terms so you can leave with your records intact.

## Final verdict

Start free with Zoho Projects or Trello plus Zoho Books, and add Fieldwire or Procore only when site volume justifies a paid quote.
