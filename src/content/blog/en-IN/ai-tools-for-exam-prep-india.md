Competitive exams in India are not won by reading more. They are won by attempting tests under time pressure, finding your weak topics, and revising them before you forget them. That is exactly where AI exam tools help, and exactly where they mislead: a tool can generate a beautiful 90-day plan and still fail you if you never attempt a full mock test, or if you trust its answers without checking them against your books.

The short answer for India is this: start with [Embibe](https://www.embibe.com/) free mock tests and [Testbook](https://testbook.com/online-test-series) free tests for your exact exam, add [Oliveboard](https://www.oliveboard.in/test-series/) free mocks and [Adda247](https://www.adda247.com/mock-tests) free mocks for comparison, and use [Anki](https://apps.ankiweb.net/) every day for revision. [Physics Wallah](https://www.pw.live/test-series) (PW) and [StudyIQ](https://www.studyiq.com/course-detail/free-upsc-prelims-2027-test-series) are strong for specific exams, especially SSC and UPSC. Use [ChatGPT](https://chatgpt.com/) or [Gemini](https://gemini.google.com/) free only for planners and practice questions, never as your only source of facts. Do not buy a paid test series in your first week. Attempt the free tests first, then pay for one series for the exam you are actually taking.

This article is written for India on purpose: UPSC, SSC, banking, railway, teaching and state exams, Hindi and English medium, a normal phone, UPI payment, and no VPN. It also carries a warning that matters more here than in any other category: an AI answer about a constitutional article, a government scheme, a date, or a cutoff can sound confident and still be wrong. Always check AI answers against the official syllabus, standard books, and previous-year papers. Prices and free limits below are what the official pages showed at research time, and they change often, especially during sale seasons. Re-check the official pricing and help page before paying or promising yourself a deadline.

## Embibe: the AI mentor and free mocks for the most exams

Embibe is an Indian platform built around personalised learning. Its app lists personalised journeys for more than 400 exams in English, Hindi and 10 other Indian languages, covering school, engineering, medical, banking, SSC, railways, defence, insurance and teaching exams. Its AI coach, called MB, tracks your performance, points out learning gaps, and offers instant doubt resolution where you can type a question or upload a photo of it.

The free part that matters here is its mock tests. Embibe's own help page invites users to sign up to take free mock tests, and its app store listing says all plans include a 7-day free trial. The paid plans listed at research time were Achieve Unlimited at ₹709 per month billed annually, Achieve Sprint at ₹734 per month billed quarterly, and Achieve Now at ₹899 per month billed monthly. Those prices can change, so check the subscription page before paying.

There is one honest disagreement to state plainly. Embibe advertises free mock tests, but recent user reviews say that much of its content now sits behind a subscription, and that its coin system does not unlock as much as it seems to promise. Both things can be true at once: the free mocks exist, and the full learning journeys are paid. The safe way to use Embibe is to create the free account, attempt its free mocks for your exam, and only pay if the analysis and personalised plan actually help your weak topics. Do not pay on the strength of the word unlimited alone.

## Testbook: the widest mock-test library for government exams

Testbook is the most practical starting point for SSC, banking and railway aspirants because of its sheer coverage: its test series pages list more than 814 exams. Each test series mixes full-length mocks, chapter tests, live tests and current-affairs tests. The free part is small but real: in the series sampled at research time, a series offered a few free tests, for example 5 free tests inside an RRB NTPC series of 837 tests, and 2 free tests in an RRB Station Master series. The exact free count varies by exam and changes over time, so open your exam's series page and count the free tests before deciding anything.

Testbook series are available in English, Hindi and several more languages, which matters for SSC and railway aspirants who do not study in English. The analysis after each test shows accuracy, speed and weak topics, and that analysis is the real product. A mock test without analysis is just a score. Use the free tests to learn the exam interface, practise time management, and find your weak sections. Pay for the full series only for the one exam you are preparing for, not for three exams at once. The full series is sold as a paid Testbook Pass, and its price is shown on the exam's page.

## Oliveboard: free mocks with AI-based analysis for banking and SSC

Oliveboard is strongest for banking and SSC aspirants. Its free mock test section offers full-length mocks, sectional tests and topic tests for exams including SSC CGL, SBI Clerk, IBPS Clerk, RBI Grade B, railways and teaching exams. The feature that sets it apart is its AI-based analysis engine, which breaks down your performance by topic and suggests where to focus next. That is genuinely useful for banking exams, where the difference between selection and rejection is often a few marks in one weak section.

To attempt a free mock, you register with an email address or Google account, open the test series section for your exam, and choose a full-length or sectional test. The free tests give you the real exam interface and the analysis. The full test series for an exam is paid, and the price varies by exam, so check the exam's own page rather than trusting a single price quoted anywhere, including here. Oliveboard is a good second opinion after Testbook: attempt one free mock on each platform for the same exam, compare which analysis helps you more, and then commit to one paid series if you need it.

## Adda247: free mocks, previous-year papers and Test Prime

Adda247 is another Indian platform built specifically for banking, SSC, railways, teaching, defence and state government exams. Its mock test pages list free mocks alongside full-length, subject-wise and section-wise tests, with detailed solutions and performance analysis. Previous-year papers are a big part of its library, and for SSC and banking exams, previous-year papers matter at least as much as new mocks, because the exam pattern repeats itself.

The paid option is Test Prime, a single subscription for all exams. At research time its store page showed a sale price of ₹249 against a regular price of ₹999, with claims of more than 1 lakh mock tests and more than 25,000 previous-year papers across 600-plus exams, in 8-plus languages. Sale prices change constantly, so treat ₹249 as a sale price you saw, not a permanent price. Before buying Test Prime, check two things: whether your exact exam's latest-pattern tests are included, and whether you will actually attempt enough tests to justify it. A subscription you do not open is the most expensive test series of all.

## Physics Wallah: free SSC mocks and Test Pass for 35-plus exam categories

Physics Wallah, known as PW, runs test series for more than 35 exam categories, including JEE, NEET, GATE, SSC, banking, state PSC, CUET, UGC NET, railways, defence, judiciary, MBA, teaching and nursing. For government-exam aspirants, its useful free part includes free mock tests, and at research time it listed 15 free online CBT mock tests for all SSC exams. Its tests follow the exam pattern, include previous-year questions and detailed solutions, and come with performance analytics.

PW's paid option is the PW Test Pass. Like the other platforms, its price depends on the exam and the pass you choose, so check the test series page for your exam. PW is worth trying first if you are an SSC aspirant, because the 15 free CBT mocks give you a real feel for computer-based testing, and because PW's low-price reputation means its paid pass is often cheaper than rivals. Cheap is not the same as suitable, though. Compare its analysis with Testbook or Oliveboard before paying, and pick the platform whose weak-topic feedback you actually act on.

## StudyIQ: a free UPSC Prelims test series to start with

StudyIQ is best known for UPSC content, and its most useful free offer for this article is its free UPSC Prelims 2027 test series. The series page describes structured, exam-oriented practice with quality questions and performance insights meant to help you evaluate your preparation and find gaps. For a UPSC aspirant, a free structured prelims series is worth more than a pile of random questions, because prelims is a test of breadth, accuracy and negative-marking discipline.

StudyIQ also sells paid courses and test series, and its prices vary by course, so this article makes no claim about them. Use the free prelims series first. Attempt it under timed conditions, not as an open-book exercise. If your score improves across the series and the explanations teach you something, consider a paid course only after you have finished the free one. For non-UPSC exams, StudyIQ is less central than Testbook, Oliveboard or Adda247, so do not stretch one platform across every exam you dream of taking.

## ChatGPT free and Gemini free: planners and mock questions, not fact books

General chatbots are the most misused exam tools in India. Used well, [ChatGPT](https://chatgpt.com/) free and [Gemini](https://gemini.google.com/) free are excellent for three jobs: building a study plan, turning a chapter into practice questions, and explaining a concept in simple language. Ask for a weekly plan with your exam date, your daily hours, and your weak topics. Ask for 20 multiple-choice questions on one topic, then answer them without looking. Ask it to explain a topic like a teacher would, then check that explanation against your book.

Used badly, they are dangerous. Both free plans work in India without a VPN, but both have usage limits that reset over time and change without a fixed public number you can rely on. Do not plan your whole preparation around unlimited free chatbot use. More importantly, never trust a chatbot's answer about a date, a constitutional article, a scheme's eligibility, or a cutoff without verifying it. Chatbots invent facts smoothly. For exam facts, the book, the official notification, and the previous-year paper outrank any chatbot, every time.

A practical rule: use chatbots for planning and practice, and use mock-test platforms for measurement. If a chatbot and a mock test disagree, believe the mock test's official-style answer and check your book. And never paste your admit card, application number, password, or phone number into any AI tool. Study material goes in. Personal exam credentials never do.

## Anki: the revision tool that actually prevents forgetting

Most aspirants fail not because they never studied a topic, but because they studied it once and forgot it. [Anki](https://apps.ankiweb.net/) solves exactly that problem with spaced repetition: it shows you a flashcard again just when you are about to forget it, and it shows hard cards more often than easy ones. You make decks for your exam, for example polity articles, banking abbreviations, railway general awareness, or SSC vocabulary, and you review them daily in short sessions.

Anki is free on Windows, Mac and Linux, and its free AnkiWeb sync service keeps your cards across devices. The Android app is free as well. The official iPhone app is a one-time paid purchase, so check its price on the App Store if you use an iPhone; many aspirants use Anki on a computer or Android phone and avoid that cost entirely. Anki has no AI mock tests and no syllabus of its own. Its power is that it turns everything else you study, from mocks, books and notes, into durable memory. If you use only one tool from this article every single day, make it Anki. Ten to twenty minutes of daily revision beats a ten-hour reading marathon the week before the exam.

## Quizlet: flashcards and practice tests for quick revision

[Quizlet](https://quizlet.com/) is the simpler alternative to Anki. Its free plan lets you make and study flashcard sets, which is enough for vocabulary, formulas, abbreviations and one-line facts. Its AI tools, including an AI flashcard maker, an AI study guide generator and practice tests made from your notes, exist, but they are mainly part of the paid Quizlet Plus plan, and the free tier's AI access is limited. Check the current plan page before depending on Quizlet's AI features.

For exam prep, Quizlet works best as a shared-deck library and a quick revision tool. If your exam has standard lists, for example banking terms, SSC idioms, or UPSC articles, search Quizlet for existing sets made by other aspirants, but verify every card against your book before trusting it. Other students' cards contain errors too. Use Quizlet for fast, light revision and Anki for the long, daily spaced-repetition habit. You do not need both every day. Pick the one whose daily review you will actually finish.

## Using exam AI in India: language, payment, and no-VPN reality

All the platforms in this article work in India without a VPN, on a normal phone and a normal connection. That matters because many global tools assume a US student with a card and a campus offer. Here, the practical questions are different. Language first: Testbook series come in English, Hindi and more languages, Embibe supports English, Hindi and 10 other Indian languages, and Adda247 lists 8-plus languages. Check that your exam's tests are available in your medium before paying, because not every series is fully translated.

Payment second: Indian platforms accept UPI and cards, and their prices move with sales, especially around exam notifications. That is why this article quotes sale prices as sale prices and tells you to re-check. Never pay for a test series from a screenshot or a forwarded message. Open the official page, read what is included, and check the refund and validity terms. A test series that expires before your exam date is useless, however cheap it was.

Data and device third: mock tests run in the browser or the app, and they are lighter than video classes. If your connection is weak, attempt mocks on Wi-Fi where possible, and use Anki and downloaded notes for daily revision offline. Do not let a heavy app decide your preparation. The exam is a test of your knowledge under time pressure, and the best tool is the one you open every day.

## What to avoid

- Do not buy three test series for three different exams. Finish the free tests, pick one exam, and buy one series for it.
- Do not trust AI answers for facts, dates, articles, scheme details, or cutoffs without checking the book or the official notification.
- Do not skip previous-year papers. Mocks test your preparation. Previous-year papers reveal the exam's real pattern.
- Do not fall for "AI guaranteed selection" claims from any app, ad, or coaching page. No tool can guarantee selection, and any tool that says so is selling hope, not preparation.
- Do not share your admit card, application number, password, or OTP with any AI tool, app, or person claiming to check your result.
- Do not read AI summaries instead of the syllabus. If a topic is in the official syllabus and not in your notes, the syllabus wins.
- Do not judge yourself by one mock score. Judge yourself by the trend across five mocks and by whether your weak topics are shrinking.

## Frequently asked questions

**Which free mock test is best for SSC CGL?** There is no single best one. Attempt the free mocks on Testbook, Oliveboard and Adda247, plus Physics Wallah's free SSC CBT mocks, and compare their analysis. Buy a paid series only for the platform whose feedback you actually use.

**Can I prepare for UPSC only with free AI tools?** Free tools cover a lot: Embibe free mocks, StudyIQ's free UPSC Prelims test series, ChatGPT or Gemini for planners, and Anki for revision. But free tools do not replace standard books, the official syllabus, and previous-year papers. Use free tools for practice and measurement, and books for knowledge.

**Are Embibe's mock tests really free?** Embibe's own pages advertise free mock tests on a free account, with paid plans for full learning journeys. Recent user reviews say much of its content now needs a subscription. Both can be true, so create the free account, attempt the free mocks, and pay only if the full plan helps you.

**Do these tools work in Hindi?** Yes, mostly. Testbook, Embibe and Adda247 all offer Hindi and other Indian languages, but coverage varies by exam and series. Open your exam's page and confirm your medium before paying.

**Is it safe to upload my notes to AI tools?** Study notes are fine. Personal data is not. Never upload your admit card, application number, password, phone number, or any document with personal details to a chatbot or a new app.

**Can AI make my whole study plan?** It can make a good first draft. Give it your exam date, daily hours, and weak topics, then adjust the plan yourself every week after your mock analysis. A plan you never revise is a plan you will abandon.

**Should I buy two test series at once?** No. Finish the free tests first, buy one series for your exact exam, and spend the saved money and time on revision. Two series double your tests and halve your revision, and revision is where marks come from.

**Do mock test scores predict selection?** They predict readiness, not selection. A rising trend across mocks, shrinking weak topics, and steady accuracy under time pressure are good signs. One high score means nothing, and one low score is a diagnosis, not a verdict.

## The final verdict, and a checklist before you pay

Start with Embibe's free mocks and Testbook's free tests, then revise daily with Anki. Pay only for the test series of the exam you are taking. That is the whole strategy, and it works because it separates three jobs: mocks measure you, analysis fixes you, and revision keeps you fixed.

Before you pay for anything, run this checklist. First, have you attempted the free tests for your exact exam on at least two platforms? Second, is the paid series for your exam's latest pattern, and does its validity cover your exam date? Third, have you checked the price on the official page today, not in a screenshot? Fourth, do you have a daily revision habit in Anki or Quizlet that you have kept for at least two weeks? Fifth, have you worked through previous-year papers, not just new mocks? If any answer is no, fix that first. The cheapest test series is the free one you actually finish, and the most expensive one is the paid one you never open.
