A coaching institute in India does not fail for lack of teaching talent. It fails, quietly, on operations: batches that nobody tracks, tests that take a week to check, fees chased on phone calls, and results announced without proof. AI tools and teaching apps can fix much of that work, but only if you pick one tool per job and refuse to believe the sales pitch that a single app will do everything perfectly.

The direct answer is this: run your batches and classes on [Teachmint](https://www.teachmint.com/) or [Google Classroom](https://support.google.com/edu/classroom/answer/6025224?hl=en), test students on [Wayground](https://wayground.com/home/en/plans) (formerly Quizizz) or Google Forms quizzes, collect fees with [Razorpay](https://razorpay.com/solutions/education/) payment links, keep parents informed on the WhatsApp Business app, and make your study material with [Canva for Education](https://www.canva.com/en_in/education/) and Gemini Notebook. Start with the free tiers, prove the system on one batch for one month, and only then pay for a branded app such as [Classplus](https://classplusapp.com/new-web). No tool in this article can guarantee a student a rank, a selection, or a board score, and any institute that promises that in an advertisement is selling hope, not education.

This article is written for Indian coaching institutes: JEE, NEET, CUET, CA, UPSC, SSC, banking, board exams, spoken English, and local tuition batches, in Hindi, Hinglish, and English, on ordinary phones and laptops, with UPI and cash still common in fee payment. Every tool below works in India without a VPN, on a normal Indian internet connection. Prices and free limits change often, so before you pay for anything, or before you promise a parent a deadline that depends on a tool, re-check the official pricing and help page of that tool. Where sources disagree, this article says so rather than picking the friendlier number.

If you read nothing else, remember three rules. First, one tool per job: the batch app runs classes, the test app checks tests, and the payment link collects fees, and mixing them up is how records get lost. Second, copy every result out: export test scores, fee records, and attendance into your own spreadsheet every week, because a free plan can change its storage limit without asking you. Third, never let an AI tool write a result claim: only publish a result you can prove with a roll number, a scorecard, or a parent's written permission.

## Teachmint: the closest thing to a full coaching office

Teachmint describes itself as an all-in-one teaching app for teachers, tutors, and coaching institutes, and for a small institute that is a fair description. You can run live classes with video, chat, and screen sharing, record sessions for students to watch later, share notes, PDFs, and videos, create homework and tests with AI help, track submissions and attendance automatically, and watch student performance reports. Its AI tools generate homework and quizzes from what you just taught, which saves the most time in the subjects where you repeat the same chapter for three batches in a week.

What breaks first on Teachmint free is the recording and premium boundary. In a public support reply, Teachmint said live classes remain free while recordings and other premium features require a subscription. That matters for coaching because students who miss a class expect the recording, and parents judge you on it. Before you advertise recorded classes for every batch, check which plan your account actually holds, and test one recording end to end: record it, play it on a student's phone on mobile data, and confirm the audio is clear. A recording that only plays on your laptop is not a recorded class.

Use Teachmint this way. Create one class per batch, never one class for the whole institute, so attendance and homework stay separable. Turn on automatic attendance, but still call out the roll for the first week, because an automatic system that marks a student present when their phone was merely logged in will destroy parent trust. Generate quizzes with AI, then read every question yourself before assigning it, because an AI quiz can contain a wrong option key, a repeated question, or a question from outside your syllabus. Teachmint is strongest when a teacher checks its output, not when a teacher forwards it blindly.

## Classplus: a branded app, and a real bill

Classplus is a different kind of purchase. It does not just run your classes; it gives your institute its own branded Android app and website, lets you sell recorded and live courses, create online tests, and protect your content. Its own site claims more than one lakh teachers and five crore students across more than 3,300 cities. For an institute that already has paying students and wants to look like a brand rather than a tuition teacher with a WhatsApp group, that is the appeal.

What breaks first on Classplus is the price and the commitment. Its plan page, at the time of checking, showed a one-year plan at 29,999 rupees, marked down from 34,999 rupees, with unlimited recorded courses, live classes, student enrolments, and storage. Third-party summaries of Classplus pricing do not always match that page, and plan names and inclusions change, so treat any number in this article as a snapshot and confirm it on the Classplus plan page before you pay. A one-year plan is a serious commitment for an institute whose admissions come in waves; do not buy it in a lean month on the hope that admissions will follow.

Buy Classplus only when three things are true. You already have at least one full paid batch that attends regularly. You have recorded material worth protecting, not just a plan to record it someday. And someone on your team, not the owner alone, can manage the app, upload content, and answer student problems on it. If those three are not true, Classplus will sit empty while you still teach on WhatsApp, and you will have paid for a brand before you built the habit that fills it.

## Google Classroom: the free batch room that never pretends to be more

Google Classroom is the least glamorous tool here, and for many small institutes it is the right first step. It is free, it works on any phone, it organises materials, assignments, and announcements by class, and it connects to Drive, Docs, and Sheets, so a teacher who already uses Google does not need to learn a new system. For a tuition batch of forty students, it is more than enough.

Its limits are published, and you should know them before you build on it. With a personal Google account, a class holds 250 members, you can create 30 classes a day, and you can send 100 class-member invitations a day per teacher. With a Google Workspace or school account, a class holds 1,000 members and there is no daily limit on classes created. Google also states that to use Classroom with students at a school, the school must sign up for Google Workspace for Education Fundamentals, and that teachers and students then get additional privacy and security protection. A coaching institute is not a school, so check with your own setup whether a personal account, a Workspace account, or a school account fits your case, and do not collect student data in a personal account and call it a school system.

Classroom's weakness is also honest: it is not a coaching business system. It does not collect fees, it does not run a branded app, and it does not produce the kind of batch-wise result analytics a large institute needs. Use it for teaching and assignments, keep fees and admissions in a separate system, and move to Teachmint or Classplus when your batches outgrow a classroom, not before.

## Wayground: tests that check themselves

Wayground, formerly Quizizz, is the test tool most coaching teachers enjoy using, because students actually finish its quizzes. It offers interactive lessons, asynchronous assignments, engaging assessments, and Quizizz AI, with anti-cheating features even on the basic plan. Teachers can run a live quiz in class or assign it as homework, and the reports show which questions the batch got wrong, which is exactly the information a coaching teacher needs for the next revision class.

What breaks first on the free Basic plan is storage and scale. The plan page describes limited activities and library access, storage for up to 20 activities, and up to 100 participants per session, against up to 1,000 participants on paid plans. Twenty stored activities is enough for a weekly test cycle if you delete old tests, and it is not enough for a year of chapter tests kept forever. The practical fix is simple: after every test, export the report, save the questions in your own document, and then delete the activity. A test you cannot export is a test you will lose.

Use Wayground for weekly tests and revision quizzes, not for final mock exams that decide a batch's ranking, unless you have tested it at your full batch size first. Run one mock with every student logged in at the same time, on the phones they will actually use, and watch for late joins, dropped connections, and questions that render badly on small screens. A test platform that works for thirty students in a demo can still struggle when three hundred students open it at once on patchy mobile data.

## Google Forms: the boring quiz tool that never lets you down

Google Forms does not look like a coaching tool, and that is its strength. It is free with a Google account, it works on every phone, and its quiz mode lets you set an answer key, assign points to each question, add automatic feedback, and see automatic summaries including frequently missed questions, average, median, and range of scores. For a daily ten-question practice test, a homework check, or a scholarship screening test, Forms is often faster than any specialised app, because students already know how to open a form link.

Its limits are about dignity, not technology. A form looks plain, it has no gamification, and a student can open another tab during an unsupervised test, so Forms is honest only as a practice and homework tool, not as a secure examination. Never use Forms alone for a test whose result decides admission, a scholarship, or a batch promotion, unless you also supervise it in person or accept that the score measures homework conditions, not exam conditions. Also, collect email addresses only if you need them, and tell students why, because a form that silently harvests parent phone numbers and emails is a trust problem waiting to happen.

The best coaching workflow pairs Forms with Wayground: Forms for daily practice and homework, where speed matters, and Wayground for the weekly test, where engagement and question-level reports matter. Both are free to start, both export to Sheets, and neither requires a student to install a new app.

## Razorpay: fees without chasing phone calls

Fee collection is where most small institutes lose money and goodwill. Cash gets forgotten, bank transfers arrive without a student's name, and the owner spends evenings matching screenshots to batches. Razorpay's education offering is built for exactly this problem: it covers schools, colleges, coaching classes, and freelance coaches, with or without a website. Payment links can be shared on WhatsApp, SMS, or Instagram, with automated reminders, and Smart Collect gives each payer a unique identifier so a payment arrives already labelled with who paid.

The price is published and simple to state, with a warning attached. Razorpay states a standard rate of 2 percent per transaction excluding GST, with no setup or maintenance fee, and a typical settlement cycle of 2 to 5 days, with instant settlement available for certain amounts. Two percent matters on a large fee: on a 50,000 rupee course fee, that is 1,000 rupees plus GST, so decide in advance whether the institute absorbs it or the parent pays a convenience charge, and write that decision on the receipt. Also, never promise a parent that a refund will arrive on a particular date until you have checked Razorpay's settlement and refund timelines on its official help page for your account type, because settlement is not instant by default.

Two rules protect you on fees. First, give every student one payment link or identifier per fee instalment, and never accept a fee on a teacher's personal UPI without a receipt, because personal UPI mixes institute money with personal money and makes tax and dispute records painful. Second, keep a fee ledger outside Razorpay too, in a spreadsheet or accounting tool, with batch, instalment, date, and receipt number, so a payment gateway problem never becomes a lost-fee argument with a parent. This article is general information only, not tax, legal, or accounting advice, and fee, GST, and refund rules for your institute should be checked with your accountant or a professional adviser before you publish a fee policy.

## WhatsApp Business: parent communication, with a 256-person ceiling

Parents in India live on WhatsApp, so your institute must too, but the free WhatsApp Business app has a ceiling you must plan around. Each broadcast list holds up to 256 contacts, and a broadcast reaches only people who have saved your business number in their own phone. An institute with 900 parents therefore needs at least four lists, and parents who never saved the number will receive nothing at all, which is how fee reminders and test notices silently fail.

Use the free app well within that ceiling. Keep one broadcast list per batch, ask every parent at admission to save your institute number and send a test message, and keep urgent messages short: batch, date, time, and one action. Do not send test papers, fee links, and motivational forwards in the same message, because parents stop reading long messages. When your parent list grows past a few hundred, or when you need a shared team inbox instead of one phone, that is the point to price the WhatsApp Business API through an approved provider, where messages are charged per message and the 256 cap no longer applies, but the cost and the opt-in rules change completely. Do not buy API messaging because a vendor called you; buy it when your broadcast lists are actually failing.

Also, protect student privacy on WhatsApp. Never post a student's photo, score, or rank in a group without that student's and parent's permission, never share a batch group link publicly, and never let a teacher run institute communication from a personal number that leaves with the teacher. The institute's number, the institute's lists, and the institute's records belong to the institute.

## Canva for Education and Gemini Notebook: material that looks professional

A coaching institute's study material is its advertisement, and Canva for Education is the cheapest way to make it look professional. Canva states that its education offering is 100 percent free for K-12 educators, their students, and qualified school districts and institutions, with most premium features included, and that verification takes a few days after signing up with an education email or uploading proof of employment. A coaching institute is not automatically a K-12 school, so check Canva's eligibility guidelines for your case before you build a year's material on it, and start verification weeks before you need it, not the night before a batch launch.

Gemini Notebook, formerly NotebookLM, solves a different problem: turning your own notes into study material. You upload your class notes, previous year questions, or a chapter PDF, and it can build study guides, quizzes, and summaries grounded in those sources. Its free tier is described in third-party summaries of Google's pages as 100 notebooks with 50 sources per notebook under standard limits, while Google's own student offer, for eligible university students aged 18 and above, gives a free year of Google AI Plus with higher limits and 400 GB of storage, redeemable until 31 December 2026, after which it charges 399 rupees a month unless cancelled. Sources disagree on details of these limits because Google changes them, so check the current plan page before you build a course around a limit. The golden rule for both tools is the same: AI output is a first draft. A teacher must check every fact, every formula, and every answer key against the syllabus before it reaches a student, because an AI-generated error in a coaching note is not a typo, it is a mark lost in an exam.

## How to run this in an Indian institute, week by week

A system only works if it survives a normal week. Here is a week that works for a batch of fifty to two hundred students. On Monday, the batch timetable and study material go out on Teachmint or Classroom, and the fee reminder for the next instalment goes out on WhatsApp to parents who saved the number. On Wednesday, a ten-question practice quiz goes out on Google Forms, and the teacher reads the frequently missed questions before Thursday's class. On Saturday, the weekly test runs on Wayground, the report is exported the same evening, and the top three most-missed topics become Monday's revision. On Sunday, fee receipts for the week are reconciled against Razorpay and the ledger, and any unpaid instalment gets one polite reminder, not five.

For a multi-branch institute, add one more layer: one owner for the system. One person owns batch creation, so two branches do not create the same batch name twice. One person owns fee links, so a parent never receives two different links for the same instalment. One person owns result publication, so a rank is never announced from a screenshot. Small institutes fail on systems because everyone owns them, which means nobody does.

Results deserve a special rule. Publish only what you can prove. If ten students cleared an exam, publish ten names with their permission and their scorecards, not a poster claiming five hundred selections. If your topper studied with you for one month, say one month, not since Class 9. If a result came from a test series rather than a full course, say test series. Parents in India have learned to distrust coaching posters, and the institute that publishes modest, provable results will keep parents longer than the institute that publishes a spectacular poster nobody believes.

## What to avoid

Avoid the all-in-one promise. No single app runs live classes, branded courses, secure exams, fee collection, parent messaging, and result analytics equally well, and the vendor who claims it does is selling the demo, not the daily use. Avoid free AI grading of descriptive answers without a teacher reading them, because an AI grader can reward fluent wrong answers and punish correct short ones. Avoid collecting Aadhaar numbers, full addresses, or photographs in a form or a chatbot when a name, batch, and phone number would do, because every extra field is a data breach waiting to happen. Avoid announcing results, ranks, or selections on social media before the student and parent have agreed in writing, because a wrong announcement cannot be undone with an apology post. Avoid paying for a year of any platform in your first month, and avoid promising a parent a refund date, a batch timing, or a result that depends on a tool whose pricing or limit page you have not re-checked that week.

Also avoid the cheapest trap of all: teaching entirely inside a free chatbot. A free chatbot is a drafting assistant, not a batch system. It does not know your batches, it does not track attendance, it does not collect fees, and its answers change between sessions. Use chatbots to draft a lesson, a notice, or a quiz, then put the finished version into your batch system, where students and parents can actually find it again.

## FAQ

**Which single tool should a new coaching institute start with?**
Start with Google Classroom if your batches are small and your teaching is mostly in person, because it is free, familiar, and hard to break. Start with Teachmint if you run live online classes, need attendance and homework tracking, and want AI help making quizzes, because Classroom alone will not run a live online batch well. Do not start with a paid branded app until you have a full paid batch attending regularly.

**Is Teachmint really free for coaching institutes?**
Live classes are described as free, while recordings and other premium features require a subscription, according to a Teachmint support reply. That means the free tier is genuinely usable for teaching, but a recorded-class promise needs a paid plan, so check your own account's plan and test one recording before you advertise recordings to parents.

**How much does Classplus cost in India?**
Its plan page showed a one-year plan at 29,999 rupees, discounted from 34,999 rupees, at the time of checking, with unlimited recorded courses, live classes, enrolments, and storage. Third-party pages do not always match that figure, so re-check the official Classplus plan page before paying, and do not treat this article's figure as a quote.

**Can Google Classroom handle a batch of 500 students?**
With a personal Google account, no, because a class holds 250 members. With a Google Workspace or school account, a class holds 1,000 members, so 500 students fit in one class, but invitations, guardians, and domain rules differ by account type. Check Google's published Classroom limits for your account type before you enrol a large batch.

**Is Wayground free enough for weekly tests?**
For one batch with weekly tests, yes, if you export and delete old activities, because the Basic plan stores up to 20 activities with up to 100 participants per session. For several batches, or for keeping a full year of tests stored in the app, the Basic plan will run out, and you will need a paid plan or a habit of exporting everything to your own documents.

**Should fees be collected on a teacher's personal UPI?**
No. Use a business payment link, such as Razorpay, with one link or identifier per instalment, and issue a receipt for every payment. Personal UPI mixes institute and personal money, makes reconciliation painful, and leaves no clean record if a parent disputes a payment. This is general information only, not tax or legal advice, and your accountant should confirm the right setup for your institute.

**Can I send a fee reminder to 1,000 parents on free WhatsApp?**
Not in one broadcast, because each free WhatsApp Business broadcast list holds up to 256 contacts and reaches only people who saved your number. You would need at least four lists, and parents who never saved the number would still miss the message. For that scale, price the WhatsApp Business API through an approved provider, and confirm opt-in rules and per-message charges first.

**Can AI tools guarantee my students better results?**
No tool can guarantee a rank, a selection, or a score, because results depend on the student, the exam, and the teaching, not on an app. AI tools can save preparation time, check tests faster, and show which topics a batch missed, and those are real benefits. Any advertisement that promises a guaranteed result from a tool should be treated as a warning sign, not a selling point.

**Is student data safe on free teaching tools?**
Only as safe as your habits make it. Never paste student names, photos, answer sheets, or scores into a free chatbot, never upload student work to a tool your institute has no agreement with, and collect only the data you actually need. A tool's privacy page is not a substitute for your institute's own rule, and for school students, a guardian's consent and the school's policy matter more than a teacher's convenience.

**What should I check before paying for any tool in this article?**
Re-check the official pricing page, the free-plan limits, the refund and cancellation terms, and whether your institute type is eligible, on the day you pay. Prices, limits, and eligibility change without notice, and a figure in any article, including this one, is a snapshot from the day it was checked, not a promise from the vendor.

## Final verdict

Start free with Classroom plus Wayground, add Teachmint or Classplus only when paid batches grow, and never promise a rank no tool can guarantee.

That verdict is deliberately boring, and boring is what survives in a coaching business. A free classroom and a free test tool will teach you whether your batches attend, whether your tests get attempted, and whether your parents read your messages, before you spend a rupee. Teachmint earns its place when live classes and attendance become daily work. Classplus earns its place when you have paying students who deserve a branded app. Razorpay earns its place the first week you stop matching screenshots to batches. And none of them, alone or together, replaces the teacher who reads every AI-generated question, checks every answer key, and publishes only the results that can be proved. Build that habit first, and the tools will multiply it. Build the tools first, and they will only multiply the mess.
