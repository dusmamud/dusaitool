Maths homework in India is not one problem. A Class 10 board question, a Class 12 calculus problem, a JEE practice question, and a college engineering integral all need different help, and a tool that gives an answer fast does not always teach the method. AI maths tools help in two ways: they solve a problem **step by step**, and they generate **practice**. The tool that solves best does not always teach best, and the tool that teaches best does not always solve fastest.

The short answer for India is this: check steps in [*Photomath*](https://photomath.com/) free, because its Basic plan at zero cost includes step-by-step explanations, and practise in [*Khan Academy*](https://www.khanacademy.org/) free, because practice, not answers, is what builds exam skill. Use [*Mathway*](https://www.mathway.com/) free on the web for fast steps without signing up, knowing its subscription unlocks the full work for every step. Use [*Symbolab*](https://www.symbolab.com/) free for answers and its AI chat, knowing full access to solution steps is a Pro feature. **Never copy** a solver answer into homework you must understand, because an exam will ask the same method with different numbers, and a copied answer teaches you nothing.

This article is written for India on purpose: school students, board exam students, JEE and NEET aspirants, and college students, CBSE, NCERT, state board, and competitive exam syllabi, Hindi, Hinglish, and English explanations, a normal phone and laptop, and no assumption that you have a paid plan or a US account. It also carries a warning that matters for maths students more than for any other group in this series: a solver answer is not learning, and a school or exam that catches copied homework will treat it as cheating, even if the answer is correct.

## Photomath: the free steps app, with a pricing caveat

[*Photomath*](https://photomath.com/) is the app most people picture when they hear the phrase maths solver, and it earns the top spot for the reason that matters most to a family on a budget: **the steps are free**. Its pricing page lists a Basic plan at zero dollars that includes step-by-step explanations, and one source also lists custom visual aids and extra how and why tips in Basic, while another source says those tips are paid, so check your own account before you rely on them. On the US App Store the in-app purchase is called Photomath Plus, with rows at 5.99, 7.99, and 9.99 dollars, while the official site lists monthly at 9.99 dollars and annually at 69.99 dollars, labelled Best Value. Photomath's US App Store seller is Google LLC, with a rating of 4.8 from 733,000 ratings.

Photomath is best for algebra through early calculus problem sets where the problem is already written down. Point your phone at a printed or handwritten problem, including word problems, and get a step-by-step solution. Its limit is that it is maths-only, and it solves the problem you enter rather than generating practice from your material, so it will not teach you a topic from scratch, and it will not generate a practice set for your weak areas. Use Photomath in a specific way:

1. Try the problem yourself first.
2. Scan it.
3. Read every step.
4. Close the app and redo the problem without it.

Reading steps is not the same as doing steps, and an exam will not let you scan the question.

For Indian students, Photomath's camera input matters, because board and JEE problems are often printed in textbooks, and typing a long equation is slow and error-prone. Photomath reads printed and handwritten problems, which saves typing time, but handwriting recognition varies, especially for Hindi numerals or messy handwriting, so check that the scanned problem matches your textbook problem before you trust the steps. For word problems, Photomath can help, but word problems need translation into maths first, and a solver that misreads the word problem will solve the wrong equation confidently.

Its free plan says it is free to use and gives step-by-step solutions without signing up, while an optional subscription unlocks the full work for every step. On the US App Store, Steps costs 9.99 dollars a month, with one row at 19.99 dollars, and 39.99 dollars a year, with one row at 79.99 dollars. Mathway's owner is Chegg, Inc., with a US App Store rating of 4.9 from 425,000 ratings. Its limit is that it solves the problem you enter rather than generating practice from your material, so it is best for checking your work when you already understand the topic, not for learning a topic from zero.

[*Symbolab*](https://www.symbolab.com/) is best for showing every algebraic move, with an AI maths solver that breaks algebra, calculus, and trigonometry problems into steps and adds graphing, practice exercises, and an AI chat. Its free plan opens online solvers without a subscription, but its homepage does not itemize which steps need Pro, and Pro lists full access to solution steps, full access to its AI chat, and no ads as Pro features. Symbolab Pro costs from 2.49 dollars a week, 7.99 dollars a month or 9.99 dollars a month, half-year 24.99 or 32.99 dollars, and yearly 34.99 or 39.99 dollars, with its site now pairing Symbolab Pro with QuillBot writing tools. Its US App Store seller is Eqsquest Ltd, a Learneo business, with a rating of 4.6 from 26,000 ratings. Its distinctive features are AI chat, called Symbo, about any step, photo input, and a Chrome extension.

The practical difference comes down to the **free tier**. Photomath's own pricing page lists step-by-step explanations in the zero-dollar plan. Mathway now says on its homepage that you can get step-by-step solutions without signing up, and that an optional subscription unlocks the full work shown for every step. Symbolab gives answers free and lists full access to solution steps as a Pro feature. If you will pay, Mathway's annual Steps row at 39.99 dollars and Symbolab's yearly row at 34.99 dollars are the cheapest annual options of the three on the US App Store, while Photomath's own site lists 69.99 dollars a year. For Indian students, the cheapest annual option is not the point: free steps from Photomath, plus free practice from Khan Academy, cover most school and JEE preparation without paying at all.

## Khan Academy, WolframAlpha, and the rest

That is its strength, because practice with hints teaches the method, while a solver answer does not. Khanmigo, Khan Academy's tutor, costs 4 dollars a month or 44 dollars a year, for US learners and parents, with free access for teachers only, so Indian students should not plan around Khanmigo, and should use Khan Academy free practice instead. For board exams, Khan Academy's NCERT-aligned practice is useful, but it does not replace your textbook, your teacher, or your board's question pattern, so practise with Khan Academy, then solve board sample papers yourself.

[*WolframAlpha*](https://www.wolframalpha.com/) free gives answers, but steps are a Pro feature, with Pro at 9.99 dollars a month or 59.99 dollars a year, which makes it best for advanced maths, science, and data, not for free homework steps. For a college student who needs to check an integral or a data calculation, WolframAlpha free is useful, but for a school student who needs steps, Photomath free is better. [*Gauth*](https://www.gauth.com/) free is limited, with Gauth PLUS from 11.99 dollars a month, for multi-subject homework help, not just maths, which makes it useful for a student who needs help in several subjects, but its free limit is low, so it is a trial, not a workflow. [*Mathos AI*](https://www.mathos.ai/), formerly MathGPTPro, free is limited, with Basic at 5.98 dollars a week, for word problems and higher-level maths, which is expensive per week, so it is not a budget option for Indian students.

Microsoft Math Solver and Socratic are retired, which matters because old recommendations, old YouTube videos, and old blog posts still tell students to download them. **Do not download a clone app** that claims to be Microsoft Math Solver or Socratic, because the originals are gone, and a clone may be unsafe, may charge you, or may give wrong answers. Use Photomath, Mathway, Symbolab, or Khan Academy free instead, because those are the tools that actually exist and are actually free in 2026.

## Using maths AI in India: board, JEE, and language

Indian maths follows a syllabus, and AI solvers do not automatically know yours. NCERT, CBSE, state board, JEE, and NEET syllabi differ in method, notation, and question pattern. A solver step that is correct in general may use a method your board does not accept, or a notation your teacher does not use, and a board examiner **marks the method, not just the answer**. Always check solver steps against your textbook method, and rewrite the solution in your board's method before you submit it, because a correct answer with a foreign method may still lose marks. For JEE, a solver step that skips a shortcut your coaching teaches will be slower in an exam, even if it is correct, so learn the shortcut, not just the solver step.

Language for Indian maths students is mostly English for textbooks and exams, but explanations in Hindi or Hinglish help understanding. AI solvers give steps in English, and a student who understands the step in English but cannot explain it in Hindi may still struggle in a Hindi-medium classroom. Use Photomath steps in English, then explain each step to yourself in Hindi or Hinglish, out loud, because **explaining a step in your own language proves you understand it**, while reading it in English only proves you can read it. For Hindi-medium students, Khan Academy's Hindi videos and practice can help, but board sample papers in your medium are still the best practice, because they match your exam's language and pattern.

Cheating is the most important rule in this article, and it is simple: **do not copy a solver answer** into homework, an assignment, or an exam, because your school, your teacher, and your exam board can tell, and the consequence is worse than a wrong answer. A solver is for checking your work, for understanding a step you are stuck on, and for practising, not for submitting. Try the problem yourself, check your answer with Photomath or Mathway, understand the step where you went wrong, and redo the problem yourself, because that redo is the learning, and a copied answer skips it entirely.

## What to avoid

- **Do not copy** a solver answer into homework you must understand, because an exam will ask the same method with different numbers, and a copied answer teaches you nothing.
- **Do not trust** a solver step without checking it against your textbook method, because a correct answer with a foreign method may lose board marks.
- **Do not plan around retired tools** like Microsoft Math Solver or Socratic, and do not download a clone that claims to be them.
- **Do not pay for Khanmigo** as an Indian learner, because it is US only for learners and parents, with free access for teachers only, and Khan Academy free practice is the Indian option.
- **Do not pay for Mathos AI weekly** at 5.98 dollars a week, because it is expensive for Indian students, and Photomath free plus Khan Academy free cover most needs.
- **Do not use a solver for a word problem** without checking that it read the problem correctly, because a misread word problem gives a confident wrong answer.

A final trap is solving many problems and learning none. Beginners often scan fifty problems, read fifty solutions, and feel they have studied, when they have only read answers. Maths skill comes from doing problems yourself, not from reading solutions, and a solver that does the work for you does not build skill. Use a solver to check, to understand one stuck step, and to practise, then close the app and solve the next problem yourself, because that self-solved problem is the one that counts in an exam.
