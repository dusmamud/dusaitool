## Short answer

Three free tools cover most of a journalism student's work in India: Otter.ai turns interviews into searchable transcripts, NotebookLM answers questions only from your own research, and Grammarly catches grammar and tone errors before filing. Start free, verify every AI claim against a primary source, and never file AI-written copy as your own.

## At a glance

| Job | Best free pick | Free limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Interviews | Otter.ai | 300 min/month, 30 min per call |
| Research | NotebookLM | 100 notebooks, 50 sources each |
| Grammar | Grammarly | Free plan, 100 AI prompts/month |

## 1. Otter.ai — interview transcription

Otter.ai records interviews and produces a live transcript with speaker labels, so you can search for quotes instead of replaying audio. Free: 300 transcription minutes a month, but each conversation stops at 30 minutes, with only three file imports ever. Short interviews fit fine; long press conferences will not. Tell interviewees you are recording, and check transcripts against audio before quoting.

## 2. NotebookLM — research from your own sources

Google's NotebookLM, now also called Gemini Notebook, lets you upload press releases, reports and PDFs, then ask questions answered only from those sources, with citations. Free, it has offered 100 notebooks, 50 sources each, 50 chats a day and three audio overviews a day. Google moved to compute-based limits in September 2026, so re-check the official help page first.

## 3. Grammarly — clean copy before filing

Grammarly checks spelling, grammar and tone as you type. The free plan costs nothing and includes 100 AI prompts a month, enough for assignments and internship copy. Rewrites, tone adjustment and plagiarism checks need a paid plan; in India, a Plus plan costs ₹392 a month on annual billing. Use it to polish your own writing, not to generate the story.

## Using these tools in India

All three work in India without a VPN. Campus Wi-Fi and mobile data are enough, though long audio uploads are slow on weak connections. Grammarly shows rupees on its India page; Otter.ai bills in dollars. Check the final amount before paying, and re-check every price and limit on the official page before promising a deadline. Portfolio builders can compare [AI tools for bloggers](/blog/ai-tools-for-bloggers-india/).

## What to avoid

Never publish AI-generated text as your own reporting, never quote an unchecked transcript, and never upload unpublished or sensitive material — such as a source's identity — to a free AI tool without your editor's permission. AI invents quotes and facts, so check every fact against a primary source.

## FAQ

**Can AI tools write my news story for me?**

No. They can draft, summarise and polish, but the reporting — interviews, verification and judgement — is yours. Undisclosed AI-written copy is treated as an ethics breach by most newsrooms and schools.

**Is NotebookLM free for students in India?**

Yes, with a Google account, and it works in India without a VPN. Its free limits changed during 2026, so check Google's help page first.

**Do these tools work in Hindi?**

Otter.ai does not transcribe Hindi, so Hindi interviews need another approach. NotebookLM accepts Hindi sources and can generate audio overviews in many languages, including Hindi.

## Final verdict

Otter.ai, NotebookLM and Grammarly cover interviews, research and clean copy — free, if you respect their limits.
