A pet does not tell you when something is wrong. A dog that eats a little less, a cat that hides a little more, or a puppy that misses a vaccine by two weeks will not file a complaint. The owner has to notice, remember, and act, and that is exactly where pet care apps help. They keep the vaccine dates, the deworming dates, the weight log, and the medicine schedule in one place, and a few of them now add an AI layer on top: a symptom check, a food or calorie estimate, or a photo-based check that tells you whether a vet visit can wait until morning or needs to happen today.

The short answer for India is this: use [11pets](https://www.11pets.com/en/) free for pet profiles, medical records, and reminders, because it is built around preventive care schedules rather than a single clinic. Use [MaunaPet](https://maunapets.com/pricing) free for one pet if you want an AI assistant that reads your pet's profile before answering, knowing the free plan keeps only up to 10 health records and allows 10 AI messages every 3 hours. Use the [PetMD Symptom Checker](https://www.petmd.com/symptom-checker) free in a browser when your dog or cat shows a symptom, because it was built with veterinarians around more than 50 common symptoms and tells you when to call a vet. Use [Supertails](https://supertails.com/pages/consult-a-vet) for an Indian vet consultation, because new members get a complimentary online consult and the same platform sells food and medicines with delivery in 24 cities. Use [DogLog](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/doglog-track-your-dogs-life/id1229529595) free for daily walk, feeding, and medicine logs, knowing its Premium costs $3.99 per month or $39.99 per year. Keep the [Red Cross Pet First Aid](https://www.redcross.org/get-help/how-to-prepare-for-emergencies/mobile-apps.html) app on your phone for emergencies, because it is free and works offline for first aid steps. Use [Dog Scanner](https://siwalusoftware.com/dog-scanner/) free with ads if you adopted a mixed-breed dog and want a breed guess from a photo.

This article is written for India on purpose: dogs and cats in Indian homes, Indian summer heat, monsoon skin problems, street rescues and adopted Indies, a normal phone, and no assumption that you have a US vet account or a paid plan. It also carries the warning that matters more for pets than for any other topic in this series: an AI check is a screening aid, not a diagnosis. If your pet is vomiting repeatedly, has trouble breathing, has eaten something toxic, or is in visible pain, do not wait for an app to decide. Call a vet or go to a clinic at once. Prices and free limits change, so re-check the official pricing or help page of any tool before paying or before promising yourself a deadline based on a free tier.

## 11pets: the free record book that remembers the dates

[11pets](https://www.11pets.com/en/) is the most old-fashioned tool in this list, and that is a compliment. It does not try to diagnose your pet. It keeps a pet profile, medical records including x-rays, test results, blood work, treatments, allergies, and surgeries, and it maintains an automatic schedule for vaccinations and deworming, reminding you when the next action is due. You can also create your own categories for extra treatments, add hygiene care, and share the complete medical history with any veterinarian or specialist, which matters in India because many owners change vets when they move cities.

The free account covers the core job: a pet profile, medical records, and reminders tailored to your pet. The [pricing page](https://www.11pets.com/en/price) lists a Premium plan on top, and the extras named there are more than one pet, unlimited data storage, adding family members, behaviour monitoring, and sharing data with caregivers. One honest note: different listings describe the free plan's exact boundary differently, so this article states only what the official pricing page makes clear, which is that a free account exists and that multi-pet use and unlimited storage sit in Premium. If you have two dogs and a cat, check the Premium boundary before you build three years of records in the free plan.

11pets works in India without a VPN because it is a normal app and website, not tied to one country's clinic system. It does not book Indian vet appointments for you, and its promised remote monitoring from vets and 24-hour vet support are listed as coming soon on the pricing page, so do not buy it for those features today. Use it for what it does now: dates, records, and reminders that you control.

## MaunaPet: the AI assistant that reads the profile first

[MaunaPet](https://maunapets.com/pricing) is the clearest AI tool in this list. Its assistant is described as context-aware, which means it reads your pet's breed, age, and health history before answering, instead of giving the same generic answer to every owner. It also gives a daily wellness score that tracks vaccines, weight, and preventive care, and it offers free clinical web tools including calorie, water, age, and toxicity calculators. That last set matters for food: a calorie calculator stops you from overfeeding a sterilised indoor cat, and a toxicity calculator tells you quickly whether a food your dog just ate is on a danger list.

The free plan is precise, and the precision is the point. MaunaPet's pricing page says: free forever for one pet, up to 10 health records per pet, smart reminders for vaccines, deworming, medication, and vet visits, an AI assistant limited to 10 messages every 3 hours, a daily wellness score, activity, meals and water tracking, manual expense tracking, and cloud sync with no ads. Premium costs $9.99 per month or $89.99 per year, and it unlocks multi-pet homes, unlimited records, AI photo analysis, and PDF reports for your vet.

Two limits deserve attention. First, 10 health records is not many for an older pet with a long history, so MaunaPet free fits a young, healthy pet better than a senior pet with years of reports. Second, the AI assistant's 10-message window means you cannot have a long back-and-forth in one sitting on the free plan. The prices are listed in US dollars on the pricing page, so the amount you actually pay in India will appear in rupees through the app store and may differ. MaunaPet works with any vet, which is an advantage over clinic-locked apps, and it offers offline access in emergencies.

## PetMD Symptom Checker: the free browser check before you panic

The [PetMD Symptom Checker](https://www.petmd.com/symptom-checker) is not an app you install. It is a free web tool that asks a series of questions about your pet's symptom and then gives a summary page with possible causes, guidance on when to call your vet, and links to related education. PetMD says the tool was designed with veterinarians, covers more than 50 of the most common symptoms and combinations, and includes the problems owners report most often: vomiting, diarrhoea, not eating, itching, eye problems, shaking the head, and acting oddly.

Its strength is also its limit. A symptom checker can organise your worry into a clear next step, and it can tell you which details to note before you call the vet, such as when the symptom started, whether your pet is still eating and drinking, and whether there is blood. It cannot examine your pet, run a blood test, or feel a swelling, so its output is guidance, not a diagnosis. Use it at 11 pm when you are deciding between waiting until morning and calling an emergency clinic, and treat any result that says "call your vet" as an instruction, not a suggestion.

Because it runs in a browser, the Symptom Checker works in India without a VPN and without an install. It is written for dog and cat owners generally, so Indian owners should still confirm medicine names and doses with an Indian vet, since drug brands and availability differ by country. PetMD itself is an education site from Chewy, a US pet company, and its vet chat feature is tied to Chewy's own service, so Indian owners should use the free checker and take the summary to their own vet rather than expecting a US telehealth chat to treat an Indian pet.

## Supertails: the Indian option for vet consults, food, and medicine

[Supertails](https://supertails.com/pages/consult-a-vet) is the only tool in this list built for India first. It is an Indian pet store and care platform with more than 17,000 pet products from over 100 brands, delivery within 24 hours across 24 major Indian cities, and a pharmacy for dog and cat medicines. Its online vet consultation lets you book a video or chat consult, and the site says the vet calls within 10 minutes of the scheduled time, sends a prescription over WhatsApp when medicine is needed, and offers 10 percent off plus free delivery on medicines ordered through its pharmacy partner. New members get a complimentary vet consult, and the site reports more than 50,000 pets treated and an average rating of 4.96 across its consultations.

For a pet owner, the practical value is that health advice, food, and medicine live in one place. A vet who tells you to switch to a prescription diet can point you to the same platform's pharmacy, and the post-consultation kit includes medical records, a treatment plan, the prescription, and a complimentary pet health report. Supertails also runs clinics in Bangalore and offers at-home services at your doorstep in some cities, so an online consult can turn into an in-person visit when needed.

The honest limits are these. The complimentary consult is for new members, so a repeat consult will cost something, and the site does not put one single repeat price in the pages checked for this article, so check the booking page before you assume a price. Delivery in 24 hours covers 24 cities, not every town in India, so owners outside those cities should check delivery time for their PIN code. And an online consult has the same limit as every telehealth service: a vet on video cannot run a blood test or feel a lump, so serious or unclear cases still need a clinic.

## DogLog: the daily routine log for multi-person homes

[DogLog](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/doglog-track-your-dogs-life/id1229529595) answers a different question from the tools above. It does not diagnose anything. It tracks the daily routine: walks, feeding, potty breaks, medicine, and activities, and it keeps the whole household in sync so that one person does not feed the dog twice because nobody logged the first meal. That sounds small until you have a puppy on a strict feeding schedule, a dog on twice-daily medicine, or a family where three people share dog duty.

The app is free to download with in-app purchases. DogLog's own pricing post puts Premium at $3.99 per month or $39.99 per year, and Premium is described as turning simple tracking into control: reminders for medicines and feeding, trend spotting for weight, glucose, symptoms, and behaviour, clean reports to share with your vet, and pack-wide updates for the whole family. The free version covers basic logging, and the premium price will appear in rupees through the app store in India.

DogLog is for dogs only, which is worth saying plainly, since cat owners need a different routine tool. It also has no AI diagnosis and no vet booking, so it pairs best with a record tool like 11pets or a vet service like Supertails: DogLog for the day-to-day, 11pets for the medical history, and a vet for the decisions. If you live alone with one healthy dog and you already remember the routine, DogLog may be more than you need, but for a shared home it prevents the most common pet care mistake, which is a missed or doubled dose.

## Red Cross Pet First Aid: the free emergency app to install before you need it

The [Red Cross Pet First Aid](https://www.redcross.org/get-help/how-to-prepare-for-emergencies/mobile-apps.html) app is the one tool in this list you should install on a calm day and hope you never open in a panic. It is published by the American Red Cross, free to download on Apple and Google stores, and rated 4.6 stars on Google Play. It gives step-by-step first aid for more than 25 common pet situations, with videos, including size-specific CPR techniques, bleeding, choking, toxic ingestion, and heatstroke, plus a guide to common toxic substances, an early warning sign checker for preventive care, and a locator for the nearest emergency vet hospital.

Its value in India is specific. Summer heatstroke, monsoon-related skin infections, and accidental poisoning from household items are common Indian pet emergencies, and the app's offline access means the steps stay available when the network does not. The emergency vet locator is built around US listings, so Indian owners should save their own vet's number and the nearest 24-hour clinic's number inside the app, using its programmable vet contact feature, rather than relying on the locator alone. The app is a reference, not a tracker: it does not keep vaccine records or daily logs, so pair it with 11pets or MaunaPet for the routine side.

Install it, add your vet's number, and run through one scenario, such as choking or heatstroke, before an emergency happens. In a real emergency, first aid buys time, and time is what gets a pet to a vet alive.

## Dog Scanner: a fun, useful check for adopted and mixed-breed dogs

[Dog Scanner](https://siwalusoftware.com/dog-scanner/) identifies dog breeds from a camera photo or a gallery upload, and it handles mixed breeds by telling you which breeds appear to be mixed. It gives a result within seconds, and you can share results with a community. The free version carries ads, and Premium removes ads, adds an offline mode, and gives faster or more accurate scans depending on the setting you choose.

Breed identification is not just a party trick for Indian owners. A large share of Indian pet dogs are Indies and rescues whose breed mix is unknown, and breed guesses help you predict adult size, coat care, exercise needs, and some breed-linked health risks to discuss with your vet. Treat the result as a guess, not a pedigree certificate: photo-based breed apps are estimates, and two photos of the same dog can give different mixes. If a result suggests a breed with a known health risk, take that question to your vet instead of changing food or medicine on the app's word alone.

Dog Scanner is free to try, so the cost of checking is only the ads in the free version. It is for dogs only; the same developer's cat equivalent is a separate app, and this article does not cover it because its pricing and features were not verified here.

## Using pet AI in India: language, payment, and no VPN

Most of these tools work in India without a VPN because they are normal apps and websites, not region-locked services. The exceptions to watch are not technical but commercial: prices shown in US dollars, features tied to another country's clinics, and locators built for another country's vets. MaunaPet and DogLog show dollar prices, and the amount you pay will appear in rupees through the app store. Supertails is priced and delivered for India, which is why it is the natural first choice for a paid consult or medicine order. PetMD's checker is free in a browser, so it costs nothing to try from any Indian connection.

Language is the second India question. These tools are in English, and the Indian pet owner who is more comfortable in Hindi will find the interfaces English-first. That is a real limit, and it is one reason this article keeps its advice simple: record dates in any language your vet understands, keep the pet's medical file in English where possible because most Indian vets write prescriptions in English, and use the app's reminders even if the app's own language is English. Payment is the third question: free tiers need no payment at all, and paid tiers go through the app store or the vendor's own checkout, so check whether the price is shown in rupees before you subscribe, and check the cancellation path in the same sitting, because a pet app subscription is easy to forget and hard to notice on a bank statement.

## What to avoid

Avoid treating any AI result as a diagnosis. A symptom checker, a photo check, and a breed guess are all estimates, and pets hide pain well, so an app that says "probably fine" is not a reason to skip a vet visit when the symptom persists, worsens, or involves breathing, bleeding, seizures, or suspected poisoning. Avoid apps whose core feature depends on another country's system. PetDesk is a well-known free app, but its booking works only with partner clinics, which does not help an Indian owner book a local vet, so use it only if you want its reminders and not its booking. Avoid Korea- or US-tied subscription plans that assume a local telecom or store: TTCare's own pricing page presents its service as a KT telecom add-on service in Korea, which is a sign that its paid plans are not built for an Indian owner, even if the app itself installs.

Avoid paying for a tracker subscription before checking whether the hardware and the network work in India. GPS pet trackers sold for the US market often depend on a US carrier or a subscription that does not cover Indian networks, and a tracker that cannot connect is a dead weight on a collar. Avoid pasting your pet's full medical history, your home address, and your payment details into every free app that asks for them. Pet health data is health data, so keep the detailed file in one record tool you trust, share a PDF summary with a vet when asked, and read the privacy note of any app that wants your pet's photos for training. For more on keeping health data private, see the guide to [privacy and safety for free tools](/blog/ai-privacy-safety-free-tools-india/). Avoid buying food or medicine on an app's recommendation alone. A calorie calculator and a food suggestion are starting points, and a vet who knows your pet's age, weight, and condition should confirm a diet change, especially for puppies, senior pets, and pets with kidney, liver, or allergy problems.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can a free app really replace my vet?** No. A free app replaces the notebook, the calendar, and the guesswork, not the vet. Records, reminders, and symptom guidance help you reach the vet with better information, but examination, tests, and treatment stay with a veterinarian.

**Which free tool should I start with for a new puppy?** Start with 11pets for vaccination and deworming reminders, because a puppy's first year is mostly a schedule, and add the Red Cross Pet First Aid app for emergencies. Add MaunaPet free later if you want an AI assistant and a wellness score for one pet.

**Is MaunaPet free really free?** Yes, for one pet, with limits that are stated on its pricing page: up to 10 health records, 10 AI messages every 3 hours, and reminders, wellness score, and tracking included. Premium at $9.99 per month or $89.99 per year removes those limits and adds multi-pet use, unlimited records, AI photo analysis, and PDF vet reports.

**Can I get a vet consult online in India?** Yes. Supertails offers online vet consultations in India, with a complimentary consult for new members, and the vet can send a prescription over WhatsApp when medicine is needed. Serious or unclear cases still need an in-person clinic visit.

**Are AI symptom checkers safe to use?** They are safe as a first step and unsafe as a final answer. PetMD's checker is vet-reviewed and free, and it tells you when to call a vet, which is the right way to use it. Never use a checker to decide a dose of medicine, and never wait on a checker when breathing, bleeding, seizures, or poisoning are involved.

**Do these tools work for cats as well as dogs?** Most do. 11pets, MaunaPet, PetMD's checker, Supertails, and the Red Cross app all cover cats and dogs. DogLog and Dog Scanner are dog-only, so cat owners should skip those two and use the rest.

**What about my pet's photos and data?** Keep the detailed medical file in one trusted record tool, share summaries rather than full histories when you can, and check an app's privacy note before uploading photos. For the general rules on free-tool privacy, see the [privacy and safety guide](/blog/ai-privacy-safety-free-tools-india/).

## The final verdict, and a checklist before you pay

Start with 11pets free for records and reminders, and Supertails for an Indian vet consult. AI checks never replace a real vet visit. Before you pay for anything, run this checklist: first, confirm the free tier covers your actual need, such as one pet or three, for at least a month. Second, check the price in rupees in the app store or on the vendor's checkout, not just the dollar price on a pricing page. Third, confirm the feature you are paying for works in India, such as booking, delivery to your PIN code, or a vet who can actually see your pet. Fourth, save your vet's number and the nearest 24-hour clinic's number in the first aid app before an emergency. Fifth, re-check the official pricing and help page before paying, because free limits and prices change, and a limit you read today may not be the limit next month.
