## 1. Qure.ai qXR — chest X-ray screening

Qure.ai's qXR analyses chest X-rays and flags tuberculosis, pneumonia, pneumothorax, and lung findings within seconds. It holds CDSCO approval in India and EU MDR Class IIb certification, and an Indian public-health assessment found it cost-saving for TB screening. It runs at over 2,600 sites in 67 countries, with pricing on request.

## 2. Aidoc — urgent-case triage

Aidoc watches CT scans and flags time-critical findings such as intracranial haemorrhage, pulmonary embolism, and large-vessel occlusion, and pushes urgent cases up the worklist. Its algorithms hold US FDA 510(k) clearance and CE marks, and it is used in more than 900 hospitals. It is an enterprise product for institutions, with pricing quoted on request.

## 3. Rad AI Omni — reporting speed

Rad AI Omni drafts the impression section of a report from the radiologist's dictated findings, matching each doctor's style, and its Continuity module tracks follow-up imaging that might otherwise be missed. Pricing is not published and is quoted per radiologist or per study. Its reporting products are not FDA-cleared devices, and every draft needs radiologist review and sign-off.

## Using these tools in India

qXR fits government TB programmes, screening camps, and high-volume X-ray centres. Aidoc suits large hospitals with emergency CT loads. Teleradiology networks such as 5C Network use AI-assisted workflows, so remote radiologists should confirm which tools their network provides. All three work in India without a VPN, but onboarding can take weeks.

## What to avoid

Do not treat any AI output as a final diagnosis, and do not upload patient scans to free consumer chatbots or unvetted tools. Check CDSCO status, data residency, and patient-consent rules before connecting any tool to your PACS.
