## The direct answer

If you farm fish in ponds or fish at sea in India, the most useful AI tools are not exotic. They are a small set of practical helpers: a government advisory that points sea fishermen toward likely catch zones, a government weather warning that tells you when not to go out, a farm app that turns your pond readings into feeding and disease advice, and a general chatbot that drafts your sales messages and keeps your records tidy. Start with the free government services and one farm app. Add paid pond sensors only when you already record readings every day and the free advice is genuinely saving you fish, feed, or fuel.

That order matters because fisheries work punishes guesswork. A wrong feeding decision wastes expensive feed and fouls pond water. A missed weather warning can cost a boat, a net, or a life. A tool that looks impressive but needs daily data you will never enter is worse than a simple notebook used honestly. The tools below are chosen for India: they work on an ordinary Android phone, they do not need a VPN, and the government ones are free.

A note on prices and limits before anything else: prices and free limits change often, and different comparison pages disagree about exact caps because the providers themselves often do not publish a fixed number of free messages. Treat every price or limit in this article as a snapshot from early November 2026. Before you pay, or before you promise a buyer a delivery date that depends on a tool, re-check the tool's official pricing or help page. Where a price is not published, this article says so plainly rather than guessing.

## 1. INCOIS Potential Fishing Zone advisory: where the fish may be

The Indian National Centre for Ocean Information Services (INCOIS), an autonomous body under the Ministry of Earth Sciences, publishes a Potential Fishing Zone (PFZ) advisory for the Indian coast. It uses satellite data on sea surface temperature and chlorophyll to identify zones where fish are likely to gather, and it issues these advisories daily for 586 fish landing centres, divided into 14 sectors from Gujarat around to West Bengal and the island territories.

Why this helps a fisherman is simple: searching blindly burns diesel and hours. INCOIS itself notes that the advisory is meant to reduce searching time and fuel cost, and that feedback from fishermen showed higher catches in PFZ areas than elsewhere, particularly for artisanal, motorised, and small mechanised fishers doing pelagic fishing such as ring seining and gill netting. The advisory gives latitude and longitude, depth, and distance and direction from a known coastal landmark, and it is provided in the native languages of each sector. PFZ maps also carry wind speed and direction, which matters because a zone can shift by the time you reach it.

How to use it well: check the advisory the evening before or early on the morning of a trip, note the nearest zone to your landing centre, and combine it with the IMD warning for your coast (see below). Never treat a PFZ as a guarantee. The ocean moves, the advisory itself warns that zones can shift, and the service pauses during the government fishing ban period and during adverse sea conditions such as cyclones. A PFZ tells you where to look first, not where fish certainly are.

It works in India without any VPN or payment, and it is delivered through the INCOIS website, telephone, email, radio, television, and display boards at major harbours, so even a fisherman without a smartphone habit can get it through a harbour board or a fellow fisherman.

## 2. IMD fishermen warnings and marine forecast: the safety tool that comes first

The India Meteorological Department (IMD) publishes fishermen warnings and a marine forecast for India's coasts. This is the tool to check before the PFZ advisory, not after it, because no catch is worth a dangerous sea. Warnings are issued coast by coast — for example, separate warnings for North and South Gujarat, Maharashtra, Goa, Karnataka, Kerala, Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh, Odisha, and West Bengal — and they change with live conditions such as a low-pressure area or a developing cyclone.

A sensible sea routine is: first read the IMD warning for your stretch of coast; if there is a warning or a "be cautious" advice, stay back or stay close to shore, whatever the PFZ says. If there is no warning, then use the PFZ advisory to choose where to head. Keep both checks in the same ten minutes every morning so the habit survives busy days. Fishermen who check only the catch advisory and skip the warning are using half the system.

Both INCOIS and IMD are government services, so they are free, they work across India, and they do not require a subscription. Their limitation is the opposite of a commercial app's: they give you a zone or a warning, not a personal plan for your boat, your net, or your crew. You still have to apply local knowledge about your own boat's range and your harbour's bar conditions.

## 3. AquaConnect and FarmMOJO: the pond manager for shrimp and fish farmers

AquaConnect is a full-stack aquaculture platform for shrimp and fish farmers in India, built around its FarmMOJO mobile app. According to the platform's own description and the UN Food and Agriculture Organization's digital solutions listing, the app uses AI and machine learning to analyse pond data — including feed and growth patterns — and gives context-specific alerts and suggestions on water quality, aquatic health, and feed use. It also has a Pond Diary feature where a farmer records farm activities, including in multimedia form, so the advice is based on that farm's own history rather than generic tips.

The same platform also connects farmers to inputs and to buyers, which is why it appears again in the sales section below. Its geographic footprint, per the FAO listing, includes Gujarat, Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh, Odisha, West Bengal, and Uttar Pradesh, and it describes itself as free for farmers, earning from inputs, trade, and services rather than from a farmer subscription. That is a genuinely useful model for a small farmer, but it also means you should check, before ordering inputs through any platform, whether the price beats your local dealer once transport is included.

AquaConnect has also worked with partners on predicting shrimp disease using machine learning, analysing feed and growth patterns in relation to animal health, with the aim of warning farmers before disease spreads. Shrimp disease is one of the biggest sources of loss in Indian aquaculture — Andhra Pradesh alone accounts for a large share of Indian shrimp production — so an early warning, even an imperfect one, can be worth more than a perfect diagnosis that arrives after the pond has crashed.

How to use it well: enter pond data honestly every day, including feed given, mortality noticed, and any water test you did, even if the numbers look bad. An AI adviser trained on clean data cannot help a farm whose diary hides problems. If the app suggests a change in feeding, try it on one pond first and compare with a pond you manage the old way for a week before changing everything.

## 4. AquaClue and Aqbot: quick answers for fish and shrimp ponds

AquaClue is an Indian AI-powered aquaculture management platform for fish and shrimp farmers that works on any Android phone and is pitched as needing no complex setup. Its central feature is Aqbot, an AI assistant that answers farm questions at any hour — its own site gives examples such as high ammonia, fish not eating, and white spots on shrimp — and it tracks eight or more water parameters so that risks are flagged before disease or stress hits the stock.

For a small pond farmer, this is the lowest-friction way to start with AI: there is no sensor to install and no hardware to maintain. You type or speak the problem, you get an answer immediately, and you can act the same morning. The site describes the app as free to install with access to Aqbot, but as with every app that promises "free", check inside the app whether any advanced feature is paid before you build your routine around it, and never let a chatbot answer replace a water test when fish are actually dying.

Use AquaClue or AquaConnect, not both at once, at least for the first month. Running two diaries means neither has your full history, and both will give weaker advice. Pick the one whose language, layout, and local support feel easiest on your phone, commit to daily entries for a month, and only then judge it.

## 5. AquaSense by Keleno: sensors that watch the pond for you

AquaSense, by Keleno, is a connected aquaculture system: physical sensors in the water, solar and LoRa connectivity at the pond edge, and a live AI dashboard in the cloud. It monitors water quality continuously, sends smart alerts by SMS, email, and voice, covers multiple ponds and locations in one dashboard, and keeps trend history so you can see whether a problem is a one-off spike or a slow drift.

This is the right tool for a farmer who already knows the value of daily readings but cannot be at every pond at dawn, or who runs several ponds and currently discovers problems only at feeding time. Continuous monitoring catches the overnight dissolved-oxygen crash that a morning visit finds too late. Its honest drawback is cost and maintenance: sensors need cleaning, calibration, power, and connectivity, and the vendor's product page does not publish a price, so you must ask for a quote that includes installation, sensor replacement, and any monthly dashboard fee. Do not compare it with a free app; compare it with the cost of one lost crop, and with the salary of a worker doing manual testing, before deciding.

A related warning applies to every sensor system, not just this one: a sensor that is not cleaned and calibrated will confidently report wrong numbers. If you buy sensors, budget time every week for cleaning and a monthly check against a manual test kit. An AI dashboard fed by a dirty sensor is worse than no dashboard, because it replaces your own judgement with a confident error.

## 6. ICAR-CCARI AI water-quality system: promising research, not yet a shop product

The ICAR-Central Coastal Agricultural Research Institute in Goa is developing an AI-based water-quality monitoring system for aquaculture, in collaboration with a Kerala technology venture. It uses sensors to monitor parameters such as pH and dissolved oxygen continuously, sends an alert to the farmer's phone when a value crosses a safe threshold — for example, dissolved oxygen falling too low — and aims to recommend corrective action, such as how much lime to apply when pH goes out of range.

Why mention a system you cannot buy? Because it shows where the field is heading, and because it carries an honest lesson. The researchers themselves say the system is still being validated, that their institute farm does not have the high stocking densities of a commercial farm, and that it must still be tested under intensive commercial conditions where feed and waste drive much bigger water-quality swings. The prototype device is estimated to cost around Rs 3 lakh to Rs 4 lakh, largely because of sensor costs, and the team is working on a lower-cost version. Treat any seller who claims to sell you this exact ICAR system today with suspicion, and treat the research as a reason to expect cheaper, better sensors in the coming years — not as a reason to wait. Your ponds need management this season, with the tools that exist now.

## 7. ChatGPT and Gemini: the general helpers for records, maths, and sales

General chatbots such as ChatGPT and Gemini are not fisheries tools, but they are the most flexible helpers a fish farmer or fisherman can carry. Used well, they draft a WhatsApp message to buyers in polite, correct language, turn a messy notebook of daily sales into a tidy weekly table, calculate feed cost per kilogram of fish grown, write a simple loan or subsidy application draft, and explain a government scheme in plain language. A fisherman can dictate a message in his own language and ask for it rewritten for a buyer; a pond farmer can paste a month of feed and harvest numbers and ask for the feed conversion trend.

Their free tiers are real but limited in ways that are hard to pin down, because the providers do not publish a fixed number of free messages. Recent third-party comparisons in October 2026 describe ChatGPT's everyday text chats as effectively uncapped on the free plan, with limits applying to uploads, image creation, voice, and deep research, while Gemini's free usage refreshes every few hours within a weekly cap, with heavier features consuming it faster. Those two descriptions do not fully agree with each other, and neither replaces the provider's own page. The practical rule is: do your routine record work in the free tier, keep a copy of anything important outside the chatbot, and check the official pricing page before you pay for a plan or promise a buyer a deadline that depends on heavy chatbot use.

Two cautions specific to fisheries. First, a chatbot can confidently invent a medicine dose, a legal rule, or a market price. Never apply a chatbot's medicine, chemical, or legal advice to a pond or a sale without checking it against a fisheries officer, a veterinarian, or the official scheme document. Second, do not paste a buyer's personal details, bank details, or your own identity documents into a chatbot. Sales messages need only the fish, the quantity, the price, and the pickup time.

## 8. Selling the catch: prices, markets, and online buyers

Catching or growing fish is only half the business; selling at a fair price is the other half, and AI tools help here too, mostly by giving you price information and wider buyer reach before you accept the first offer at the landing centre.

The National Fisheries Development Board's Fish Market Price Information System collects wholesale and retail prices for commercially important marine and inland fish from 111 wholesale and retail markets across 29 states and union territories. Checking it before you sell tells you whether the price offered at your local market is fair or whether transporting the catch a short distance would pay. Use it as a bargaining reference, not as a promised price: the price on a screen is not the price a buyer will pay for your specific size, quality, and quantity today.

For online selling, the Department of Fisheries has signed an agreement with the Open Network for Digital Commerce (ONDC) to help fishermen, fish farmer producer organisations, and fisheries entrepreneurs buy and sell through digital marketplaces, and the Pradhan Mantri Matsya Sampada Yojana (PMMSY) supports e-trading and e-marketing platforms for fish and fisheries products, along with fish transport, cold-chain, wholesale markets, retail markets, and kiosks. The practical meaning for a small seller is to join through a seller app or a producer organisation rather than trying to build an online shop alone, and to sort out cold storage and transport before listing, because a fish that spoils in transit earns a refund demand, not a repeat buyer.

AquaConnect's market linkage fits here as well: because it already holds your farm and harvest data, it can connect you to buyers without you re-entering everything. Compare its buyer price, payment timing, and transport arrangement with your local mandi on at least three sales before committing your whole harvest to one channel. A chatbot can then help you write the listing: species, size grade, quantity, harvest date, ice and packing, pickup point, and price — the six facts every serious buyer asks for first.

## Using these tools in India: a daily routine that actually sticks

For a pond farmer, the routine that works is boring and short. Morning: check overnight alerts from your farm app or sensor dashboard, walk the ponds, and note anything unusual. Midday: enter feed, mortality, and any water test into one app only. Evening: read the next day's weather for your district and decide feeding and aeration for tomorrow. Weekly: compare feed used against growth or harvest, check market prices, and message two buyers rather than one. The AI helps at each step, but the routine is what makes the AI useful; without daily entries, even the best app is guessing.

For a sea fisherman, the routine is even shorter and more safety-critical. Evening: check the IMD warning and the INCOIS PFZ advisory for tomorrow, and decide go or no-go with your crew. Morning before departure: re-check the warning, because it can change overnight. After landing: note catch, price, and buyer in a notebook or a chatbot-made table the same day, while the numbers are fresh. Monthly: review which zones actually produced, so you learn whether the advisory or your own experience served you better in each season.

For both, language and connectivity matter more than fancy features. Choose tools that work in your language, that work on a weak network, and that let you use voice input if typing is slow. A tool that needs a strong signal at the pond bund or on a boat will fail exactly when you need it most. Download advisories and price lists while you have signal, and keep a paper backup of emergency numbers, the local fisheries office, and your buyers.

## What to avoid

- **Do not go to sea on a PFZ advisory while ignoring an IMD warning.** The catch advisory is about profit; the warning is about safety. Safety wins every time.
- **Do not trust a single water reading or a single chatbot answer.** Re-test a surprising reading, and cross-check any medicine, chemical dose, or legal claim with a fisheries officer or veterinarian before acting.
- **Do not run two pond diaries at once.** Split data gives both apps a partial picture and weaker advice. Pick one app for a month.
- **Do not buy sensors before you have a daily recording habit.** A sensor dashboard on top of an unrecorded farm just automates neglect, and dirty, uncalibrated sensors report confident errors.
- **Do not pay for a tool on the basis of a price quoted in an article.** Prices and free limits change, and some vendors do not publish prices at all. Re-check the official pricing or help page first.
- **Do not share bank details, identity documents, or buyers' personal data with a chatbot or an unknown seller app.** Sales need fish, quantity, price, and pickup time — nothing more.
- **Do not promise a buyer a quantity or date that depends on a forecast.** Weather shifts, zones move, and ponds crash. Promise a range and a confirmation time instead.
- **Do not break the seasonal fishing ban or local mesh and size rules because an app suggested a good zone.** The PFZ service itself pauses during the ban, and local rules still apply. Check with the state fisheries department when unsure.
- **Do not let an app replace your own eyes.** Fish gasping at the surface, a change in water colour, or a strange smell at the landing centre are data too, and they arrive faster than any dashboard.

## FAQ

**Which single free tool should a sea fisherman start with?**
Start with the IMD fishermen warning for your coast, then the INCOIS PFZ advisory. The warning decides whether you go at all, and the PFZ advisory decides where you look first. Both are free government services and need no subscription.

**Which single free tool should a pond farmer start with?**
Start with one farm app — AquaConnect or AquaClue — and enter pond data daily for a month. Daily entries matter more than the choice between the two apps, so pick the one that feels easiest on your phone and stick to it.

**Can AI really predict fish disease?**
Partly. Machine-learning models can flag rising risk from feed, growth, and water patterns before visible disease spreads, and AquaConnect has worked on exactly this for shrimp disease. But a flag is a warning to inspect, test, and call an expert — it is not a diagnosis, and it cannot replace water testing or veterinary advice.

**Do these tools work without a VPN in India?**
Yes. INCOIS, IMD, AquaConnect, AquaClue, and the general chatbots all work in India without a VPN on an ordinary internet connection. If any tool asks you to use a VPN, treat that as a warning sign and check whether you have reached the genuine official app or site.

**Are pond sensors worth the money for a small farmer?**
Only after the free routine works. If you already record daily, lose fish to overnight oxygen crashes, or run several ponds, sensors can pay for themselves. If you do not yet record daily, start with the free app first; sensors on an unrecorded farm will not fix the underlying habit.

**How reliable is the PFZ advisory?**
It is a useful probability guide, not a guarantee. It is based on satellite temperature and chlorophyll data, zones can shift with wind and currents, and feedback shows better catches in PFZ areas than elsewhere, but on any single day your experience, your net, and local conditions can beat the advisory. Keep your own catch log and compare over a season.

**Can I sell fish online without a shop?**
Yes, through a seller app or a fish farmer producer organisation, including channels linked to the ONDC fisheries initiative and PMMSY-supported e-marketing. Sort out ice, packing, transport, and payment timing before your first listing, and start with nearby buyers before shipping far.

**What should I do if fish start dying suddenly?**
Stop feeding, check dissolved oxygen and ammonia if you can, aerate, and contact your local fisheries officer or a veterinarian the same day. Note what changed — new feed, a water change, rain, or new stock — and keep dead fish aside for inspection rather than throwing them away. Do not dose medicine on a chatbot's advice alone.

**Do I need an expensive phone for these tools?**
No. The government advisories work through the web, radio, and harbour display boards, and AquaClue is pitched for any Android phone. A basic Android phone with a data connection is enough; spend money on water test kits and ice before you spend it on a better phone.

**Where can I get help with subsidies for ponds, cold storage, or markets?**
Through your state fisheries department and the Pradhan Mantri Matsya Sampada Yojana, which supports pond and hatchery work, post-harvest infrastructure, transport, markets, and e-marketing. Scheme details, eligibility, and deadlines change, so confirm the current rules with the state fisheries office or the official scheme page before applying or spending money in expectation of a subsidy.

## Final verdict

Start free with INCOIS, IMD and AquaConnect or AquaClue; pay for pond sensors only after the free routine works.
