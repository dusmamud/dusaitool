Planning a trip in India used to mean ten open tabs, a spreadsheet, and a group chat full of screenshots. AI trip planners promise to compress that mess into one conversation: tell the tool your dates, your budget, and who is travelling, and it returns a day-wise plan with places, food, stays, and transport. Some of them do this genuinely well. Others produce a confident-looking plan with a train that does not exist, a hotel price from last year, or a day so packed that nobody in your family would enjoy it.

The short answer for India is this: for trips inside India, start with [ixigo PLAN](https://ai.ixigo.com/) because it is built for Indian cities, trains, and flights, and it is free inside the ixigo app. For a trip you are planning with friends or family, use [Wanderlog](https://wanderlog.com/) free, because shared planning, maps, and expense splitting are free forever there. For the first draft of any trip, use [Gemini](https://gemini.google.com/app) free, because it turns a plain-language brief into a day-wise itinerary in minutes and it understands Indian places through Google Maps data. And before you pay for any flight or hotel, set a free price alert on [Skyscanner](https://www.skyscanner.com/flights/advice/always-get-best-airfare-available-skyscanner-price-alerts) and check the final price on the airline or hotel website. Everything else in this article is for a specific kind of trip, not for every traveller.

This article is written for India on purpose: Indian trains and domestic flights, UPI and Indian payment realities, Hindi and English, family trips and solo trips, a normal phone and laptop, and no assumption that you have a paid plan or a foreign credit card. It also carries a warning that matters more for travel than for most AI uses: an AI itinerary is a draft, not a booking. Train timings, flight prices, hotel availability, and entry tickets change constantly. Always re-check timings, prices, and cancellation terms on the official website or app before you pay, and never promise a family member a hotel, a train seat, or a deadline on the basis of an AI answer alone.

## ixigo PLAN: the strongest free planner for trips inside India

[PLAN by ixigo](https://ai.ixigo.com/) is the most India-specific tool in this list, and that is exactly why it leads it. It is a free generative AI trip planner inside the ixigo app and website, and ixigo describes it as using retrieval, real-time APIs, and its own recommendation engine rather than a bare chatbot answer. In practice, you describe your trip and it builds an itinerary with places, restaurants, and points of interest, and it factors in real-time weather and air quality for your destination, which genuinely matters in India: a rainy day in Mumbai, a high-AQI week in Delhi, or a hot afternoon in Jaipur should change what you do that day, and PLAN claims to adjust for exactly that.

The practical advantage is booking. ixigo already books Indian trains, flights, and hotels, so a plan made in PLAN sits next to the booking flow instead of in a separate app. You can plan a Delhi to Jaipur weekend, check trains, and book from the same company, paying by UPI or card as you normally would on ixigo. The free plan is the whole planner: ixigo's own AI page presents PLAN as its AI trip planner without a separate paid tier for planning, and a third-party review of the tool describes it as a free generative AI trip planner available on the web with a mobile app.

The limits are honest ones. PLAN's recommendations are AI suggestions, not confirmed bookings, so train seat availability, hotel prices, and restaurant hours still need a check before you pay. Its restaurant recommendations started with Indian cities, and coverage outside India is thinner. And like every AI planner, it can suggest a day that looks good on a map but is too rushed on foot in Indian heat or traffic. Use it to build the plan, then walk through each day yourself and cut one stop per day. Your trip will be better for it.

## Wanderlog: the best free tool for planning a trip together

[Wanderlog](https://wanderlog.com/) is the tool to use when more than one person is deciding. Its help centre says, plainly, that the website and app for planning with friends will remain free forever, for as many trips as you want to plan. The free plan covers building an itinerary, putting stops on a map, planning with friends, and tracking and splitting shared expenses. That combination is why it wins for family trips and friend groups: one person can build the days, everyone can see the map, and the shared costs stop living in a chat thread.

The paid tier is Wanderlog Pro, and this is one place where sources disagree, so the article states both. Wanderlog's own website advertises Pro from $39.99 a year, while US App Store offers checked in late October 2026 ranged from $31.99 to $59.99 a year, with monthly offers as well. Pro adds offline trip downloads, automatic Gmail booking imports, route optimisation for a day's stops, and Google Maps export. None of those are needed to plan a trip, which is the point: the free plan is not a trial with a trip limit. It is the planner, and Pro is for power users.

Wanderlog's weakness for India is that it is a general world planner, not an India specialist. It will not book your IRCTC train or know Indian train classes the way ixigo does, and its place data is only as good as its maps. Use it as the shared whiteboard for the trip, and do the actual Indian bookings on ixigo, IRCTC, or the airline website. For a group trip, that division of labour works well: Wanderlog holds the plan, Indian apps hold the tickets.

## Mindtrip: chat-first planning with booking built in

[Mindtrip](https://mindtrip.ai/) is a chat-based personal travel assistant. You describe how you like to travel, and it remembers your style, helps you choose flights, stays, and restaurants by weighing trade-offs, and watches for price drops and flight changes. In a test reported in October 2026, it turned a seven-day Tokyo brief into seven dated, themed days in about ninety seconds from a single message. That speed is the appeal: one message in, one structured plan out.

The free position is clear but with a caveat worth stating. Mindtrip is free for planning, and as of October 2026 it published no consumer subscription; it earns on bookings made through it. The caveat is that its pricing page led to a sign-in screen when checked from outside an account in October 2026, so no paid tier could be seen without signing in. You also need an account, by email, Google, or Apple, before you can plan at all; there is no sample plan to look at first. For India, Mindtrip works in a browser without a VPN, but its booking flow is built for international travel, so treat it as a planner and a price watcher, and book Indian trains and domestic stays on Indian apps where UPI and cancellation terms are clearer.

## Layla: AI planning with a human expert when you want one

[Layla](https://layla.ai/faq) takes a different approach from the pure chat planners: AI builds the first plan, and a human travel expert can refine it, arrange bookings, and support you during the trip. You start by chatting about your ideas, preferences, and budget, and you can either book yourself or let a travel expert handle the arrangements once you approve the plan. Layla also offers 24/7 support during travel, including over WhatsApp, which is a genuinely useful safety net on a trip that goes wrong.

The money question has a clear answer and a common misunderstanding, so both are stated here. Layla's own FAQ says using Layla to plan your trip is free, with no subscription or planning fee, and that if you book through Layla you pay for the travel arrangements in your booking. The figure that sometimes gets misread as a fee is its recommended minimum budget of €100 per person, per night for tailor-made trips. That is a budget guide for the kind of trips its experts specialise in, not a charge for planning. For a budget Indian family trip, that guide alone tells you Layla's expert service is aimed at higher-budget, tailor-made travel rather than a low-cost domestic weekend, and the free AI planning is the part most Indian readers will actually use.

## GuideGeek: the trip planner that lives in your chat app

[GuideGeek](https://guidegeek.com/) is an AI travel assistant you message on WhatsApp or Instagram, which makes it the lowest-friction tool in this list for many Indian travellers. There is no app to install and no itinerary builder to learn: you text it like a friend, and it answers with destination ideas, real-time information on flights, stays, restaurants, and experiences, price comparisons, and local tips. It also says it can chat in many languages, which matters on a trip where your group speaks Hindi, English, or both.

On price, the honest statement is this: GuideGeek's official site lists no subscription or planning fee for chatting with it, and it presents itself as a free-to-message assistant, while bookings themselves are paid to the airline, hotel, or partner, not to GuideGeek. That is the same shape as the other free planners here: the plan is free, the travel costs money. The weakness is the same as the strength: a chat thread is a poor place to hold a five-day itinerary, so use GuideGeek for quick questions on the go, such as what is near you right now or whether a flight price looks high, and keep the full plan in Wanderlog or ixigo PLAN.

## Gemini: the fastest first draft for any trip

[Gemini](https://gemini.google.com/app) is not a travel app, and that is why it belongs in this list anyway. It is Google's free AI assistant, and for trip planning it has one advantage no specialist planner can match: it can draw on Google Maps data, recent reviews, peak hours, traffic, and restaurant information while it builds your plan. One detailed report of planning a Goa trip with Gemini describes it mapping routes visually, suggesting the best times to visit attractions, and shaping each day by location and weather, all from a single budget-and-preferences brief. That is the experience most readers will recognise: a usable first draft in minutes.

The right way to use Gemini for travel is as a drafter, not a booker. Give it everything that matters in one brief: starting city, dates, number of days, who is travelling, total budget and what it must cover, and how you want to travel, by train, flight, bus, or car. Then treat its answer as a draft to verify, because its timings, prices, and opening hours can be outdated, and it does not book anything for you. The free plan is the Gemini app with a Google account, which most Indian readers already have. For [translation on a trip](/blog/ai-translation-tools-hindi-english-india/), and for learning the English you will need at hotels and airports, Gemini sits alongside the dedicated tools covered in those articles, not instead of them.

## Skyscanner: free flight price alerts that save real money

[Skyscanner](https://www.skyscanner.com/flights/advice/always-get-best-airfare-available-skyscanner-price-alerts) does one job in this list, and it does it for free: it watches flight prices for you. You search a route and date, save the flight or switch on tracking, and Skyscanner emails you when the price changes. Its own help page explains that you need a free account to set an alert, that you can create one from the "Track prices" option on a search, and that you can stop all alerts from the bottom of any alert email. There is no fee for searching or for alerts.

The important limit is that Skyscanner is a comparison site, not a seller. When the alert says the price has dropped, you still book on the airline website or a booking partner, and the final price, baggage allowance, and cancellation terms are whatever that seller shows at checkout. For Indian domestic routes, compare Skyscanner's result with the airline's own website and with ixigo, because direct airline prices sometimes beat aggregator prices once baggage and convenience fees are included. Used that way, a free alert is the cheapest way to avoid buying a flight the week it was most expensive.

## TripIt: one itinerary for every booking you already made

[TripIt](https://help.tripit.com/en/support/solutions/articles/103000063396-tripit-or-tripit-pro-) solves the problem that appears after the planning is done: your flight is in one email, your hotel in another, and your train ticket in a third. TripIt's free plan organises your travel plans into one itinerary, creates it from confirmations forwarded from your inbox, syncs with your calendar, shares plans with others, and shows transport options, weather, and a map view. It also stores travel documents, up to three per trip on the free plan, and shows neighbourhood safety scores and nearby places.

The paid tier, TripIt Pro, adds real-time flight alerts, fare-refund monitoring, better-seat help, alternate-flight finding, check-in reminders, terminal and gate reminders, baggage-claim information, and document uploads up to twenty-five per trip. This article deliberately does not state a Pro price, because it was not verified from TripIt's official pages in this check, and prices change by store and region. The free plan is enough for most Indian travellers who mainly need one clean itinerary to show the family or the hotel desk. One practical note: forwarding booking emails to a third-party service means sharing travel details with it, so read its privacy terms before you forward anything sensitive, and never forward emails containing full payment card details.

## Rome2Rio: how to actually get from A to B

[Rome2Rio](https://www.rome2rio.com/) answers the question every other planner skips: not where to stay, but how to get there. It searches routes by plane, train, bus, ferry, and car across the world, and it lists thousands of operators, including nearly two hundred thousand train lines and close to a million bus routes in its own counts. For India, that means a single search can show you the combination of a flight to the nearest airport, a train onward, and a bus for the last stretch, instead of you guessing which town to connect through.

Rome2Rio is free to search. It makes its money when you click through to book with a partner, so the route is free and the ticket is paid, like Skyscanner. Its Indian coverage is best for the well-connected routes and thinner for remote local buses, so for the last mile in a small town, check with the hotel or a local bus stand as well. Used before you book, it prevents the classic India trip mistake: a hotel booked in a town with no sensible way to reach it on your travel day.

## Using travel AI in India: language, trains, payment, and no VPN

All nine tools in this article work in India in a normal browser or app without a VPN, which is the first test any tool in this series has to pass. The differences that matter are practical, not technical. Language comes first: most of these planners work in English, and Gemini and GuideGeek handle other languages, including Hindi prompts, better than the rest, but hotel names, station names, and addresses should always be double-checked in the local script before you show them to a driver. Trains come second: no foreign planner books Indian Railways tickets directly, so plan on any tool you like, but book trains on IRCTC or ixigo, and keep the PNR and the coach position in TripIt or Wanderlog, not only in email.

Payment comes third. Indian apps take UPI, which is how most readers will actually pay for trains, domestic flights, and hotels. Foreign planners that book international stays or flights will charge in dollars or euros, so check the conversion and any foreign-transaction fee your bank adds before you confirm. And documents come fourth: never paste your passport number, full Aadhaar details, or card numbers into a chatbot planner. A planner needs dates, cities, and a budget, not your identity documents. If a tool asks for more than that to build an itinerary, close it and use another one.

## What to avoid

Avoid trusting any AI itinerary without a verification pass. Check every train number and departure time on IRCTC or the railway app, every flight price on the airline website, every hotel's cancellation policy on its own page, and every attraction's opening hours on its official listing. AI planners are confident about details they have not checked, and a wrong train time is not a small error when your family is standing on a platform.

Avoid paying for planning that is free elsewhere. Several tools in this list plan for free and only charge for bookings, so a website or agent that charges a "planning fee" for the same itinerary is selling you something you can get free from ixigo PLAN, Wanderlog, or Gemini. Avoid booking through links you have not verified, especially links sent in a chat thread or a forwarded message, because a fake booking page looks exactly like a real one. And avoid planning a trip around a single AI answer for anything with a hard constraint: visa rules, passport validity, health requirements, and restricted areas must be checked on official government pages, never on a chatbot's word alone. For [chatbot answers in general](/blog/ai-chatbots-beyond-chatgpt-gemini-india/), the same rule applies: useful for drafting, never the final authority.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I plan a full India trip using only free AI tools?** Yes, for most trips. Use ixigo PLAN for the India-specific plan, Wanderlog for the shared plan, Gemini for the first draft, and Skyscanner alerts for flights. You will still book trains on IRCTC or ixigo and hotels on their own sites, but the planning itself does not need a paid plan.

**Which free tool is best for a family trip in India?** Wanderlog for the shared plan, because everyone sees the same days, map, and expenses for free, with ixigo PLAN alongside it for Indian trains and bookings. Keep one person in charge of the final version, or a shared plan becomes five conflicting plans.

**Do these tools book Indian trains?** ixigo books trains, because it is an Indian booking app. The foreign planners in this list plan routes and suggest trains, but they do not issue IRCTC tickets. Plan anywhere, book trains on IRCTC or ixigo, and keep the PNR in your itinerary app.

**Is Mindtrip really free?** Planning on Mindtrip is free, and as of October 2026 it published no consumer subscription, earning on bookings instead. It does need an account before you can plan, and its pricing page required a sign-in to view, so treat any future paid tier as something to check inside your account rather than assume.

**Why do Wanderlog Pro prices differ across sources?** Because Wanderlog's website advertises Pro from $39.99 a year while app-store offers vary by store, region, and billing period, with US offers checked in late October 2026 ranging from $31.99 to $59.99 a year. Always check the price shown in your own store at checkout, not a price quoted in an article.

**Can I trust AI hotel and flight prices?** No, not without a check. Prices change by the hour, and AI answers can repeat an old price with full confidence. Use AI to shortlist, then confirm the live price, baggage rules, and cancellation terms on the airline or hotel website before you pay.

## The final verdict, and a checklist before you pay

The verdict is the one in the frontmatter, and it is deliberately boring: start with ixigo PLAN for India trips and Wanderlog for shared plans, use Gemini for the first draft, and set Skyscanner alerts before you pay. Boring is good here, because the tools that win are the free ones that stay free, work in India without a VPN, and keep the plan where your family can see it.

Before you pay for anything on a trip planned by AI, run this checklist. First, every train time verified on IRCTC or the railway app. Second, every flight price verified on the airline website, with baggage and cancellation terms read. Third, every hotel booking confirmed with a cancellation policy you can live with. Fourth, every day of the itinerary walked through on a map, with one stop cut per day. Fifth, no passport, Aadhaar, or card details ever pasted into a chatbot. And sixth, prices and limits re-checked on the official pricing or help page before paying or promising a deadline, because every free limit and price in this article can change, and the official page is the only version that counts. If you do that, AI planning saves you hours. If you skip it, AI planning only makes your mistakes faster.
