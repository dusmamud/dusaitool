An Indian accountant's day is mostly repetition: entering bills, chasing clients for documents, matching purchases with GSTR-2B, making GST invoices, and answering the same "when is the due date?" question ten times. AI and modern accounting software cannot sign a return for you, and they cannot replace professional judgement. What they can do is remove a large part of the typing, matching and reminding, so the accountant spends time on review instead of data entry.

The short answer is: keep one proper accounting system as the single source of truth — Zoho Books if you want cloud software with a free plan, or TallyPrime if your clients already run on Tally. Use Zoho Invoice for free GST invoicing, ClearTax or Refrens for GST filing preparation and reconciliation, Vyapar or Khatabook for small clients who only need billing and ledgers, and a general AI chat tool only for drafts, summaries and explanations that a human then checks. Nothing in this article is a substitute for a chartered accountant's advice, and no AI answer should ever be filed without checking it against the official GST portal, the Income Tax portal, or the relevant Act and notification.

This article covers only tools that work in India without a VPN. Prices and free limits are stated as found at the time of writing, but they change often — always re-check the official pricing or help page before paying, promising a client a deadline, or quoting a fee that depends on a tool's cost.

## Zoho Books: the best cloud starting point for most practices

Zoho Books is a full double-entry accounting system built for Indian businesses, with GST invoices, e-invoicing support, bank reconciliation, expense tracking, purchase and sales orders, and a client portal where customers can view their invoices and pay. Its free plan is aimed at solopreneurs and micro businesses and covers invoices, quotes, bills, expenses, journals, receipt autoscans, online payments, automated payment reminders and bank reconciliation. The first paid tier, Standard, costs ₹749 per organisation per month and allows three users, which makes it realistic for a small practice to give each staff member a login.

For an accountant, the strongest reason to pick Zoho Books is that it is the same system many small Indian clients already use or can be moved to cheaply. When the client and the accountant share one system, there is no monthly ritual of importing messy spreadsheets and fixing broken formulas. The accountant can be added as a user, review the books directly, and close the month without asking the client to email yet another file. The receipt autoscan feature is genuinely useful: photograph a bill, and the system reads the vendor, amount and date, leaving the accountant to verify rather than type. Verification still matters, because scans misread handwritten bills and faded thermal receipts regularly.

The weakness is the learning curve for clients who have used Tally for twenty years. If a client refuses to leave Tally, forcing Zoho Books on them will cost more in training and resistance than it saves. Zoho Books also works best with a stable internet connection; a client in an area with weak connectivity may prefer a desktop tool. Prices and plan features change, so confirm the current free-plan limits on the official pricing page before telling a client that their books will cost nothing.

## TallyPrime: the tool your clients probably already run

TallyPrime is the standard accounting software of Indian small and medium businesses, and that familiarity is its biggest advantage for an accountant. Clients know it, their staff know it, and most local accountants can open a client's Tally data without a tutorial. It handles accounting, inventory, GST, payroll basics and e-invoicing, and it works offline on a Windows computer, which matters for shops and offices with unreliable internet.

Pricing needs care. TallyPrime Silver, for a single computer, is available on rental from ₹750 per month plus 18% GST, while a lifetime licence costs around ₹22,500 plus GST according to reseller listings. Gold, for multiple users on a network, rents from around ₹2,250 per month plus GST, with a lifetime price near ₹67,500 plus GST. These figures come from reseller pages, not only from Tally's own site, and Tally's own page shows the rental figures — so treat the lifetime prices as indicative and confirm them with an authorised partner before quoting a client. The rental model is usually the sensible choice for a small practice: it includes updates and support, and it avoids a large one-time payment for a client who may not stay.

TallyPrime's weakness, compared with cloud tools, is collaboration. Sharing Tally data still often means sending files or giving remote access, and two people cannot comfortably work on the same company data at the same time without a network setup. Its interface also looks old-fashioned next to Zoho Books, and younger staff may need training. Still, for an accountant whose client base is already on Tally, learning TallyPrime deeply — GST reports, cost centres, e-invoice generation, data synchronisation — pays more than learning yet another new app. Do not promise a client that Tally alone files their returns: Tally prepares the data, but filing happens through the GST portal or a filing tool, and reconciliation against GSTR-2B remains a human review job.

## Zoho Invoice: free GST invoicing, and genuinely free

Zoho Invoice is free forever for Indian businesses, according to Zoho's own pricing page, and that claim is unusually straightforward. It creates GST-compliant invoices in a few clicks, emails them or sends them over WhatsApp, accepts payment by UPI, cards and net banking, sets up automated payment reminders, handles recurring invoices, and gives each business a customer portal. For a freelancer, a small firm, or an accountant's smallest clients, this removes the most common excuse for sending informal bills on plain paper or in a chat message.

For an accountant, Zoho Invoice is best used as the invoicing front end for clients who do not need full accounting yet. A client can raise proper GST invoices all month, and at month end the accountant can take clean invoice data instead of reconstructing sales from a notebook or a pile of screenshots. It also integrates with Zoho Books, Zoho CRM and Zoho Analytics, so a growing client can graduate from invoicing-only to full accounting without re-entering master data. The limitation is scope: Zoho Invoice is not a full accounting system. It does not replace ledgers, bank reconciliation or final accounts, and an accountant who tries to run a whole practice on invoicing software alone will end up keeping shadow records in spreadsheets, which defeats the purpose.

## Vyapar: billing and stock for the smallest clients

Vyapar is a billing, inventory and GST accounting app made for Indian small businesses, and its Android app is free with all basic functionalities included, according to its pricing page. Paid extras cover removing Vyapar branding from invoices and syncing data across devices, with a separate licence per device for sync. The Windows desktop version offers a seven-day free trial. One honest limitation is platform coverage: Vyapar itself notes that it is not yet compatible with iOS devices, so an iPhone-only client cannot use it.

For accountants, Vyapar is a practical answer to a common problem: the tiny client — a kirana shop, a repair workshop, a single-truck transporter — who will never open Zoho Books or Tally but who still needs GST bills, stock counts and a record of who paid. If that client bills on Vyapar, the accountant receives structured data instead of a photo of a handwritten register. The accountant's job then becomes review and filing, not transcription. The risk is data fragmentation: a practice with thirty Vyapar clients, ten Tally clients and five Zoho clients spends real time just collecting data. Set a rule early — one fixed monthly export date, one format, one folder per client — or the time saved on entry will be lost on chasing files. ## ClearTax: GST filing preparation and reconciliation at scale

ClearTax offers cloud-based GST software that covers the full return cycle — GSTR-1 through 9C, including GSTR-2B matching, the Invoice Management System, input tax credit reconciliation, and handling of changes such as the non-editable inter-state data in GSTR-3B that the GSTN introduced in July 2025. It is designed for accountants and firms handling many GSTINs, with PAN-level views that consolidate returns across registrations on one screen. That multi-client view is the real selling point: a practice filing for fifty clients needs bulk import, bulk reconciliation and exception reports, not fifty separate logins.

The honest caveat is that ClearTax is a professional, paid product, and its pricing depends on the number of clients, returns and features. This article deliberately does not quote a ClearTax price, because plan structures for firms change and a wrong number here could mislead a reader into budgeting wrongly. Check the current plan on ClearTax's official site and ask for a demo with your own client data before buying. Also remember that no filing tool removes the accountant's responsibility: if GSTR-2B and purchase records disagree, the software flags the difference, but only the accountant can decide whether the credit is eligible, whether a supplier's invoice is genuine, and whether a notice reply needs a professional's signature. A tool that files wrong data faster is not an improvement.

## Refrens: all-in-one accounting with GST, starting free

Refrens is a free cloud accounting software for India that covers GST-compliant invoicing, e-invoicing with IRN and QR code generation, e-way bills, GSTR reports, bank reconciliation, financial reports, double-entry bookkeeping and inventory management. Its free plan lets a business start without paying and add premium features as it grows, and it verifies GSTINs against GSTN in real time so that inactive or cancelled GSTINs are caught before invoicing. It also matches GSTR-2B against purchases and flags mismatches, and it covers the return cycle from GSTR-1 and 3B through GSTR-9 and 9C, including QRMP, IFF, RCM and TDS handling.

For an accountant, Refrens is worth testing as a middle path: more complete than invoicing-only software, cheaper to start than a paid plan elsewhere, and Indian in its GST logic rather than adapted from another country's tax system. Its AI feature, which answers cash-flow questions from live books, is a convenience for business owners, not a replacement for an accountant's analysis — an owner asking "how is my cash flow?" still needs a human to explain why receivables are rising and whether that is a collection problem or a sales-mix problem. As with every free plan, read the limits: free tiers commonly restrict users, documents or advanced reports, and a practice should confirm those limits before moving a client whose transaction volume will outgrow them in three months.

## Khatabook: the simple ledger clients actually maintain

Khatabook is a free app for recording udhaar — who owes money, who paid, and how much is left — with payment reminders sent to customers. It will not do GST, final accounts or audit, and it should never be presented as accounting software. Its value is behavioural: small clients who would never maintain a ledger in a proper system will maintain Khatabook, because it is simple, in their language, and on the phone they already carry.

For an accountant, a client who keeps Khatabook honestly is far better than a client who keeps nothing. At month end, the accountant can reconcile the Khatabook balance against bank credits and the sales register, and the gaps become visible immediately. The danger is double counting: if a client records the same sale in Khatabook and in Vyapar or Zoho Invoice, revenue can be counted twice unless the accountant defines one system as the sales record and the other as a collection tracker. Make that rule explicit on day one, in writing, in the client's language. ## AI chat tools: excellent drafts, dangerous conclusions

ChatGPT, Gemini and similar general AI chat tools are useful for an accountant in a narrow, well-defined way: drafting a client email, turning rough notes into a memo, summarising a long circular or contract that the accountant then reads in full, explaining a concept in plain words for a client, drafting a working-paper outline, writing an Excel formula, or brainstorming audit procedures. They are fast at language, and they are genuinely good at turning a terse note into a polite, complete client message.

They are dangerous in exactly the places where an accountant's reputation lives. They can invent section numbers, case names and figures with complete confidence. They may not know the latest Finance Act amendment, notification or due-date change. Their arithmetic can be wrong, and the same question can produce different answers on different days. They do not know a client's books unless someone pastes the data in — and pasting client data into a consumer chatbot is a confidentiality risk that a practice should never take casually. Firm policy should be simple: use AI chat tools only for work a qualified person will review, strip out names, PAN numbers, bank details and figures before pasting anything, and check every rule, rate and date against the primary source. Never let an AI tool produce a final tax opinion, an audit conclusion, or a reply to a department notice without a professional rewriting and owning it. The signing accountant is responsible for the output, not the tool.

Free tiers of chat tools exist, but with usage limits that change frequently, and paid plans add higher limits and newer models. This article does not quote chat-tool prices, because they change several times a year and differ between India pricing and dollar pricing. Check the official pricing page of the tool you actually use. If a client asks why the accountant will not simply "ask AI" for a tax answer, the honest reply is that AI predicts plausible wording, while a tax answer needs the current law applied to that client's exact facts — and those are different jobs.

## Spreadsheet AI: the quiet productivity win

Most accountants already live in Excel or Google Sheets, and the AI features inside spreadsheets are often more valuable than a separate AI app. Formula suggestions, data cleanup, error explanations and natural-language questions such as "total GST paid by supplier" can save hours across a month of reconciliations. Microsoft 365 plans include Copilot features at higher tiers, and Google Sheets includes Gemini help in Workspace plans, while free Google Sheets offers basic formula assistance. The exact feature set depends on the plan, so verify what your subscription actually includes before paying for an upgrade.

The rule for spreadsheet AI is the same as for chat tools: test every formula on dummy data first, and never trust a total you cannot reproduce by another method. A formula that looks right and sums the wrong column is worse than a slow manual total, because it produces a confident wrong number that flows into a return. ## Dext: receipt scanning for practices drowning in paper

Dext is a bookkeeping tool that extracts data from receipts, bills and bank statements, with AI assistance that prepares entries while leaving the final word to the bookkeeper. Its published pricing starts at about $25 per month, billed annually at about $302, for a plan including a set number of documents and bank-statement extractions. Because it is priced in US dollars, an Indian practice should convert the cost at the current exchange rate and check whether GST or card charges apply, before comparing it with Indian tools that price in rupees.

Dext makes sense only for a practice with a genuine paper problem — clients who send bags of bills, or a firm processing hundreds of expense documents a month. For a practice whose clients already raise digital invoices, paying in dollars for scanning adds cost without benefit. Also check data handling: scanned financial documents are sensitive, and the practice should confirm where data is stored and who can access it before uploading a client's bills. If the volume does not justify Dext, Zoho Books' built-in receipt autoscan on its free plan covers the same need for most small clients at no extra cost.

## Using these tools in India: GST, clients and practical realities

India-specific fit decides more than features. First, GST logic must be native: CGST, SGST and IGST splitting, HSN and SAC codes, e-invoicing through the Invoice Registration Portal, e-way bills, GSTR-2B matching and the Invoice Management System are not optional extras for an Indian practice — they are the daily work. Tools built for the US or UK market often lack them entirely, which is why this article recommends Indian-first tools for the core books and reserves global tools for drafting and scanning.

Second, connectivity and language matter. A tool that needs constant high-speed internet will frustrate a client in a small town, and a tool with no Hindi or regional-language support will frustrate their staff. TallyPrime's offline working and Vyapar's and Khatabook's phone-first design exist for this reason. Third, payment collection must fit India: UPI, net banking and cards, with automated reminders in a language the client understands. Zoho Invoice's UPI support and WhatsApp delivery are practical advantages here, not marketing points.

Fourth, set the data-collection discipline before the tool choice. Agree with every client on one monthly cut-off date, one export format, and one channel for sending documents. Decide who owns each step: the client raises invoices, the accountant reconciles and files, and nobody edits a closed month without a documented correction. The best software cannot fix a practice where five clients send data in five formats on five different dates. Finally, keep backups outside any single tool. Export ledgers, returns and filed acknowledgements regularly, and store them where the practice — not only the software vendor — controls them. A vendor account problem should never mean losing a client's books.

## What to avoid

Avoid QuickBooks for Indian GST work. Intuit discontinued QuickBooks products in India — sources disagree on the exact cut-off, with one giving 31 January 2023 and another 30 April 2023, so this article does not assert a single date — and the global QuickBooks Online product does not support Indian GST, e-invoicing, TDS or Indian bank feeds. Recommending it to an Indian client today creates a migration problem later.

Avoid pasting client data into free AI chat tools. PAN numbers, Aadhaar details, bank statements, salary data and full ledgers do not belong in a consumer chatbot, and a free tier's data terms are rarely read before use. If the firm has no written AI-use policy, write one before the next busy season: what may be pasted, what must be stripped, and who reviews AI output.

Avoid trusting AI tax answers, AI-generated section references, or AI-computed totals without checking the primary source and recomputing the figure. Avoid buying software on a single feature demo: run it with one real client's data for a full month, including a filing cycle, before moving the whole practice. Avoid keeping the same transaction in two systems without a rule for which one is authoritative — double counting is the most common error when a practice adopts new tools quickly. And avoid promising a client a filing date that depends on a tool's free tier: free limits change, accounts get downgraded, and the accountant, not the vendor, faces the client when a deadline slips.

## Frequently asked questions

**Which is the best AI tool for accountants in India?**
There is no single best tool, because bookkeeping, invoicing and filing are different jobs. For most small practices, Zoho Books or TallyPrime for the books, Zoho Invoice for free GST invoicing, and ClearTax or Refrens for filing preparation is the strongest combination, with AI chat tools used only for drafts that a human reviews.

**Is Zoho Invoice really free for Indian businesses?**
Yes, according to Zoho's own pricing page, Zoho Invoice is free forever for Indian businesses, including GST-compliant invoices, UPI payments, automated reminders and recurring invoices. It is invoicing software, not a full accounting system, so it does not replace ledgers and reconciliation.

**Should a small practice choose TallyPrime or Zoho Books?**
Choose TallyPrime if most of your clients already run on Tally and value offline working on Windows. Choose Zoho Books if you want cloud collaboration, a free starting plan, and shared access between client and accountant. Many practices run both, one per client, and that is fine as long as each client's system of record is defined clearly.

**Can ChatGPT or Gemini file my GST returns?**
No. General AI chat tools cannot file returns, cannot access the GST portal on your behalf, and cannot be trusted for final tax conclusions, rates or due dates. They can draft emails, summarise documents and explain concepts, but every rule, rate and figure must be checked against the official source and owned by a qualified professional.

**Is it safe to upload client bills to AI scanning tools?**
It can be, but only with a deliberate choice: check where the data is stored, who can access it, and what the tool's terms say about using uploaded documents. Strip or avoid sensitive identifiers where possible, use business accounts rather than personal free accounts for client work, and never upload PAN, Aadhaar or full bank statements to a consumer chatbot.

**What does QuickBooks' exit from India mean for old users?**
It means QuickBooks India products were discontinued in early 2023 — sources disagree on whether the cut-off was 31 January or 30 April 2023 — and the global product does not support Indian GST compliance. Old users should have migrated their data to an Indian system such as Zoho Books, TallyPrime or Refrens, and new Indian clients should not be started on QuickBooks for GST work.

**How much should a small accounting practice budget for software?**
It can start at zero for invoicing with Zoho Invoice and billing with Vyapar's free Android app, plus a free Zoho Books plan for micro clients. A realistic paid starting point is Zoho Books Standard at ₹749 per organisation per month, or TallyPrime Silver rental from ₹750 per month plus GST. Filing tools such as ClearTax are priced per firm and client volume, so get a quote before budgeting. Prices change, so re-check official pricing before committing.

**Do AI tools replace a chartered accountant?**
No. AI tools remove typing, matching and reminding, but judgement — whether credit is eligible, whether a transaction is genuine, how a notice should be answered, what a client should do next — remains a professional's job. This article is general information only, not professional advice, and official sources and a qualified professional must be checked for any specific tax or legal decision.

## Final verdict

Use Zoho Books or TallyPrime for the books, Zoho Invoice for GST invoices, ClearTax for filing — AI chat tools for drafts only, never final answers.

That combination covers the real work of an Indian practice: clean books, proper GST invoices, prepared filings, and clients who can send structured data instead of photographs of registers. Add Vyapar or Khatabook for the smallest clients, Refrens as a free all-in-one alternative, and Dext only when paper volume justifies its dollar pricing. Write the firm's AI policy before the busy season, define one system of record per client, and check every price, limit and tax rule on the official page before you promise it to a client. The accountant who reviews carefully will always beat the accountant who merely automates quickly.
