The best AI tools for law students in India are Indian Kanoon for free case research, NotebookLM for revision from your own notes, and SCC Online through your college library for authoritative citations. ChatGPT explains hard concepts and Perplexity checks facts with sources. No paid subscription is needed — but an AI summary never replaces reading the judgment itself.

## 1. Indian Kanoon — free case law

Indian Kanoon is a free database with over three crore documents: Supreme Court and High Court judgments and statutes. New judgments appear within hours; filters cover court, date, judge, and citation. The free plan includes 25 court-copy downloads a month. It has no editorial headnotes, so you must read the judgment yourself, and its URLs are not accepted as citations in court filings.
## 2. SCC Online — the authoritative database

SCC Online, from Eastern Book Company, publisher of Supreme Court Cases, is India's most authoritative legal research database. It covers 380-plus databases — Supreme Court judgments since 1950, all High Courts, tribunals, and statutes — with headnotes and TruePrint PDFs that courts accept. That authority matters for moot memorials and papers, where a wrong citation costs marks. It is paid, so check the official site before paying. Ask your college library first: most Indian law schools subscribe and give students remote access.

## 3. NotebookLM — your notes, turned into a tutor

NotebookLM, now branded Gemini Notebook by Google, is the best free tool for revision. Upload class notes, bare Acts, judgments, and moot problem PDFs, and it answers questions using only those sources, citing the exact passage. The free tier gives 100 notebooks with up to 50 sources each, each source up to 500,000 words. It also makes audio overviews, flashcards, and quizzes. Keep one notebook per subject, holding the syllabus, leading cases, and your notes.

## 4. ChatGPT — explainer and drafting partner

ChatGPT explains difficult doctrines in plain language, compares legal tests, and outlines moot arguments. OpenAI's free tier gives unlimited everyday text chats, with separate caps on file uploads, images, and voice, so heavy PDF-upload days still hit a wall. In India, Go costs ₹399 a month and Plus around ₹1,999 a month. But it hallucinates: ChatGPT can invent case names, citations, and quotations that sound perfectly real, so verify everything in Indian Kanoon or SCC Online.

## 5. Perplexity — fact-checking with sources

Perplexity is a search engine that answers with citations, making it better than a plain chatbot for checking facts. The free plan gives practically unlimited basic searches, plus 3 Pro searches a day for deeper research and 1 Research query a month for a full report. Use it to verify an amendment, a judgment, or a statistic, then open the cited source itself. Paid Pro costs about $20 a month. Sources disagree on whether the free Pro limit is 3 or 5 a day.

## How to use these tools in India

Start with the free stack: Indian Kanoon for cases, NotebookLM for revision, and Perplexity for quick checks. Then check your library: SCC Online and Manupatra access is commonly included in law school fees. For moot court, find cases on Indian Kanoon, verify citations on SCC Online, build arguments in NotebookLM with the judgments uploaded, and use ChatGPT only to rehearse oral arguments.

## What to avoid

Do not copy AI-generated answers into assignments, moot memorials, or exam scripts. An invented citation is worse than a missing one. Do not paste confidential client or internship material into a free chatbot, because free tiers may use your chats for training. Do not pay for a database before checking your library, and never trust one AI summary of a judgment: read the ratio yourself. Keep your notes in your own files; free limits change without warning.

## Frequently asked questions

**Which AI tool is best for law students in India?**

Indian Kanoon for free case law, NotebookLM for study from your own notes, and SCC Online through your college library for authoritative citations.

**Can law students use ChatGPT for moot court preparation?**

Yes — for outlining arguments and rehearsing submissions. Never trust its citations without verifying them in Indian Kanoon or SCC Online.

**Is Indian Kanoon really free?**

Yes. Searching judgments and statutes is free, with 25 court-copy downloads a month. Its Prism AI features are mostly paid.

**Do I need SCC Online or Manupatra as a student?**

Not necessarily — most law colleges already subscribe, so check your library first. Otherwise Indian Kanoon covers most day-to-day research.

**Can AI tools help me pass law exams?**

They speed up revision and explain hard concepts, but they cannot replace reading bare Acts and leading judgments, which is what examiners test.

**Are these AI tools legal to use for legal research in India?**

Yes, for study and research. But AI output is not a legal authority, so verify anything you plan to cite or file.

## Final verdict

Start free with Indian Kanoon and NotebookLM; use SCC Online through your college library before paying for anything. Add ChatGPT and Perplexity for explanations and fact checks, verify every citation, and keep notes in your own files so no changing limit strands your revision.
