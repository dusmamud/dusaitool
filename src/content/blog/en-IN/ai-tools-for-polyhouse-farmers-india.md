A polyhouse gives you climate control, but it only pays when you act on live data. The best tools do three jobs: sense temperature and humidity, spot disease from a photo, and forecast irrigation and spray needs. Start free, and pay for hardware only after a trial proves value.

| Need | Best pick | Cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Climate, irrigation, disease forecasts | Fasal | Paid hardware plus subscription |
| Disease diagnosis from a photo | Plantix | Free |
| Fan and fogger automation | Sonoff + eWeLink | Hardware only |

## 1. Fasal — climate, irrigation and disease forecasts

Fasal places IoT sensors inside the polyhouse to track temperature, humidity and soil moisture, then sends phone advisories on irrigation, fertigation and spray timing. It forecasts disease risk before symptoms appear. It supports capsicum and tomato. NDTV reported it serves around 12,000 farmers, with installs reported at $57–$287 — re-check current pricing on Fasal's official site before buying.

## 2. Plantix — free disease diagnosis from a photo

Plantix is a free Android app: photograph a sick leaf and get a diagnosis and treatment fast. It covers 30 crops and 780+ pest, disease and nutrient problems, in 19 languages including Hindi, with over 10 million downloads, about 80% of them in India. It needs a signal, and blurry pictures lower accuracy, so treat it as a first opinion.

## 3. Sonoff + eWeLink — low-cost automation for fans and foggers

Sonoff's temperature and humidity sensors and smart switches, sold in India through sonoff.in, switch on exhaust fans, foggers, cooling pads and pumps automatically when set limits are crossed. The eWeLink app gives phone alerts and remote control, with Alexa and Google Home support. No monthly fee — you pay for hardware only — but wire them safely, ideally with an electrician.

## Using these tools in India

Polyhouse tomato, capsicum, cucumber and strawberry earn best with uniform quality and harvests timed to mandi demand. Kheyti's Greenhouse-in-a-box raised median farmer income by about 73% and cut weather losses by about 58%, per a 2025 Sambodhi study. Check state horticulture missions and MIDH subsidies before building. General information only, not professional advice — check official sources and a local expert first. ## What to avoid

Do not buy sensors before knowing your crop's ideal temperature and humidity. Do not spray on one app's word alone — confirm with a local agronomist or Krishi Vigyan Kendra. Never trust a tool promising a fixed yield or income; weather, seed quality and mandi prices decide those.

## FAQ

**Which AI tool is best for a small polyhouse in India?** Start with free Plantix, then add Fasal or Sonoff once you know whether heat, water or disease costs you most.

**Do these tools work without internet?** Plantix needs connectivity to analyse photos. Fasal logs data locally, but advisories need a connection. Sonoff with a local hub automates without the cloud.

**Can AI tools replace an agronomist?** No. They spot patterns and warn early, but a local agronomist or KVK expert knows your soil, variety and mandi — use both.

**Are polyhouse subsidies available in India?** Yes, through state horticulture missions and MIDH. Rules and amounts change, so check your state horticulture department's current scheme first.

## Final verdict

Start free with Plantix, automate climate with Sonoff, and add Fasal when disease forecasting is worth paying for.
