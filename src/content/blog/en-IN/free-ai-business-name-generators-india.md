A good business name is short, easy to say on a phone call, and still free as a domain. That last part is where most naming sessions fail: you fall in love with a name, then discover the .com is taken, the Instagram handle belongs to someone else, and a similar trademark already exists. The tools below solve the first half of that problem. They generate name ideas and check domain availability in the same step, so you only shortlist names you can actually use.

The direct answer is simple. If you want brandable, invented-sounding names, start with [Namelix](https://namelix.com/). If you want the strongest free availability check in one place, start with [Namify](https://namify.tech/), because it checks domains, a preliminary trademark screen, and social usernames together. For a quick, no-sign-up list of names with instant domain results, [Shopify's business name generator](https://www.shopify.com/tools/business-name-generator) and [NameSnack](https://www.namesnack.com/) are the fastest. Every tool in this article works in India in a normal browser without a VPN, and every one of them lets you generate names for free. What costs money is registering a domain, buying a premium name, or downloading a full logo kit — and those prices change, so always re-check the official pricing page before you pay or promise a launch date.

One honest disagreement between sources, stated plainly: several tools call themselves "free" while meaning different things. Namelix and NameSnack are free for the generation itself, with no stated search limit. Looka and Wix are free to generate ideas, but the logo files and brand kits that follow are paid products. ## Namelix: the strongest free tool for short, brandable names

[Namelix](https://namelix.com/) is the best starting point if you want a name that sounds like a brand rather than a description. Instead of gluing two dictionary words together, it generates short, invented-style names that are relevant to the keywords you enter. You can filter by whether you want a brandable word, a compound name, or an alternate spelling, and you can ask it to prefer shorter names or a specific domain extension. When you save a name you like, the algorithm learns your taste and improves its later suggestions, which makes the second round of ideas noticeably better than the first.

The free part is genuinely generous. Namelix is free to use for generating business names, with no account required and no stated search limit, and it checks domain availability as you browse. Each name also gets a quick logo preview, so you can see whether the name looks right in a wordmark before you commit to it. The paid part is separate and clearly marked: full logo and brand identity packages are sold through Brandmark, starting at $35 for Basic, $95 for Designer, and $195 for Enterprise as one-time purchases. You do not need any of those to use the name generator itself.

The main limitation matters in India. Namelix generates English-language names. If your shop, coaching class, or food business needs a Hindi or Hinglish name, Namelix will not produce it directly. The practical workaround is to generate English-style options, then test a Hindi transliteration yourself — say the name aloud, write it in Devanagari, and check whether customers can spell it back to you after hearing it once.

## Shopify business name generator: the fastest no-sign-up list

[Shopify's business name generator](https://www.shopify.com/tools/business-name-generator) asks for one thing: a word or short description of your business. It then returns AI-generated name ideas and checks domain availability instantly, with no sign-up required. The whole flow is designed to take you from a keyword to a name and then to a domain in a few clicks, which is exactly what a new seller wants.

This tool is at its best for online stores, Instagram shops, and small sellers who need a store name today rather than a perfect brand after a month of workshops. The trade-off is depth. Shopify's generator is quick and practical, but it does not check trademarks and does not screen social handles the way Namify does. Treat its output as a raw shortlist: pick three or four names you like, then run them through the trademark and handle checks described later in this article before you print packaging or register anything.

## Namify: the best free availability check in one flow

[Namify](https://namify.tech/) is the most complete free tool in this list, because it answers three questions at once: is the domain free, is there an obvious trademark conflict, and are the social usernames free? You describe what you are building, and its AI generates brand names with matching domain names across more than 300 extensions, plus a free tagline for every name. You can refine results by category, tone, style, and target audience, and every suggestion shows its domain availability, so you can move from an idea to a registered domain without switching between websites.

Namify is free to use to generate names and to check domain, trademark, and username availability. You only pay when you register a domain, and on select domain extensions a free, designer-quality logo is included with registration. That "free logo on registration" detail is worth reading carefully: the logo is tied to registering a domain on an eligible extension, not a free download for every name you generate.

The important limitation is geography. Namify's trademark screen checks United States USPTO records, and Namify itself says this is a preliminary screen, not legal advice. A clean result in the United States does not mean the name is free in India. For an Indian business, Namify's screen is a useful early filter, but the decisive check is India's own trademark public search, covered in the India section below.

## NameSnack: 100% free, and honest about what it does not check

[NameSnack](https://www.namesnack.com/) combines machine learning with keyword combining and instant domain search to produce thousands of unique, memorable names with available domains. You enter words related to your business, pick an industry so its AI can suggest directions you had not thought of, and you get names with their domain availability shown immediately. It also offers a logo generator for the names you shortlist.

NameSnack states plainly that it is 100% free, funded by advertising on the site and by a small commission if you register a domain through one of its partners. That funding model is actually a strength for a free user: there is no trial clock and no paywall between you and the name list. The honest gap is the mirror image of Namify's strength. NameSnack says it does a domain search but does not check trademark databases, and it tells users to do that check themselves or hire a lawyer if they intend to trademark the name. Use NameSnack for fast, free idea generation, and do the trademark check separately.

## Wix business name generator: free ideas that lead straight to a website

[Wix's business name generator](https://www.wix.com/tools/business-name-generator) gives you a free list of memorable company name ideas with matching domain options, using AI to produce unlimited suggestions so you can keep exploring until a direction feels right. Every name idea comes with a matching domain whose availability you can check in real time, which means you can secure the website address the moment you find a name you like.

Wix's own advice on its generator page is worth repeating, because it matches this article's method: once you narrow down your options, do a Google search and a trademark search to see whether other businesses are using the name, then check whether the domain and social handles are available, and finally test the name with potential customers, including mockups of a logo, website, or packaging. The commercial catch is also stated on the page: a free domain for one year comes with an annual website plan. The name ideas are free; the website and the free domain year are tied to a paid plan, so check the plan price before you assume the whole package is free.

## Looka business name generator: free names, paid logo kit

[Looka's business name generator](https://www.looka.com/business-name-generator/) works in three steps: enter keywords or pick an abstract idea, review generated names filtered by length, and instantly check domain and social availability. Clicking through to "See Logos" takes you into Looka's logo maker, where you can see instant design ideas for the name you picked. The generator produces invented names, compound words, and traditional industry-related names, so it covers both the brandable and the descriptive ends of the spectrum.

The free-versus-paid line here is the sharpest in this article, so it deserves emphasis. Generating names and checking availability is free. Downloading logo files and the full Brand Kit is a paid annual subscription product with hi-res and vector files, social profiles, business cards, email signatures, and brand guidelines. That subscription may be good value once your name is final, but do not pay for it while you are still choosing between five names. Shortlist first, pay for the brand kit last.

## Atom business name generator: a free ranked shortlist, plus a paid marketplace

[Atom](https://www.atom.com/business-name-generator), formerly Squadhelp, takes a different approach from the keyword tools. You describe your business in a sentence plus three to five root words, and its AI returns about 100 ranked, brandable name ideas with available domains in roughly 30 seconds, checked across .com, .ai, .io, and more. You can save favourites to a shortlist and then register or buy from there. The ranking is genuinely useful when you are drowning in options, because it forces a top ten instead of an endless scroll.

Atom also runs two paid paths that are easy to confuse with the free generator, so keep them apart. First, its marketplace sells curated premium names with the .com domain included — those are paid listings, not free suggestions. Second, a naming contest with human naming experts starts at $299 including the creative award, with hundreds of entries and full rights to the winning name. Neither is required to use the free generator, but if a name you love turns out to be a premium listing, its price is the cost of that name plus its domain, and you should compare it against registering a different free name yourself before paying.

## Hostinger AI business name generator: free to start, built for domain purchase

[Hostinger's AI business name generator](https://www.hostinger.com/business-name-generator) follows the simplest possible flow: enter keywords based on your niche or industry, pick a brandable name from the AI-generated ideas, and register the domain. It is free to start, and it is most useful when you already know you want hosting and a domain from the same provider, because the tool is built to carry you from an idea to a registered domain in three steps.

Hostinger's generator is less of a brainstorming studio than Namelix or Atom. It does not offer the same depth of style filters or ranking, and it does not screen trademarks or social handles. Its strength is convenience for a buyer who has already decided to build a website: name idea, domain check, and registration in one place. As with every tool here, the domain itself is a paid registration, and its price depends on the extension and the registrar's current offer, so check the checkout price rather than trusting any price quoted in an article.

## Using name generators in India: language, domains, and trademark checks

Language comes first. Almost every free generator in this article works in English. That is fine for a tech startup, an export business, or an English-first Instagram brand, but a local shop, tuition centre, or food business often needs a name customers can say in Hindi, Hinglish, or a regional language. The reliable method is to generate English options, then manually test the Hindi version: write it in Devanagari, say it aloud to five people who match your customers, and ask them to spell it back. If two or more cannot, the name will cost you customers every time someone tries to search for you. Also check the name's meaning in the languages your customers speak — a clever English pun can be an unfortunate word in another language.

Domains are the second India-specific step. A .com is the default most generators check, but for an Indian business a .in domain is often cheaper, more available, and clearer to local customers. Namify's check across 300-plus extensions is useful here because it surfaces .in alongside .com, and the other tools will show .in availability when you filter or search for it. Before you pay, confirm the total checkout price, including renewal: many registrars discount the first year and charge full price on renewal, and that renewal price is the real annual cost of the name.

Trademarks are the step most free tools skip or only half-cover. Namify's screen covers United States records, not Indian ones. For India, use the government's own trademark public search at the IP India portal, which lets you search existing trademarks after an OTP login. A free search there is the single most valuable ten minutes in this whole process: it can save you from building a brand on a name someone else already owns in your class of goods or services. If you plan to register a private limited company, remember that the company-name approval step is separate from both the domain and the trademark — a free domain does not mean the company name will be approved, and an approved company name does not give you the trademark.

Payment and access are straightforward. All eight tools open in a normal browser in India without a VPN, and the free generation steps need no payment details. When you do pay for a domain, check whether the registrar accepts the payment method you actually use, and whether the price is shown in rupees or dollars, because currency conversion and taxes can change the final amount. Never pay for a premium name, a logo kit, or a naming contest while the name is still undecided — shortlist first, verify second, pay last.

## What to avoid

First, do not buy a name under pressure. Premium marketplaces and domain landing pages are designed to make you feel that a name will vanish tonight. In almost every case, a different good name exists, and the cost of rebranding later is far higher than the cost of one more day of searching now. If a premium name costs more than a month of your expected revenue, it is almost certainly the wrong purchase for a new business.

Second, do not skip the trademark and handle checks because the domain was free. A free .com for a name that clashes with an existing Indian trademark is not a bargain; it is a future legal notice and a forced rename after you have printed packaging, built an audience, and taught customers the old name. The same applies to social handles: a name whose Instagram and YouTube handles are taken will confuse customers even if the domain was available.

Third, do not pick a name you cannot say or spell. Long names, unusual spellings, and clever letter swaps (replacing "s" with "z" or dropping vowels) look distinctive in a logo and fail in real life, because customers mishear them, mistype them, and give up. If you have to explain the spelling every time, pick a simpler name.

Fourth, do not lock the name to one product or one city unless you are sure you will never expand. A name like "Delhi Cake Box" is perfect for one shop and a problem the day you ship to Gurgaon or add savouries. Namelix's brandable style and Atom's ranked shortlists are useful here precisely because they push you toward names that can grow.

Fifth, do not pay for a logo, brand kit, or website plan before the name is verified. Looka's Brand Kit, Wix's annual plan with a free domain year, and Atom's premium listings are all legitimate products, but each of them becomes wasted money if the name underneath changes. The order that protects your money is: generate, shortlist, check domain, check trademark, check handles, test with customers, and only then pay.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I really get a business name completely free?** Yes, for the idea itself. Namelix, NameSnack, Shopify's generator, Namify's generator, Wix's generator, Looka's generator, Atom's generator, and Hostinger's generator all let you generate names for free. What is not free is registering the domain, buying a premium name that someone already owns, or downloading a full logo and brand kit. Budget for the domain; treat everything else as optional.

**Which free generator is best for a small shop in India?** Start with Namelix if you want a short, brandable name, and run your shortlist through Namify for its domain, trademark screen, and username checks. Then confirm the name on IP India's free trademark public search, because Namify's screen covers United States records, not Indian ones. For a fast store name with instant domain results, Shopify's generator is the quickest alternative.

**Do these generators work for Hindi or Hinglish names?** Not directly. The generators in this article produce English-language names, and their keyword and style systems are built around English. For a Hindi-first business, generate English options, then create and test the Hindi or Hinglish version yourself: write it in Devanagari, say it to real customers, and check that they can spell it back and search for it.

**Is a free .com domain really free?** No. A free domain check means the domain is available to register, not that registration costs nothing. Domain registration is paid, prices vary by extension and registrar, and first-year discounts usually renew at a higher price. Always check the renewal price at checkout, not just the first-year offer.

**Does a free domain mean I own the trademark?** No. A domain registration, a social handle, and a trademark are three separate things. Owning the domain does not give you trademark rights, and a trademark search is a separate step. Check IP India's trademark public search before you invest in packaging, signage, or advertising under a new name.

**Should I buy a premium name from a marketplace?** Only if the name is clearly worth more than a free alternative and you have verified its trademark position in India. A premium name with its .com included can save months of searching for the right brand, but for most new businesses a free, verified name leaves more money for the product itself. Never buy under time pressure.

**What if my favourite name's .com is taken?** Try the .in version, which is often available and well understood by Indian customers, or try a short, brandable variation from Namelix or Atom rather than a long descriptive name with extra words. Check the social handles for the variation too, because a name that works as a domain but fails on Instagram will still confuse customers.

## The final verdict, and a checklist before you pay

Start with Namelix for names and Namify for domain plus trademark screening, then check the name on IP India's trademark search before paying. That sentence is the whole method in one line: generate widely, screen cheaply, verify in India, and only then spend money. Before you pay for anything, run this checklist. First, is the domain available, including the renewal price, not just the first-year offer? Second, is the name clear on IP India's trademark search for your class of goods or services? Third, are the main social handles free or at least not owned by a competing business? Fourth, can five real customers say the name and spell it back after hearing it once? Fifth, does the name still work if you add a product or move to a new city? If all five answers are yes, register the domain, and only then consider a logo kit or a website plan. Prices and free limits change, so re-check the official pricing and help pages of the tool you choose before paying or promising a launch deadline.
