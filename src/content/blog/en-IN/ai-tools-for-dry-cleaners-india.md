## The direct answer

A dry cleaner in India handles hundreds of garments a week, and every one of them belongs to someone who will notice if it comes back late, damaged, or mixed up with a neighbour's suit. The work is pickup in the morning, cleaning through the day, and delivery in the evening, and the whole chain breaks when a bag is not tagged, a delivery address is forgotten, or a customer is not told their clothes are ready. The second problem is the season: wedding and festival weeks triple the load, and the cleaner who cannot say which day a garment will be ready loses the customer to the shop that can.

The direct answer is simple: start with WhatsApp Business, Google Business Profile, Google Sheets, ChatGPT and Canva, all free to begin with. Together they cover pickup and delivery, garment tracking and repeat customers, and none of them needs a laptop or an expensive subscription to start.

## 1. WhatsApp Business: orders, confirmations and reminders in one place

WhatsApp Business is the daily counter of a small Indian business. A customer messages what they want, the owner confirms the slot or price, and the work is booked without a missed call. The Business app adds quick replies for the questions that come every day, labels that separate regular customers from new ones, and a catalogue that lists services and prices, so a customer chooses before arriving. The app itself is free; only mobile data or Wi-Fi costs apply, though some advanced messaging features are paid, so check the official page.

How to start: Set up a catalogue with your main services and prices, write quick replies for price and timing questions, and message last month's customers once with a useful reminder. Watch out for: Never spam customers with daily offers, and never share a customer's phone number or personal details with anyone else.

## 2. Google Business Profile: be found when nearby customers search

Google Business Profile is the free listing that decides whether a nearby customer finds your business or your competitor's. When someone searches for the service near them, Google shows the profile with photos, opening hours, phone number, directions and reviews. A complete profile with real photos, correct hours and replies to every review builds trust before the customer ever visits or calls. Creating and managing a profile is free; only optional ads cost money, so check the official page before spending anything.

How to start: Claim your profile, add ten real photos of your work, correct hours and phone number, and reply to your last five reviews. Watch out for: Fake reviews or wrong hours damage trust fast, so keep the details honest and reply politely even to unhappy customers.

## 3. Google Sheets: customers, orders and cash in one sheet

Google Sheets is the record book for a business that has outgrown memory. One sheet lists each customer, what they ordered, the date, and whether they have paid, so the owner can greet a regular by name and know exactly what is due. A second sheet tracks daily cash, UPI payments and expenses such as materials, transport and staff pay, so the owner knows the real daily profit instead of guessing. The sheet works on any phone, and a staff member can update it after each job in ten seconds. Google Sheets is free with a Google account; check the official page for paid Workspace features you probably do not need yet.

How to start: Make one sheet with this month's customers, orders and payment status, and use it to send reminders next week. Watch out for: Do not store a customer's full address, ID or payment details in a shared sheet; keep only what the business actually needs.

## 4. ChatGPT: messages, replies and planning in Hindi and English

ChatGPT handles the writing a small business owner does every day: a polite reminder message, a reply to a customer asking about price, a festival offer, or a calm answer to a complaint. Give it the facts, the service, the price and the date, and it will write a short message in Hindi or English that sounds friendly, not pushy. The free plan is enough for daily messages and replies; check current pricing on the official page before paying for a Plus plan.

How to start: Ask it to write one short Hindi message for your most common customer question, read it carefully, and send it yourself. Watch out for: AI can invent a price or a service you do not offer, so check every number and every promise before sending a message.

## 5. Canva: offer posters, price cards and photos that sell

Canva helps a small business look professional without hiring a designer. Its free plan has templates for offer posters, price cards and festival discounts, and its photo tools can brighten a real photo so the difference is obvious on a phone screen. The rule is simple: use only real photos of your own work, because a stock photo of something you did not do misleads the next customer. The free plan covers most templates and basic editing; Pro is paid, so check current pricing on the official page before upgrading.

How to start: Make one festival offer poster with your real prices and phone number, and share it on WhatsApp Status this week. Watch out for: Do not use another business's photos, and do not use a heavily edited photo that hides what the customer will actually get.

## Using AI in India: dry cleaners

Dry cleaning in India is a neighbourhood business, so most customers live within a few kilometres and pay by UPI or cash on delivery. Pickup and delivery is the service that wins regulars, especially in housing societies where a weekly pickup slot is worth more than a small discount. Delicate fabrics, wedding wear and woollens need honest handling, so never promise a stain will vanish if it may not, and always check pockets and buttons before cleaning. A festival offer on blankets or wedding outfits brings a rush of work, so plan staff and space before advertising it.

## What to avoid

Avoid any tool that promises guaranteed customers for a fee, because regulars come from good work, not software. Avoid sharing customer phone numbers, posting photos without permission, and promising a price or a date you cannot honour. Avoid paying for a yearly plan before a free trial, and avoid collecting data nobody checks each week. If a tool adds typing without saving time, drop it. Never mix up garments from two customers, and never deliver a garment without checking its tag against the bill. A dry cleaner who loses one expensive saree loses ten customers by word of mouth.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the best AI tool for dry cleaners in India?**
There is no single best tool, because dry cleaners need pickup and delivery, garment tracking and repeat customers. Start with WhatsApp Business for pickup reminders and Google Business Profile for local search. Add a paid tool only when the free set starts losing work or wasting time.

**Can AI help me get more customers?**
Yes, indirectly. A complete Google profile with real photos, quick WhatsApp replies and one good post a week bring in new customers. But repeat business still comes from good work at an honest price.

**How much do AI tools cost for dry cleaners?**
The starting set is free: WhatsApp Business, Google tools, Canva and ChatGPT all have free plans. Paid plans are a later step, so check current pricing on the official page before committing to anything.

**How do I remind customers without spamming them?**
Send one polite reminder at the right time, only to customers who agreed to messages, and stop if they ask you to. One useful reminder beats ten daily offers that get your number blocked.

**Should a dry cleaner offer free pickup and delivery?**
It works well in housing societies and nearby areas, where one trip can collect many orders. Set a minimum order or a fixed pickup day, so the trips stay profitable, and confirm the pickup time on WhatsApp the evening before.

**Can I use photos of my work in advertising?**
Yes, if they are real photos of work you actually did and the customer agreed to them. Never use another business's photos, and never show a customer's personal details clearly in a public post.
