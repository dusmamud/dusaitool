Homeschooling in India is mostly a planning job. You are teacher, timetable, and record keeper at once. AI tools can carry the repetitive half: drafting lesson plans, making worksheets, writing practice questions, and recording what your child finished.

The short answer: start with Khan Academy as your free curriculum backbone. Add MagicSchool free for lesson plans, worksheets, and quizzes. Use ChatGPT free for planning, like turning a library book into a week of activities. Use Quizlet free for revision flashcards, and Canva free for printable worksheets. Pay for nothing in the first month; re-check official pricing before paying.

## Khan Academy: the free curriculum backbone

Khan Academy is the closest thing to a free, structured, full-length curriculum, with no ads and no paid upsell. Courses run from roughly Class 3 to Class 12, and Khan Academy Kids covers ages two to eight. You assign a unit, the platform teaches it and quizzes your child, and the progress view shows what was mastered or left stuck. Note: Khanmigo costs about $4 a month, but its parent plan reportedly needs US billing.

## MagicSchool: lesson plans in minutes

MagicSchool was built for classroom teachers, but homeschool parents are its natural second audience. Enter a subject, grade, and goal, and its Lesson Plan Generator returns a structured plan with objectives and discussion questions. The same account also makes worksheets, quizzes, and rubrics. Its Forever Free plan includes more than 80 teacher tools at no cost, while Plus costs about $8.33 a month billed annually and adds unlimited generations. The free generation limit is not published, so save every plan into your own document at once.

## ChatGPT: your planning assistant

ChatGPT is the most flexible tool here, and the one needing the most supervision. Ask it to turn one NCERT chapter into a five-day plan or write ten word problems using your child's interests. It is excellent at first drafts, but it can invent facts and get maths wrong with total confidence. The free plan in India lists unlimited everyday text chats, with separate limits on uploads, images, and voice. Paid plans are Go at about ₹399 a month and Plus at about ₹1,999 a month. Never paste your child's name, photo, or school records into it.

## Quizlet: revision that sticks

Quizlet turns notes into flashcards, matching games, and practice tests, using spaced repetition, a study method with strong evidence behind it. Build a set from your own chapter notes in minutes, and your child can revise it on a phone in short bursts. Two limits matter: some AI features are paid, and its AI Practice Tests carry a minimum age of 18 in India, so younger children should use ordinary flashcards. Check every AI-generated set yourself, because the questions can contain errors.

## Using these tools in India

Homeschooling in India sits in a legal grey area. No central law bans homeschooling, and the Right to Education Act requires education for children aged six to fourteen without mandating that it happen inside a school building. But there is no national registration system, and states interpret the rules differently. For certification, the National Institute of Open Schooling (NIOS) lets home-educated children sit Class 10 and 12 exams, held twice a year. Check the NIOS website and your state education department first; these notes are general information only, not professional advice.

For the daily routine, a simple weekly rhythm beats a complicated system. On Sunday, draft the week's plans in MagicSchool and print worksheets with Canva's free templates. Each morning, let Khan Academy carry the lesson; spend ten minutes nightly on Quizlet revision. Follow NCERT textbooks, and check every AI output against them. ## What to avoid

Do not let an AI tool become the teacher. A chatbot that hands your child every answer teaches them to ask the machine instead of thinking, so keep AI on planning and practice, and keep the explaining and checking with you. Run a full month on free tiers before buying any paid plan. Do not trust an AI worksheet without reading it, and never upload your child's personal details to a free AI tool. Do not promise a board exam result based on any tool's claims.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is homeschooling legal in India?**

No central law bans it, and the Right to Education Act requires education for ages six to fourteen, not necessarily in a school building. Certification comes through boards like NIOS. Check the NIOS website and your state education department first.

**Which AI tool is best for homeschool lesson plans?**

MagicSchool is the best dedicated option, because its free plan includes a Lesson Plan Generator plus more than 80 teacher tools for worksheets, quizzes, and rubrics. ChatGPT is the flexible option for turning a chapter into a custom week of activities.

**Can I homeschool in India completely free with AI tools?**

Yes, for the core work. Khan Academy courses are free, MagicSchool has a Forever Free plan, ChatGPT's free plan covers everyday text chats, Canva Free covers worksheet templates, and Quizlet's free plan covers flashcards. You will still pay for printing and internet.

**How do I track my child's progress while homeschooling?**

Use Khan Academy's mastery reports as your main record, keep a weekly log of lessons finished, and save dated worksheets as a portfolio.

## Final verdict

Start free with Khan Academy and MagicSchool, add ChatGPT for planning, and check NIOS rules before promising any board exam.
