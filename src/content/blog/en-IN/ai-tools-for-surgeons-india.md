A surgeon's day is split between the OPD, the operation theatre, and the paperwork that follows both. The right AI tools do not replace surgical judgement — they take the typing, the scheduling calls, and the follow-up chasing off your plate. The shortlist is small: a free AI scribe, an Indian EMR, and a scheduling system patients will actually use.

## 1. Heidi Health — best free AI scribe

Heidi Health listens to a consultation, with the patient's permission, and drafts a structured note — history, examination, plan — which you review, correct, and sign. Its free plan includes unlimited transcription and note generation with standard templates, covering most solo outpatient work. Advanced features such as custom templates and its Ask Heidi assistant are capped at about 10 actions a month on the free plan. It supports 110+ languages, works on web and mobile, and needs no VPN in India.

## 2. EkaScribe — best for Indian languages

EkaScribe, from Eka Care, is built for Indian clinics. It supports 20+ languages including Hindi, Bengali, Tamil, Telugu, and Marathi, on web, mobile, and WhatsApp. The free plan covers 5 consultations a day, and the Pro plan is reported at about ₹1,499 a month per doctor. Caution: its policy says recordings may be used to train its AI unless you switch that off — check privacy settings first.

## 3. Augnito — best for dictating operative notes

Augnito is an Indian medical speech-recognition tool built for dictation: you dictate, it types, and it understands Indian English accents with no voice-profile training. It covers 50+ specialties with surgical vocabulary and templates, on Windows, Mac, web, and mobile. Sources disagree on price: comparison sites quote roughly $29–$79 a month, Indian guides ₹2,000–₹5,000. Ask for a quote. Unlike an ambient scribe, Augnito does not generate a structured note from a conversation; you dictate deliberately.

## 4. Practo Ray — best for scheduling and practice management

Practo Ray handles appointment booking, an advanced calendar, SMS reminders and confirmations, billing, patient charting, and sharing records with patients. Its own pricing page lists plans from ₹999 a month to ₹1,999 a month, with a 15-day free trial for each new practice. But the subscription is only part of the bill: marketplace bookings can add per-appointment fees, so a busy clinic pays more than the headline plan.

## 5. HealthPlix — best fast EMR for Indian OPDs

HealthPlix is a doctor-first EMR used by a large network of Indian doctors — one guide cites 80,000+ doctors. Its strength is speed: prescriptions in about 30 seconds, a drug database, specialty templates, ABHA linking, and prescriptions in 14 regional languages over WhatsApp. Pricing is reported at roughly ₹1,500–₹3,000 a month, with some sources citing ₹17,999 a year; confirm before paying. Its limits matter: IPD and inpatient features are limited, so it suits OPD-heavy surgical practice better than a hospital with beds. No EMR here replaces a hospital's OT board.

## Using these tools in India

Start with one tool, not five. A sensible setup for a solo surgeon is Heidi Health free for notes, Practo Ray for appointments and reminders, and WhatsApp Business — free — for post-op check-ins and wound-photo follow-ups with patient consent. Keep patient data inside Indian-compliant systems: prefer ABDM- and ABHA-supported tools, and never store identifiable patient records in a general-purpose chatbot. Block OT time as a separate calendar so OPD never collides with surgery.

## What to avoid

- **Recording patients without consent.** An AI scribe without consent is a legal and ethical problem, not a shortcut.
- **Signing AI notes unread.** Scribes invent details. Every note needs your eyes before it becomes part of the record.
- **Paying in dollars when an Indian option exists.** US scribes at $100+ a month are hard to justify when free or ₹1,499 options cover the same OPD work.
- **Putting patient data in free general AI chatbots.** They are not built for health records and may retain what you paste.

## FAQ

**Which AI scribe is best for surgeons in India?**

Heidi Health is the best free starting point, with unlimited standard notes and no VPN needed. If your patients speak Hindi or regional languages, EkaScribe's 20+ language support may fit better. For operative notes, Augnito's dictation is the stronger pick.

**Can AI tools plan my OT list?**

Not really. These tools manage OPD scheduling, records, and follow-ups. OT planning still needs your hospital's theatre schedule, anaesthetist availability, and equipment lists. Protect OT blocks from OPD bookings, but do not expect it to run the theatre.

**Is it legal to record consultations for an AI scribe in India?**

You must get the patient's consent before recording, and explain how the recording is used and stored. This article is not legal advice — check with your hospital's medico-legal team first.

**Do these tools work without a VPN in India?**

Yes. Heidi Health, EkaScribe, Augnito, Practo Ray, and HealthPlix are all available in India without a VPN. They work on ordinary Indian broadband and 4G/5G.

## Final verdict

Start free with Heidi Health for notes and Practo Ray for scheduling; add HealthPlix when your OPD volume grows.
