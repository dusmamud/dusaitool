If you want an AI plan for workouts and food in India without paying first, start with two free combinations: FitOn for home workouts and HealthifyMe for tracking Indian food. Both work in India without a VPN, and neither forces you to pay before you can train or log a meal. Everything else in this article is for a specific need — gym strength training, a structured coach, or deeper food analysis — and each paid wall is named honestly below.

The short version is this: free tiers in fitness apps are genuinely useful, but they are not all the same. Some apps give you full workouts for free and charge only for extras like meal plans and offline downloads. Others give you a small free taste and put the actual personalised plan behind a subscription. Knowing which kind you are signing up for saves you the most common disappointment in this category: finishing a setup quiz and only then discovering the plan costs money.

A safety note before you start any new plan: these apps are not medical advice. If you have a health condition, an injury, are pregnant, or take regular medication, check with a doctor before starting a new workout or diet plan. An AI coach does not know your medical history, and a free plan is not a diagnosis.

## HealthifyMe: the Indian food tracker, with a paid AI coach

HealthifyMe is the most India-specific tool on this list, and that is its real advantage. Its food database understands Indian meals — dal, roti, sabzi, biryani, idli — in a way that global apps often do not, and its calorie tracking is built around the way people in India actually eat. If your main problem is "what am I eating every day," this is where to start.

The free side is basic tracking: log meals, follow calories, and use the app's standard features without paying. The AI part, however, is paid. Ria, HealthifyMe's personal AI coach, is part of the paid Healthify+ plan, which the company's store page describes as a 24/7 AI health coach with macro and micronutrient tracking, photo-based meal logging (called SNAP), unlimited healthy recipes, and daily and weekly insights. At the last check, the store page showed the Healthify+ plan at Rs. 399, marked down from Rs. 799, with a 12-month option also listed — but treat that as a snapshot, not a promise, and confirm the current price on the store page before paying.

Use HealthifyMe free if you want honest Indian food logging and you are comfortable building your own plan. Pay for Healthify+ only if you specifically want Ria's feedback on every meal, photo logging instead of typing, and weekly reports — and only after checking what the current plan actually costs you. Do not pay for the AI coach expecting a human nutritionist: Ria is an AI assistant, not a doctor or a registered dietitian, and its suggestions are only as good as what you log.

## FitOn: genuinely free home workouts

FitOn is the clearest free deal in this article. Its own help centre says, in plain words, that all its workouts and personalised fitness plans are 100% free, with no catch, and that it does not sell your data or target you with ads. That is unusual in this category, and it is the reason FitOn is the first recommendation for home workouts.

What you get free covers cardio, yoga, strength, pilates and toning, with no equipment needed, on any screen. The paid tier, FitOn PRO, sits on top of those free workouts and adds structured fitness and nutrition programmes, personalised meal plans, more than 500 recipes, unlimited offline downloads, real-time heart rate with a connected device, and premium music. Note one honest limit from FitOn's own help pages: premium music has restrictions for users outside the US, so do not buy PRO mainly for the music if you are in India.

Use FitOn free if you want to train at home with no equipment and no payment. Pay for PRO only if you want the structured programmes and meal plans, or offline downloads for travel and low-data days. For most beginners, the free workouts alone are enough for weeks.

## Nike Training Club: free Nike coaching, with a region catch

Nike Training Club (NTC) is Nike's free workout app, and its Google Play listing for India describes it as a portal to Nike trainers with free access to strength, conditioning, yoga, Pilates, recovery and mindfulness content for Nike Members. Workouts cover the gym and home, including bodyweight training for small spaces, and the app logs completed workouts and syncs with Google Fit. There is no subscription price to compare here because the core content is free.

There is one region catch worth knowing, stated on the same Play Store listing: video-on-demand classes are listed as available only in certain countries — the US, UK, Brazil, Japan, China, France, Germany, Russia, Italy, Spain, Mexico and South Korea — and NTC TV is listed as US-only. India is not on that video-on-demand list. That does not make the app useless in India, but it does mean you should expect the programme and trainer-led content that is available to you locally, and not assume every premiere workout or video class shown in global marketing will play here. Check inside the app, on your own phone, before building a routine around a specific video series.

Use NTC free if you like trainer-led structure and Nike's coaching style. It pairs well with FitOn: use one for variety and the other for a structured programme, and keep whichever one you actually open every week.

## Freeletics: free bodyweight workouts, paid AI Coach

Freeletics is built for people who want short, hard, planned bodyweight workouts without a gym. Its own help centre is clear about the split: the app is free to download and gives you access to many signature workouts, plus a library of single exercises, runs and audio sessions. That free content is meant as a taste of a full training journey. The personalised plan — the AI Coach that builds a training journey around your goals and adapts to your feedback — is a paid subscription.

On price, sources disagree in a way you should know about. Freeletics' own help pages confirm the free-versus-Coach split but do not pin down one price, and a third-party review of the US App Store in October 2026 listed Training Coach plans from $34.99 to $79.99 for 3-, 6- and 12-month terms, with a Training and Nutrition Bundle up to $89.99, and no monthly plan. Those are US store figures, not Indian prices, and the review itself notes the term-to-price mapping is not confirmed by Freeletics. So: trust the free-versus-paid split, but check the price shown in your own Google Play or App Store in India before paying anything, and remember Freeletics charges the full term up front — a weekly price shown on a website is only a comparison reference, per its own help centre.

Use Freeletics free if you want bodyweight training and you are happy picking workouts yourself. Pay for the Coach only if you want a structured, adaptive plan and you are comfortable paying for several months up front.

## MyFitnessPal: free food logging, paid insights

MyFitnessPal is the classic food diary, and its free tier is still the right starting point if you mainly want to see where your calories and macros come from. According to its own help pages, the free version lets you log food and exercise, create your own recipes, track measurements, and connect partner apps. That covers the core habit: writing down what you eat.

The paid tiers add interpretation, not logging. Premium adds faster logging tools like Meal Scan and the barcode scanner, deeper nutrition breakdowns, goals by meal or by day, and no ads. Premium+ adds meal planning, meal prep tools, diet preferences and grocery lists. The common misconception to avoid: the barcode scanner is a Premium feature, not a free one — if you planned to scan every packet for free, that plan will hit the paywall quickly. MyFitnessPal's help pages do not pin a single price in the material checked for this article, so check the current Premium price in your app store before paying.

Use MyFitnessPal free if you want a simple, global food diary and you are comfortable typing or searching foods. Use HealthifyMe instead if Indian food logging matters more to you, because its database and recipes are built for Indian meals first.

## Jefit: free gym workout planner

Jefit is for gym training rather than home workouts. Its workout planner lets you create and customise routines from a library of more than 1,400 exercises, reschedule workout days, plan rest days, and track progress with detailed metrics. If you go to a gym and want a structured strength plan, this is the most focused free tool here.

The free tier, called Basic on Jefit's Elite page, includes free and custom workout routines, the full exercise library with guided instructions, exercise logging and history, and community access. The paid Elite tier adds professionally designed workout plans, advanced analytics and tracking, and unlimited watch workouts. The Elite page lists $12.99 per month, with an annual option shown at a discount — again, confirm the current price in your app store, since the annual figure shown on the page was ambiguous at the last check.

Use Jefit free if you already go to a gym or have weights at home and want to plan and log strength sessions. It is not an AI coach in the Freeletics sense — it gives you the planner and the library, and you build the routine — but for lifters, that control is often the point.

## Using fitness AI in India: language, payment, and data

Language first: most of these apps are English-first. HealthifyMe is the most comfortable for Indian users because its food content is Indian, but do not assume Hindi voice guidance or Hindi plans in any app on this list unless you see that option inside the app itself. If you need Hindi, check the app's language settings on your own phone before committing to a plan.

Payment: prices in this article mix rupees and dollars because the apps bill differently. HealthifyMe prices in rupees on its own store. Global apps bill through Google Play or the App Store, which show you the price in rupees at checkout — always read that checkout price, not a dollar figure from a review, before paying. Avoid yearly plans until a free tier has survived two full weeks of your actual routine; the cheapest plan is the one you still use in week three.

Data and privacy: fitness apps collect sensitive data — your weight, meals, location, and sometimes heart rate. Before logging anything personal, read the app's data safety section on its store page and its privacy policy, and use a separate password. For a fuller guide to keeping free tools safe, see the [privacy and safety tools](/blog/ai-privacy-safety-free-tools-india/) article. Health data deserves more caution than a to-do list.

Two India-specific notes. First, a popular global strength app, Fitbod, does not list India among the countries in its own availability FAQ — its list covers the US, UK, Spain, Mexico, several Latin American countries, Portugal and Brazil, but not India. Do not build your routine around Fitbod in India until its own pages say it is available here. Second, India has its own large fitness community in FITTR, which runs coaching and community features through its app listed on the App Store in India — worth a look if you want Indian coaches and community, but check its current free community access and coaching prices yourself, since this article does not rely on FITTR as a verified free tier.

For workout music to train with, the [free music generators](/blog/free-ai-music-generators-india/) article covers free background tracks, and the [best free AI tools](/blog/best-free-ai-tools-india/) list is a good general starting point if you want to compare across categories.

## What to avoid

Avoid any fitness app that asks for payment before showing you a single workout or meal log. A genuine free tier lets you train or log first. Avoid "AI body scan" or "AI diet diagnosis" claims that promise medical results from a photo — no app in this article diagnoses anything, and any app that claims to should not be trusted with your health.

Avoid buying a yearly plan on day one, especially for Freeletics-style plans that charge the whole term up front. Avoid sharing health data with apps whose privacy policy you have not read, and avoid plans that demand daily perfection: a plan you follow four days a week for three months beats a perfect plan you abandon in week one. Finally, avoid copying a stranger's diet or workout volume from social media — your plan should match your body, your schedule, and your food, not an influencer's.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I get a full AI fitness plan in India completely free?** Yes, for the basics. FitOn gives you free workouts and personalised fitness plans, HealthifyMe gives you free food logging, and Jefit gives you free workout planning. What stays paid is the human-style coaching layer: a dedicated AI coach, structured meal plans, and premium analytics. If "free" is your hard rule, combine those three free tiers instead of buying one app.

**Which app is best for Indian food?** HealthifyMe, because its food database and recipes are built around Indian meals. MyFitnessPal works, but you will spend more time searching for or adding Indian dishes yourself. If you eat mostly Indian food at home, that difference matters every single day.

**Do I need a gym for these apps?** No. FitOn, Nike Training Club and Freeletics all have no-equipment home workouts. You need a gym only if you want Jefit's strength plans with weights, and even Jefit lets you set bodyweight or limited equipment instead of a full gym.

**Is the Nike Training Club app really free in India?** The core workouts and programmes are free for Nike Members through the Google Play listing for India. The catch is regional: some video-on-demand and premiere content is limited to listed countries that do not include India, so check inside the app rather than assuming every video will play.

**Why does Freeletics charge for its AI Coach if the app is free?** Because the free app is a library of workouts, while the Coach is a personalised, adaptive plan built around your goals and feedback. That distinction — library versus plan — is the same split you will find in most fitness apps, and it is worth checking before you sign up anywhere.

**Should I pay for a fitness app at all?** Only when a specific feature blocks you. Pay when you need a structured meal plan, a coach that adapts to you, offline downloads for travel, or advanced analytics — and only after two weeks of actually using the free tier. If the free tier already covers your routine, paying adds cost, not fitness.

## The final verdict, and a checklist before you pay

Start with FitOn free for home workouts and HealthifyMe free for Indian food tracking; pay only when a coach or meal plan actually blocks you. That is the whole strategy: two free apps, one for moving and one for eating, and money spent only on the exact feature that is missing.

Before you pay for anything, run this checklist. First, have you used the free tier for two full weeks? Second, is the price shown in your own app store in rupees, and do you know whether it is monthly or a full term charged up front? Third, does the paid feature solve a problem you actually have — a meal plan, a coach, offline access — or does it just sound impressive? Fourth, have you read the data safety section for an app that will hold your weight and food history? If any answer is no, stay free for another week. Fitness comes from the routine you keep, not the subscription you buy.
