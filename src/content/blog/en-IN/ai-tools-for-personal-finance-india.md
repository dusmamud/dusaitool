Personal finance in India in 2026 is mostly a tracking problem, not a knowledge problem. Most people already know they should spend less than they earn, but they do not know where the money actually went, which bill is due this week, or how much of the month is left in each category. The tools in this article fix that specific gap: they record spending, sort it into categories, remind you before a bill is due, and in some cases suggest a budget based on your own numbers. None of them is investment advice, none of them promises returns, and none of them can save money for you. This article is educational only. It will not tell you where to invest, and any tool that promises guaranteed returns or guaranteed savings should be treated as a warning sign, not a feature.

The short answer for India is this: use [expenseOS](https://expenseos.app/) for an AI budget, because its free plan includes expense tracking, budgets, predictive analytics, anomaly detection, and five AI questions a day. Use [Moneyview](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?gl=IN&hl=en_IN&id=com.whizdm.moneyview) or [ET Money](https://www.etmoney.com/) for automatic tracking, because both read your bank and bill SMS messages and sort spending for you without you typing every entry. Use [CRED](https://cred.club/) for bill reminders if you have a credit card, because it tracks card bills, sends payment reminders, and covers utility bills and autopay. Use [Splitwise](https://www.splitwise.com/) for shared bills with flatmates or family, because its free plan covers everyday splitting. Use [Goodbudget](https://goodbudget.com/) if you want the envelope method, because its free plan gives you twenty envelopes and one account. Use [INDmoney](https://www.indmoney.com/) if you want one free net-worth view across what you own and owe. And use a free chatbot only to draft a first budget from numbers you already know, never by sharing passwords, OTPs, or bank login details.

## expenseOS: the AI budget built for India

[expenseOS](https://expenseos.app/) is the most directly AI-shaped tool on this list, and its pricing page is in rupees, which is a good sign for India availability. Its free plan, listed as forever free at zero rupees, includes up to five financial accounts, up to five savings goals, five Finie AI queries per day, expense tracking and budgets, predictive analytics, anomaly detection, a goal completion outlook, Finie insights, suggestions and reminders, and a Credit Card Equaliser. The paid Pro plan is 299 rupees a month or 2,999 rupees a year, and a Pro Family plan at 499 rupees a month adds shared household budgets and goals for up to five members. The free plan is therefore genuinely usable, not a demo: tracking, budgets, predictions, and reminders all work without paying.

The practical way to use it is to connect or enter up to five accounts, set up to five goals, and spend the five daily AI questions on decisions, not data entry. Ask Finie why one category overshot this month, what a realistic next-month budget looks like from your last three months, or which upcoming bills threaten a goal. Do not waste the daily questions asking for your balance, which the dashboard already shows. The anomaly detection is the quiet feature that matters most for India: an unusual charge, a doubled subscription, or a sudden jump in one category is flagged without you hunting for it. The Credit Card Equaliser is worth a look if you carry card balances, but it is a planning view, not a promise that any balance will disappear. Prices and limits change, so re-check the official pricing page before paying or promising yourself a deadline based on a paid feature.

## ET Money: automatic tracking with an investment business behind it

[ET Money](https://www.etmoney.com/) began as an expense manager and is now primarily an investment platform, and that history explains both its strength and its trade-off. Its free side reads your SMS messages and sorts spending into categories like travel, restaurants, and groceries, shows where you spend the most so the biggest leaks are visible, and runs a bill calendar that alerts you about payment deadlines once you add due dates. Cash spending is the exception: cash expenses are tracked only if you feed in the details yourself, because there is no SMS for cash. That rule applies to every SMS tracker on this list, not just ET Money.

The trade-off is stated plainly here because sources disagree about how to describe it. Roundups call ET Money a free tracker, and its tracking is free, but the company also earns commission from the mutual funds, insurance, and loan products sold through it, and it offers a paid Genius tier on top. It also uses transaction data to build a financial and risk profile of you. That is a reasonable trade if you want investing and tracking in one app. It is the opposite of what you want if you would rather no company modelled your spending history. If you use ET Money, use it for tracking and bill reminders, and treat every investment suggestion as a sales screen, not advice. This article gives no investment advice, and neither should an app's recommendation feed.

## Moneyview: the simplest automatic tracker, with loans attached

[Moneyview](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?gl=IN&hl=en_IN&id=com.whizdm.moneyview) Money Manager is the most India-plain tracker on this list: it reads your financial SMS messages from banks and billers, presents expenses automatically, shows credit and debit balances in one place, gives payment reminders, and tracks reimbursements, all without manual entry for card and bank spending. Its Play Store listing in India shows over one crore downloads and a 4.5 rating, which makes it one of the most used trackers in the country. Like ET Money, cash spending needs manual entry, because cash leaves no SMS trail.

The warning matters as much as the feature. Moneyview's core business is lending: the same listing advertises instant personal loans up to ten lakh rupees, and shows loan interest from 16 percent to 39 percent a year with processing fees from 2 to 8 percent. A tracker that also sells loans will show you loan offers, and a loan taken because an app suggested it is still a loan with interest. Use Moneyview for tracking and reminders, ignore the loan screens unless you have separately decided, on paper, that a loan is necessary, and never borrow to cover ordinary monthly spending. That single rule protects more money than any budgeting feature.

## Splitwise: shared bills without the monthly argument

[Splitwise](https://www.splitwise.com/) solves a different finance job: money shared between people. Flatmates splitting rent, electricity, and groceries, families sharing a holiday, or friends splitting dinners all create the same monthly argument about who paid what. Splitwise records each expense once, divides it by the rule you choose, and keeps a running balance per person, so the month ends with one settlement instead of ten arguments. Its free plan covers everyday splitting, but it has a limit that matters: free users can add up to four expenses each day, and unlimited expenses, receipt scanning and itemization, charts, currency conversion, and default splits sit in Splitwise Pro. Four expenses a day is enough for most households, since most days have zero or one shared expense, but on a trip with many small shared payments you will hit the cap, and the honest workaround is to add expenses the next day or ask a friend to add them. For a household, that limit is rarely a reason to pay. For a group trip, it might be.

Splitwise is also the right place for a privacy note that applies to every tool here: shared expenses are visible to the group by design. Do not record private spending in a shared group, and do not add expenses for people who did not agree to split them. A finance tool should reduce arguments, not create new ones about what was shared.

## Goodbudget: the envelope method, done properly

[Goodbudget](https://goodbudget.com/) is the oldest idea on this list, and for some people it is the only method that works. Envelope budgeting means dividing your monthly money into envelopes, one per category, and stopping when an envelope is empty. Goodbudget's free plan gives ten regular envelopes plus ten more envelopes, and one account, forever free. The paid plan adds unlimited envelopes and accounts, sharing with five devices, seven years of history, and one-on-one support. The catch for India is specific and verified on the official subscribe page: linked accounts for automatic bank sync work for US banks only. In India, Goodbudget is a manual-entry tool. That is not a flaw if you want the method, because typing each expense is part of what makes envelope budgeting work, but it is a flaw if you expected automatic sync. Do not buy the paid plan expecting Indian bank sync that the official page says is US-only.

Goodbudget suits a single person or a couple who want hard category limits, for example a fixed grocery envelope, a fixed transport envelope, and a fixed eating-out envelope, with the rule that an empty envelope means waiting until next month. It suits nobody who hates typing entries. If you hate typing, use an SMS tracker instead and set category budgets there. The method matters more than the app, and the honest test is one month: if you did not open the app in week three, the method failed, and a different tool will not fix it.

## CRED: bill reminders for credit card users, with a membership gate

[CRED](https://cred.club/) is a members-only Indian app for credit card users, and its membership rule is the first thing to check: you need a credit score of 750 or above to join. Inside, it manages multiple credit cards, tracks spends, monitors EMIs, spots hidden charges and duplicate spends, sends timely bill payment reminders, and covers utility bills, DTH, mobile recharge, rent, and more, with autopay for recurring bills. It uses the Bharat Bill Payment System platform for bill payments for supported banks. For a card user, that combination is genuinely useful: one place for every card bill, reminders before due dates, and a check for charges you did not expect.

The limits are equally clear. If you have no credit card, or your score is below the gate, CRED is not available to you, and that is fine, because its reminders can be replaced by any other tool's bill calendar plus your bank's own alerts. CRED also earns attention through rewards, and rewards are not savings: a reward for paying a bill you had to pay anyway is a bonus, but a purchase made to earn a reward is spending, not saving. Use CRED for reminders and charge-spotting, ignore the rewards store unless the reward is for something you were already buying, and never carry a card balance to earn points. Interest on a carried balance costs far more than any reward pays back.

## INDmoney: one free view of what you own and owe

[INDmoney](https://www.indmoney.com/) answers a question the trackers above do not: what is my total position? Its features page describes a unified net-worth view that brings assets, liabilities, goals, and family wealth into one place, with family members addable for a combined household view, plus portfolio analytics and income-tax filing listed as free. For a household that has a salary account, a few investments, and a loan, that single view is more useful than five separate app balances, because it shows the gap between what you own and what you owe in one number. That number is educational, not a judgement: a negative or small net worth at the start of tracking is normal, and the point of tracking is to watch it move over months, not to feel bad about one month.

The caution is the same as for ET Money. INDmoney is an investment platform as well as a tracker, so its screens will also show investment products. Use the free net-worth view and analytics for understanding, and treat product screens as advertising. This article gives no investment advice, and a net-worth number does not tell you what to buy. If you want investment decisions, that is a separate decision with its own research, and no free tracker replaces it.

## ChatGPT and Gemini: a first budget draft, not a tracker

A free chatbot is useful for exactly one finance job: drafting a first budget from numbers you already know. Tell it your monthly income, your fixed bills, and your last month's category totals, and ask for a budget that keeps fixed costs, variable costs, and a savings line separate. [ChatGPT](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/9275245-chatgpt-free-tier-faq) is free to use, with a free tier whose default model and limits change over time, and higher limits on paid plans. [Gemini](https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/16275805?hl=en) is free with usage limits that refresh every five hours until a weekly limit, and those limits may change. Because both sets of limits change, check the official help pages before relying on either for a deadline.

The safety rules for chatbots are non-negotiable. Never paste bank passwords, OTPs, card numbers, account numbers, or full statements into a chatbot. Use rounded totals, not account details. Never accept a chatbot's investment pick, tax claim, or loan suggestion without checking it against an official source, because chatbots can state wrong numbers confidently. And never let a chatbot's budget become a promise: a budget is a plan, and the plan works only if you track against it in one of the tools above. Draft with a chatbot, track with a tracker, and the two together are worth more than either alone.

## Using these tools in India: language, cash, UPI, and no VPN

All seven tools above work in India without a VPN, through the Play Store or the web, and that was a condition for inclusion. Language is the honest limitation: most finance apps are English-first, with Hindi availability varying by app and screen, so check inside the app before assuming a full Hindi interface. Cash is the second limitation: SMS trackers only see card, bank, and UPI spending, so cash spending must be entered by hand in every tool here, and a budget that ignores cash will always look better than reality. UPI spending usually appears through bank SMS, but if a UPI transaction produces no SMS, enter it manually the same day, because a week-old cash or UPI entry is usually a guessed entry.

Privacy is the third limitation, and it deserves its own paragraph. SMS trackers need permission to read your transaction messages, investment platforms build profiles from your transactions, and shared-bill apps show expenses to the group. Those are real trade-offs, not fine print. Read the permission screen before granting it, use a tracker whose business you understand, and keep passwords and OTPs out of every finance app and chatbot. If you want the privacy side in more depth, the [privacy tools](/blog/ai-privacy-safety-free-tools-india/) article in this series covers the habits and tools that protect you most. If your income is irregular, the [freelancer money tools](/blog/ai-tools-for-freelancers-india/) article covers the same tracking problem with variable monthly income, and if you prefer planning in a sheet, the [data and Excel tools](/blog/ai-tools-for-data-excel-india/) article covers budget spreadsheets you control yourself.

## What to avoid

Avoid any app that promises guaranteed returns, guaranteed savings, or a fixed monthly income from an investment, because no legitimate tool can guarantee those. Avoid sharing OTPs, passwords, or bank login details with any app, agent, or chatbot, because no legitimate tracker needs them. Avoid linking a bank account by username and password where an SMS-read or manual-entry option exists, because a password link gives far more access than tracking needs. Avoid taking a loan offered inside a tracker unless you decided on paper, separately, that the loan is necessary, because an in-app offer is designed to be accepted quickly. Avoid paying for a premium tier on day one: use the free tier for a full month first, and pay only for a specific feature you hit a limit on, such as expenseOS Pro for more AI questions or Splitwise Pro for unlimited trip expenses. Avoid recording private spending in shared groups, and avoid treating a chatbot's numbers as verified. A budget that survives contact with real spending is worth more than a beautiful budget nobody tracked.

## Frequently asked questions

**Which free tool is best for a first budget in India?** Start with expenseOS for the AI budget itself, because its free plan includes tracking, budgets, predictions, and five AI questions a day, and add Moneyview or ET Money if you want automatic SMS tracking alongside it.

**Do SMS trackers see my cash spending?** No. SMS trackers only see card, bank, and UPI transactions that produce a message, so cash spending must be entered by hand in every tool on this list, and a budget that ignores cash will look better than reality.

**Is CRED free, and who can join?** CRED membership itself is free, but it is members-only and needs a credit score of 750 or above, so it is only available to credit card users who clear that gate.

**Can Splitwise free handle a whole household?** Yes, for most households, because free users can add up to four expenses a day, and most days have zero or one shared expense, but on a trip with many small shared payments you will hit the cap.

**Does Goodbudget sync with Indian banks?** No. Its official subscribe page says linked-account bank sync works for US banks only, so in India Goodbudget is a manual-entry envelope tool.

**Should I trust an app's investment suggestions?** No, not as advice. ET Money and INDmoney are investment platforms as well as trackers, so treat their product screens as advertising, and make investment decisions only after separate research.

**Is it safe to give a tracker SMS permission?** It is a real trade-off, not a formality. SMS permission lets the tracker see transaction messages, so grant it only to a tracker whose business you understand, read the permission screen first, and never share OTPs or passwords with any finance app or chatbot.

## The final verdict

Start free with expenseOS for AI budgets, Moneyview for SMS tracking, and CRED for bill reminders. Track first, pay for nothing yet. That combination covers the three jobs that matter: a plan, automatic recording, and a reminder before a bill is due. Add Splitwise only if you share bills, Goodbudget only if you want hard envelope limits, and INDmoney only if you want one net-worth view. Use a chatbot only to draft, never to decide, and re-check every price and limit on the official page before paying, because free limits change and a tool that is free today can gate a feature tomorrow. One month of honest tracking teaches more about your money than any premium plan, and it costs nothing.
