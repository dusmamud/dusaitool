## The short answer

For most Indian skin clinics, the best starting pair is a local EMR plus an AI scribe. HealthPlix SPOT or Practo Ray handles appointments, prescriptions and records, while Heidi Health turns the consultation into a note. Add DermEngine only for regular mole work, and treat SkinVision as a triage aid, never as a diagnosis.

## 1. HealthPlix SPOT — the Indian EMR built for doctors

HealthPlix SPOT is a mobile-first EMR made in India, and the most practical start for a solo dermatologist. It covers appointments, video consultations, prescriptions, billing and records in one app. Prescriptions in 14+ Indian languages take about 30 seconds, with AI suggestions. SMS and WhatsApp reminders chase follow-ups for acne and pigmentation care. It is listed as free for doctors, but some users report a later subscription prompt, so confirm first.

## 2. Practo Ray — appointments, records and patient discovery

Practo Ray is the clinic software from Practo, a large Indian health platform. Its advantage is discovery: patients already search Practo, so clinics on Ray get found and booked. It covers scheduling, records, prescriptions and reminders, and supports ABHA, India's digital health identity. The catch is cost, and sources disagree: Practo's page lists plans from around ₹999 a month; reviews report ₹1,500–₹2,000 a month plus marketplace fees. Take the trial and get a written quote first.

## 3. DermEngine — serious imaging for mole and lesion clinics

DermEngine, by MetaOptima, is the specialist here: a cloud platform for imaging and documenting skin conditions, built around dermoscopy. It works with MoleScope, a smartphone dermoscope attachment. Its AI finds similar pathology-labelled images to support your decision. Total-body photography highlights new or changing lesions, and teledermatology lets a colleague review a case. Listings put subscriptions from about $99 a month; the official price is quoted. You also need a dermoscope and photo consent.

## 4. SkinVision — a triage aid patients use on their own phones

SkinVision is aimed at patients, not clinics. A person photographs a mole, and the AI gives a risk indication — low, low with symptoms, or high — within seconds. Its algorithm was validated against biopsy-confirmed cases, with sensitivity above 90 percent. Clinics can use it for education: ask patients to photograph a changing mole and bring the result. The app is free to download, but checks and subscriptions are paid. It works best on lighter skin types. SkinVision does not diagnose skin cancer and does not replace a doctor's visit.

## 5. Heidi Health — an AI scribe that writes the notes

Heidi Health is an AI scribe: it listens to the consultation and drafts a note, letter or summary for you to review. It supports 110+ languages and specialty templates, useful when consultations switch languages. The free plan offers unlimited basic transcription and standard notes, with advanced actions capped at 10 a month. The paid plan was reported at about $150 a month in early 2026, so check first. Notes do not sync into your EMR on cheaper plans, and you need patient consent before recording.

## Using these tools in an Indian skin clinic

Start small and in this order. First, move records and appointments onto one EMR and use it for every patient for two weeks. Second, add Heidi's free plan and check it saves time. Third, standardise photos: same phone, lighting and distance, filed against the patient record. Fourth, trial DermEngine on a few real cases first. Dollar-priced tools cost more when the rupee weakens, so prefer Indian payment options. If your EMR supports ABHA, linking records smooths referrals — with patient consent.

## What to avoid

Never present AI output as a diagnosis — none of these tools is licensed to do that in India. Do not store patient photos on a personal phone or shared folder without encryption and consent. Trial for two weeks before signing a yearly contract. Do not pick a tool just because it is free: a capped free plan costs more in disruption than a modest paid one. Do not let any tool train on your patient data — read the privacy terms and ask the vendor in writing.

## Frequently asked questions

**Which AI tool should an Indian dermatologist try first?**
Start with HealthPlix SPOT or Practo Ray — appointments, prescriptions and records are the daily bottleneck. Add Heidi's free plan for notes later.

**Can AI diagnose skin cancer from a photo?**
No. SkinVision and DermEngine give a risk indication or similar-image support, but they do not diagnose. Only a dermatologist can diagnose skin cancer.

**Is HealthPlix really free for doctors?**
App stores list it as free for doctors, but some users report a subscription prompt after a free period. Confirm the terms in writing first.

**How much does Practo Ray cost?**
No single published price. Practo's page shows from about ₹999 a month; reviews report ₹1,500–₹2,000 plus marketplace fees. Ask for a written quote.

**Do these tools work without a VPN in India?**
Yes. HealthPlix and Practo Ray are Indian, and the other three work on Indian internet without a VPN.
