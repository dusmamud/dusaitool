Learning English in India in 2026 is not one problem. Grammar, vocabulary, pronunciation, speaking confidence, and writing are different problems, and no single free app solves all of them. The app that builds a daily habit does not teach you to hold a conversation, and the app that corrects your pronunciation does not teach you grammar. The free stack that actually works is a combination, not a single app.

The short answer for India is this: build a daily habit free with Duolingo, whose every course is free with energy limits, but with no AI conversation on the free plan. Speak free with Fluvia, whose conversations are free with no expiry, no account, and no card, correcting grammar, vocabulary, and phrasing the moment you slip. Fix pronunciation free with ELSA Speak, whose free tier gives limited daily lessons and sample AI conversations, with a seven-day trial, and whose Premium costs 19.99 dollars a month or from 71 dollars a year in the US, or from 499 rupees a month in India. Write free with Grammarly, whose free tier covers core grammar, spelling, and clarity checks, explaining why a correction is suggested.

This article is written for India on purpose: students, job seekers, professionals, and homemakers, Hindi, Hinglish, and regional language speakers learning English, a normal phone and laptop, and no assumption that you have a paid plan, a tutor, or a US account. It also carries a warning that matters for English learning more than for any other tool in this series: speaking costs money to run, so almost everyone caps speaking and gives away the rest, and the question worth asking is which part is capped, because a daily clock that resets, a trial that asks for a card before it starts, and an allowance that simply does not expire are three different things being called free.

## Duolingo, British Council, and Grammarly: habit, grammar, and writing

Duolingo is the habit app: every course is free, with energy limits, and no AI conversation on the free plan, with Super from 9.99 dollars on the US App Store, or from 199 rupees in India, and Max, with Video Call and Roleplay, above Super and mobile only. Its free plan corrects exercise answers, and its Explain My Answer feature, which gives detailed reasoning behind correct and incorrect responses, is now free for all users, while Roleplay feedback on accuracy and complexity is Max only. For an Indian beginner, Duolingo free is the best daily habit, because gamified lessons, streaks, and short exercises will get you to practise every day, but Duolingo free will not teach you to hold a conversation, because AI conversation is Max only, and a course app is not a speaking partner.

British Council LearnEnglish is the grammar library: 10,000-plus activities and exercises free, with a free account adding a level test and the community, and paid self-study, flexible live-class courses, and private tutors priced by country, with no AI conversation in the free part. Its grammar and vocabulary are explained properly by a teaching institution, not by an AI guessing, which makes it the best free resource for Indian students who need correct grammar for exams, jobs, or writing. Use British Council free for grammar and reading, and Duolingo free for a daily habit, because together they cover study, but neither covers speaking, and speaking needs a different tool.

Grammarly is the writing teacher: its free tier covers core grammar, spelling, and clarity checks, analysing context and explaining why a correction is suggested, turning everyday writing tasks into learning opportunities, which is often enough for essays, emails, and assignments. For Indian students and professionals who write emails, reports, or assignments in English, Grammarly free is the best writing tool, because it does not just fix your English, it teaches you why the fix is right, and that why is what builds grammar awareness over time. Use Grammarly free on every email and assignment, read its explanations, and do not just accept corrections blindly, because accepting without reading teaches you nothing.

## Fluvia, Gliglish, and ChatGPT voice: speaking free, with different caps

Fluvia is the most generous free speaking app in this article: spoken conversation with 18 AI voices in seven accents, correcting you as you speak, with conversations free with no expiry, no account, and no card, and listening tests, dictation, writing correction, idioms, phrasal verbs, and vocabulary unlimited, with paid plans from 5 dollars a month billed yearly. Its corrections happen the moment you slip, for grammar, vocabulary, and phrasing, and the mistakes you repeat come back, which makes Fluvia the best free speaking partner for Indian learners who freeze when someone asks them something, because it corrects mid-sentence rather than in a report at the end. For a learner who wants to speak every day without a daily clock, Fluvia free is the answer, because its conversations do not expire.

Gliglish is the no-sign-up speaking partner: 10 minutes a day, no sign-up, with feedback on the conversation, and pronunciation feedback paid, at 8 dollars a month or 49 dollars a year in USD everywhere. Ten minutes a day is enough for a short daily speaking rep, like ordering coffee, small talk, or a job interview question, but not enough for a long conversation, and Gliglish's daily clock resets, so you must practise every day to use it fully. ChatGPT voice mode and Gemini Live work the same way: voice on the free plan, with a limit OpenAI does not publish as a number, resetting every 24 hours, and Gemini Live free with a Google account, correcting whatever you ask for if you ask each session, with no memory of your mistakes, which means you must remind the assistant what to correct every session.

Use the speaking trio in a specific way: use Fluvia free for daily conversation without a clock, because it does not expire, use Gliglish for ten minutes a day with no account when you want a quick rep without signing up, and use ChatGPT voice or Gemini Live for open-ended conversation about your own day, because Gliglish and Fluvia are structured, while ChatGPT and Gemini will talk about anything. For Indian learners, speak about your own life, your job, and your city, because a conversation about your own day is harder than a scripted scenario, and harder practice builds real confidence faster than easy practice.

## ELSA, Speak, and the trial trap: pronunciation, scenarios, and cards

ELSA Speak is the pronunciation instrument: a pronunciation coach with sample conversations, limited daily lessons and sample AI conversations free, with a seven-day trial, and Premium at 19.99 dollars a month or from 71 dollars a year in the US, or from 499 rupees a month in India, correcting sounds and accent sound by sound, with grammar barely. Another source says ELSA's free tier offers unlimited phoneme-level pronunciation drills that pinpoint exactly which sounds you are mispronouncing, which disagrees with the limited daily lessons account, so this article states both: ELSA free is strong for pronunciation drills, but open conversation practice is locked behind a paywall, so treat it as a pronunciation gym rather than a chat partner. For Indian learners, ELSA's sound-by-sound correction is unmatched for specific sounds that Hindi speakers struggle with, like v versus w, or th sounds, because it pinpoints exactly which sound you mispronounced, not just that you sounded wrong.

Speak is the scenario app: a guided speaking course with AI, with sources disagreeing on its free tier, one saying a seven-day trial that asks for a payment method first with no permanent free tier, another saying a limited number of daily conversations free, enough to build a consistent habit, with Premium at 17.99 dollars a month or 83.99 dollars a year in the US, or from 1,199 rupees a month in India, correcting inside its lessons sentence by sentence. Another comparison says Speak's free tier gives about five to ten minutes of AI conversation across two to three short scenarios per day, with scripted conversations around fixed scenarios like ordering coffee, job interviews, and small talk, and no free-flowing chat about your own day. This article states the disagreement plainly: if Speak free gives you daily conversations, use them for short scenario reps, but if it asks for a card for a seven-day trial, do not enter a card unless you intend to pay, because a trial behind a card is not a free tier.

The trial trap is the most important warning in this article: three different things are being called free, and they are not the same. A permanent allowance with a daily cap, like Gliglish's ten minutes, TalkPal's ten minutes, ChatGPT's voice minutes, and HelloTalk's whole app. A trial that ends, like Speak's seven days behind a card, and Loora's short trial. A small free version you reach by closing the paywall, like Praktika's three tutors. And a free tier that is permanent for some features and capped for the expensive one, like the British Council, Duolingo, and Fluvia. Before you sign up, ask which part is capped, because speaking costs money to run, so almost everyone caps that and gives away the rest, and what differs is how honest the cap is about itself.

## Using English AI in India: Hindi speakers, exams, and jobs

Hindi speakers learning English face specific problems that AI tools can help with, and specific problems they cannot. Pronunciation problems, like v versus w, th sounds, and word stress, are best fixed by ELSA's sound-by-sound drills, because a human tutor may not pinpoint the exact sound, while ELSA does. Grammar problems, like articles, prepositions, and tenses, are best fixed by British Council explanations and Grammarly's why explanations, because a rule explained properly sticks better than a correction without a reason. Speaking confidence problems, like freezing when someone asks you something, are best fixed by daily speaking with Fluvia, Gliglish, or ChatGPT voice, because confidence comes from speaking, not from studying, and a learner who speaks ten minutes a day will outperform a learner who studies an hour a day but never speaks.

Exams in India, like IELTS, TOEFL, and job interviews, need English that is correct, fluent, and confident, and AI tools can help with each part, but not alone. For IELTS speaking, practise with Fluvia and ELSA daily, because IELTS speaking tests pronunciation, fluency, and coherence, and Fluvia corrects fluency while ELSA corrects pronunciation. For job interviews in English, practise behavioural answers out loud with ChatGPT voice or Gemini Live, because an interview answer you think is not an answer you can speak, and speaking out loud is the only way to find filler words and pauses before a real interviewer does. For writing exams, use Grammarly free on every practice essay, and read its explanations, because a writing score improves when you understand why a correction is right, not just when you accept it.

Jobs in India increasingly need English, and AI tools are the cheapest way to build it, but they are not a replacement for real conversation. HelloTalk, with most of the app free and real people as partners, gives you real conversation that AI cannot, because a human partner will correct what they choose, react naturally, and speak about real life, while an AI partner will correct what it is programmed to correct. Use HelloTalk free alongside AI tools, talking to real people weekly, because real conversation builds the stakes that build real confidence, and AI practice only rehearses you. For a learner who cannot afford a tutor, Preply's marketplace, with lessons from 3 to 40-plus dollars per 50-minute lesson, is the paid human option, but start free with AI and HelloTalk first, and pay for a tutor only when you need human feedback on a specific weakness.

## What to avoid

Do not enter a card for a trial unless you intend to pay, because Speak's seven-day trial asks for a payment method first, and a trial behind a card is not a free tier. Do not assume Duolingo free teaches conversation, because AI conversation is Max only, and Duolingo free is a course app, not a speaking partner. Do not use ELSA alone, because it corrects pronunciation sound by sound but barely teaches grammar, so pair it with British Council or Grammarly for grammar. Do not use ChatGPT voice without reminding it what to correct each session, because it has no memory of your mistakes, and a session without instructions is a chat, not a lesson. Do not trust a single source on free tiers, because sources disagree on ELSA's free drills and Speak's free tier, and free tiers change, so check the app's current free offer before you plan around it.

A final trap is studying English without speaking it. Beginners often complete Duolingo lessons daily, read British Council grammar, and feel they are learning, but they freeze when someone asks them something in English, because studying is not speaking, and a course app is not a conversation. Speak ten minutes a day, every day, with Fluvia, Gliglish, or ChatGPT voice, because ten minutes of speaking builds more confidence than an hour of studying, and confidence is what an interviewer, an examiner, or a customer hears, not your streak.
