For most Indian horticulturists, Plantix is the best place to start. It is free, works in Hindi and other Indian languages, and diagnoses pests, diseases and nutrient problems from a phone photo. Add Plant.id to identify unknown nursery plants, and consider Fasal only for high-value crops like grapes or pomegranate, where sensors can pay for themselves.

This is general information only, not professional agronomic advice. Confirm important decisions with a local agronomist, and re-check prices and limits on each tool's official page before paying.

## Plantix — free crop doctor

Plantix turns an Android phone into a crop doctor. Photograph a sick leaf and it suggests the likely pest, disease or nutrient deficiency within seconds. It covers about 30 major crops, including tomato, chilli and grapes. Sources disagree on coverage — one says over 400 problem classes, another says 780+ — so treat the number with caution. It is free, supports 19 languages, and most users are in India.

## Plant.id — identify any plant

Plant.id identifies plants from photos. Its web demo allows 10 free identifications per calendar month; paid plans start at about €5 a month for 50 identifications, or €35 a year for 600. It claims to recognise more than 35,000 taxa. For a nursery, it helps label unknown stock before advising care. Business use needs the paid Kindwise API.

## Fasal — AI irrigation for orchards

Fasal pairs on-farm IoT sensors with AI to tell you when and how much to irrigate. It tracks soil moisture, temperature and humidity, and its models cover grapes, pomegranate, tomato and chilli. It is a paid, hardware-first system: older reports put the device near ₹25,000–₹35,000 with a monthly fee of ₹500–₹750, so get a current quote first. It suits larger orchards, not small gardens.

## Using these tools in India

Use Plantix in your own language and photograph problems in daylight. Use Plant.id to catalogue nursery stock and answer customer questions with a name, not a guess. Photograph healthy stock too — clear listings sell faster. ## What to avoid

Do not spray a chemical on an app's word alone — confirm serious diagnoses with a local agronomist or agriculture university helpline. Do not trust one photo taken in poor light, and do not buy sensors before checking your crop and area are supported.

## FAQ

**Which AI tool is best for Indian horticulturists?** Plantix: it is free, works in Indian languages and focuses on the crops grown here. Use Plant.id alongside it.

**Can AI really diagnose plant disease from a photo?** It can suggest the likely problem, but light, blur and rare diseases reduce accuracy. Treat it as a first opinion, not a final one.

**Is Fasal worth it for a small nursery?** Probably not — the hardware and subscription suit larger orchards. Small growers get more value from the two free tools.

**Do these tools work without the internet?** Photo diagnosis needs a connection to send the image. Check offline claims before relying on them.

## Final verdict

Plantix first for diagnosis, Plant.id for plant ID, Fasal only for high-value irrigated crops.
