## The short answer

If you export from India, the most useful AI tools are not general chatbots. They are tools built around real trade data: which companies already import your product, what HS code it falls under, which documents customs will ask for, and what freight actually costs on your route. Start with an India-focused buyer-discovery tool such as XIMPEX or Eximium, use a freight-comparison platform such as Freightos or Shiprocket X to price the shipment, and use ChatGPT or DeepL only for drafting and translation — never as the final authority on a code, a duty, or a document.

That order matters. Most first-time exporters fail not because their product is bad, but because they chase the wrong buyers, quote without knowing the duty or freight, or send documents that do not match each other. A commercial invoice that says one quantity, a packing list that says another, and a shipping bill that says a third is exactly how a shipment gets held at customs. AI can draft all three in minutes, but a human — you, your customs house agent (CHA), or your freight forwarder — must check that every number agrees before anything moves.

One warning that applies to every tool below: prices, free limits, and features change often. This article reports what the official and comparison pages showed at research time, and where sources disagreed, it says so. Before you pay for anything, or promise a buyer a price or a delivery date based on a tool, re-check the official pricing and help page of that tool. An AI answer about a duty rate or a document rule is a starting point for verification, not a clearance.

## How to use these tools, in order

Do not buy five subscriptions on day one. Work through the export in the same order a real shipment follows.

First, confirm the product and the market. Enter your product or its HS code into a buyer-discovery tool and see which countries actually import it, and which companies buy it repeatedly. A country that imports a lot of your product is a better first target than a country you simply like the idea of selling to.

Second, verify the buyer before you spend anything on samples. Check that the company is real, that it buys your category, and that it is not on a sanctions list. Several tools below build this check in; if yours does not, ask your CHA or freight forwarder before shipping.

Third, quote properly. That means product price plus freight plus insurance plus duty at the destination, under the right Incoterm. An AI tool can draft the quotation, but the freight rate must come from a live quote and the duty from a current tariff source, not from a chatbot's memory.

Fourth, prepare documents that agree with each other. The usual set for an export from India includes the commercial invoice, packing list, shipping bill, bill of lading or airway bill, and, depending on the product and destination, a certificate of origin and inspection certificates. India's Foreign Trade Policy describes the bill of lading or airway bill, the commercial invoice cum packing list, and the shipping bill or bill of export as the mandatory documents, with other documents required as the case demands. Keep one master set of numbers — quantity, weight, value, HS code — and copy from it into every document.

Fifth, book freight and track it. Compare at least two options, confirm transit time and customs clearance responsibility in writing, and share tracking with the buyer. A shipment that arrives on time with documents that match is what turns a first order into a repeat order.

## XIMPEX: the India-first, end-to-end option

XIMPEX describes itself as a complete export solution for Indian manufacturers, powered by AI. Its pitch covers the whole journey: it reads trade data to suggest what to sell and where, finds overseas buyers, writes the first approach, and drafts documents through to shipping and payment. That breadth is its main appeal for a first-time exporter who does not yet have a CHA, a forwarder, and a buyer list.

In practice, the features worth testing first are the market finder, which uses years of trade data to show which countries buy your product and which are growing; the buyer finder, which returns real importers for your product rather than a generic company directory; and the document pack, which drafts the proforma, packing list, and certificate of origin. Its AI assistant, called AISHA, writes the first buyer approach in the buyer's language and follows the thread, and it also offers meeting preparation, live meeting translation, and AI minutes.

What to check before paying: XIMPEX's pages checked for this article did not publish a price list, so do not budget from a blog figure. Ask for a trial on your own product, check how many of its buyers are genuinely active importers in your category, and confirm whether freight, inspection, and CHA services are included or quoted separately. Use it for discovery and drafting; keep your CHA in the loop for the shipping bill and customs filing.

## Eximium: find importers who already buy what you sell

Eximium is built around one strong idea: stop guessing at cold leads and instead search shipment records to find overseas companies actively buying your product. It claims more than 80 million shipment records, and its workflow is to find verified active importers, give you an always-on storefront on its B2B marketplace so buyers can discover you, and generate export documents in minutes rather than hours.

Its AI trade advisor, EximExpert, answers questions on HS codes, duties, documentation rules, and country requirements, with answers it describes as grounded in real trade regulations rather than guessed. That is useful, but treat it the same way you would treat any AI advisor: use it to prepare questions and shortlists, then confirm the HS code and duty with your CHA or the destination country's tariff schedule before quoting.

Eximium suits an exporter who already has a product and a price, and whose main problem is a thin pipeline. If you have no buyers at all, the shipment-record search is the feature to test first. As with XIMPEX, pricing was not published on the pages checked, so ask for the current plan and what is included — buyer records, outreach, and documents are often sold separately even when a homepage presents them as one product.

## TREQ: India's trade gateway with AI search

TREQ presents itself as India's global trade gateway, connecting verified buyers and suppliers worldwide. Its headline claims, as shown on its site, include 12 high-growth sectors, more than 100 countries served, more than 14,000 verified exporters, and 2.4 billion dollars in trade facilitated. Treat such self-reported totals as marketing claims, not independently verified figures — but the underlying model is clear and practical: search products, meet trusted companies, and build B2B relationships across borders.

Its AI search lets you ask in plain language — for example, "find buyers of cotton towels in the USA" — and returns ranked, real trade-data matches with full company profiles you can save and contact. It also runs a hosted buyer programme that brings pre-qualified international delegations to Indian sellers, and a factory-visit route that invites buyers to your own production floor.

TREQ suits exporters who want verified connections and are willing to meet buyers, not only email them. Before paying, ask which sectors its buyer data is strongest in, whether the buyers in your category are verified and recently active, and what a delegation or factory visit actually costs. A marketplace profile is only worth paying for if buyers in your category genuinely use that marketplace.

## Vujis: buyer search plus AI cold emails

Vujis is built for Indian exporters who want to find verified global buyers without agents or exhibitions. You enter a product keyword, an HS code, or a company name, and it returns importers, distributors, and competitors already trading your product, with analytics on who buys what, from whom, in which countries, and in what volumes. It then gives you email addresses and phone numbers of decision-makers, and an AI cold-email generator that writes personalised outreach based on the buyer's activity, volumes, and import patterns.

Its scale claims are large — 3 billion buyer and supplier activity records, 650 million verified contacts, 195 countries, and 15 million companies — and again, these are the company's own figures, not independently audited numbers. What matters for you is narrower: in your product category, are the contacts real purchasing managers, and do the emails get replies? Test that with a small batch before buying a large plan.

Vujis suits an exporter comfortable with outbound selling: you research, you write, you follow up. Its deep-research view, which shows what an importer buys and which suppliers it already uses, is genuinely useful for writing a first email that does not sound like a mass blast. Mention the buyer's actual import pattern, your certification, your minimum order quantity, and your lead time — four facts that separate a serious supplier from spam.

## Torbi MSME: prospecting built for small Indian businesses

Torbi MSME positions itself as an AI prospecting teammate for small businesses, and it is the most MSME-friendly option in this list in how it talks about pricing: free to start, with no credit card, and pay on outcome rather than on monthly leads. Check the current terms on its official site before relying on that, because "pay on outcome" can mean different things — a fee per meeting, per order, or per verified lead — and the definition matters more than the slogan.

Its coverage is broader than only foreign importers. It matches you with foreign importers, large Indian enterprises, distributors and wholesalers, and government and public-sector buyers, ranked by who is buying right now. Outreach can be drafted in English, Hindi, Tamil, Gujarati, Marathi, Bengali, Telugu, and Malayalam, and delivered on email or WhatsApp, wherever buyers actually reply. It also bundles credibility checks and trade compliance help: buyer verification, matching your order to the right export scheme, letter-of-credit terms, HS code, and certifications, all in one place.

Torbi suits a small manufacturer — a handicraft unit, a food processor, a textile job-worker — that cannot afford a trade fair or a full-time export manager. Test it on one product, measure replies and meetings rather than leads delivered, and keep your own spreadsheet of every lead, as described in the marketplace section below.

## Federation Labs: AI agents for the compliance decisions

Federation Labs takes a different approach from the buyer-discovery tools. It organises every trade into five decisions — what the product is, where it comes from, what it will cost, whether it can move, and how to prove it with documents — and gives each decision its own AI agent. The agents find evidence and build the result, and people review and approve.

Concretely, that means an agent for HS classification, one for origin, one for duty and cost, one for regulation, and one for documents. The regulation agent checks strategic-goods controls, sanctioned parties, and import certification and inspection requirements, and flags items when rules change. The documents agent then builds invoices, packing lists, certificates of origin, and declarations from those decisions, cross-checking quantities, weights, and amounts, and flagging only what does not match.

This is the tool to look at when your product is compliance-heavy — chemicals, food, medical goods, electronics, or anything with certification, testing, or restricted-party risk — or when you ship to many countries with different rules. It is not a buyer-finding tool, and it does not replace a licensed customs broker: its own description keeps humans in review and approval. Ask for a demo on your actual product and destination, and confirm whether sanctions and credit checks on counterparties are included in the plan you are quoted.

## Freightos: compare freight rates before you commit

Freightos is a freight platform that lets shippers compare and book across many forwarders. Its AI tools for forwarders read unstructured enquiries — pasted packing lists, foreign-language PDFs, or customer emails — match commodities, ports, and container dimensions automatically, and generate branded quotes with pre-set profit markups. For an exporter, the practical value is simpler: search a route, compare options side by side, and see spot rates against contract tariffs without collecting quotes one forwarder at a time.

A third-party comparison reports that Freightos is free to compare rates, with roughly a 3 percent platform or booking fee on top of the carrier rate, and no subscription. Treat that as a third-party report, not Freightos's own published price list, and confirm the fee on the official site before quoting a buyer. Fees, fuel surcharges, and destination charges change, and a rate that looks cheap at origin can become expensive at destination if customs, handling, or last-mile delivery is excluded.

Freightos suits exporters who ship regularly enough to care about rate comparison, but not so much that they need a dedicated forwarder account team. Always confirm in writing what the quoted rate includes: origin handling, ocean or air freight, destination handling, customs clearance, and delivery to the buyer's door are often separate lines.

## Flexport: a full-service forwarder with AI built in

Flexport is a freight forwarder and customs broker with its own software platform for ocean, air, and trucking shipments, customs, and fulfilment. Its AI now reaches beyond that platform: an assistant connection lets your own AI assistant track shipments, filter exceptions and demurrage risk, search contract and spot rates, browse quotes, and create bookings, while AI agents behind the scenes handle quoting, rate sheets, and vessel-change updates. Its AI customs tools draft tariff codes and check entries against your rules, with a licensed broker still confirming every code.

The honest limit is pricing. Flexport does not publish a software price list; freight, customs brokerage, and fulfilment are quoted for your shipments and lanes. A third-party comparison puts the practical minimum spend for meaningful service at around 5,000 dollars a month, and describes Flexport as designed for shippers with recurring, predictable volume. That figure is a third-party estimate, not Flexport's published rate, so treat it as a warning sign, not a quotation: if you ship one container a quarter, Flexport is probably not your first call.

Flexport suits an established exporter with steady volume who wants one accountable partner for freight, customs, and tracking, with AI reducing the email-chasing. If you are at that stage, ask whether AI features, customs classification, and tracking carry separate fees or come with your forwarding services, and get the answer in writing.

## Shiprocket X: cross-border shipping from India for small shipments

Shiprocket X is the international arm of Shiprocket, aimed at Indian e-commerce sellers and small exporters. It offers shipping to more than 220 countries and territories, with air and ocean options, door-to-door B2B delivery, and help with export documentation, duties, tariffs, and tracking. For a small seller, that combination — courier network plus paperwork help plus a dashboard — is often more practical than negotiating directly with an international courier.

Its pricing needs a careful read, because its own pages disagree. Its cross-border page advertises shipping worldwide starting at ₹230 per 50 g, while another page on the same site says rates start at ₹306 per 50 g. Both figures cannot be the current starting rate for the same service, so do not quote a buyer from this article: check the live rate for your product, weight, and destination on Shiprocket X itself. Delivery promises also vary by service — for example, economy shipping to the US, UK, and Canada in 12 to 15 business days, and duty-paid shipping to the US in 10 to 12 days — so match the service to the buyer's deadline, not to the cheapest headline.

Shiprocket X suits handicrafts, apparel, jewellery, Ayurvedic products, and other small-parcel exports, and it is the natural next step if you already sell online in India. For bulk or container cargo, compare it with Freightos or a forwarder rather than assuming a parcel rate scales.

## ChatGPT and DeepL: drafting and translation, done honestly

ChatGPT and DeepL are not export tools, but almost every exporter ends up using them, so their limits deserve honest reporting. ChatGPT's free tier, according to OpenAI's own help centre, gives free users access to its current default model with unlimited everyday text chats, subject to abuse-prevention safeguards, plus web search, file and image uploads, data analysis, and image creation — but file uploads, image generation, voice, and data analysis have separate usage limits, and you will be told in the product when you reach one. Use it to draft buyer emails, quotations, product descriptions, packing checklists, and follow-up sequences, and to brainstorm which markets to research next.

DeepL is the better choice when translation quality matters — a buyer email, a product specification, or a document that must read naturally in the buyer's language. Its free plan, as shown on DeepL's own comparison table and reported by independent comparisons, covers 50,000 characters a month and 1 file translation a month up to 5 MB, with one glossary. Paid plans raise those limits, but the file count can run out before the character allowance if you translate many documents, so check the file limit first if documents are your main use.

The rule for both: never let either tool be the last word on a fact that costs money. If ChatGPT gives you an HS code, a duty rate, a certification requirement, or a document rule, verify it against the tariff schedule, the buyer's requirement, or your CHA. If DeepL translates a contract clause, have a human who knows the language and the trade terms read it. Drafting is where these tools save hours; verification is where you protect the shipment.

## IndiaMART and Alibaba: marketplaces that bring buyers to you

Buyer-discovery tools are outbound — you find buyers. Marketplaces are inbound — buyers find you. IndiaMART, India's largest online B2B marketplace, connects a very large number of buyers with sellers and offers a free listing in a few steps; Alibaba gives Indian sellers access to a global buyer base. TradeIndia and ExportersIndia are other Indian options in the same model. Most platforms offer a free basic listing and paid tiers that improve ranking, add verification badges, or give access to buyer requests.

The marketplace game is different from cold outreach, and the discipline is measurement. Start with a free or entry-level listing with complete, accurate product pages: HS code, material specifications, dimensions in metric and imperial, certifications such as ISO, CE, FSSAI, APEDA, or GOTS where they apply, and a clear minimum order quantity. Then, for four to eight weeks, log every enquiry with country, whether it is genuinely export or domestic, company name, whether the company is real and buys your category, how specific the requirement is, and whether it reached a quotation or sample stage. That log tells you whether a paid tier is earning its fee; enquiry volume alone does not, because a hundred vague domestic enquiries are worth less than three real export buyers.

Marketplaces suit almost every exporter as a second channel, alongside a discovery tool. They do not suit a seller who lists once and waits: buyers reply fastest to suppliers who answer within hours, with photos, certifications, and a clear price basis. If you cannot respond quickly, fix that before paying for ranking.

## Using these tools in India: the paperwork that AI cannot skip

AI can draft documents, but it cannot give you the legal right to export. Before your first shipment, confirm the basics with official sources. The Importer-Exporter Code (IEC) from the Directorate General of Foreign Trade (DGFT) is mandatory for exports and imports; since GST, the IEC number is the same as the PAN of the firm, but it is still separately issued by DGFT. Customs filing in India runs through ICEGATE and your CHA, and the shipping bill is the customs document that records the export. Your bank also matters: after shipment, documents are generally presented to the bank within 21 days for payment processing under the relevant arrangement, such as a letter of credit or collection.

A practical India checklist: IEC from DGFT; GST registration and a current account for drawback credits where applicable; a CHA or forwarder who files on ICEGATE; a master document set with matching quantity, weight, value, and HS code; certificates that apply to your product — for example, FSSAI for food, APEDA registration for scheduled agricultural products, or a certificate of origin where the buyer or destination requires it; and a payment term agreed before shipping, not after. AI tools help you prepare each item faster, but the IEC, the shipping bill, and the bank presentation are official steps, and no AI output substitutes for them.

Also, keep your data discipline tight. Do not paste a buyer's commercially sensitive pricing, a full customer list, or unpublished product formulations into a free AI tool without checking its data-use terms. Paid plans often promise not to train on your text; free plans may differ. When in doubt, anonymise the example and keep the real numbers in your own quotation sheet.

## What to avoid

Avoid paying for a buyer list before you have tested it on your own product. A list of ten thousand "importers" is worthless if none of them buys your category, and several tools in this space sell access, not outcomes. Ask for a sample search on your product, check three companies yourself, and only then pay.

Avoid quoting from an AI answer. If a chatbot gives you a duty rate, a freight rate, or a certification rule, and you put it in a quotation without checking, you own the error when the buyer is charged more at destination. Get freight in writing from the forwarder or platform, and duty from the current tariff source or your CHA.

Avoid sending documents that were generated separately and never reconciled. Generate the invoice, packing list, and shipping-bill data from one master sheet, and read all three side by side before filing. A single mismatched weight or value can hold a shipment longer than any tool saves.

Avoid the cheapest-freight trap. A rate that excludes destination handling, customs clearance, or last-mile delivery is not cheaper; it is incomplete. Compare door-to-door totals, transit times, and who clears customs, not headline origin rates.

Avoid sharing sensitive commercial data with free AI tools without reading their terms, and avoid promising a buyer a delivery date that depends on a tool's estimate rather than a booked shipment. Tools estimate; carriers and customs decide.

Avoid one more common mistake: buying every tool at once. Pick one buyer-discovery tool, one freight option, and one drafting and translation pair. Run them on one real product for one month, measure replies, quotations, samples, and shipments, and only then add a second tool. Exporting rewards a working pipeline, not a large subscription stack.

## FAQ

**Which AI tool is best for finding export buyers from India?**

For most Indian manufacturers, start with XIMPEX or Eximium, because both are built around real trade data and Indian exporters rather than generic company directories. Test each on your own product: search your HS code or product, check whether the importers it returns genuinely buy your category, and measure replies before paying. TREQ and Vujis are strong alternatives if you want verified delegations or deep contact data, and Torbi MSME is worth testing first if you are a small business with a tight budget.

**Do I still need a customs house agent if I use AI export tools?**

Yes, for any serious shipment. AI tools draft documents and suggest codes, but the shipping bill, customs filing on ICEGATE, and destination clearance still need a licensed CHA or forwarder, and a licensed broker must confirm tariff codes. Use AI to prepare faster and to ask better questions, and use your CHA to file correctly.

**What documents do I need to export from India?**

The usual set includes the commercial invoice, packing list, shipping bill, and bill of lading or airway bill, plus a certificate of origin and inspection certificates where the product, buyer, or destination requires them. India's Foreign Trade Policy lists the bill of lading or airway bill, the commercial invoice cum packing list, and the shipping bill or bill of export as mandatory, with other documents required as the case demands. Confirm the exact set for your product and destination with your CHA before shipping.

**Can AI tools really write export documents I can use?**

They can draft them well, and tools such as XIMPEX, Eximium, and Federation Labs are built for exactly that, including cross-checks between documents. But a draft is not a filing. Check that quantity, weight, value, and HS code match across every document, confirm the buyer's and destination's special requirements, and have your CHA review the set before submission.

**How much does it cost to start using AI export tools?**

You can start free or nearly free: ChatGPT's free tier for drafting, DeepL's free plan for limited translation, a free basic marketplace listing, and free freight-rate comparison. Buyer-discovery and compliance platforms are mostly quote-based, and their homepages often do not publish prices, so budget only after a trial and a written quotation. Shiprocket X advertises small-parcel rates from ₹230 per 50 g on one page and ₹306 on another, which is why you should always check the live rate for your shipment.

**Is it safe to share my product and buyer details with AI tools?**

Share carefully. Avoid pasting unpublished prices, full customer lists, or sensitive formulations into free tools without checking their data-use terms, because free and paid plans often differ on whether your text is used for training. Anonymise examples where you can, and keep real commercial numbers in your own quotation sheet.

**Which tool is best for small-parcel exports from India?**

For small parcels and e-commerce exports, Shiprocket X is the most India-specific option, with shipping to more than 220 countries and documentation help. Compare its live rate and delivery time against an international courier for your destination, and for bulk or container cargo, compare Freightos or a forwarder instead of scaling a parcel rate.

**Can I rely on an AI tool for HS codes and duty rates?**

No, not alone. AI tools such as EximExpert and Federation Labs are good for shortlisting codes and estimating duties, but HS classification and duty depend on the exact product, its composition, and the destination's current tariff schedule. Confirm the final code and rate with your CHA or the official tariff source before quoting, because a wrong code can mean a wrong duty, a delay, or a penalty.

## Final verdict

Start with XIMPEX or Eximium to find real buyers, compare shipping on Freightos or Shiprocket X, and never ship on an AI document alone.
