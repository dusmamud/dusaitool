The tool that gives role-specific mock interviews does not coach your delivery, and the tool that coaches your delivery does not judge whether your answer is factually strong. The free stack that actually works is a combination, not a single coach.

## Final Round AI: the strongest free tier for serious candidates

On the free plan, you get access to AI Mock Interview with a set of practice sessions covering behavioural, technical, and role-specific questions, with question banks for common roles including Software Engineer, Product Manager, Data Analyst, and Marketing. You can run complete mock interviews, not just individual questions, receive answer feedback with specific suggestions, and review transcripts of your sessions afterward.

What makes Final Round AI different from every other tool on this list is the data behind it: the question bank is built from real interview patterns observed across hundreds of thousands of sessions with users who were preparing for or actively in interviews at companies like Amazon, Google, Meta, Microsoft, and Stripe. When you practise a behavioural question tagged Amazon Leadership Principles, that question reflects what Amazon interviewers actually ask, not what a writer assumed they might ask. For Indian candidates targeting Amazon, Google, Meta, Microsoft, or Stripe, this is the closest free mock to the real interview, because the questions come from real patterns, not generic lists.

The free tier's limits are session volume and live support: advanced features like the full Interview CoPilot, real-time live interview support, and unlimited session history require a paid plan, and the free tier is capped in session volume, which means it works well for early-stage prep but not for the high-repetition final week before a target interview. Entry pricing is around 25 dollars a month billed annually, 300 dollars a year, with monthly shown at checkout higher, so plan your free sessions for early prep, and pay only in the final week if you need unlimited sessions and live support, because paying for a month you do not use is worse than practising free for a month you do use.

Use Final Round AI free in a specific way: pick your exact role, run complete mock interviews, not just single questions, and read your session transcripts after each mock, because transcripts show you what you actually said, not what you thought you said, and structure scoring shows you whether your answer had a beginning, a middle, and a result. For Indian candidates, practise behavioural questions in English even if your first language is Hindi, because most Indian company interviews are in English, and a Hindi answer that is strong in Hindi may sound weak in English if you have not practised it in English.

## Yoodli, LinkedIn, and Huru: delivery, behaviour, and real job posts

Yoodli is delivery coaching: it analyses speech patterns including pace, filler words, eye contact via webcam, and answer length, with a free tier that allows around three sessions a month on one account, or five lifetime roleplays on another account, with Pro at 8 dollars a month billed annually, 96 dollars a year, or 11 dollars monthly. It does not evaluate whether your answer is factually strong or role-relevant, so it is best used alongside a content-focused tool like Final Round AI, where Final Round judges your content and Yoodli judges your delivery. For Indian candidates, Yoodli's filler-word and pacing feedback is especially useful, because filler words like um, uh, and actually are more common when you are translating from Hindi to English in your head, and Yoodli shows you the pattern so you can reduce it.

LinkedIn Interview Prep is free for all LinkedIn members, covering behavioural questions organised by theme such as leadership, conflict, and motivation, showing sample answers from other professionals, and letting you record a video response to review yourself. It does not give automated feedback on your content, only on pacing if you use the teleprompter feature, which means you must judge your own progress, but its sample answers from real professionals are useful for Indian candidates who have never seen a strong behavioural answer. Use LinkedIn free to learn what a good answer looks like, then practise that answer in Final Round AI, where you get structure scoring, and in Yoodli, where you get delivery feedback.

Huru is video-first behavioural practice from a real job post, with a free trial giving a five-question session and a report, Starter at 24.99 dollars a month, and Growth at 99 dollars a year, about 8.25 dollars a month. Huru's strength is that it practises you on the actual job post you are applying for, not on generic questions, which makes it useful for a specific Indian job you are targeting, like a data analyst role at a specific company. Use Huru's free trial for one target job, with its five-question session and report, and decide whether video-first practice helps you, because a five-question trial is enough to judge a tool, but not enough to prepare for an interview.

## The Google Interview Warmup disagreement, stated plainly

Google Interview Warmup was the most accessible tool on older lists: there was no account to create, no email required, and no paywall, and you opened the site, picked a job category, and started answering questions out loud, with the tool transcribing your speech in real time and surfacing observations about your answer, like which talking points you covered, which you missed, filler word frequency, and how often you answered directly versus circled around the point. Google built it for candidates preparing for jobs in data analytics, e-commerce, IT support, project management, UX design, and general interview situations, with question sets that were solid for these tracks, like SQL query optimisation, stakeholder communication, and data storytelling for data analysts.

A source verified in October 2026 says Google retired Interview Warmup in April 2026 without a prominent announcement, with the old address now redirecting to a general interview-tips article on Grow with Google, and the last live capture of the tool dating to March 2026. Older guides still list it as completely free with no login, which is why this article states the disagreement instead of picking the nicer number: if you open Interview Warmup today and it works, it is the best no-login warmup, but if it redirects to a tips article, it is retired, and you should use Yoodli free or Huru's trial instead. For Indian first-timers, this matters because Warmup was the only no-login tool, and its absence means every current free tool needs an account, a session limit, or a trial, so plan for an account from the start.

The lesson from Warmup's retirement is broader: free interview tools can disappear without notice, and your prep should not depend on one tool. Practise in Final Round AI, Yoodli, and LinkedIn together, save your transcripts and reports, and do not assume a free tool will still be free, or still be live, in the final week before your interview, because a tool that disappears the week before your interview is worse than a tool that was never free.

## Using interview AI in India: language, roles, and the final week

Language for Indian interviews is English for most companies, Hindi for some government and local roles, and Hinglish for informal conversation, and AI interview tools are best in English. Practise behavioural answers in English even if you think in Hindi, because translating in your head during an interview causes filler words, pauses, and circling around the point, which Yoodli will show you and Final Round will score you down for. Record yourself answering in English, listen to the recording, and notice where you pause, because a pause you hear in a recording is a pause an interviewer will hear in a real interview, and practising the same answer three times in English will reduce those pauses more than any AI feedback will.

Roles for Indian interview prep are software engineer, product manager, data analyst, marketing, and general roles, and each needs a different tool mix: software engineers need Final Round AI for technical and behavioural questions plus Interviewing.io for real-person mocks if they can get a session, product managers need Final Round AI for PM-specific questions, data analysts need Final Round AI for data questions plus LinkedIn for behavioural themes, and marketing roles need Final Round AI for marketing questions plus Yoodli for delivery. Do not practise generic questions for a specific role, because a generic question bank will not ask you about SQL optimisation if you are a data analyst, or about Amazon Leadership Principles if you are targeting Amazon, and Final Round's role-specific banks are built for that specificity.

The final week before a target interview is where free tiers fail, because Final Round free is capped in session volume, Yoodli free is capped at around three sessions a month or five lifetime roleplays, and Huru free is a single five-question trial. Plan your free sessions for early prep, and save your paid week, if you pay at all, for the final week, when high-repetition practice matters most, because a paid month in the final week is worth more than a paid month three months before, when you are still learning what to practise. For the final week, practise complete mocks daily, review transcripts nightly, and sleep well, because a rested candidate with ten mocks will outperform a tired candidate with thirty mocks.

## What to avoid

Do not plan your prep around Google Interview Warmup, because an October 2026 verified source says it retired in April 2026, and older guides listing it as live are outdated. Do not use Yoodli alone, because it analyses delivery but does not evaluate whether your answer is factually strong or role-relevant, so pair it with Final Round AI for content. Do not use LinkedIn alone, because it gives no automated feedback on content, only pacing with the teleprompter, so pair it with Final Round for structure scoring. Do not rely on Interviewing.io free as a plan, because its free tier is limited and waitlisted, and a waitlist is not a prep plan. Do not pay for Interview Sidekick's two-minute trial as if it were a free tier, because two minutes is not prep, and its paid tier starts around 80 dollars a month, which is the most expensive tool in this article.

A final trap is practising answers without speaking them out loud. Beginners often read questions, think of answers, and feel prepared, but an answer you think is not an answer you can speak, and an interviewer hears your spoken answer, not your thought answer. Speak every practise answer out loud, record it, and listen to it, because a spoken answer with filler words, pauses, and circling is a weak answer, even if the thought behind it was strong, and speaking out loud is the only way to find those weaknesses before a real interviewer does.
