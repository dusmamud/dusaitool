A daycare or creche does not need the fanciest artificial intelligence on the market. It needs four things to work every single day without fail: children signed in and out safely, staff knowing who is responsible for which room, parents getting a short honest update before they worry, and records that can be found again next week. The tools below are judged on exactly those four jobs, for Indian centres, not on how impressive their demos look.

The short answer is this. If you run a small centre and money is tight, start with the free WhatsApp Business app plus Google Sheets and Google Forms, and use a free chatbot only to draft messages, menus and activity plans. If you run a centre with fifty or more children, or more than one branch, look seriously at Illumine, an India-based childcare management platform, and compare it with Brightwheel and Procare before you pay anything. Do not put a child's full name, photograph, medical detail or home address into a public chatbot. That one rule matters more than any feature list in this guide.

## Illumine: the India-first option to check first

Illumine describes itself as AI-powered childcare management software, built for attendance, observations, parent communication, enrolment and fee collection in one place. Its public site lists contactless attendance with QR ID cards and kiosk codes, leave and time-off tracking, student-staff ratio tracking, late check-out and early check-in fees, geofencing and e-signatures. For parent communication it lists AI-assisted activity updates and daily reports, media sharing, real-time translation in more than twenty languages, drop-off and pick-up management, medical requests, AI-powered newsletters, digital surveys and consent forms. Its listed business address locality is Bangalore, India, and it has Android and iOS parent apps.

Why this matters for an Indian centre: translation alone solves a real daily problem. A centre in Bengaluru, Mumbai or Delhi often serves families who speak different home languages, and a short update that a parent can actually read is worth more than a long update in English only. QR check-in also solves the most stressful moment of the day, the gate, because the record shows who collected the child and when, instead of relying on memory at 6 pm.

What to check before paying: Illumine says its pricing depends on centre size and needs, across Standard, Business and Enterprise plans, with no hidden fees claimed. It does not publish a single simple price on the page returned in our research, so treat any number quoted by a reseller or a blog as unverified. Book the demo, ask for the price for your exact number of children and staff, ask what happens to your data if you cancel, and ask how photos are stored and who can download them. Prices and limits change, so re-check the official pricing and help pages before paying or promising a deadline to parents.

Best for: centres that want one system for attendance, parent updates and fees, and that serve multilingual families.

## Brightwheel: strong all-rounder, but built for another market

Brightwheel is a well-known childcare platform whose pricing page lists billing and payments, parent and staff messaging, activity and photo sharing, newsletters, student check-in and check-out, ratio tracking, attendance reporting, custom forms, lesson plans, observations, assessments, admissions and waitlists, staff scheduling, time tracking and payroll. Its free plan covers daily updates with photos only, while its premium plan adds videos, newsletters, emergency SMS alerts and prospective-family messaging, according to its own comparison page.

That breadth is genuinely useful. A centre that uses Brightwheel well can replace a paper register, a photo group, a fee spreadsheet and a newsletter tool with one login. The caution for India is just as real. Brightwheel is built around the United States childcare system, including subsidy management and tax reporting that do not map neatly onto Indian fee structures, and its paid pricing is custom rather than a single published Indian price. Before committing, check whether fee collection works smoothly with the payment methods your parents actually use, such as UPI, and whether support replies in your working hours.

Best for: larger centres that want one polished system and are willing to test whether its billing fits Indian practice.

## Procare: the operations-heavy choice

Procare, which absorbed the Kinderlime app, is pitched as a complete childcare management system covering registration, attendance, schedules, family and child records, staff management, billing and payments, and family engagement. Its Play Store listing for India describes contactless sign-in and sign-out with QR codes or geolocation, a parent kiosk with a four-digit PIN, staff timecards, real-time ratio tracking, photo and video sharing, daily sheets for diapers, bottles, naps, meals and bathroom visits, incident reports with parent signatures, and billing from the app, with an offline mode for attendance.

Procare also advertises AI translation of messages in its childcare app, so a parent can read a teacher's message in their preferred language. That is a small feature with a large effect on trust. Procare claims its own comparison that two out of three centres that use software choose Procare, but that is the vendor's own marketing claim, not an independent survey, so treat it as a claim to weigh, not a fact to repeat to parents.

The caution is the same as with Brightwheel. Procare is a United States platform with United States licensing and accounting assumptions, including QuickBooks integration. Indian centres should test enrolment, fee receipts and reports against their own needs, and confirm pricing for their size rather than assuming a blog's figure is current. Prices and limits change, so re-check the official pricing and help pages before paying or promising a deadline.

Best for: centres that care most about tight operations, ratios, incident records and staff timekeeping.

## ChatGPT and Gemini: draft with them, never store children in them

ChatGPT and Gemini are general chatbots, not daycare software, and that distinction should decide how you use them. They are excellent for drafting a weekly menu in simple language, turning a rough activity idea into a step-by-step plan, rewriting a polite fee reminder, or creating a rainy-day activity list for three-year-olds. They are not a safe place for a child's name, photograph, allergy list, medical note or pickup password.

Their free limits are also moving targets, and sources disagree on the details, which is itself useful information. One comparison checked in October 2026 describes ChatGPT free as offering everyday text chats without a fixed message cap, with uploads, image creation, voice and deep research limited but with no numbers published. The same comparison describes Gemini free as having standard usage limits that refresh every five hours up to a weekly cap, with no usage numbers published, and heavier features using the allowance faster. A separate review describes free ChatGPT as holding roughly 27,000 tokens of context and free Gemini as holding roughly 32,000 tokens, while noting that Gemini's free tier had moved to its smallest model for most replies. Do not build a rota or a deadline around any of these numbers. Re-check the official pages on the day you depend on them, because free limits change every few weeks.

Practical rules that keep a centre safe: draft in general terms, such as "plan a 30-minute rainy-day activity for four-year-olds using paper cups and crayons", never "plan an activity for Aarav, who has asthma and...". Read every AI plan before using it, because chatbots invent facts, including unsafe instructions and wrong allergy advice. Never copy an AI answer about a child's health, medicine or injury into a parent message without checking it against your centre's own policy and, where needed, a qualified professional. Keep a human in charge of every message that goes to a parent.

Best for: drafting, brainstorming and rewriting, in general terms only.

## Canva: notices parents actually read

Canva Free is available to anyone, according to Canva's own India pricing page, and it includes one brand kit limited to three colours plus AI usage. Its free tier allows up to twenty uses of standard or premium AI tools, with no access to ultra AI tools, while Canva Pro is listed at 144 US dollars per year for one person on that page. K-12 educational organisations and not-for-profits can get most premium features free through Canva Education, but a private daycare should not assume it qualifies, so check the eligibility requirements first.

For a daycare, Canva's real value is not fancy design. It is a clean holiday notice, a readable weekly menu, a simple admission poster and a consistent look across everything parents see. Use the same colours and the same layout every time, keep text large, and never put a child's photograph in a public poster without written parental consent. Batch your AI-assisted designs, because the free AI allowance runs out quickly if staff experiment all month.

Best for: notices, menus, posters and parent handouts that look trustworthy.

## WhatsApp Business: where parents already are

Parents in India already check WhatsApp, which is why the free WhatsApp Business app remains the most practical parent channel for a small centre. It gives a business profile, quick replies for repeated questions, labels to sort families, and away messages for evenings. The key safety upgrade is to stop using one giant parent group, where every parent sees every other parent's phone number, and instead message families individually or through broadcast lists with consent.

Larger centres may outgrow the free app, which works for one person on one phone. The WhatsApp Business API lets a whole office team reply from one number, run auto-replies and approved broadcasts, and connect to other tools, but it costs money per conversation and requires approved message templates and opt-in from parents. Third-party guides quote utility message prices in paise per message and monthly platform fees, but those figures differ between providers and change with Meta's rate card, so re-check the official pricing before budgeting. The rule that never changes is consent first: message only parents who agreed to hear from you, keep messages useful and occasional, and never use a broadcast for a child's private information.

Best for: daily communication for small centres, and a shared office inbox for larger ones.

## Notion: the staff handbook that does not live in one person's head

Notion's free plan is listed at zero dollars per member per month and includes a trial of Notion AI, basic forms, basic sites, Notion Calendar and databases. Its Plus plan is listed at ten dollars per member per month and also includes only a trial of Notion AI, so do not buy Notion expecting unlimited AI. For a daycare, Notion works best as a boring, reliable home for the staff handbook, room checklists, allergy-aware meal notes written in general terms, incident-report templates and onboarding steps for new staff.

Keep children's private records out of Notion unless you have checked its access controls, sharing settings and your own data policy. A handbook anyone on staff can open is good. A file of children's medical details that any ex-employee can still open is a serious failure. Review who has access whenever someone leaves.

Best for: handbooks, checklists, templates and shared staff knowledge.

## The free backbone: Google Sheets and Google Forms

Before buying anything, a small centre can run surprisingly well on Google Sheets for attendance and fees, Google Forms for admission enquiries and consent, and Google Drive for policies. The weakness is human error: one overwritten formula, one shared link sent to the wrong person, or one staff phone holding the only copy. Set one owner for each sheet, limit sharing to named staff, turn on version history awareness, and move to dedicated childcare software once the centre grows past what a spreadsheet can safely hold.

## Using these tools in India, day by day

Morning gate: use QR or PIN check-in where you have it, and keep a paper backup for the day the internet fails. A system that stops working during a power cut is not a safety system. Mid-morning: staff log meals, naps and activities in short notes, not essays, because parents trust a short true update more than a long polished one. Afternoon: send one daily summary per child, with a photo only where consent exists, and keep group photos out of public channels. Evening: the manager checks ratios, unresolved incidents and tomorrow's rota before closing, rather than discovering a gap at 8 am.

Fees deserve the same honesty. Tell parents the fee, the due date and the payment method in one short message, give a proper receipt, and keep fee chasing private. Never discuss one family's fees in a group. For staff, publish the rota at least a week ahead where possible, record leave in the same place as the rota, and keep a list of trusted backup staff for sick days. The best AI tool in this guide cannot fix a rota that only exists in the manager's head.

Language matters too. If your families speak Hindi, Tamil, Bengali, Marathi or another language at home, use the translation features in Illumine or Procare, or draft in simple Hindi or English and have a staff member check it, rather than sending a long English notice that half your parents cannot use. Keep medical and safety words exact in translation, and have a human verify them, because a mistranslated allergy note is dangerous.

## What to avoid

Avoid putting children's full names, photos, medical details, addresses or pickup passwords into public chatbots, public social media or shared AI tools. Avoid buying software on the basis of a blog's price, because prices and free limits change, and the official pricing and help pages must be re-checked before paying or promising a deadline. Avoid tools that demand a VPN to work in India, because staff will stop using them, and every tool in this guide is meant to work in India without one, though you should still test it on your own phones and network before committing.

Avoid the trap of collecting data you do not need. If a form asks for a child's blood group, caste, religion or other sensitive detail that your centre has no reason to hold, remove the question. Avoid keeping children's photos forever; set a retention rule, delete old photos on a schedule, and tell parents the rule. Avoid letting one staff member hold the only password, the only admin login or the only copy of the register. Avoid AI-generated safety, medical or legal advice: an AI answer is not a doctor, a lawyer or your licensing authority, and it must never override your centre's written policy.

Finally, avoid announcing a new system to parents before staff have practised with it for a week. A parent app that staff cannot use confidently creates more worry than a simple WhatsApp message sent on time.

## FAQ

**Which AI tool is best for a small daycare in India?**
For a small centre, the best starting combination is the free WhatsApp Business app plus Google Sheets, with ChatGPT or Gemini used only for drafting. If you want one dedicated system, test Illumine first, because it is India-based and covers attendance, parent communication and fees in one place. Start free, prove the daily routine works, and only then pay for a platform.

**Can a daycare use ChatGPT or Gemini for children's records?**
No. Do not enter a child's name, photograph, medical detail, address or pickup information into a public chatbot. Use chatbots only for general drafting, such as menus, activity plans and polite reminders, written without identifying any child. Children's records belong in a controlled system with named access, not in a chat window.

**Is Illumine really made for India?**
Illumine's public site lists a Bangalore locality and India-focused childcare features, including QR attendance, ratio tracking and multilingual parent communication, which fit Indian centres well. That does not mean it fits every centre. Test it with your own staff, your own fee structure and your own parents before paying, and confirm the current price for your size.

**Do Brightwheel and Procare work in India?**
Their apps are available on Indian app stores and their core features, such as attendance, messaging and photo sharing, work anywhere with internet. However, both are United States platforms, so their billing, subsidy and tax features may not fit Indian practice. Test fee collection with UPI and your own receipt format, and check support hours, before committing.

**What is the cheapest safe way to update parents daily?**
Individual WhatsApp messages or broadcast lists with parental consent, plus one short daily summary per child, are the cheapest reliable option. Keep messages short, send photos only with consent, never share private details in a group, and keep a copy of what was sent. A free tool used consistently beats an expensive platform that staff avoid.

**How should a creche handle pick-up safety?**
Use an authorised pickup list, check identity at the gate, and record who collected each child and when, using QR, PIN or a signed register. Never hand a child to an unknown person on the basis of a phone call alone. Have a written policy for late pickups, unknown collectors and emergencies, and make sure every staff member, including substitutes, knows it.

**Can AI write our activity plans and menus?**
Yes, with a human check. Ask for general plans by age group, check every activity for safety, allergies, choking hazards and age suitability, and adapt it to your rooms and staff. Never follow an AI suggestion about food, medicine or first aid without checking it against professional guidance and your centre's policy.

**What should we check before paying for daycare software?**
Check the price for your exact number of children and staff, what happens to your data if you cancel, who can see and download photos, whether fee collection supports your parents' payment methods, whether support replies in your hours, and whether the system works during an internet outage. Ask for a trial with your real daily routine, not just a sales demo.

**Do we need parental consent to share photos?**
Yes, in practice you should always get clear written consent before sharing a child's photo, even inside a private parent app, and separate consent for any public use such as social media or posters. Record what each parent agreed to, respect a refusal without argument, and never use a child's photo in marketing without explicit permission.

**What if parents speak different languages?**
Use short, simple messages and a translation feature you have tested, such as the translation options in Illumine or Procare, and have a staff member verify safety-critical words. Keep the message short enough that translation stays accurate, and offer a phone call for anything urgent or sensitive, because a translated message is no substitute for a human conversation in an emergency.

## Final verdict

Start with Illumine or free WhatsApp plus Sheets, and use ChatGPT or Gemini only for drafting — never for children's private data. That combination is honest about cost, safe about children's information, and practical for staff who have to use it every morning. Grow into a paid platform when your daily routine proves you need one, not before.
