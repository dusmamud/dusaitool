Finding a scholarship in India is rarely about one big website. It is about three different jobs: discovering schemes you are actually eligible for, preparing an application that is complete and honest, and submitting it before a deadline that will not wait. AI tools help most with the first and second jobs, and almost not at all with the third unless you set up your own tracking. No AI tool can apply on your behalf on the National Scholarship Portal, and no chatbot result is proof that a scholarship exists. The official scheme page is always the final authority.

The short answer for students in India is this: start with the government portal and an Indian scholarship platform, then use a free AI assistant only as a helper. Search and apply through the [National Scholarship Portal](https://scholarships.gov.in/), which is the official government portal for central and state schemes, and through [Buddy4Study](https://www.buddy4study.com/), which lists Indian scholarships and sends profile-based alerts. Add [Vidyasaarathi](https://www.vidyasaarathi.co.in/) for corporate-funded scholarships in India. If you are also planning to study abroad, look at [Scholly](https://www.sallie.com/scholarships/scholly) and Bold.org, but understand that both are built mainly for students in the United States, so most of their listings will not apply to a student studying in India. Draft essays and emails with [ChatGPT](https://chatgpt.com/) free or Gemini free, and keep every deadline in your own calendar, because AI search results can be out of date, and a missed deadline cannot be appealed back into existence.

## How to use AI for scholarships, in four steps

First, build a true profile before you search. Write down your class or course, your board or university, your state, your category, your family income range, your marks, and whether you are a girl student, a student with a disability, a minority student, or a first-generation learner, because each of these opens or closes different schemes. A vague search such as "scholarship for students" returns thousands of results, most of them useless to you. A specific search such as "Class 11 scholarship for OBC students in Uttar Pradesh" returns a short list you can actually check. AI matching tools work the same way: the better your profile, the fewer false matches you get.

Second, search in two places, not one. Use an official or Indian platform for the scheme itself, and use an AI assistant to explain it. For example, find a scheme on NSP or Buddy4Study, then ask ChatGPT or Gemini to explain its eligibility in simple language, list the documents it usually needs, and draft a checklist. Then go back to the official page and verify every point. If the chatbot names a scholarship you cannot find on any official or reputable site, treat it as unproven until you find the original notification.

Third, draft with AI but write in your own voice. Scholarship essays and statements of purpose are judged on whether they sound like you and whether the facts match your certificates. Use AI to fix grammar, shorten a long paragraph, or turn rough notes into a first draft, then rewrite it yourself, remove anything you cannot prove, and check names, dates, marks and income figures against your documents. A copied or exaggerated essay is worse than a simple honest one, because verification at the institute level can reject an application that does not match its records.

Fourth, track deadlines yourself, outside any chatbot. Make one list with four columns: scholarship name, official website, last date, and documents needed. Put each last date in your phone calendar with two reminders, one week before and one day before. Many Indian portals close applications at midnight on the last date, and institute verification often has a separate, later date, so missing the student deadline still ends your chance even if verification is still open. Never trust a deadline you saw only in an AI answer, a social media post, or a forwarded message.

## National Scholarship Portal (NSP)

The National Scholarship Portal at scholarships.gov.in is the single most important tool for Indian students, and it is not an AI product at all. It hosts central government schemes, state schemes and AICTE and UGC schemes, and it is where most government scholarship applications in India are actually submitted. For the academic year 2026-27, the portal opened on 1 June 2026, and it uses a One Time Registration (OTR) number, a unique 14-digit number based on Aadhaar or the Aadhaar enrolment ID, which stays valid for your entire academic career on the portal. You generate the OTR once, and you do not register afresh every year.

NSP matters because it is also where the warning about deadlines is most visible. Different schemes close on different dates, renewal applications follow different dates from fresh applications, and institute verification and higher-level verification have their own closing dates. Students may apply for one merit-based scheme and one or more welfare-based schemes, as per the scheme eligibility criteria, but duplicate applications in the fresh category when you are eligible for renewal can be rejected. The practical rule is simple: read the scheme guidelines on NSP itself, check whether your institute is registered on the portal, and apply well before the last date, because document uploads and Aadhaar-based verification can fail on the final day.

## Buddy4Study

Buddy4Study is an Indian scholarship platform that lists scholarships for school, college and study-abroad students, and it claims more than 10,000 scholarships on its platform. You register, create a profile with your class, course and background, and it shows scholarships matched to that profile, with alerts for new opportunities. Its own foundation scholarships illustrate the range: for example, its India Foundation scholarship for Class 11 and 12 students offers amounts in the range of 6,000 to 24,000 rupees, while its engineering scholarship for students at top NIRF colleges offers 35,000 to 75,000 rupees, and its medical scholarship offers 20,000 to 1,00,000 rupees. These figures describe specific Buddy4Study foundation schemes, not all scholarships on the site, so always open the individual listing to see the current amount, eligibility and deadline.

Buddy4Study is useful in India because it covers private, corporate and trust scholarships that never appear on NSP, and because its alerts solve the discovery problem for students who do not check portals every week. Its weakness is the same as every aggregator: listings can go out of date, and a listing page is not the same as an official notification. Before you apply, follow the listing to the provider's own page or notification, and if the listing and the provider disagree on the deadline or the amount, believe the provider.

## Vidyasaarathi

Vidyasaarathi is a technology-enabled scholarship platform run by Protean eGov Technologies Limited, formerly NSDL e-Governance Infrastructure Limited. It connects underprivileged students with corporate-funded scholarships, funded through corporate social responsibility programmes, and students can search and apply for education finance schemes they qualify for. The platform reported more than 10 lakh registered students as of January 2022, which is a dated figure, and the current number is likely higher, but it should not be quoted as today's figure without a newer source.

Vidyasaarathi suits students who need corporate or trust funding alongside or instead of a government scheme, especially for professional courses where fees are high. Because each scheme on the platform is created by a different funder, eligibility, documents and deadlines differ from scheme to scheme, and there is no single closing date for the whole portal. Treat each Vidyasaarathi listing the way you treat an NSP scheme: read the funder's own terms, keep your own deadline note, and never pay anyone who claims they can guarantee selection on the platform.

## Scholly

Scholly is an American scholarship search app and website that matches students to scholarships based on profile details such as gender, state, grades and background, and shows a targeted list with links and deadlines. It was founded by Christopher Gray, and in 2023 Sallie Mae acquired its key assets, after which Scholly's search was made free for all students, where it had previously cost 2.99 dollars to download. It also includes an AI-powered proofreader, which can help clean up an essay draft.

For an Indian student, Scholly is a secondary tool, not a primary one. Its database is built around postsecondary scholarships in the United States, so a student studying in India will find few directly usable matches, while a student applying to colleges in the United States may find it genuinely useful. Use it for study-abroad planning, check every match against the provider's own page, and do not judge it by its total scholarship value claims, which count the value of all listed scholarships, not money any one student will receive.

## Bold.org

Bold.org describes itself as a scholarship and student rewards platform, claims more than 20,000 donors powering scholarships, and says it is 100 percent free with no fees, ever. It publishes exclusive scholarships that are awarded only on Bold.org, lets students browse by category, including scholarships that do not require essays, and runs a points system called Bold Rewards for completing a profile, applying and using its extension. Independent coverage has also described it as free to use, with exclusive scholarships not found elsewhere.

Like Scholly, Bold.org is mainly a United States platform, so its value for a student in India is mostly for study-abroad plans or for understanding how no-essay and category scholarships work. A no-essay scholarship is not a guaranteed scholarship: it usually has far more applicants and a lottery-like selection, so it should never replace applications to merit and need-based schemes where your marks and documents actually count. Apply to no-essay options only after your serious applications are submitted.

## Going Merry

Going Merry, now part of the Earnest family, matches students to scholarships from their profile, and its most useful feature is practical rather than glamorous: it can autofill application forms with profile information for scholarships that accept direct applications on its site, and it sends deadline reminders. It lists both direct-apply scholarships, where you stay on Going Merry to submit, and external-apply scholarships, where you are sent to the provider's website. Its eligibility tables show a green check or a red cross for each requirement, so you can see at a glance which conditions you meet and which you do not.

Going Merry is free to use for matching, and its deadline reminders are exactly the habit this article recommends, built into a tool. Its limitation for Indian students is the same as Scholly and Bold.org: its listings are centred on the United States, so it is best used by students applying abroad, or as a model for how to organise your own tracking. Do not let a long matched list on any US platform distract you from NSP and Buddy4Study deadlines, which are the ones that actually fund study in India.

## ScholarshipOwl

ScholarshipOwl takes a different approach: it gives you a matched list of scholarships for free, and charges for the convenience of applying to many of them with a single application form. Its help centre states that without a membership you still keep your list of eligible scholarships for free, and you can apply to any of them for free on the provider's own website. The paid membership, which unlocks the single-application feature and full services, costs 20 dollars a month, 45 dollars for three months, 60 dollars for six months, or 69 dollars for a one-month VIP membership with extra help, and it can be cancelled from the membership tab or by contacting support.

For Indian students, the honest verdict on ScholarshipOwl is narrow. The free matched list costs nothing and can be useful for study-abroad research, but the paid single-application feature is priced in dollars, aimed at US scholarships, and unnecessary for Indian schemes, where you must apply on NSP or the provider's portal anyway. Never pay for a scholarship application service before checking whether the same scholarships can be applied to for free on the provider's own site, because in most cases they can.

## ChatGPT

ChatGPT is the most useful free AI assistant for scholarship work, not because it finds scholarships, but because it turns rough material into clean applications. Use it to turn your notes into a first essay draft, rewrite a statement of purpose in simpler English, draft a polite email to a scholarship officer or your institute, make a document checklist for a scheme, and prepare answers to common interview or application questions. It is also the tool most students already have on their phones, which matters when you are working from a shared device or a low-cost phone.

Its free plan in India gives broad access to everyday text chats, with separate limits on file uploads, image creation, voice and deep research, and the exact numbers are not published as fixed caps. That is the honest way to describe it: free for drafting, limited for heavy features, and the limits can change without notice. Never paste your Aadhaar number, bank account details, or full address into a consumer chatbot, and never copy an AI-generated list of scholarships without verifying each entry, because chatbots can invent plausible-sounding schemes, deadlines and links.

## Gemini

Gemini is Google's AI assistant, and for Indian students its advantage is that it already lives inside a Google account, which most students already use for email and documents. Use it to summarise a long scheme guideline, compare two scholarships side by side, draft an essay outline, or turn a Hindi or Hinglish draft into formal English. If your college uses Google Workspace, Gemini can also help organise notes and drafts without installing anything new.

Gemini's free access in India is a standard level of access with compute-based limits that depend on the model, the feature, the prompt and the length of the conversation, and the limits refresh over hours up to a weekly cap, with no fixed message number published. In practice this means short drafting and summarising tasks work well on the free plan, while long research sessions may slow down or pause at peak demand. As with ChatGPT, treat Gemini as a drafting and explaining tool, verify every scholarship fact on the official page, and keep personal documents out of the chat.

## Perplexity

Perplexity is different from ChatGPT and Gemini because it is built around search: it answers with sources, which makes it the best free AI tool for checking a scholarship claim. When a forwarded message claims a new scholarship, a new deadline, or a new amount, paste the claim into Perplexity and read the sources it cites, then open the official source yourself. This single habit prevents most scholarship misinformation, because fake schemes almost never have an official notification behind them.

Perplexity's free plan offers practically unlimited basic searches, but only a small allowance of advanced Pro searches, with basic file uploads and automatic model selection. Different trackers report slightly different Pro search allowances, and the number has changed over time, so treat any exact figure as temporary and check the current plan page before relying on it. For scholarship work, basic searches are usually enough: you are checking facts, not running deep research reports.

## Using these tools in India: a practical plan

Start with NSP, because government money is the largest and most reliable source for students studying in India. Generate your OTR early, even before you pick schemes, because OTR generation and Aadhaar verification can take time, and you do not want to discover a problem on the last day. Then check whether your institute is registered on NSP, because institute verification is a required step for most schemes, and an unregistered institute can block an otherwise eligible application.

Next, add one Indian aggregator, Buddy4Study or Vidyasaarathi, and set up alerts with a complete profile. Check alerts once a week, not once a day, and keep a single list rather than screenshots scattered across your phone. Then, only if you are applying abroad, add one US platform such as Scholly, Bold.org or Going Merry, and keep its list separate from your India list, so that US deadlines do not crowd out Indian ones.

Prepare documents before the season starts, because most rejections are document rejections, not merit rejections. Keep scanned copies of your Aadhaar card, previous mark sheets, income certificate, caste or category certificate where applicable, domicile certificate, fee receipt, and bank passbook or account details ready, and make sure the name on every document matches your Aadhaar and your institute record. Use an AI assistant to make a checklist for each scheme, but check the checklist against the scheme guidelines, because required documents differ, and a missing income certificate can delay an application past its deadline.

Language should never be a barrier. If a scheme guideline is in difficult English, ask Gemini or ChatGPT to explain it in simple Hindi or Hinglish, then verify the explanation against the original. If your essay needs to be in English, draft it first in the language you think in, then ask AI to help translate and polish it, and finally read it aloud to check it still sounds like you. Students who study in Hindi medium or regional languages lose nothing by using AI as a translator, as long as the facts in the final version are theirs. ## What to avoid

Avoid any scholarship that asks for an application fee, a processing fee, or a payment to guarantee selection. Genuine government schemes on NSP are free to apply for, and genuine private scholarships do not charge you to apply. Agents who promise a scholarship for a fee, messages that ask for your bank OTP or Aadhaar details to release a scholarship, and websites that copy the NSP design but use a different address are scams. The government never asks for your OTP, and no real scholarship needs your ATM PIN or your full bank login.

Avoid trusting AI answers about deadlines, amounts and eligibility without an official source. Chatbots can mix up last year's dates with this year's, confuse a state scheme with a central scheme, and invent links that look real. If an AI tool gives you a deadline, open the scheme page and confirm it, and if the scheme page says something different, the scheme page wins. Also avoid applying to every scholarship you see: ten careful, complete applications beat fifty rushed ones with missing documents, and a rejected application due to a wrong category or incomplete form wastes the time you could have spent on a scheme you actually qualify for.

Avoid sharing personal data carelessly across scholarship tools. Scholarship platforms legitimately need your class, course, marks and category to match you, but they do not need your Aadhaar number in a chatbot, your bank password anywhere, or your family's full financial documents uploaded to an unverified site. Read the privacy policy of any new platform before creating an account, prefer platforms with a clear Indian presence and a grievance contact, and keep a note of where you have created accounts so you can delete unused ones. ## Frequently asked questions

**Which AI tool is best for finding scholarships in India?**

There is no single best tool, because discovery and application live in different places. For finding Indian scholarships, NSP and Buddy4Study are the strongest combination: NSP for government schemes and Buddy4Study for private and trust scholarships. AI assistants such as ChatGPT and Gemini are best used after discovery, to explain guidelines, draft essays and build checklists, not as the source of the scholarship list itself.

**Can AI apply for a scholarship on my behalf?**

No, not for Indian government schemes. You must apply yourself on NSP or the provider's portal, with your own OTR, documents and institute verification. AI can prepare your drafts and your checklist, and a platform such as Going Merry can autofill some US applications, but nobody else, human or AI, can complete your NSP application for you, and anyone offering to do so for a fee should be treated as suspicious.

**Are scholarships on NSP really free to apply for?**

Yes. Applying on the National Scholarship Portal is free, and genuine government schemes do not charge an application fee. If a website or agent asks for money to submit your NSP application, stop and report it, and apply directly on scholarships.gov.in instead. The only costs you may face are your own, such as getting a certificate issued by your local authority.

**What is OTR on NSP, and why do I need it?**

OTR stands for One Time Registration, a unique 14-digit number based on your Aadhaar or Aadhaar enrolment ID, which is valid for your entire academic career on NSP. It was made required for applying on NSP from the academic year 2024-25 onwards, and it replaces the need to register afresh every year. Generate it early, keep the number safe, and make sure the details match your Aadhaar and institute records.

**Do Scholly, Bold.org and Going Merry work for students in India?**

They work as websites and apps in India without a VPN, but their scholarship databases are mainly for the United States, so most listings will not apply to a student studying in India. They are worth using if you are applying to colleges abroad, and worth understanding as examples of matching and deadline reminders, but they should never replace NSP and Buddy4Study for Indian scholarships.

**Can I use ChatGPT to write my scholarship essay?**

You can use it to draft, organise and polish your essay, but the final essay must be yours. Write your own story, facts and reasons first, let AI help with structure and grammar, then rewrite anything that does not sound like you and remove anything you cannot prove. Selection committees and institute verifiers check facts against documents, and an essay that invents achievements or income details can get an application rejected.

**How do I check whether a scholarship message is fake?**

Search for the scheme name on Perplexity or a search engine and look for an official notification on a government portal, a university site, or the provider's own website. A real scheme has a published guideline, an eligibility list, a deadline and a contact. A fake one usually has only a WhatsApp forward, a deadline of today or tomorrow to create panic, and a request for money, OTP or bank details. When in doubt, ask your institute or check NSP before paying or sharing anything.

**What should I do if I miss a scholarship deadline?**

Check whether the scheme has a renewal window, a second phase, or a state-level equivalent with a later date, and note the scheme for next year with a calendar reminder set a month before it usually opens. Do not pay anyone who promises to reopen a closed portal, because closed government portals do not reopen for individual students. Use the extra time to prepare your documents, so that next year's application is complete on the first day.

## Final verdict

Start on NSP and Buddy4Study for Indian scholarships, use ChatGPT or Gemini free to draft, and re-check the official deadline first.
