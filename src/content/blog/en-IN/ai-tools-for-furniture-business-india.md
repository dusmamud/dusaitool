A furniture business in India lives or dies on four jobs, and AI helps with each of them differently: showing the customer what a piece will look like in their room, taking an order with correct measurements and a clear price, knowing what wood, hardware and finished stock you actually have, and getting the piece delivered and installed without a payment dispute. No single tool does all four jobs well. The tool that makes the prettiest picture is often the worst tool for handing measurements to a carpenter, and the tool that tracks stock perfectly will not help you convince a customer to choose teak over engineered wood.

The short answer for India is this: start free with [Planner 5D](https://planner5d.com/) for editable room plans that show a customer where a sofa, bed or wardrobe will actually fit, because its free plan covers unlimited projects and half of its furniture catalogue. Use [SketchUp Free](https://sketchup.trimble.com/en/plans-and-pricing/sketchup-free) in the browser when you need a real three-dimensional model of a piece itself, with dimensions a carpenter can work from, because it runs without a download and gives 10 GB of cloud storage. Use [Zoho Inventory](https://www.zoho.com/in/inventory/) for stock, orders and supplier reordering, because its free plan covers 50 orders a month, and use [Vyapar](https://vyaparapp.in/) for GST billing, estimates and tracking what customers still owe you, because furniture selling in India runs on advances, credit and delivery challans, not just one-time card payments. Use [Canva](https://www.canva.com/) for your catalogue, price list and social media posts, because a furniture shop without clear photos and prices loses customers to the next shop on Instagram or WhatsApp. Use [ChatGPT](https://chatgpt.com/) for writing product descriptions, answering common customer questions and drafting follow-up messages, but never let it invent a measurement, a wood type or a delivery date.

Every tool below works in India in a normal browser or app without a VPN, as far as its own public pages and help pages show. Prices and free limits change often, so re-check the official pricing and help page before paying for any plan or promising a customer a deadline based on a free limit. Where sources disagreed, this article says so instead of picking the friendlier number. ## How a furniture business actually breaks down

Before choosing tools, it helps to separate the four jobs, because mixing them up is how money is lost. Design is the promise: what the piece looks like, what size it is, what material and finish it uses, and where it will sit in the customer's room. Ordering is the contract: the customer's name, phone number, address, measurements, chosen material, colour, advance paid, balance due, and delivery date. Inventory is the truth: how many boards, hinges, handles, foam sheets and finished pieces you hold, what is running low, and what you have already promised to another customer. Delivery and installation is the ending: transport, carrying the piece upstairs, fitting it, removing packaging, and collecting the balance.

Most small furniture businesses in India run all four jobs on WhatsApp and memory. That works for five orders a month and breaks at twenty. A customer changes the fabric colour in a chat, the carpenter remembers the old colour, the stock register says a board is available when it was cut last week, and the delivery team arrives while the balance is still unpaid. The tools below do not replace judgement or craftsmanship. They replace memory with a written record that two people can check.

## Planner 5D: show the customer the room, not just the piece

Planner 5D is the best free starting point for the selling conversation. A customer rarely buys a wardrobe by looking at a wardrobe alone; they buy it by imagining it in their bedroom, next to their bed, with the door able to open fully. Planner 5D lets you draw the room, place furniture in 2D and 3D, try layouts, and show the customer a visual before any wood is cut. Its free plan costs nothing, covers an unlimited number of projects, and gives access to half of its furniture catalogue, which is enough to test layouts and present an idea.

The honest limit is that Planner 5D is a room planner, not a manufacturing drawing. It will not give your carpenter a cutting list, a joint detail or a board size. Its premium features, including the full catalogue of more than 10,000 items, AI floor-plan recognition and standard renders, sit behind a paid plan, and its own pages show different premium prices, so check the official pricing page on the day you decide rather than trusting any single figure quoted here. Use Planner 5D to close the design conversation, then move the agreed measurements into a written order and, where needed, a SketchUp model.

A practical workflow looks like this. Measure the customer's room and doorways first, on site or from a video call where the customer holds a measuring tape against the wall. Build the room in Planner 5D, place the piece at true size, and screenshot the plan view as well as the 3D view. Send both to the customer on WhatsApp and ask for a written confirmation of size, material and colour before taking an advance. That single habit prevents the most common furniture dispute: the piece that is beautiful and does not fit through the door.

## SketchUp Free: a model a carpenter can actually use

When the piece itself is custom, a room planner is not enough. SketchUp Free runs in a web browser, needs no download, and lets you build a three-dimensional model of a bed, table, wardrobe or modular kitchen unit with real dimensions. Its free version includes 10 GB of Trimble Connect cloud storage, access to the 3D Warehouse of user and manufacturer models, and the core modelling tools. For a furniture maker, the value is not the pretty render; it is the ability to rotate the model, check a clearance, confirm that a drawer opens without hitting a door frame, and share the model with a colleague or a customer.

The free limits matter and should be stated plainly. Free users can export STL files, while wider export options and high-resolution printing sit behind paid plans, and PNG downloads from the free version carry a watermark. Extensions are not available in the free web version, and some advanced tools, such as the full Solid Tools set and the Outliner panel, are limited or absent. That does not make SketchUp Free useless; it makes it a design and checking tool rather than a full production system. For cutting lists and factory files, larger Indian manufacturers use specialist platforms, and a small workshop should check those separately rather than expecting a free web modeller to do everything.

SketchUp Free works best when one person owns the model and the measurements. Keep a simple naming habit: customer name, piece, date, and version number, for example Sharma-wardrobe-Nov-2026-v2. When the customer changes a dimension, save a new version instead of overwriting the old one, so nobody builds from the wrong file. Print or screenshot the dimensioned views for the workshop, because a carpenter working from a phone screen in a dusty workshop needs a clear drawing, not a spinning model.

## Homestyler and RoomGPT: fast pictures, with a warning label

Homestyler and RoomGPT belong to a different category: they restyle a photo of a room quickly. A customer uploads a photo of their living room, picks a style, and sees the room redecorated. Homestyler adds a fuller design environment, with a cloud 3D floor planner, unlimited 1K rendering on its free plan with coins on limited types, and AI tools for mood boards and image-to-3D experiments, while 4K renders are bought separately. RoomGPT is simpler: upload one photo, get a redesigned image, and its site does not publish its pricing or free limits clearly, so treat any claim about its free allowance as unverified until you check its own site.

These tools are useful for inspiration and for showing a customer a style direction. They are dangerous when treated as a promise. An AI restyle can change the size of a sofa, invent a window, remove a pillar, or show a wood finish that your workshop cannot supply. Never send an AI restyle to a customer without a caption in plain words: this is a style idea, not the final design, and the final size, material and price will be confirmed in writing. For the same reason, never use an AI-generated room photo as a catalogue photo of a piece you actually sell, unless you label it clearly as a design concept. A customer who orders the chair in the picture and receives a different chair will not accept that the picture was only an idea.

## ChatGPT: the writing assistant, not the measurer

ChatGPT earns its place in a furniture business as a writing and thinking assistant. It can turn a rough note into a clear product description, write a WhatsApp message explaining why solid teak costs more than engineered wood, draft a polite payment reminder, suggest ten names for a new collection, or list questions to ask a customer before quoting a modular kitchen. It can also help a shop owner who writes more comfortably in Hindi to draft a polished English listing, and then translate it back for checking.

Its limits are equally clear, and they matter more in furniture than in many other businesses. ChatGPT does not know your stock, your carpenter's schedule, or the price of plywood this week. It can invent a plausible-sounding wood name, a standard size that is not standard in your workshop, or a delivery time that your team cannot meet. Every number it produces must be checked against your own price list, stock record and calendar before it reaches a customer. Treat its output as a first draft, and keep a human approval step for every price, measurement, material claim and date.

A safe way to use it is to give it facts and ask for wording, not the other way round. For example: give it the true size, material, finish, price and delivery time, and ask for a short listing, a longer catalogue paragraph, and a one-line WhatsApp message. That way the facts stay yours and the tool only improves the language. Do not paste a customer's full address, phone number or payment details into any public AI tool; share only what the draft needs.

## Canva: catalogue, price list and social posts

Most furniture customers in India discover a shop on Instagram, Facebook, WhatsApp Status or Google, and they judge it in seconds from its photos and prices. Canva offers free furniture templates for Instagram posts, Facebook posts, flyers, catalogue pages and short videos, which a small shop can customise with its own photos, logo, prices and phone number. Its value is speed: a shop owner can make a festival offer post, a new-arrival post or a price-list page without hiring a designer for every small change.

The honest catch is that many templates and stock assets are marked Pro, and a free user will hit that wall often. The fix is to build around your own photographs rather than stock furniture pictures. Photograph each piece in daylight, against a plain wall, from the front, from an angle, and with a detail shot of the joint, handle or finish. Use Canva to add the name, size, material, price or price range, and delivery area, and keep the layout the same across posts so customers learn to read your catalogue quickly. A catalogue that shows ten real pieces with real prices will outsell a feed full of borrowed luxury photos that your shop cannot deliver.

## Zoho Inventory: know what you actually have

Zoho Inventory is the stock and order backbone in this stack. It tracks items, stock levels, sales orders, purchase orders, suppliers, warehouses and shipments, and it connects with online stores and Indian shipping partners. Its free plan covers 50 orders a month, one user and one location, which suits a single shop or workshop that is just moving from notebooks to software. Composite items, dropshipment and backordering are included even on the free plan, which matters for furniture, where a finished wardrobe is assembled from boards, hinges, handles, mirrors and polish.

Pricing needs care, because sources disagree. The United States pricing page lists Standard at 29 dollars per organisation per month billed annually, while Indian guides quote a Standard plan around 999 rupees per month billed annually with GST extra, and higher plans for more orders, users and locations. Do not treat either figure as settled; open the official Zoho India inventory pricing page before paying, confirm whether GST is included, and confirm how many orders, users and locations your plan covers. The free plan itself is the safest place to start, because it lets you learn the system on real orders before committing money.

Set up Zoho Inventory around how furniture actually moves. Create one item for each finished piece and separate items for key raw materials, so a wardrobe sale reduces both the finished stock and, through a composite or bundle setup, the materials it consumes. Record supplier purchase orders for boards and hardware, so a stock alert tells you to reorder before a carpenter stands idle. If you sell on Shopify, Amazon India or Flipkart, connect the store only after your item names, prices and stock counts are clean, because a messy catalogue will sync messily everywhere at once.

## Vyapar: GST billing, estimates and udhaar

Vyapar is built for Indian small shops, and its fit for furniture comes from the way furniture is actually sold: a customer asks for an estimate, pays an advance, waits weeks while the piece is made, and pays the balance on delivery. Vyapar handles estimates and quotations that convert into a sale invoice when the customer confirms, sale and purchase orders, delivery challans, GST invoices with GSTIN, HSN codes and the correct CGST, SGST or IGST split, and a record of udhaar with payment reminders. It runs on Android, Windows and Mac, which matters for a shop where the owner bills from a phone and the accountant checks from a computer.

Furniture shops that deliver out of town usually need a higher plan for e-way bills, and heavier accounting work such as audits and complex ledgers is still typically done in Tally by a chartered accountant, according to guides written for furniture shops. That is not a criticism of Vyapar; it is a division of labour. Use Vyapar for daily billing, estimates, stock summaries and customer balances, and keep your chartered accountant in the loop for tax filing and year-end work. Before buying, check Vyapar's current plan page for what your plan includes, especially e-way bill limits, because those limits decide whether an outstation delivery is smooth or stuck.

The daily habit that pays for itself is simple: every order gets an estimate first, the estimate becomes an invoice only when the customer confirms, the advance and the balance are recorded against the same order, and a delivery challan is raised when the piece leaves the workshop. When a customer asks, months later, what they paid and what is still due, the answer should come from the software, not from searching old chats.

## India-use section: what makes or breaks it here

India adds conditions that international tool guides rarely mention, and a furniture business ignores them at its own cost. First, language and phones come before laptops. Customers will ask for photos and prices on WhatsApp, often in Hindi or Hinglish, and they will expect a reply the same day. Keep your catalogue, price list and order confirmations ready to send from a phone, and write material and size details in words a customer understands, such as solid sheesham, teak veneer on plywood, or 18 mm plywood with laminate, rather than a brand code alone.

Second, measurements are a trust issue. Indian homes vary widely: a lift that fits a sofa in one building will not fit it in another, a staircase turn can block a wardrobe, and a door frame can be narrower than the piece. Always record door width, lift size, staircase turns and room dimensions before promising delivery, and put a line in the order that the customer has confirmed the piece will fit through the entry route. It feels formal, and it prevents the most painful delivery-day argument.

Third, advances and balances need written rules. Decide in advance what advance you take for a custom piece, when the balance is due, what happens if the customer changes the design midway, and what your cancellation policy is, and put those rules on the estimate itself. A polite written rule protects both sides far better than a verbal assurance remembered differently by each side.

Fourth, wood and material honesty is the brand. Do not describe engineered wood as solid wood, do not call a veneer finish solid teak, and do not let an AI listing upgrade your material for you. Customers in India often know wood well, they ask relatives who know wood, and one discovered exaggeration will cost more future orders than the single sale was worth. State the material, the finish, the warranty on structure and hardware separately, and what the warranty does not cover, such as water damage or misuse.

Fifth, delivery and installation are part of the product. A wardrobe that arrives scratched, a bed that is fitted loosely, or packaging left in the customer's home will undo a good design. Budget for transport, carrying, fitting and packaging removal, and record who is responsible for each step. If installation needs a second visit for handles, mirrors or wall fitting, say so before the sale, not on delivery day.

Sixth, festivals, weddings and house-moving seasons create peaks. Diwali, wedding season and the months when families move into new flats can double orders, and that is exactly when stock records and delivery calendars break. Use the quiet weeks to photograph pieces, update the catalogue, count stock, and confirm supplier prices, so the busy weeks run on records rather than improvisation.

## What to avoid

Avoid selling from an AI render alone, without a measured drawing, a material sample and a written order. Avoid copying another brand's catalogue photos or claiming a design as your own when it is not. Avoid letting any AI tool set your price; it does not know your wood cost, labour cost, transport cost or wastage. Avoid keeping orders only in chat, because chats get deleted, phones get replaced, and staff change. Avoid promising a delivery date before checking stock, the carpenter's queue and the transporter's availability. Avoid collecting full customer addresses and payment details in AI prompts or shared documents that do not need them. Avoid buying a paid plan for every tool at once; start free, prove the workflow on ten real orders, and then pay only for the tool that is actually saving time or winning sales. Avoid hiding behind jargon: if a customer does not understand the material, the size or the price, the sale is not ready.

## Frequently asked questions

**Which single AI tool should a small furniture shop start with?**
Start with the job that hurts most. If customers cannot visualise pieces, start with Planner 5D. If orders and stock are chaotic, start with Zoho Inventory or Vyapar. Most small shops benefit most from fixing orders and billing first, because a beautiful design that is billed wrong or built from missing stock still loses money.

**Can AI design a complete wardrobe or modular kitchen by itself?**
No, not to a buildable standard. AI tools can suggest layouts, styles and colour combinations, and SketchUp Free can hold a measured model, but the final design still needs a human to confirm dimensions, clearances, hardware, material thickness and how the piece will be assembled and transported. Treat AI as a drafting and inspiration assistant, and keep a human approval step before cutting.

**Is Planner 5D really free for a furniture business?**
Its free plan costs nothing, covers unlimited projects and gives access to half of its furniture catalogue, which is enough for layout and presentation work. The full catalogue, AI floor-plan recognition and standard renders need a paid plan, and premium prices differ across its own pages, so check the official pricing page before paying.

**Is SketchUp Free enough for a carpenter?**
It is enough for a measured three-dimensional model, dimensioned views and a shared visual reference, especially for custom pieces. It is not a full factory system: free export is STL only, PNG downloads carry a watermark, and advanced production features sit behind paid plans. Pair it with a written cutting list and a printed drawing for the workshop.

**What is the cheapest honest way to track furniture stock in India?**
Start with Zoho Inventory's free plan, which covers 50 orders a month, one user and one location, and learn the system on real orders before paying. If your main pain is billing rather than stock, Vyapar may be the better first purchase, because it handles GST invoices, estimates and udhaar in a way built for Indian shops. Do not pay for both at full scale until you know which job each one is doing.

**How do I handle advances, balances and udhaar for custom furniture?**
Raise an estimate first, convert it into an invoice when the customer confirms, record the advance against that same order, and record the balance as due on delivery. Send a written reminder before delivery, not after, and keep a separate record of udhaar so no credit sale is forgotten. Put your advance, change and cancellation rules on the estimate itself.

**Can I use AI photos in my furniture catalogue?**
You can use AI images for style ideas and concept posts if you label them clearly as concepts, but your catalogue should lead with real photographs of pieces you actually sell, with true size, material and price. Customers order what they see, and a catalogue photo that does not match the delivered piece creates refunds, disputes and lost trust.

**Do these tools work in India without a VPN?**
As far as their own public pages and help pages show, Planner 5D, SketchUp Free, Homestyler, ChatGPT, Canva, Zoho Inventory and Vyapar all work in India in a normal browser or app without a VPN. Vyapar and Zoho Inventory are built for Indian businesses, with GST billing and Indian payment and shipping connections. If any tool behaves differently on your connection, check its official help page before assuming a block.

**What should I check before paying for any of these tools?**
Check the official pricing page on the day you pay, confirm whether GST is included, confirm the free limit you are relying on, confirm how many users and locations are included, and test the tool on a real order first. Prices and limits change, and a figure quoted in any article, including this one, can go out of date.

## Final verdict

Start free with Planner 5D, SketchUp Free and Zoho Inventory's free plan; add Vyapar for GST billing. Never sell from an AI render alone.

That verdict is deliberately boring, because furniture is a business where boring records beat exciting pictures. Show the room, model the piece, write the order, count the stock, bill correctly, and deliver what was promised. AI makes each of those steps faster, but it does not remove the need for a human to measure, confirm and sign off. A shop that does the boring steps well will keep customers, get referrals, and survive the busy season; a shop that relies on renders alone will spend its time settling disputes instead of making furniture.
