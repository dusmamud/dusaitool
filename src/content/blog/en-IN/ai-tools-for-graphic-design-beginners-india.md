## Canva: the default free editor, with a premium-element trap

Canva remains the default free design app for a simple reason: you can open it in a browser, pick a template sized for the exact post you need, and finish a decent graphic in minutes. The free plan covers thousands of templates, a large stock photo library, and basic AI features, which is enough for most [social posts](/blog/ai-tools-for-social-media-india/), flyers, and classroom materials, and its drag-and-drop editor is easy for a beginner who has never designed anything. For an Indian small business, student, or creator, Canva free is the best starting point, because a social post, a flyer, or a classroom poster can be made free in minutes, with a template sized for Instagram, YouTube, WhatsApp, or print.

The catch shows up when a project needs polish: background removal, one-click resizing, and brand kits all require Pro at 144 dollars a year, and premium elements can slip into a free design and add a watermark or a paywall at export. A premium photo, a premium font, or a premium element inside an otherwise free design will lock the export, forcing you to pay, replace the element, or accept a watermark, and a beginner who does not check elements will discover this only at export, after the design is finished. Use Canva free in a specific way:

- Filter for **free elements only**.
- Check each element before you export.
- Avoid premium photos, fonts, and elements.

A design built only from free elements exports free, while a design with one premium element does not.

For Indian designs, Canva's template sizes matter: pick the template sized for your exact platform, because an Instagram post, a YouTube thumbnail, a WhatsApp status, and a print flyer all have different sizes, and a design made for one size will crop badly on another. For Hindi designs, check Hindi fonts carefully, because Canva's free Hindi fonts vary in quality, and a Hindi headline in a poor font will look worse than the same headline in a clean font, so test two or three Hindi fonts before you commit to one.

## Adobe Express: Adobe fonts and Firefly, with a narrower library

Adobe Express pairs a template editor with Firefly, Adobe's image generation model, so you can generate an image and drop it straight into a layout, with a free plan needing no payment card, a large set of templates plus stock assets, and Adobe Fonts included, which makes it a solid Canva alternative for people who prefer Adobe's type and photo libraries. Its free plan is genuinely usable for social graphics, and Firefly built in means you can generate an image without leaving the editor, which saves time for a beginner who needs an image and a layout in one place. If you already pay for a Creative Cloud plan, Express Premium may already be included, so check your subscription before paying twice, because paying for Express Premium separately when Creative Cloud already includes it is wasted money.

Where Express falls short is depth: the free template range is narrower than Canva's, and the features that save the most time, such as resize and brand kits, sit behind the 9.99 dollars monthly Premium plan. For an Indian creator who needs many templates, Canva free's wider library is better, but for a creator who cares about typography, Adobe Fonts included in Express free is a real advantage, because good type makes a simple graphic look professional, while poor type makes a good image look amateur. Use Express free in a specific way:

- Generate an image with Firefly.
- Drop it into a template.
- Use Adobe Fonts for your headline.

That combination, generated image plus good type, is what makes an Express design look better than a generic Canva template.

For a beginner who wants to learn design, Express free plus Canva free together are better than either alone, because Canva gives you breadth, templates for every platform, while Express gives you depth, Adobe Fonts and Firefly images, and switching between them for different jobs teaches you what each editor does best. Do not pay for Express Premium until you have used free Express for a month and hit a specific limit, like resize or brand kits, repeatedly, because a free month will show you whether you need Premium or whether free is enough.

## Microsoft Designer, Firefly, and Ideogram: prompt to design, prompt to image, prompt to readable text

Microsoft Designer takes a different approach from template editors: you describe what you want, and it returns finished design options with images and layouts already assembled, which makes it a good fit for someone who needs a birthday invite, a social post, or a thumbnail and does not want to browse templates. It is free with a Microsoft account, and its prompt-to-design approach is fastest for a beginner who knows what they want but does not know how to build it, because Designer builds the layout for you, instead of making you pick a template and edit it. For an Indian user who needs a quick invite, a festival post, or a thumbnail, Designer free is the fastest route, because a short prompt in English, or in Hindi if the model supports it, returns finished options in seconds.

Adobe Firefly is the commercial-safe image generator: AI images trained on licensed content for commercial use, with free generations capped, and alongside Microsoft Designer, it is the strongest free route to AI-generated images. For an Indian small business that needs images for ads, posts, or a website, Firefly's licensed training matters, because an image trained on licensed content is safer for commercial use than an image trained on unknown content, and a business that uses an image commercially should care about its licence. Use Firefly free for commercial images, and check its free generation cap in your account, because free generations are capped, and a cap that runs out mid-campaign will stop you.

Ideogram stands out when the image must contain readable text, such as a poster headline or a logo wordmark, because most AI image generators garble text, rendering letters wrong, misspelling words, or inventing characters, while Ideogram is built to render text correctly. For an Indian creator making a poster with a Hindi or English headline, Ideogram free is the best image generator, because a poster with a misspelled headline is useless, while a poster with a correct headline is usable, and Ideogram's text accuracy is what makes it different. Use Ideogram free for posters, headlines, and wordmarks, and use Firefly or Designer for images without text, because matching the generator to the job, text versus no text, saves you from regenerating a garbled image ten times.

## Using design AI in India: templates, text, and brand

Templates are the fastest way for Indian beginners to design, but they are also a trap: a template used by thousands of businesses will look generic, and a customer who has seen the same template ten times will not remember your business. Use a template as a starting point, not a finished design:

- Change its colours to your **brand colours**.
- Change its fonts to fonts that suit your language.
- Replace its stock photo with your own photo.
- Rewrite its text in your own words.

A template edited this way looks original, while a template used as-is looks copied. For Hindi businesses, rewrite template text in Hindi yourself, because a template's English text translated by AI can sound generic, and a Hindi customer will trust Hindi written by a human more than Hindi written by a template.

Text is where Indian designs most often fail, because Hindi, Hinglish, and English text all need different fonts, sizes, and spacing, and a design that looks good in English can look cramped in Hindi, because Hindi characters are taller and wider. Test your Hindi headline at full size before you export, because a headline that fits in English may overflow in Hindi, and a headline that overflows will be cropped, making your design look broken. For a logo wordmark or a poster headline, use Ideogram, because readable text matters more than any other feature, and a wordmark with a misspelled word is not a logo, it is a mistake.

Brand is what separates a beginner from a professional, and brand kits, which store your colours, fonts, and logo in one place, are paid in both Canva and Express, which means a free beginner must build brand manually:

- Pick **two colours** and **two fonts**, and use them on every design, because consistency, the same colours and fonts every time, is what makes a small business look like a brand, even without a paid brand kit.
- Save your colours as hex codes in a note, save your fonts by name, and reuse them.

A business that uses random colours and fonts on every post looks amateur, while a business that uses the same colours and fonts looks professional, even on free tools.

## What to avoid

- Do not export a Canva design without checking each element, because a premium element can add a watermark or a paywall at export, and a design that looks free in the editor is not free if a locked item hides inside it.
- Do not pay for Express Premium before you check whether Creative Cloud already includes it, because paying twice is wasted money.
- Do not use a generic template as-is, because a template used by thousands will look copied, and a customer will not remember your business.
- Do not generate a poster headline with a generic image generator, because most garble text, while Ideogram is built for readable headlines and wordmarks.
- Do not assume free means unlimited, because Firefly and Designer cap free generations, and Canva and Express lock background removal, resize, and brand kits behind paid plans.

A final trap is designing for yourself instead of for your audience. Beginners often make a design they like, with colours they like, fonts they like, and images they like, but their audience, customers, students, or followers, may not like it, or may not read it, because a design is for its viewer, not its maker. Show your design to three people in your audience before you publish it, ask them what it says, and if they cannot read the headline, find the call to action, or remember your business, redesign it, because a design that its audience cannot read is not a design, it is decoration.
