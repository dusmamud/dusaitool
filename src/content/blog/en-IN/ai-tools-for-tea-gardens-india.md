For most tea gardens in India, the best starting point is three free tools used together: Plantix for leaf health, Google Gemini for worker communication and records, and Canva for packaging and direct sales. They cover plucking, processing notes, and selling, with no VPN or paid subscription.

| Task | Free tool |
| --- | --- |
| Plucking and leaf health | Plantix |
| Messages and records | Google Gemini |
| Packaging and sales | Canva |

### 1. Plantix — leaf health in the field

Plantix is a free Android app that diagnoses crop problems from a leaf photo and suggests treatments. It covers 30 major crops and over 780 types of plant damage; the free version shows ads. One honest limit: tea is not named among its listed crops, so treat it as an early warning and confirm serious cases locally.

### 2. Google Gemini — messages, translation, and records

Gemini is Google's free AI assistant on web and phones, and it works in India without a VPN. Draft plucking instructions in Assamese, Bengali, Hindi, or Nepali, turn rough attendance and leaf-weight notes into daily records, and summarise Tea Board notices. The free tier costs ₹0 with 15 GB of storage and refreshing limits. AI Plus is listed at ₹399 a month; re-check before paying.

### 3. Canva — packaging, price lists, and sales posts

Canva Free costs ₹0, with 5 GB of storage, over 1.6 million templates, and a limited monthly AI allowance. Design tea packet labels, a one-page price list, and WhatsApp and Instagram posts for direct sales. Background remover and Magic Resize are paid features, and sources disagree on Pro pricing — around ₹500 a month or ₹4,000 a year — so confirm it on Canva's pricing page first.

## Using these tools in Indian tea gardens

Gardens in Assam, Darjeeling, and the Nilgiris often have patchy signal, so photograph leaves and draft messages while online, then share them when the signal returns. Keep one notebook — paper or Gemini — for daily leaf weights, wages, and auction prices. Small growers can also sell directly through WhatsApp and local markets, not only through auctions. For wider farm guidance, see [AI tools for farmers in India](/blog/ai-tools-for-farmers-india/).

## What to avoid

Do not spray chemicals on one photo, do not promise buyers a fixed price or delivery date from an AI estimate, and do not share workers' personal details or wage records with any chatbot. AI output can be wrong and auction prices change, so verify money or health decisions with an official source first.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can AI replace a tea garden manager?**
No. AI can draft messages, flag sick leaves, and organise records, but plucking quality, processing, and worker welfare still need a human daily.

**Is Plantix free for tea growers?**
It is free to download and diagnose on Android, with ads, but tea is not named among its listed crops, so use it as an early warning and confirm serious cases locally.

**Can small tea growers sell without the auction?**
Yes, many sell green leaf to nearby factories or made tea directly through WhatsApp, local shops, and markets, but auction prices still set a fair-rate reference.

## Final verdict

Start free with Plantix, Gemini, and Canva, verify every leaf diagnosis and price locally, and only pay once a tool earns its keep.
