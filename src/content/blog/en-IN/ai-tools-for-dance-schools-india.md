If you run a dance academy in India, the fastest free starting point is this: use Google Forms and Sheets for attendance and enquiries, WhatsApp Business for fee reminders and parent messages, and Canva for posters and class announcements. All three cost nothing to start, all three work in India without a VPN, and none of them asks you to change how you teach. Everything else in this article is for a specific pain — chasing fees every month, selling recital tickets, or cutting performance reels — and each paid wall is named honestly below.

A dance school is not one job. It is at least four jobs at once: filling batches by style and level, collecting monthly fees on time, keeping parents informed without answering the same question fifty times, and putting on a show that actually fills seats. A tool that promises to do all four badly is worse than two tools that each do one job well, so do not buy an all-in-one platform until you know which of the four jobs is hurting you most. Most small academies in India hurt in exactly one place first: fees. Parents forget, cash goes unrecorded, and the teacher spends the first week of every month chasing payments instead of teaching. Fix that job first, and only then spend on the rest.

Prices and free limits change often, and some of the prices below come from the provider's own pricing page as found during research for this article. Before you pay for anything, or promise a parent a deadline that depends on a paid plan, re-check the official pricing and help page of that tool. Where sources disagreed, or where a price could not be confirmed from the official page, this article says so instead of guessing.

## Batches, fees and recitals: YourFee

YourFee is the most dance-specific tool in this list, and the one built most clearly for India. It organises students into batches by style, level or age group — Bharatanatyam beginners, contemporary intermediate, hip-hop for teenagers — and tracks attendance and fees per batch, rather than in one long register. That batch-first design matters, because a dance academy rarely teaches one group: the same teacher may run five batches at different levels on different days, and a flat student list hides which batch is actually full, which one is shrinking, and who has paid for which class.

Its fee system is built around how Indian parents actually pay. Monthly tuition sits in a ledger with automatic carry-forward, so an unpaid month does not quietly vanish. Automated WhatsApp reminders go out for monthly tuition, each with a direct payment link, which removes the awkward phone call and the group-chat chase. Payments come through UPI, card and net banking via Razorpay or Cashfree, cash payments can be logged with an instant PDF receipt, and a parent portal shows fee history and receipts. YourFee states that it charges a flat monthly subscription starting at ₹299 and takes no commission on fees, with payments settling directly to your bank through your own payment account. That is an important distinction: the subscription is what you pay YourFee, while the payment gateway may still charge its own transaction fee, so check both before you quote a parent a total.

Recital ticketing is the second reason YourFee stands out for dance schools. A recital can be listed as a public event with its own ticket price and a capacity cap, tickets can be sold to extended family and friends rather than only to enrolled students, and tickets can be scanned at the door. For a school that has ever sold recital seats from a paper list at the studio door, that alone can justify the subscription for show month. Drop-in students are handled alongside regular enrollees without breaking batch rosters or fee ledgers, and multi-branch support exists if you run more than one studio. Instructors get a staff portal to mark attendance, so the teacher is not the only person who can update the register.

The honest limit is that YourFee is a management platform, not a teaching tool. It will not choreograph a routine, edit a performance video, or write your Instagram captions. Judge it on whether fee-chasing and recital-week chaos shrink in the first two months. If your academy has fewer than twenty students and fees already arrive on time, the free combination below will serve you better, and you can move to YourFee when growth makes the register unmanageable.

## A studio platform with an AI assistant: DanceHall Studios

DanceHall Studios is a studio management platform that explicitly describes itself as built for Indian classical dance schools, including Bharatanatyam schools. Its pitch is different from YourFee: alongside bookings, calendars and fee collection, it offers Sangs, an AI assistant that learns your schedule, answers questions, chases unpaid invoices, and acts as a kind of chief of staff for the studio owner. Students and parents log in from your website to open class recordings and materials, and monthly tuition can be collected automatically, with payments paused when a student takes a break — a common situation in dance, where students pause for exams, travel or injury and then return.

Its plans, as listed on its own site, are Studio at $49 per month for a booking page and payment links, and Studio Pro at $149 per month for full studio management including attendance, materials, reminders, promotions and pauses, with a custom plan priced on a call for organisations that need newsletters, donor management or ticketing. Families pay tuition only, through your own Stripe account, straight to your bank. That Stripe detail is the point to check carefully from India: before paying, confirm on the official site and with Stripe whether your Indian account, currency and payout setup are supported for your academy, and what the total cost is in rupees after conversion and gateway fees. A dollar-priced plan can look reasonable on the pricing page and feel very different after conversion, so do not commit until you have that rupee figure.

DanceHall Studios suits a school that wants one branded system — booking page, payments, calendar, materials and an AI assistant — and is willing to pay a monthly dollar subscription for it. It is not the right first step for a small academy that only needs fee reminders, and its AI assistant should be treated as a helper, not a replacement for the teacher's judgement: let it draft reminders and answer routine questions, but check anything it sends about fees, dates or refunds before it reaches a parent.

## Free attendance and enquiries: Google Forms + Sheets

Google Forms with Google Sheets is the free backbone that many small academies already use without calling it a system, and it deserves respect for exactly that reason. A teacher creates a form titled something like "Dance Class Attendance", adds name, date, batch and a comments field, and shares the link with students or parents. Responses land in a Sheet where they can be filtered by date, batch or student, and the same pattern works for trial-class enquiries, workshop registrations and recital volunteer sign-ups. It costs nothing beyond a Google account, it works on any phone, and it works in India without a VPN.

Its limits are equally plain. Forms are not built for attendance, so there is no live check-in view, no automatic reminder to an absent student, and no link between a registration form and a fee ledger unless you build that link yourself. Students must enter their own details, which invites spelling mistakes and wrong-batch selections; a drop-down list of names reduces that problem but does not remove it. As the academy grows past a few batches, the manual work of reconciling a form, a Sheet and a payment record grows faster than the student count. That is the moment to move to YourFee or a similar platform, not before. Until then, Forms plus Sheets is the honest free answer, and any article that tells a twenty-student academy to buy a platform first is selling software, not solving a problem.

## Fee reminders and parent messages: WhatsApp Business

WhatsApp Business is the free-to-download app that turns the messaging tool parents already use into a business tool, and for a dance academy that is its whole advantage: parents do not need to install anything new. The app adds a business profile, automated quick replies and away messages, labels to organise and filter conversations, a catalogue to showcase classes, and status updates to share offers or news. Labels are the underused feature here: label chats by batch, by fee status or by recital group, and the first week of the month stops being a scroll through hundreds of chats to find who has not paid.

The app itself is free to download and use, with a mix of free and paid features, according to its own store listing, and it supports a business account alongside a personal account on one device if you have two phone numbers. Two cautions apply. First, WhatsApp is a messaging tool, not a fee ledger: a parent saying "paid" in chat is not proof of payment, and every payment still needs to be recorded in your ledger or platform, with a receipt issued. Second, bulk messaging rules and spam complaints are real risks — send reminders to parents who have agreed to receive them, keep messages short and factual, and do not add numbers to broadcast lists without consent. Used that way, WhatsApp Business is the cheapest communication upgrade a dance school can make, and it pairs naturally with the payment links in the Razorpay section below.

## Posters and announcements: Canva

Canva is the design tool most small academies reach for first, and for posters, trial-class announcements, fee-date cards and recital flyers it is the right choice. Its free plan includes a drag-and-drop editor, a video editor, templates, and resizing for different feeds, with no watermark and no credit card required for the free video editor. A teacher can make a batch announcement in ten minutes, resize it for WhatsApp status, Instagram and a printed notice board, and keep the school's colours consistent across all three. That consistency matters more than fancy design: parents trust a school whose notices always look like they came from the same place.

The free plan has real limits, and they are worth naming. Premium stock photos, videos, fonts and brand-kit features sit behind the paid Pro plan, and some AI features such as background removal for video and Magic Video are limited or paid. Before paying, check the official Canva pricing page for India, because Pro pricing and the cheaper Pro Lite option have been reported at different rupee figures by different sources, and this article will not quote a figure it could not confirm from Canva's own page at the time of writing. The practical rule is simple: if the free plan makes every poster you need, stay free. Pay only when you repeatedly hit the same paywalled asset or when a brand kit would save real time every week.

## Performance reels: CapCut

CapCut is the free video editor most dance teachers use to cut class clips and performance reels for Instagram and YouTube, and its strength is speed: trim, split, splice, layer clips on a timeline, add text and transitions, and export a vertical reel without learning a professional editor. For a dance academy, that speed is the point. A thirty-second clip of a batch performing, posted the same evening, brings more trial enquiries than a polished video delivered three weeks later.

Music is where CapCut needs care, and dance schools are exactly the users who must read the licence terms. CapCut's own materials licence distinguishes between Sounds, its music library for personal entertainment and non-commercial use, and Commercial Sounds, its library for brands, businesses and commercial users, which may be shown on CapCut, TikTok and TikTok for Business but need separate permission for use elsewhere. A reel that promotes your academy is commercial use, even if the academy is small, so do not assume that any song available in the app is cleared for a promotional post. Use the commercial library for academy promotion, use original or properly licensed music for everything else, and check the licence for the specific track before posting. A muted or removed reel is a minor embarrassment; a copyright complaint against a business page is a bigger one. If you are unsure, the safe choice is a track from the commercial library or music you have a written licence for.

## Writing fast, with a human check: ChatGPT and Google Gemini

ChatGPT and Google Gemini are general AI chat tools, not dance tools, but they earn their place here because dance teachers spend surprising amounts of time writing: batch announcements, fee reminder wording, trial-class replies, recital programme notes, Instagram captions and class descriptions. A free tier of either tool can draft those in seconds, and the teacher's job becomes editing — fixing dates, fees, batch names and tone — rather than staring at a blank screen. That editing step is not optional. An AI draft will confidently invent a date, a fee or a venue if you let it, and a wrong fee in a parent message is worse than a late message.

Free limits and paid offers for these tools change frequently, and India has seen time-limited promotions that later end, so treat any "free for a year" claim with caution. Research for this article found reports that ChatGPT Go was offered free for a year in India from November 2025, that the promotion ended for new sign-ups in January 2026, and that existing free years then renew at the current Go fee unless cancelled. Those reports disagree on details, so the only safe advice is to check your own billing page or app-store subscription page for your renewal date and price before assuming anything is still free. The same rule applies to Gemini offers tied to a mobile plan: check the offer terms, the eligible age or plan conditions, and the renewal price in your own account. For a dance academy, the free tier of either tool is usually enough for drafting messages and captions; pay only when you hit message limits every week.

## Collecting fees by link: Razorpay Payment Links

Razorpay Payment Links solve the simplest fee problem: how does a parent pay without coming to the studio with cash? You create a payment link from the Razorpay dashboard, share it by SMS, email or WhatsApp, and the parent pays through UPI, cards, wallets or net banking. Features include partial payments and advances, bulk link creation from a spreadsheet, and real-time tracking of each transaction. For a dance academy, the useful pattern is a monthly link per batch, or a single link per student with the fee amount filled in, sent alongside the WhatsApp reminder.

Razorpay's own pricing page lists no setup fee and no annual maintenance charge for its payment gateway, with a flat platform fee of 2% per successful domestic transaction plus 18% GST on that fee, deducted at source, and UPI QR payments listed at a lower platform fee. Those figures were found on the provider's pricing page during research, but they can change, and the exact fee for your payment method, settlement cycle and plan should be confirmed on that page before you set fees. The honest cost question for a teacher is whether 2% plus GST is cheaper than the hours spent chasing cash and reconciling a register. For most academies above a handful of students, it is — but do the arithmetic with your own monthly collection before deciding, and never promise a parent that the full fee reaches the academy when a gateway fee is deducted first.

## Using these tools in India

India changes the practical answer in four ways. First, payments run on UPI, so any fee tool that cannot accept UPI or produce a receipt a parent trusts is a poor fit, whatever its features. Second, communication runs on WhatsApp, so a platform whose reminders arrive only by email will be ignored by half your parents. Third, language matters: many parents read Hindi or a regional language more comfortably than English, so announcements and fee reminders should be available in the language your parents actually read, and AI drafts should be checked by a person who speaks that language, not posted straight from the tool. Fourth, most dance students are children, which raises the bar on privacy: do not post a child's photo or performance video publicly without a parent's clear permission, do not share batch lists with children's names outside the school, and keep attendance and fee records in a system with proper access control rather than a shared spreadsheet link that anyone can open.

A sensible India setup for a small academy looks like this: Forms and Sheets for attendance and enquiries, WhatsApp Business with labels for reminders, Canva free for posters, CapCut with commercial music for reels, and Razorpay links for fees. That stack costs nothing except gateway fees until you choose to pay for something. A growing academy adds YourFee when the register, the reminders and the recital tickets need one home. A large or classical academy with multiple branches and an online programme considers DanceHall Studios when it wants bookings, materials, calendars and an AI assistant in one branded system. Each step up should be triggered by a real pain — missed fees, a chaotic recital, a register that no longer fits in a Sheet — not by a sales page.

## What to avoid

Do not buy an all-in-one platform before you know which job hurts most, because you will pay for five features and use one. Do not let an AI tool send fee messages, dates or refund promises without a human reading them first, because a wrong number in a parent message damages trust faster than any late message. Do not post children's photos or videos without parental permission, and do not use a popular song in a promotional reel just because the app offered it — check whether the licence covers commercial use. Do not promise a parent a deadline, a discount or a refund that depends on a tool's paid plan until you have re-checked the official pricing page, because free limits and prices change and a promotion that was free last month may renew at full price this month. Do not keep fee records only in chat messages: a chat is not a ledger, and a dispute about whether a month was paid needs a receipt, not a screenshot. Do not share attendance sheets or fee lists through open links, and do not collect more personal data from children than the academy actually needs. Finally, do not hand your whole timetable, student list and fee history to a new tool on day one — run it with one batch for a month, check that reminders, receipts and attendance all work, and only then move the rest of the school onto it.

## FAQ

**Which single tool should a small dance academy start with?**
Start with WhatsApp Business for parent communication, because that is where fee reminders actually get read, and add Google Forms and Sheets for attendance. Those two are free, work on any phone, and cover the two jobs that break first: parents not knowing, and the teacher not remembering who came and who paid.

**Is YourFee really free of commission on student fees?**
YourFee states that it charges a flat monthly subscription starting at ₹299 and takes no commission on fees, with payments settling directly to your bank through your own Razorpay or Cashfree account. That refers to YourFee's own charge; the payment gateway may still charge its own transaction fee, so confirm both charges on the official pages before setting your fee structure.

**Can I run a dance academy on free tools alone?**
Yes, up to a point. Forms, Sheets, WhatsApp Business, Canva free and CapCut free cover attendance, reminders, posters and reels for a small academy. Free tools stop being enough when reconciling fees, attendance and recital tickets takes more hours than teaching, or when more than a few batches make a spreadsheet unreliable — that is when a paid platform earns its subscription.

**Do these tools work in India without a VPN?**
Yes, for the tools in this article. YourFee, Razorpay, WhatsApp Business, Canva, CapCut, ChatGPT, Google Gemini and Google Forms and Sheets are all available in India through their normal apps and websites without a VPN. If a tool ever asks you to use a VPN, treat that as a warning sign and look for an alternative, because support and payments become unreliable.

**How do I handle fees for students who pause for exams or injury?**
Record the pause in your ledger with a start date and an expected return date, stop the reminders for the paused months, and restart them on return. DanceHall Studios explicitly supports pausing payments when a student takes a break, and YourFee supports carry-forward and batch-level fee tracking that makes a pause auditable. Whatever tool you use, confirm the pause to the parent in writing so there is no dispute later about which months were charged.

**What about music rights for dance reels posted online?**
Use music you have a licence for, or tracks from a commercial library that covers business promotion on the platform where you post. CapCut's personal Sounds library is described for personal entertainment and non-commercial use, while its Commercial Sounds library is the one intended for brands and businesses, with its own platform limits. When in doubt, use a commercial track or licensed music, and keep a note of which track and licence you used for each promotional post.

**Should I post children's performance videos publicly?**
Only with a parent's clear permission, and preferably with a written consent taken at admission that covers photos and videos, the platforms they may appear on, and the right to ask for removal. Even with consent, avoid posting full names, school names or batch lists alongside a child's video, and never post a video a parent has asked you to take down.

**Can AI write my fee reminders and class announcements?**
Yes, as drafts. ChatGPT or Gemini can draft a polite reminder, a trial-class reply or a recital announcement in seconds, but a human must check every date, fee, batch name and venue before sending. AI tools can invent details confidently, and a wrong fee or date in a parent message is a trust problem, not a typo.

**When is it worth paying for a dance school platform?**
When fee-chasing, attendance reconciliation or recital ticketing regularly eats teaching time, or when the student count makes a spreadsheet unreliable. Pay for the job that hurts: a fee and recital platform for fee and show chaos, a design or video upgrade for poster and reel bottlenecks, and nothing at all for jobs that the free tools already handle.

**Are prices in this article guaranteed?**
No. Prices, free limits and promotions change, and some figures here come from provider pages or reputable reports found during research rather than a live checkout. Re-check the official pricing and help page of any tool before paying, and check your own billing or subscription page for renewal dates and prices before promising a parent a deadline or a discount.

Start free with Forms, WhatsApp Business and Canva; add YourFee from ₹299/month only when fee-chasing eats your teaching time.
