A stock broker in India juggles three jobs: finding ideas, explaining them, and staying inside SEBI rules. AI tools can help with all three, but none replaces the broker's judgement or licence. The tools below work in India without a VPN, with a usable free tier.

**Short answer:** Start with TradingView for charts, Screener.in for fundamentals. Add Trendlyne for analyst consensus, Tickertape for simple screens, and ChatGPT for drafting notes — always reviewed by a human first.

## 1. TradingView — charts, screeners and alerts

TradingView is the charting standard most Indian brokers already know. Its free Basic plan covers NSE and BSE stocks, with a screener of more than 168 filters and Pine Script. Free limits are real: one chart per tab, two indicators per chart, three price alerts, data delayed about 15 minutes. Real-time data and export are paid. Use it for a morning watchlist — it never places orders.

## 2. Screener.in — fundamental research on Indian companies

Screener.in is the strongest free tool for a company's financials. Its free plan lets you write your own screening queries — high sales growth, high return on equity, low debt — across roughly 5,000 listed companies, with ten-plus years of financial statements. Downloading results and peer comparison sit behind the paid plan, listed at about ₹4,999 a year — check the site before paying. For a broker, this is where a chart idea meets reality.

## 3. Trendlyne — analyst consensus, DVM scores and deal data

Trendlyne suits brokers who want analyst-side data in one place. Its free tier gives basic screeners and limited financial data. Paid GuruQ, about ₹290 a month, adds real-time alerts, backtesting and Forecaster consensus estimates. Trendlyne tracks buy, sell and hold calls from 40-plus brokerages, shows DVM scores, and surfaces bulk deals and insider trades. Use it to answer "what do analysts think?"

## 4. Tickertape — simple screens and portfolio health checks

Tickertape is the friendliest tool here for non-analyst clients. Its free plan includes stock and mutual fund pages and a basic screener with more than 200 filters and 15-plus pre-built screens. Pro, from about ₹249 a month, adds premium filters, forecasts, export and red-flag alerts. For a broker, the best use is client-facing: walk a client through their portfolio's red flags on a screen share.

## 5. ChatGPT — drafting research notes and client explanations

ChatGPT is the drafting assistant, not the analyst. Its free tier offers everyday text chat — OpenAI's FAQ describes it as unlimited, subject to abuse-prevention safeguards — with uploads, images and voice capped separately. Sources disagree on exact limits because OpenAI has changed them before, so treat any number as temporary. In India, ChatGPT Go costs about ₹399 a month. Use it to turn a Screener.in finding into a plain-English client note. Never paste client names, PAN numbers or account details into a chatbot, and never send an AI draft unchecked.

## Using these tools in an Indian broking business

A workable routine: in the morning, scan TradingView alerts and the Trendlyne newsfeed for overnight moves in client holdings. Mid-morning, run one Screener.in query and check it against analyst consensus. In the afternoon, use Tickertape to review flagged portfolios and draft the client update in ChatGPT. Keep a log of every recommendation you share — what was said, when, and on what data — because SEBI's Research Analyst Regulations, 2014 require research reports and their rationale to be recorded; a WhatsApp message counts too. Only a SEBI-registered research analyst or investment adviser may give personalised advice.

## What to avoid

Do not let any AI tool generate a buy, sell or hold call for clients unchecked — that is a recommendation, with compliance consequences. Never promise guaranteed returns. Do not feed client data into free chatbots, and do not trust a delayed price as live. Avoid "AI stock picker" apps that claim to predict prices. And do not build your whole workflow on one free tier: limits change.

## FAQ

**Can a stock broker use AI tools to give stock recommendations to clients?**
A broker can use AI tools for research and drafting, but a recommendation itself is regulated. Only SEBI-registered research analysts and investment advisers may give formal recommendations or personalised advice, with the required disclosures, and recorded.

**Is the TradingView free plan enough for a small broking firm?**
For learning and basic watchlists, yes. For active client work, two indicators, three alerts and delayed data become the bottleneck.

**Do these tools work in India without a VPN?**
Yes — all five work in India without a VPN, and several have India-specific pricing. Confirm the current price on the official site before subscribing.

**What records must a broker keep when sharing research?**
Research reports, the rationale behind each recommendation, copies of client communications, and records of public appearances must be maintained under SEBI's Research Analyst Regulations. Check the current rules and your compliance adviser.

TradingView plus Screener.in covers most broker research free; add Trendlyne for analyst data, and never send an AI-written recommendation unchecked.
