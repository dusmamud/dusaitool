Self-publishing in India no longer means finding a printer, printing a thousand copies, and hoping a bookshop stocks them. An author in Pune, Patna, or Kochi can now write a book with AI help, design a cover on a phone, format it for free, and sell the ebook on Amazon India the same week — while print copies are made only when a reader orders one. The catch is that not every tool in a global "best AI tools" list works the same way for an Indian author. Prices are in dollars, some print options do not reach Amazon India at all, and the royalty you actually earn depends on choices like KDP Select that are easy to get wrong.

The short answer is this: draft with a free AI chatbot such as ChatGPT, Gemini, or Claude, clean the language with Grammarly free, design the cover in Canva free, format the ebook with Amazon's own free Kindle Create or with Reedsy Studio, and publish the ebook through Kindle Direct Publishing (KDP), which is free to use. For print in India, add an Indian print-on-demand platform such as Pothi.com or Notion Press, because KDP's own paperback distribution does not cover Amazon India — a fact Amazon's own help pages state plainly, and one that surprises many first-time Indian authors. If you want bookshops and libraries abroad as well, IngramSpark's Global Connect programme includes India among its print-partner countries. Get a free ISBN from India's official ISBN agency before you need one, keep your rights, and do not pay for a "publishing package" until you know exactly what it adds beyond these free tools.

That stack costs nothing to start, and it covers the whole journey: idea, draft, edit, cover, format, ISBN, publish, and sell. Everything after that — a paid editor, a professional cover designer, a formatting tool such as Atticus — is an upgrade you buy once a book is earning or once a specific weakness is costing you readers. This article walks through each stage with honest free limits, India-specific warnings, and the mistakes that cost Indian authors the most money.

## Writing and drafting: ChatGPT, Gemini, and Claude

Every self-publishing workflow starts with the same problem: a blank page and a book that only exists in your head. AI chatbots do not write a good book for you, but they are excellent at turning a rough idea into an outline, turning an outline into a first draft of a chapter, and turning a finished chapter into a back-cover blurb, a chapter summary, or ten possible titles. The trick is to use them for structure and momentum, and to keep the facts, stories, and voice firmly yours.

ChatGPT's free tier is the simplest starting point for most Indian authors. It is available in India without a VPN, handles everyday text chat, web search, file uploads, and image creation, and places separate caps on extras such as image generation and voice rather than one single message count. That makes it a good daily drafting tool: outline a chapter in the morning, draft a section after lunch, and ask for a tighter rewrite in the evening. Its weakness is exactly its fluency. It will happily invent a statistic, a quotation, or a historical detail that sounds right, so every factual claim in a nonfiction book must be checked against a real source before publication. For fiction, the risk is different: long books drift, characters change names, and plots contradict themselves, so keep a short "story bible" — a document listing characters, places, and rules of your world — and paste the relevant part into each new chat.

Gemini's free plan suits authors who already live in Google Docs and Gmail. Its free limits work as a compute budget that refreshes over time up to a weekly cap, rather than a simple message count, and long conversations use more of that budget, so starting a fresh chat for each chapter is practical advice, not just tidiness. Gemini is convenient for research summaries and for brainstorming inside the Google apps where many Indian authors already write. Its free context window is smaller than the paid plans, so do not paste an entire 300-page manuscript into one chat and expect a careful edit; work chapter by chapter instead.

Claude's free plan is the strongest of the three for long, structured documents, with tools for files, web search, and document creation, but it has a rolling session limit and no fixed published message count, so a long afternoon of editing can run out mid-chapter. Use it in focused sessions: one chapter, one clear job, such as tightening dialogue or checking that an argument flows. If you hit the limit, switch to another free tool rather than paying in frustration; that rotation is a legitimate free-tier strategy, as long as you keep your manuscript organised and never paste anything you would not want stored on a third-party server.

A word on originality and honesty that matters more for authors than for bloggers. Readers forgive a rough first book; they do not forgive a book that turns out to be stitched together from AI output with no author in it. Amazon and other stores also have their own disclosure expectations for AI-assisted content, and those rules change, so check KDP's current content guidelines before you publish, not after. The safest rule is simple: AI drafts, you decide. Keep your notes, your interviews, your experiences, and your revisions. The book should still be yours if every AI sentence were deleted.

## Editing: Grammarly free, then a human

AI drafting creates a second problem: a manuscript full of small errors that the author can no longer see. Grammarly's free plan catches grammar, spelling, and basic clarity problems in English, and it is genuinely useful as a first pass, especially for authors writing in English as a second language — a large share of Indian self-published authors. Run it chapter by chapter, accept the obvious fixes, and reject anything that changes your meaning or flattens your voice. Grammarly free does not replace a proofreader. It will miss a wrong date, a repeated paragraph, a character who changes age between chapters, and a joke that only works in Hindi.

The honest editing order for a zero-budget book is: first, your own read-aloud pass, because hearing the text catches clumsy sentences that eyes skip; second, Grammarly or a similar free checker for surface errors; third, two or three beta readers who actually read books in your genre and will tell you where they got bored; and only then, if the budget exists, a paid human editor for the final polish. For books that make specialist claims — health, finance, law, history — a subject expert matters more than any AI tool, and no chatbot should be your only source. This article is general information only, not professional advice, and for paid plans, legal questions, or tax questions about royalties, check the official source or a professional before deciding.

## Covers: Canva free, and why thumbnails decide sales

Readers judge a book by its cover in about two seconds, on a phone screen, at thumbnail size. That is the single most important design fact in self-publishing, and it is why cover tools deserve more attention than most authors give them. Canva's free tier remains the best zero-cost way to make a cover: ebook-sized templates, typography that behaves, and exports large enough for KDP's uploader. Amazon recommends a high-resolution cover image, and Canva's own cover guide discusses sizes around the Kindle recommendation — aim for a tall, high-resolution JPEG and check the official KDP cover requirements before uploading, because a rejected cover delays launch by days.

Design small, not large. Shrink your cover to the size of a postage stamp on your phone screen and ask three questions: can you read the title, can you tell the genre, and does one image dominate? If the title is unreadable small, the cover fails whatever it looks like large. Keep the title to a few words in a bold font, keep the author name smaller than the title unless you are already famous, and use one strong image rather than five small ones. AI image generators can supply backgrounds and textures, and the guide to [free AI image generators](/blog/free-ai-image-generators-india/) covers the free options, but keep a human in charge of the title treatment: legibility at thumbnail size is a design decision, not a generation prompt.

There are two cover traps for Indian authors. The first is using a stock photo or AI image that looks fine but is used by twenty other books in the same genre; a reader who has seen the same image three times will skip yours. The second is the opposite: paying a designer before the manuscript is ready, then rewriting the book so the cover no longer fits. Finish the manuscript first, then brief a designer — or finish the cover in Canva, publish, and upgrade the cover later once sales prove the book has an audience. A cover can be changed after publication; a bad first impression can also be repaired, but only if you notice it, which is why checking your own listing on a phone matters.

For general design principles beyond covers, the guide to [AI tools for graphic design beginners](/blog/ai-tools-for-graphic-design-beginners-india/) is a useful companion, and it covers the same free-first approach.

## Formatting: Kindle Create, Reedsy Studio, Atticus, and Vellum

Formatting is the unglamorous stage where many good manuscripts stall. A book that looks fine in Google Docs can arrive on a Kindle with broken chapters, missing contents, or images floating in the wrong place. The input matters more than the tool: one font, real heading styles for chapters, chapter breaks as real breaks, and images kept simple and centred. If your manuscript uses a different font size for every paragraph and manual line breaks instead of styles, no formatter will save it without cleanup first.

Kindle Create is Amazon's own free tool for Windows and Mac, and it converts a clean manuscript into a Kindle-ready file with a clickable table of contents. If you only sell on Amazon, it is the safest choice, because compatibility with Kindle is guaranteed by the maker of the device. Its limitation is the mirror image of its strength: it locks you into the Kindle format, so authors who also want a clean EPUB for other stores need another tool.

Reedsy Studio is free to use on the web, with paid extras, and produces both ebook and print files from professional presets. It is a good middle path for authors who want one manuscript to become both an ebook and a paperback without learning a complex desktop app. Draft2Digital offers another free route: it converts manuscripts and distributes ebooks, taking about 10% of the retail price as its share rather than charging upfront, which suits authors who would rather share revenue than pay before a single sale.

Atticus, at about $147 as a one-time payment in one recent comparison, is the cross-platform professional formatter many prolific indie authors settle on, with themes for ebook and print. Vellum, at about $200 to $250 in the same comparison, is often praised for slightly more polished output but runs only on Mac, which rules it out for most Indian authors writing on Windows or a phone. Scrivener, at about $49 to $59 one-time, is a writing and compiling tool rather than an AI tool, and it rewards authors willing to learn its compile system with maximum control. None of these formatting tools is an AI tool in the chatbot sense, and that distinction matters: in formatting, you are paying for templates, typography, and reliable exports, not for a machine that writes for you. Buy Atticus or Vellum only when you have more than one book to format or when a free tool has already failed you on a specific book; for a first book, Kindle Create or Reedsy Studio is enough.

Whatever tool you choose, preview before you publish. KDP's own previewer shows how the book looks on a phone, a tablet, and an e-ink Kindle, and that ten-minute check catches most disasters: a contents page that links nowhere, a chapter that starts mid-sentence, or an image that covers the text. Print files need a second check: margins, page numbers, and a cover that includes the spine and back, not just the front.

## Publishing and distribution in India: KDP, Pothi, Notion Press, and IngramSpark

This is the section where India-specific facts change the plan, so read it before choosing a platform. Kindle Direct Publishing is free to use and lets you publish ebooks for the Kindle store, including Amazon India. For ebooks, you choose between a 35% royalty option and a 70% royalty option. The 70% option applies only in certain territories and carries list-price requirements: for the India Kindle store, KDP's help pages list a 70% royalty price band of ₹99 to ₹599, with different minimum prices under the 35% option depending on file size. Crucially, for sales to customers in India, a book must also be enrolled in KDP Select to be eligible for the 70% royalty. KDP Select is a free 90-day programme, but it requires exclusivity: during the enrolment period, the ebook can only be distributed through KDP and public libraries, although print and other formats can still be sold elsewhere. That exclusivity is the price of Kindle Unlimited page-read income, and it is a bad deal if your main audience is on another store — so decide on exclusivity deliberately, not by accident.

KDP also offers print books with up to 60% royalties on print sales according to its earnings pages, but here comes the India catch: Amazon's own paperback help page states that Amazon India, along with Brazil and Mexico, does not support paperback and hardcover distribution. In plain language, an Indian reader browsing Amazon India will not find your KDP paperback the way an American reader finds one on Amazon America. Your KDP ebook reaches Indian Kindle readers; your KDP paperback does not reliably reach Indian print buyers. Many guides skip this, and authors discover it only after launch. Plan around it from day one.

That is why Indian print-on-demand platforms matter. Pothi.com is built for exactly this gap: self-publishing and print-on-demand in India, with no minimum order quantity, so you can print one copy or ten thousand, and distribution through its own store plus, with extended distribution, other online stores including Amazon India and Flipkart. Notion Press, another major Indian platform, states that authors retain all rights under a non-exclusive agreement, that print is printed on demand based on sales, and that platform authors receive 80% of net profits depending on the distribution plan, while its Outpublish programme offers 100% of net profits — but its package prices vary widely, with comparisons quoting roughly ₹8,000 to ₹80,000, so treat any single quoted price as a starting point to verify on Notion Press's own current plans page, not as a fixed fact. The honest way to compare Indian platforms is on four questions: who owns the rights, what share of net profit you keep, where the print book is actually sold, and what happens if you want to leave. Rights and exit terms matter more than a launch discount.

For readers outside India, IngramSpark is the main route to bookshops, libraries, and retailers that do not buy through Amazon. Its distribution network includes print locations in the United States, the United Kingdom, Australia, and Sharjah, and its Global Connect programme works with print partners in countries including India, so eligible books can be printed closer to local demand rather than shipped across the world. One publisher guide notes that creating an account and uploading a print or ebook file is free, while distribution terms and any access fee should be checked on IngramSpark's current pages before uploading. Understand distribution precisely: being listed through IngramSpark means booksellers and libraries can discover and order your book; it does not guarantee that any particular shop will stock it on a shelf. Marketing is still your job.

Draft2Digital sits between KDP and IngramSpark: free to use, about a 10% share of the retail price, ebook distribution to many stores, and print options. A sensible India-first strategy for most first-time authors is therefore: ebook on KDP, with or without KDP Select depending on your exclusivity decision; print in India through Pothi.com or Notion Press; and, once the book proves itself, wider distribution through Draft2Digital or IngramSpark. You do not need all four on day one. Launching in too many places at once multiplies the metadata, tax forms, and dashboards you must manage, and a book with one good listing beats a book with five neglected ones.

## The India-use section: ISBN, tax, payments, and language

Four practical matters decide whether an Indian author actually gets paid and actually gets found. The first is the ISBN. In India, ISBNs are provided free of cost through the Raja Rammohun Roy National Agency for ISBN, under the Ministry of Education, and the agency's own pages say so directly. Apply through isbn.gov.in as the publisher — which can be you, as a self-publishing author — and remember that different formats and editions generally need different ISBNs: your ebook, paperback, and any revised edition are not the same book for ISBN purposes. Some platforms offer a free ISBN of their own, but a platform ISBN usually lists the platform as publisher, which affects how the book appears in catalogues; your own free Indian ISBN keeps you listed as publisher. Apply early, because approval takes time and a launch date should never depend on an ISBN that has not arrived.

The second is tax and payment paperwork. KDP and other global platforms ask for tax information before paying royalties, and Indian authors commonly deal with withholding-tax forms and bank details for international payments. This article does not give tax advice — the rules depend on your situation, and a chartered accountant or the platform's own tax help pages are the right sources — but do not skip the paperwork. Royalties can sit unpaid while a form is incomplete, and that is a common, avoidable disappointment. Keep a simple spreadsheet from day one: platform, book, month, sales, royalty, payment date, and any fee deducted. When a payment looks wrong six months later, that spreadsheet is your evidence.

The third is language. India is not one book market but many, and AI tools handle that unevenly. English drafting help is strong; Hindi and other Indian-language drafting, editing, and cover-text support is improving but still needs careful human checking, especially for grammar, idiom, and script rendering on covers and in ebook files. If you write in Hindi, Tamil, Bengali, Marathi, or another Indian language, test the ebook file on a real phone and a real Kindle app before launch, because a font that looks fine in Google Docs can break in an EPUB. For translation between Hindi and English, check the guide to [AI translation tools for Hindi and English](/blog/ai-translation-tools-hindi-english-india/) alongside this article, and treat machine translation as a draft, never as a finished translation of a book you put your name on.

The fourth is pricing in rupees. A price that looks modest in dollars can feel expensive to an Indian reader, and a price that feels right in India can fall below a platform's minimum for a royalty tier. Check KDP's India price bands before setting a price, look at what comparable Indian indie books charge, and remember that Kindle Unlimited readers do not buy at all — they borrow, and you earn per page read from a shared fund. Price for your actual reader, not for a global average, and revisit the price after the first month of real sales data rather than guessing twice.

## What to avoid

Avoid paying for a publishing package before you have tried the free stack. Many packages bundle a cover, formatting, and an ISBN — all things this article shows you can do free or cheaply — and charge thousands of rupees for the bundle plus a share of royalties. If a package is worth it, the seller should be able to say exactly which item you could not do yourself and why; vague promises about "marketing" and "exposure" are not an answer.

Avoid AI-generated covers that use a real person's face, a trademarked character, or a famous author's name in the prompt. You can be refused publication, or worse, face a complaint, over an image you did not create and do not own. The same applies to text: do not publish AI output that reproduces copyrighted lyrics, long quotations, or passages from another book. AI tools do not check copyright for you.

Avoid enrolling in KDP Select without understanding exclusivity. If your book is already selling on another ebook store, or you have promised it to a library platform, enrolling locks you into Kindle-only distribution for 90 days at a time. Read the current KDP Select requirements on KDP's own help pages before tapping enrol, and set a calendar reminder for the renewal date.

Avoid the "publish and pray" launch. A book with no description, no categories, no keywords, and no launch message to anyone will sell to nobody, however good the writing is. Spend one day on metadata — a clear subtitle, seven honest keywords, the right categories, and a description whose first two lines work on a phone screen — and one day telling the people who already care: friends, a mailing list, a community, a social account. That beats any paid promotion tool at this stage.

Avoid promising a deadline that depends on a tool, a platform review, or an ISBN approval you do not control. KDP review, print proof copies, and ISBN processing all take variable time. Announce a launch window, not a single date, until the files are approved and the proof copy is in your hands.

Avoid spending on ads before the book's page is ready. Paying to send readers to a listing with a blurry cover, no reviews, and a description full of errors wastes money and teaches the ad platform that your book does not convert. Fix the page first, get ten honest early readers, and only then test a small ad budget you can afford to lose.

## FAQ

**Which AI tool is best for writing a book in India?**
For most Indian authors, ChatGPT free is the best starting point because it is available without a VPN, handles everyday drafting, outlines, and rewrites, and is easy to use on a phone. Gemini free suits authors already in Google Docs, and Claude free suits focused long-document editing sessions. None of them writes a publishable book alone; use them for drafts and structure, then rewrite in your own voice and verify every fact.

**Is self-publishing really free in India?**
Publishing itself can be free: KDP charges nothing to publish an ebook, Kindle Create and Reedsy Studio format for free, Canva's free tier makes a cover, and India's official ISBN is free of cost. What costs money is optional: a human editor, a professional cover, a paid formatter such as Atticus, or a guided platform package. Start free, and spend only where a specific weakness is costing you readers.

**Can I earn 70% royalty on Amazon India?**
Yes, but only under conditions. KDP's help pages list a 70% royalty price band of ₹99 to ₹599 for the India Kindle store, and for sales to customers in India, the book must also be enrolled in KDP Select to qualify for 70%. Outside those conditions, or outside the eligible territories, the 35% option applies. Check KDP's current pricing pages before setting a price, because bands and requirements change.

**Does KDP paperback work for readers on Amazon India?**
No, not in the way most authors expect. Amazon's own paperback help page states that Amazon India does not support paperback and hardcover distribution. Your KDP ebook reaches Indian Kindle readers, but for Indian print buyers, use an Indian print-on-demand platform such as Pothi.com or Notion Press, or a global distributor with Indian print partners such as IngramSpark.

**Do I need an ISBN to self-publish in India?**
You need an ISBN for a book to be properly listed, ordered, and catalogued, especially in print, though some platforms let you publish an ebook with their own identifier. In India, ISBNs are free through the Raja Rammohun Roy National Agency at isbn.gov.in, and different editions and formats generally need separate ISBNs. Apply early and keep your own ISBN if you want to be listed as publisher.

**Should I enrol my book in KDP Select?**
Enrol only if you understand the trade. KDP Select is free and 90 days at a time, gives access to Kindle Unlimited page-read income and promotional tools, and is required for the 70% royalty on Indian sales, but it requires the ebook to be exclusive to the Kindle Store during enrolment, apart from public libraries. If your readers are mainly on other stores, stay wide and skip Select.

**Can AI design a good book cover?**
AI and template tools can produce a usable cover, and Canva free is the best zero-cost starting point, but the cover still needs your judgement at thumbnail size. Check that the title is readable when shrunk to phone size, that the genre is obvious, and that the image is not the same stock photo every book in your genre uses. For a book that carries your professional reputation, a human designer is usually the highest-value money you will spend.

**How do I sell print copies in India without stocking books?**
Use print-on-demand. Pothi.com prints from one copy upward with no minimum order, Notion Press prints on demand based on sales, and IngramSpark's Global Connect programme includes print partners in India. A reader orders, a copy is printed and shipped, and you never hold inventory. Check each platform's current distribution list and profit share before choosing, because both change.

**What should I check before paying for any publishing tool?**
Re-check the official pricing and help page on the day you pay, confirm whether the price is one-time or monthly, confirm whether it works in India without a VPN, and confirm what happens to your files and rights if you cancel. Prices and limits change often, and a comparison article — including this one — can go out of date. Never promise a launch date or a royalty figure to anyone based on a price you have not re-checked.

**Is it safe to paste my whole manuscript into a free AI tool?**
Treat free AI tools as public workspaces with good intentions but no guarantee of privacy. Do not paste unpublished work you cannot afford to leak, personal details about real people, or anything under a confidentiality agreement. Work chapter by chapter, remove sensitive details before pasting, and keep your master manuscript offline or in your own account. For a book with legal or safety implications, get professional advice rather than relying on a chatbot.

## Final verdict

Start free: draft with ChatGPT, design the cover in Canva, format in Kindle Create, and publish on KDP — pay only for editing or a pro cover.

That sentence is the whole strategy in one line, and it survives contact with the India-specific facts: KDP reaches your ebook readers, Indian print-on-demand reaches your print readers, a free ISBN keeps you as publisher, and AI handles the drafting, cover, and formatting labour that used to cost a first-time author months or thousands of rupees. Your job is the part no tool can do: a book worth reading, a cover worth clicking, a description worth trusting, and the patience to publish, learn from real sales, and write the next one better. Start with one book, one free stack, and one honest launch — and re-check every price and rule on the official page before you spend a rupee or promise a date.
