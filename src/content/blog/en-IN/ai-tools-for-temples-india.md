Running a temple in India means practical problems: long queues, a receipt book that never matches the cash box, and donors who want a proper 80G receipt. Most tools that fix these jobs are not really AI.

Start with three tools: Netra for receipts, puja bookings, and darshan tickets; Razorpay Payment Links for online donations, with no website or setup fee; and WhatsApp Business for timings and festival updates. Add Sri Mandir for online pujas, and Google Forms for sign-ups and rotas.

Prices and free limits change — check the official pricing page yourself before you pay.

## 1. Netra by SwaDharma — receipts and queues

Netra, built in Mumbai by 3ioNetra, replaces the receipt book, the seva register, and the tally sheet. Its counter has three lanes — puja, donation, darshan ticket — so one family gets one numbered, QR-coded receipt. Token numbers, daily limits, and 48 darshan slots per ticket type keep queues in order. It runs in seven Indian languages. The counter costs ₹999 a month after a three-month free trial; a kiosk is ₹999 a month per device, hardware extra. Check the pricing page first.

## 2. Sri Mandir — online pujas for distant devotees

Sri Mandir is a devotee-facing app, not a management tool, and that is why it belongs here. It connects devotees to temples across India, lets them book a puja in their name and gotra, and shares a video afterwards. Listings say 50+ temples, newer ones 100+. The app is free to download, but pujas and chadhava are paid per booking. Offerings take one to two days; videos three to five. For temples, listing pujas where devotees already are beats building an app nobody downloads.

## 3. Razorpay Payment Links — donations without a website

Razorpay Payment Links fit temples with no website and no developer. Create a link and share it on WhatsApp, SMS, or a notice board QR code. Devotees pay by UPI, card, net banking, or wallet, and money settles into the trust's bank account. No setup fee or annual charge — you pay only on success, about 2% plus 18% GST on that fee for standard domestic payments. The catch is KYC: the trust's PAN, bank account, and registration documents. Start early, and never promise an 80G receipt you cannot legally issue.

## 4. WhatsApp Business — updates your devotees actually read

WhatsApp Business is free, and the most useful communication tool for a small temple. You get a business profile with address and timings, a catalogue of sevas and prices, quick replies, and automated greeting and away messages. Labels tag chats like "monthly donor" or "volunteer". Broadcast lists hold 256 contacts each and reach only people who saved your number — ask devotees to save it, never add anyone without consent, and use a dedicated temple number. If volume outgrows one phone, the paid Platform charges per template message.

## 5. Google Forms — free sign-ups for events and volunteers

Google Forms is free with a Google account and sends every answer into a Google Sheet automatically. That suits bhajan registrations, bhandara rotas, hall bookings, and festival feedback. Set a closing date and share the link on WhatsApp in seconds. It collects information but takes no payments, approves no bookings, and sends no confirmations. A paid event needs a payment link beside the form; a hall booking needs a human to confirm. ## Using these tools in India

Pick tools in your devotees' languages — Netra covers seven Indian languages. Most devotees pay by UPI, so test your donation link with a ₹10 payment before the festival. Keep a paper backup — festival data is patchy.

## What to avoid

Do not buy big all-in-one temple software before trying the free tools for a month. Do not add devotee numbers to broadcasts without consent — that is how numbers get blocked. Do not keep donation records only in one person's phone. Do not promise online darshan or prasad delivery until tested end to end. Software records money, but the committee is still responsible for it.

## Frequently asked questions

**Which tool should a small temple start with?**
Start with WhatsApp Business and Google Forms — both free. Add Razorpay for online donations, Netra when paper becomes too much.

**Can a temple accept online donations without a website?**
Yes — share a Razorpay Payment Link on WhatsApp or as a QR code. KYC documents are needed; the fee is about 2% plus GST.

**How do temples handle long queues on festival days?**
Token numbers and time slots spread arrivals out. Netra supports tokens and 48 darshan slots per ticket type. Keep a paper backup — data can fail.

**Can devotees book a puja online if they live abroad?**
Yes — Sri Mandir lets devotees book a puja in their name and gotra and receive a video.

**Do these tools issue 80G tax receipts for donations?**
Some temple software, including Netra, generates 80G-compliant receipts — but only if your trust holds 80G registration. Confirm with your accountant first.

## Final verdict

Start with Netra for receipts and queues, Razorpay for donations, and WhatsApp Business for updates. Add more only when the queue forces you.
