A visa consultancy in India lives or dies on paperwork discipline. One missing bank statement, one expired passport scan, one client who forgot a biometrics appointment, and a file that should have moved forward sits still for weeks. AI tools cannot approve a visa, cannot book an appointment on your behalf through an official channel, and cannot guarantee any outcome. What they can do is far more practical: turn a messy pile of documents into a clear checklist, draft routine letters and messages faster, remind clients before deadlines, and show you at a glance which of your fifty or five hundred files actually needs attention today.

The short answer for an Indian visa consultant is this: start free with [ChatGPT](https://chatgpt.com/) for drafts and checklists, Google Drive with Docs and Sheets for storing and organising documents, Trello for tracking every client file on a board, and WhatsApp Business for client follow-ups. Add [Gemini](https://gemini.google.com/) if your office already lives inside Gmail and Google Docs, [Notion](https://www.notion.com/) when you want one database for clients, documents and deadlines instead of many spreadsheets, [Grammarly](https://www.grammarly.com/) for cleaning up cover letters and emails, and [Perplexity](https://www.perplexity.ai/) when you need sourced answers about a rule change. Do not pay for anything in your first month. Run your real files through the free tiers, find the one bottleneck that actually slows you down, and pay only for that.

This article is written for India on purpose: a normal phone, a normal laptop, UPI payment, no VPN, and clients who may write to you in Hindi, Punjabi, Gujarati, Malayalam or English. Every tool below works in India without a VPN. Prices and free limits below are what official help pages, pricing pages and reputable reporting showed at research time, and they change often — sources already disagree on some limits, and those disagreements are flagged where they matter. Re-check the official pricing and help page before paying, and never promise a client a deadline or an outcome based on an AI answer alone. This article is general information only, not professional, legal or immigration advice. Visa rules, fees and document requirements are decided by embassies, consulates and governments, not by any tool. Always check the official embassy, consulate or visa application centre page, and talk to a qualified professional where needed.

## ChatGPT: drafts, checklists and first-pass explanations

ChatGPT is the most useful general drafting tool for a visa office, and also the easiest to misuse. Use it to turn a rough idea into a clean first draft: a document checklist for a Canada visitor visa, a polite follow-up message to a client who has not sent bank statements, a cover-letter outline, or a plain-language explanation of why a consulate asks for travel history. Then a human edits every word before it goes to a client or into a file.

At research time, reporting on Indian pricing described a free plan at no cost with access to the current flagship model, limited file uploads, slower image generation and restricted memory, a mid-tier Go plan priced for India, a Plus plan at around 1,999 rupees per month, and a Pro plan at around 19,900 rupees per month. One November 2025 report also described ChatGPT Go being offered free in India for a year. Sources disagree on which offer is live on any given day, which is exactly why this article does not ask you to trust a price quoted here. Open the official pricing page on the day you plan to pay and read what it says for India.

The safety rules matter more than the price. Never paste a complete passport, bank statement, salary slip or medical report into a general chatbot. If you need help with a document, work from a redacted copy or a description: "a six-month bank statement with three large unexplained deposits" rather than the statement itself. Never let a chatbot invent a rule, a fee or a processing time and pass it to a client. If ChatGPT tells you a fee or a timeline, treat it as a lead, open the official embassy or visa centre page, and only then write to the client. For email drafting in general, our guide to [AI tools for email writing](/blog/ai-tools-for-email-writing-india/) covers tone and follow-up habits that apply directly to client mail.

## Gemini: the drafting tool if you already live in Google

Gemini is Google's AI assistant, and its advantage for a visa office is location, not magic. If your team already writes in Google Docs, stores files in Drive and answers clients in Gmail, Gemini sits inside those apps and can draft, summarise and tidy text where you already work. That saves the copy-paste round trip that a separate chatbot forces on you.

The free plan gives access to a fast model with limited access to the more advanced model, plus tools such as Deep Research, image generation and NotebookLM for research and writing help, and the same 15 GB of storage shared across Photos, Drive and Gmail. Paid Google AI Pro in India was reported at around 1,950 rupees per month, with a much higher Ultra tier above it. Reports also disagreed on exact free prompt counts, with one report saying the Indian support page still used vague terms such as "General Access" and "Basic Access" rather than exact numbers. The honest statement is therefore this: the free tier is genuinely useful for drafting, but its exact daily limits are not clearly published for India, so test it with your real workload for a week before building a process around it.

Use Gemini the same way you would use ChatGPT: drafts first, human check second, official source third. A Gemini summary of a long client email thread is a fine starting point for your notes. A Gemini answer about a visa rule is not a source. ## Google Drive, Docs and Sheets: the filing cabinet

Every visa consultancy, however small, needs one filing cabinet that the whole team understands. For most Indian offices that cabinet is Google Drive, because clients already know it, it works on cheap Android phones, and it needs no new login. A free Google account gives 15 GB of storage shared across Drive, Gmail and Photos. That sounds generous until you store colour scans of passports, bank statements and property papers for a hundred clients, so set the rule early: one folder per client, named the same way every time, with subfolders for identity, finance, travel and correspondence.

Drive's phone app can scan paper documents into searchable PDFs, which is the single most useful feature for an office that still receives paper. Open the app, scan, name the file properly, and put it in the right client folder on the spot, instead of letting a pile of paper grow on a desk. Docs turns a scan into editable text through optical character recognition, which helps when you need to copy a detail into a form, but always compare the extracted text with the original scan — character recognition misreads numbers, and a misread date of birth or account number in a form is a serious error.

Sheets is your tracker until you outgrow it. One row per client, columns for visa type, destination, documents received, documents pending, appointment date, submission date and next action, with a filter for "pending" that you check every morning. It is free, it is shareable, and it breaks only when two people edit it without rules. The moment two staff members overwrite each other's updates, move the tracking to Trello or Notion, where each client has its own card or page and changes are harder to lose.

## Trello: one card per client, nothing forgotten

Trello turns client tracking into a board you can see. Make one board per visa category — visitor, student, work — or one board per destination if one country dominates your work. Each client is a card. Each card carries a checklist of required documents, a due date for the next step, attachments or links to the Drive folder, and comments where staff record what was actually said to the client. Drag the card from "Documents pending" to "Ready to file" to "Submitted" to "Decision received", and the state of the whole office is visible in one glance.

At research time, comparison articles described the free plan as allowing around ten boards and ten collaborators per workspace, with paid plans adding calendar and timeline views and stronger admin controls. Those limits are reported by third parties and change, so check the official Trello pricing page before you promise a large team that the free plan will hold them. For a solo consultant or a team of two or three, the free plan is usually enough. For a team of ten, budget for a paid plan rather than fighting the limit.

The discipline matters more than the tool. A card with no due date is a forgotten client. A checklist copied from another country is a wrong checklist. Build one template card per visa type, review it against the official checklist every quarter, and never let a staff member keep a private list on their own phone. Private lists are how files get lost when someone is on leave.

## Notion: one database when spreadsheets stop working

Notion is the next step up from Sheets and Trello. Instead of a board of cards or a grid of rows, you build one database where each client is a page, with properties for destination, visa type, status, counsellor, appointment date and next action, and views that show the same data as a table, a board or a calendar. A checklist template can be attached to each new client page, so every file starts from the same standard.

The free plan is aimed at individuals organising personal projects, and includes databases with subtasks, dependencies and custom properties, plus a trial of Notion AI. Paid plans start at around 10 dollars per member per month for Plus and around 20 dollars for Business, priced in dollars, so check the rupee cost on the day you pay. One honest limit to know: a free workspace used by one person can hold as many pages and blocks as that person likes, but a free workspace shared by several members has a block limit, after which new blocks cannot be added until you upgrade. That means a solo consultant can live happily on the free plan, while a team should test with real shared use before committing.

Notion is powerful and therefore easy to overbuild. Do not build a twenty-property database in week one. Start with six properties — client, destination, visa type, status, next action, next action date — and add fields only when a real file proves you need them. An overbuilt system that nobody updates is worse than a simple board that everyone updates.

## Asana and the honest disagreement about its free plan

Asana is a solid alternative to Trello for teams that prefer structured projects with sections, subtasks, owners and comments. Its free Personal plan includes list, board and calendar views, unlimited tasks and projects, and many integrations, while timeline and dependency views sit on paid plans.

Here sources disagree, and the disagreement matters. Older descriptions said the free plan covered up to ten users. Newer reporting says the free Personal plan is limited to two users, with the change dated to November 2025. This article cannot settle that dispute from the sources available, and you should not plan a team around either number. Open Asana's official pricing page, check the current free user limit, and only then decide whether Asana fits your team size. If your team is larger than the free limit, Trello's free collaborator allowance or a paid plan is the honest comparison, not a hope that the old limit still applies.

## WhatsApp Business: follow-ups clients actually read

Clients ignore email and answer WhatsApp. That is simply how India works, and a visa consultancy that only emails will chase documents forever. The WhatsApp Business app is free and gives you a business profile with your address, hours and website, greeting messages for new chats, away messages outside working hours, quick replies for the messages you send fifty times a week, labels to sort chats, and a catalogue for your services.

The practical setup is small and worth doing properly. Create labels that match your pipeline: "Documents pending", "Appointment booked", "Submitted", "Decision awaited". Write five quick replies and nothing more to start: a document reminder, a checklist message, an appointment confirmation, a "we have received your documents" message, and a polite message for clients who have gone quiet. Use broadcast messages carefully and only to clients who expect them; a broadcast that feels like spam damages trust faster than a slow reply.

WhatsApp Business is a messaging tool, not a case file. A label is not a record. When a client sends a document on WhatsApp, save it to the Drive folder, update the Trello card or Notion page, and then reply. If the only copy of a passport scan lives in one staff member's WhatsApp chat, it will be lost, and that loss is a data-protection failure as well as an office failure. For small-business messaging habits in general, see our guide to [AI tools for small business](/blog/ai-tools-for-small-business-india/).

## Grammarly, Google Translate and DeepL: language tools with limits

Most visa files cross a language boundary somewhere: a cover letter written by a client whose first language is not English, a document in another language, or an email that must sound professional. Grammarly's free plan covers core grammar and spelling checks, while its generative AI prompts are limited per month — reporting at research time put the free figure at around one hundred prompts a month, with higher figures on paid plans. Grammarly also limits how many documents and words can be checked in a day and a month, so a bulk editing day can hit a ceiling. Use it to clean a draft, not to write the draft for you, and read the final letter yourself. A grammatically perfect letter that says the wrong thing is still a wrong letter.

Google Translate is free for people, covers almost two hundred and fifty languages, and handles documents, websites, camera input and conversation on a phone. It is the right tool for understanding a message quickly. DeepL covers over one hundred languages, offers glossaries and tone control on paid plans, and its free tier was reported at around fifty thousand characters per month with one file translation per month. DeepL's free texts may be used to improve its models for a limited time, while paid texts are not stored in the same way, so never run a sensitive client document through a free translator without reading its privacy terms first.

Neither tool produces a certified translation. If an embassy or consulate requires a certified or notarised translation, only a translator or process that the authority accepts will do. Machine translation is for understanding and drafting. The final submitted translation is a professional job, and claiming otherwise to a client is a serious mistake.

## Perplexity: checking a rule change without trusting a chatbot

Visa rules change. A fee moves, a document list grows, an appointment system changes. Perplexity is useful here because it answers with sources, so you can see where a claim came from and open that source yourself. Its free tier offers unlimited basic searches with a small number of Pro searches per day — reporting put the figure at around three Pro searches per day — and limited file uploads.

Use Perplexity as a research assistant, never as the authority. The workflow is: ask Perplexity what changed, open the cited official page, read the official page, and only then update your template checklist and tell clients. If Perplexity cites a news article rather than an official page, keep going until you reach the official page. A news article about a rule is not the rule. ## India-use section: building a workflow that survives real clients

A tool list is not a workflow. Here is a workflow that survives contact with real Indian clients, and it uses only the free tiers above.

First, when a client signs up, create the Drive folder, the Trello card or Notion page, and the WhatsApp label in the same sitting. Name the folder the same way every time, for example with the client's name, destination and visa type, so any staff member can find it without asking. Second, send the checklist on WhatsApp and by email on the same day, and ask the client to reply in the same thread rather than starting a new chat for every document. Third, hold a ten-minute morning check: filter for cards whose next action date is today or overdue, and message those clients before noon. Fourth, every Friday, review cards that have not moved in two weeks and call those clients. Silence is the earliest warning sign of a stuck file.

Money habits matter too. Do not collect passport scans, bank statements and fees in the same unorganised chat thread. Keep fees and receipts in your accounting system, documents in Drive, and conversation in WhatsApp, with the Trello card or Notion page linking them together. Never ask a client to pay through a personal UPI ID in a chat message without a proper invoice or receipt. Professional handling of money is part of professional handling of documents.

Language habits matter as much. Many clients understand a checklist better in their own language. Use Google Translate or DeepL to draft a Hindi, Punjabi or Malayalam version of your standard checklist, have a fluent staff member correct it once, and then reuse the corrected version for every client. A corrected template reused a hundred times is good service. A fresh machine translation sent uncorrected every time is a lottery.

Privacy is the part most small offices skip, and it is the part that hurts most when it fails. Limit Drive folder access to the staff working on that case. Do not share a folder with "anyone with the link" and then forward that link on WhatsApp. Remove access when a staff member leaves. Delete copies from personal phones once they are filed. Client documents are not marketing material, and a single leaked passport scan can destroy a consultancy's reputation permanently.

## What to avoid

Avoid any tool or person that promises a visa outcome, a guaranteed appointment slot, or influence over an embassy decision. VFS Global, which handles visa applications for many governments in India, states plainly that it does not sell appointments, that appointments are booked only through its official website, that it has no role in or influence over visa decisions, and that value-added services do not produce a quicker or positive decision. Any agent, tool or advertisement claiming otherwise is either mistaken or dishonest, and a consultancy that repeats such a claim to clients puts its own name behind a lie.

Avoid pasting unredacted client documents into free AI tools. Free tiers often use inputs to improve their models, and a passport or bank statement does not belong in a training set. Avoid letting AI write a final cover letter or statement of purpose without a human reading it end to end. Avoid keeping the only copy of anything in one person's phone, email or chat. Avoid building a system so complicated that staff quietly go back to notebooks and personal WhatsApp chats. And avoid quoting a price, a fee or a processing time from an AI answer, a social media post or a forwarded message. The official page is the only source that counts, and the client should be told that you checked it.

## FAQ

**Which free AI tool is best for a solo visa consultant in India?**

Start with ChatGPT for drafts, Google Drive for documents, Trello for tracking and WhatsApp Business for follow-ups. All four have genuinely useful free tiers in India, they need no VPN, and together they cover drafting, filing, tracking and messaging. Add other tools only when a specific bottleneck appears.

**Can AI tools check whether a visa document is valid?**

No, not in the sense that matters. AI can flag a possible mismatch, an expiry date that has passed, or a name spelled differently in two documents, and that flagging is genuinely useful. Only a trained staff member can decide whether a document is valid, complete and acceptable to a particular embassy, and only the embassy decides in the end. Treat AI as a second pair of eyes, never as the decision maker.

**Can a chatbot tell me the current visa fee or processing time?**

It can give you a figure, but you must not pass that figure to a client until you have checked the official embassy, consulate or visa application centre page yourself. Fees, document lists and timelines change, and chatbots confidently repeat outdated figures. The check takes two minutes and protects both the client and your reputation.

**Is WhatsApp Business enough to manage all my clients?**

No. WhatsApp Business is excellent for reminders and quick replies, but it is not a case management system. Labels help you find chats, but they do not track document checklists, deadlines or staff ownership. Pair it with Trello or Notion, and save every document to Drive, so the real record survives even if a phone is lost.

**Should I pay for ChatGPT Plus or Google AI Pro as a visa consultant?**

Not in your first month. Run real files through the free tiers and see where you actually hit a limit: message caps, upload caps, or the need for a tool inside Docs and Gmail. Pay for the one plan that removes the bottleneck you hit, and re-check the official pricing page for India before paying, because offers and prices change frequently.

**Can I use Google Translate or DeepL for documents I submit to an embassy?**

You can use them to understand a document and to draft a translation, but a submitted translation must meet whatever the embassy or consulate requires, which may mean a certified or authorised translator. Machine translation alone is not a certified translation, and submitting it as one can delay or damage an application.

**How do I protect client documents when using AI tools?**

Keep the real documents in Drive with access limited to staff on the case, never share folders with "anyone with the link", redact documents before showing them to any general AI tool, remove access when staff leave, and delete working copies from personal phones once filed. Read the privacy terms of any free tool before uploading client material to it.

**What should I do when two sources disagree about a free limit or a price?**

Trust neither until you have opened the tool's official pricing or help page yourself. Third-party comparisons go out of date quickly, and this article itself will go out of date. The official page on the day you decide is the only figure worth acting on, and the same rule applies to visa fees and document lists.

**Do I need a dedicated visa case management system instead of Trello or Notion?**

Not at first. Dedicated case systems exist, and some Indian firms have built pathway-based systems where each visa type generates its own checklist, deadlines and reminders. Those systems are worth considering once you handle hundreds of files across branches. Until then, a disciplined Trello board or Notion database, used by everyone every day, beats an expensive system that half the team ignores.

**Can AI write a cover letter or statement of purpose for my client?**

AI can draft one, and a good draft saves time. But the final letter must be true, personal and checked by a human who knows the client's real story. A generic AI letter that any applicant could have written weakens an application, and a letter containing an invented job, income or travel date is a serious problem. Draft with AI, finish with a human.

## Final verdict

Start free with ChatGPT, Google Drive, Trello and WhatsApp Business; keep a human checking every document before it is filed.

That sentence is the whole strategy. Free tools handle the drafting, filing, tracking and reminding. Humans handle the judgement: which document is valid, which checklist is current, which deadline is real, and what a client should honestly be told. Check the official page before you quote a fee or a timeline, protect client documents as if a leak would end your business, because it might, and never promise an outcome that only an embassy can decide. A consultancy that does those things will be faster with AI than without it, and — more importantly — will still be trustworthy when a file gets difficult.
