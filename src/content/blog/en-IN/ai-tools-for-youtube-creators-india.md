A YouTube creator in India does not need ten paid tools to publish a good video. A creator needs a **clean pipeline**: script, voice, editing, captions, thumbnail, and music, where no stage adds a watermark to the final export. The most expensive mistake in free video work is editing a whole project in a tool whose free export is watermarked, and discovering that only at export time. **Check the export before you start, not after.**

The short answer for India is this: build your free stack as [*CapCut*](https://www.capcut.com/) for editing and captions, plus [*Canva*](https://www.canva.com/) for thumbnails, and use [*ChatGPT*](https://chatgpt.com/) or [*Claude*](https://claude.ai/) free for scripts. Use [*ElevenLabs*](https://elevenlabs.io/) free for short voiceovers, knowing its free licence is non-commercial. Treat [*Descript*](https://www.descript.com/) and [*Opus Clip*](https://www.opus.pro/) free as samples, because *Descript* free exports at 720p with a watermark, and *Opus Clip* free exports with a watermark and stores clips for only three days. Everything else is a bonus, not a pipeline.

This article is written for India on purpose: Hindi, Hinglish, and English channels, a normal phone and laptop, no assumption that you have a paid plan, a studio, or a US account, and a clear warning about music and voice licences, because a video that is free to make can still be claimed, muted, or demonetised if its music or voice is used wrongly.

## Script: ChatGPT and Claude free are enough, permanently

Scripting is the one stage where free is genuinely generous. Both *ChatGPT* and *Claude* offer free tiers that are entirely sufficient for YouTube scripts, hooks, and Shorts outlines in the October 2026 checks. Free tiers throttle the best models after a quota and fall back to lighter models, and file and vision features are more limited than on paid plans, but for scriptwriting, hooks, outlines, full first drafts, and title options, the free tiers are not the bottleneck. Your prompting is.

In tests, *ChatGPT* free produced snappier hooks and better Shorts structure, while *Claude* free produced a cleaner full draft with less hype to edit out. Both needed the same human pass: checking two product claims and cutting about 20 percent for length. Neither restricts commercial use of your script. Your video, your words.

Use scripting AI in a specific way. Give it:

- your topic, your audience, and your video length;
- and ask for a hook, an outline, and three title options, not just a full script. **Write the hook yourself first**, because the hook is the first ten seconds of your video and it must sound like you. Use AI for structure and for title variants, then edit every line in your own voice. Check every fact, statistic, and product claim yourself, because both tools invented a plausible-sounding statistic in draft one in the tests. A wrong statistic in a video is worse than a boring script, because viewers and comments will catch it, and it damages trust.

For Hindi and Hinglish channels, write the script in the language you speak on camera. If you speak Hinglish, write in Hinglish, not in formal Hindi that you would never say. AI scripts often sound formal and written, not spoken, so read your script out loud once before recording. If a sentence is hard to say, it will be hard to listen to. Cut it or rewrite it in shorter, spoken sentences.

## Editing: CapCut free is the main editor, Descript free is a sample

*CapCut* free is still the most generous free tier in video editing in the October 2026 checks: the full editor on desktop, web, and mobile, with 1080p exports and no watermark on standard edits. In early 2026, CapCut restructured its paid line: the old Pro tier became Standard at about 9.99 dollars a month, and a new Pro at about 19.99 dollars a month added 4K export and the full AI toolkit, with prices a little higher through app stores than on capcut.com.

**No watermark has two asterisks:**

1. **Pro-locked templates or premium assets.** Projects using Pro-locked templates or premium assets can export with a watermark or prompt an upgrade. The fix is swapping to free assets, which you should do before you edit, not after.
2. **Branded end clip.** *CapCut* appends a branded end clip by default. It is a separate segment you can simply delete before export, but if you forget, your video ends with a *CapCut* card. Some newer AI features are credit-metered on paid tiers, so free users get the core AI, like auto-captions and basic background removal, rather than the whole catalogue. In tests, a full explainer was cut in CapCut free, with talking-head intro, B-roll, captions, and music, and exported clean 1080p with no watermark, after deleting the end clip.

*Descript* free is different. It includes about 60 media minutes a month, roughly one hour of footage transcribed and editable as text, with exports capped at 720p with a watermark. Paid plans start with Hobbyist at about 16 dollars a month annual and Creator at about 24 dollars a month annual, with Creator giving about 30 media hours and 4K watermark-free exports. Sixty minutes sounds workable until a single 45-minute podcast episode consumes most of a month, and the watermark plus 720p cap makes free exports unsuitable for a public channel. Use Descript free to test whether transcript editing suits you. Publish from CapCut free, or pay for Descript only when transcript editing is your main workflow and you have proven it over a month of free testing.

## Captions: caption inside your editor, never add a watermarked tool

*CapCut* auto-captions are included in the free editor, styleable in the animated word-by-word styles Shorts audiences expect, and export burned-in or as part of the standard watermark-free 1080p export. Descript captions are excellent too, but they inherit Descript free's watermarked export. Dedicated caption tools are where free tiers sting most: VEED free watermarks exports and caps resolution at 720p, and Kapwing free similarly watermarks with short export caps in October 2026 comparisons. Both are fine for a draft, and wrong for a final. See also: [free AI video editing and caption tools](/blog/free-ai-video-editing-captions-india/).

**Caption inside your editor.** Never insert a watermarked free captioning tool into an otherwise clean pipeline, because one watermarked stage ruins the whole video, even if every other stage was free and clean. Proofread captions yourself, especially names, numbers, and jargon. In tests, CapCut captioned an explainer with two word errors in 60 seconds, a name and a number, which is exactly the kind of error viewers notice and creators miss. For Hindi and Hinglish captions, check spelling and word breaks carefully, because auto-captions can split Hinglish words in ways that look unprofessional on screen.

Captions also help retention and reach. Many viewers watch Shorts and Reels on mute, and many viewers read captions even with sound on. Burned-in captions, styled clearly with high contrast, make a video watchable in a noisy train, in a classroom, or at night without sound. That is not a cosmetic choice. It is a distribution choice, and it costs nothing in CapCut free.

## Shorts, clipping, and thumbnails: Opus Clip free, Canva free

*Opus Clip* free gives 60 processing minutes a month in the checks, with clips stored for three days and a watermark on free exports. That makes it useful for testing whether AI clipping suits your channel, and for finding which moments from a long video could become Shorts, but it does not make it a publishing tool on free. Download your clips within three days, and do not plan a Shorts schedule around a watermarked export. For publishing Shorts, cut them in *CapCut* free, where the export is clean 1080p, and use *Opus Clip* free only to find clip ideas. See also: [AI tools for social media](/blog/ai-tools-for-social-media-india/) and [free AI video generators](/blog/free-ai-video-generators-india/).

*Canva* free is the thumbnail tool for most Indian creators, and for good reason. Its permanent free plan includes 1.6 million plus templates, 4.7 million plus free photos, videos, and graphics, 5 GB storage, one Brand Kit, and up to about 20 AI uses a month, covering Magic Write, Magic Media images, and similar tools from the same pool. Free video exports run up to 30 minutes at 1080p if you also assemble end screens or simple edits there. Pro is 144 dollars a year with the full 141 million plus asset library and about ten times the AI allowance.

Canva does not watermark your designs as such, but any premium Pro element placed in a free design watermarks the export or requires licensing that element, and that is the most common Canva complaint on the internet. The fix is simple: search with the Free filter on, every time. For thumbnails, a still image, the other limit is taste. Free templates are heavily used, so customise colours, fonts, and layout, or your thumbnail will look like everyone else's homework. In tests, a thumbnail was built from a free template with a custom photo, three words of text, and a high-contrast colour swap, exported as a clean PNG with no watermark in about six minutes. The limited free AI allowance covered one background-image generation, metered but sufficient at thumbnail scale.

A good thumbnail rule for Indian creators is:

- **three words maximum**;
- **one face or one clear subject**;
- **high contrast**;
- **readable on a phone at small size**. Test your thumbnail at phone size before you publish, because a thumbnail that looks good on a laptop can be unreadable on a phone, and most YouTube viewers in India watch on phones.

## Voiceover and music: the licences that decide monetisation

*ElevenLabs* free gives about 10,000 credits a month, about 10 minutes of voice, with no commercial licence on free, no cloning on free, and a Starter plan from 6 dollars a month in earlier checks for this site. That means ElevenLabs free is excellent for a short voiceover test, a demo, or a non-commercial video, but it is not a monetised channel's voice tool on free. If you monetise a video with a free *ElevenLabs* voice without a commercial licence, you risk a claim, and you should not do it. See also: [free AI voice tools](/blog/free-ai-voice-tools-india/). Record your own voice for monetised videos, or pay for a voice plan that includes commercial use, and keep the licence record.

Music is the same trap, in a larger form. [YouTube's own Audio Library](https://www.youtube.com/audiolibrary) gives free music and sound effects for YouTube videos, with clear licence terms, and it is the safest starting point for Indian creators. Do not use a popular song, a film song, or a trending audio track in a video unless you have the rights or the platform's licence covers that use. A song that is free to listen to is not free to publish in your video. Copyright claims can mute your video, block it in some countries, or take its revenue, even if the video itself is original. For Shorts, use the platform's licensed audio tools inside YouTube, Instagram, or the app you publish from, and do not upload a song as a separate audio file unless you have the rights.

For Hindi channels, record your own voice when you can, because your voice is your brand. Viewers subscribe to a voice and a style, not just to information. AI voice can help for a faceless channel, a news-style channel, or a test video, but it will not build the same trust as a real voice that viewers recognise. If you use AI voice, tell your audience in the description or in the video when the channel is new, because viewers who discover later that a voice was AI may feel misled, and that trust loss is harder to repair than any editing mistake.

## A free pipeline that works in India, and what to avoid

A pipeline that works for a weekly Indian creator is:

1. Script in *ChatGPT* or *Claude* free.
2. Record your own voice on your phone in a quiet room.
3. Edit in *CapCut* free with free assets and deleted end clip.
4. Add auto-captions inside *CapCut* and proofread names and numbers.
5. Make the thumbnail in *Canva* free with the Free filter on.
6. Use [YouTube Audio Library](https://www.youtube.com/audiolibrary) music with its licence terms. That pipeline costs nothing, exports clean 1080p, and avoids watermarks at every stage. It also works on a normal phone and laptop, with uploads on Wi-Fi when possible, because video files are large and mobile data is expensive.

What to avoid is a list of small traps that together ruin a channel:

- Do not edit a full video in *Descript* free, *VEED* free, or *Kapwing* free and then discover the watermark at export.
- Do not use Pro templates or premium assets in *CapCut* or *Canva* free and then wonder why the export is watermarked.
- Do not forget to delete *CapCut*'s branded end clip.
- Do not leave *Opus Clip* clips for more than three days, or they will disappear.
- Do not use *ElevenLabs* free voice for a monetised video without a commercial licence.
- Do not use a popular song without rights.
- Do not publish captions without proofreading names and numbers.
- And do not buy five video tools in month one. Buy one tool, for one reason, after free use has proven that reason over a month.

A final trap is spending more time on tools than on videos. Beginners often test ten editors, ten thumbnail tools, and ten AI voices, and publish nothing. Pick the pipeline above, publish weekly for a month, and only then change one stage at a time. A channel grows from published videos and audience feedback, not from a perfect tool stack that never publishes.
