Email in India is not one kind of writing. [A job application](/blog/ai-tools-for-resume-jobs-india/), a client follow-up, a leave request, a cold sales email, and a reply to your boss all need **different tones**, and a tone that works for one will fail for another. AI email tools help with drafting and polishing, but they help in different ways: some draft from a blank prompt, some polish what you already wrote, and some live inside your inbox. The tool that drafts best does not always polish best, and the tool that polishes best does not draft from scratch.

Polish in Gmail with *Grammarly* free, which gives about 100 generative prompts a month, with tone detection and sentence-level rewrites where you type. Save *MailMaestro* free for your three hardest emails each week, because it gives three compose, reply, or improve requests per week, free forever with no card, inside Outlook and Gmail. For cold outreach, *Writecream* free gives 20 credits, about 40,000 characters a month, with 20 icebreakers, and no credit card.

This article is written for India on purpose: job seekers, [freelancers](/blog/ai-tools-for-freelancers-india/), office workers, students, and small businesses, Hindi, Hinglish, and English emails, a normal phone and laptop, and no assumption that you have a paid plan, a work account, or a US account. ## Grammarly and MailMaestro: polish where you type, draft inside your inbox

Grammarly works across browser, desktop, and mobile writing surfaces, so it can help in Gmail, Outlook, and many webmail clients without replacing the inbox. It is particularly useful after a writer already knows what to say: grammar, concision, tone, and sentence-level rewrites are more portable than mailbox automation. The current Grammarly Free plan includes 100 generative AI prompts per month, while Pro provides 2,000 prompts and costs 12 dollars per member per month billed annually or 15 dollars monthly. Grammarly also documents a product-improvement and training control that allows Free and single-user paid customers to opt out of model training, which is worth using if you write sensitive emails.

Grammarly's limitation is that it is a **writing layer, not a complete inbox assistant**. It cannot reliably infer missing thread context unless that content is available to the product, and it has no native inbox search or thread management. Use Grammarly in a specific way: write your email first, even roughly, then let Grammarly fix grammar, tighten sentences, and adjust tone, choosing formal for a boss or a client, confident for a negotiation, and friendly for a colleague. For Indian English, Grammarly's tone detection helps, because Indian English politeness can sound indirect to a US reader, and a direct US style can sound rude to an Indian reader: let Grammarly show you the tone, then choose the tone your reader expects, not the tone you are comfortable with.

MailMaestro runs as an Outlook add-in or Gmail extension, and it can compose, reply, and improve drafts in 18 languages, while paid tiers add thread and attachment summaries, scheduling, and priority-inbox features. Its free plan gives a combined total of three compose, reply, or improve requests per week after a 14-day full-feature trial, with no card required, and Professional costs 15 dollars per seat monthly or 12 dollars per seat monthly on annual billing. What you get in those three weekly requests is serious corporate email power: write a short instruction and receive three distinct email drafts, choose tone, length, and language, build magic templates for repetitive emails, and set custom text shortcuts for details like your phone number or LinkedIn profile. The vendor says it anonymizes data sent to model providers, and it holds SOC 2 Type II, GDPR, and HIPAA credentials with Microsoft and Google attestation, but its privacy policy also states that the extension may process personal communications and authentication information, with retention varying by data type and contract, so read it before you use it for sensitive mail.

Use MailMaestro free in a specific way: save its three weekly requests for emails where wording actually matters, like a salary negotiation, a client complaint reply, or a job application follow-up, and draft routine emails yourself or with Rytr free. Do not waste a MailMaestro request on a one-line leave approval, because you will need it on Monday for a hard email, and it will not refill until next week.

Ten thousand characters is about 1,500 words, which covers roughly ten short emails or three long emails a month, so Rytr free is for light email writing, not for a salesperson sending fifty cold emails a day. Use Rytr for cold emails, introductions, and follow-ups: pick a template, pick a tone, give it your product, your recipient, and your ask, and edit the draft into your own voice before you send it, because a Rytr draft that sounds like a template will be ignored like a template.

Writecream is the most generous free tier, with 20 free credits, about 40,000 characters a month, 20 icebreakers, no credit card, verified on its own homepage, and cold emails and outreach are its home turf. Its icebreakers are its standout feature: a personalized first line for a cold email, based on the recipient's profile or company, which is the hardest line to write and the line that decides whether the email is opened. Use Writecream's icebreaker, then write the rest yourself, because a personalized first line with a generic body is still a generic email.

TextCortex is best for writing inside your inbox, with 20 free creations a day and a browser extension that works in Gmail and 30,000 plus other apps, with 25 plus languages, and no credit card. Twenty creations a day is the most generous daily allowance in this article, which makes TextCortex free useful for someone who writes many emails a day and needs quick drafts, rewrites, and replies inside Gmail, not in a separate app. Copy.ai is best for a free template library, with 2,000 free words a month, 90 plus copywriting templates, and 200 one-time workflow credits, built for cold emails, sales intros, and follow-ups. ## Using email AI in India: tone, language, and privacy

Tone for Indian emails is not one tone.

- A job application needs **formal and confident**.
- A client follow-up needs **polite and persistent**.
- A leave request needs **brief and respectful**.
- A cold sales email needs **personalized and direct**.

AI tools can adjust tone, but they cannot choose it for you: you must tell the tool who the reader is, what you want, and how formal to be, or it will default to a generic polite tone that sounds like every other AI email. For a US reader, be direct: state your ask in the first two lines, because US readers skim and a polite introduction that hides the ask will be ignored. For an Indian reader, be respectful but still clear: state your ask early, with polite language, because a respectful email that never asks is also ignored.

Language for Indian emails is mostly English for work, but Hindi and Hinglish emails are common for Indian clients, colleagues, and small businesses. AI email tools work best in English, and Hindi email drafting needs checking: an AI Hindi email can mix formal and informal terms, which sounds unprofessional to a native reader. Write Hindi emails yourself, or draft in English and [translate](/blog/ai-translation-tools-hindi-english-india/) with a tool you trust, then check the Hindi yourself, because an AI translation that sounds right in English can sound wrong in Hindi. For Hinglish emails, write them yourself, because AI Hinglish often mixes formal Hindi, informal Hindi, and English in the same email, which sounds machine-generated.

Privacy for email tools is simple to state and hard to follow: an extension that reads everything you type, and a tool that processes your inbox, can see personal communications and authentication information. Read the privacy policy before you install any email extension on a work machine, especially if your employer has its own policy on [writing assistants](/blog/ai-writing-assistants-free-vs-paid-india/), and use the training opt-out where it exists, like Grammarly's control for Free and single-user paid customers. For sensitive emails, like salary, legal, or medical matters, draft offline or in your builder, and do not paste the full email into a consumer chatbot, because consumer chats can be used for training unless you opt out. For routine emails, free tools are safe and useful, and their speed is a genuine advantage.

## What to avoid

- Do not send an AI email draft without editing it into your own voice, because a template draft will be ignored like a template, and a draft with a wrong name, company, or ask will damage your reputation.

- Do not waste MailMaestro's three weekly requests on routine emails, because one busy Monday burns them, and you will need them for a hard email later in the week

- Do not treat a trial as a free tier: Flowrite has no free plan, only a two-week trial then 15 euros a month, Jasper has no free plan, only a seven-day trial then around 69 dollars a month, and Writesonic's monthly free plan is gone, with only one-time credits remaining

- Do not use Grammarly as a cold email writer, because it polishes what you already wrote, it does not draft from a blank prompt

- Do not install an email extension on a work machine without reading its privacy policy and your employer's policy, and do not paste a sensitive email into a consumer chatbot without stripping personal details first.

A final trap is sending more emails and writing worse emails. Beginners often use AI to send fifty cold emails a day, feel productive, and then get no replies, because fifty generic emails are worse than five personalized emails. **Email rewards personalization, not volume**, and a personalized first line, a clear ask, and a short body will outperform a long generic email every time. Use AI to draft faster, then use that saved time to personalize the first line, to check the recipient's name and company, and to make the ask clear, because that personalization is the part AI cannot do for you.
