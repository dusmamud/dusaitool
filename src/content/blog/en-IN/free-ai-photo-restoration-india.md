A free **AI photo restoration** tool repairs damage in an old photograph: scratches, tears, creases, stains, fading, and the soft blur that comes from age. It is not the same job as upscaling. An [upscaler](/blog/free-ai-image-upscalers-india/) makes a small image larger. A restorer tries to put a damaged image back to how it looked before the damage happened. The two jobs overlap — a restored photo is often sharpened as well — but if your photo is torn, stained, or faded, you need restoration first, and upscaling only afterwards, if at all.

The short answer for India is this: start with [*MyHeritage Photo Enhancer*](https://www.myheritage.com/photo-enhancer) for faces, because it gives 10 free enhancements and is built for old family photographs. Use [*Colourise.sg*](https://colourise.sg/) for free black-and-white to colour conversion, because it costs nothing and does not store your upload, according to its own team. Use [*Canva*](https://www.canva.com/features/photo-restoration/) or [*Fotor*](https://www.fotor.com/features/photo-restoration/) for scratches, tears, and faded colour on the web, because both offer free restoration without needing an app. Use [*Remini*](https://remini.ai/) on your phone when the photo is already on your mobile and you want a one-tap face fix. Everything else below is for a specific need — a style finish, a batch of photos, or a badly damaged print that free tools cannot handle.

This article is written for India on purpose: families with old prints, students and creators working from a normal phone or laptop, Hindi and English users, and no assumption that you have a paid plan, a powerful computer, or a US account. Every tool here works in India through a browser or an app store without a VPN. One warning matters before you start: a restored photo is a reconstruction, not the original. The AI guesses what the damaged part looked like. That guess is usually good for a face or a plain background, but it can be wrong for jewellery, text, uniforms, or small details. Do not use a restored photo as proof of a detail in a document, a legal matter, or an official record. Keep your original scan untouched, and always work on a copy.

## How this list was checked, and where sources disagree

Every free limit, price, and watermark note in this article comes from the tool's own website, help page, or pricing page, or from a review that quoted those pages. Where sources disagree, this article says so instead of picking the friendlier number. The two clearest disagreements are Remini and Hotpot. Remini's own help material says each user sees a different offer on the in-app paywall, so there is no single fixed price list; one October 2026 check of the US App Store showed Lite at $4.99, $5.99, or $7.99 a week and Pro at $9.99 a week, but your own paywall may show something else. Hotpot's pricing page sells credit packs, and its free creations exist, but the site no longer states a daily free limit; older reviews quoted 75 free images a day with a watermark, and that number should now be treated as unconfirmed.

One more check matters for restoration more than for any other photo job: scan quality. A phone photo of a print, taken in dim light at an angle, gives the AI a damaged input on top of the damage already in the print. If you can, scan the print flat, in colour, at a high resolution, and save that scan as your master copy. Canva's own restoration guide gives the same advice: scan in colour to capture the full tonal range, which makes stain removal easier. If you only have a phone, photograph the print in bright, even daylight, straight from above, and avoid flash glare. A WhatsApp-compressed copy of a photo is the worst input of all, because compression has already thrown away the detail the AI needs.

## MyHeritage Photo Enhancer: the best free start for family faces

[*MyHeritage Photo Enhancer*](https://www.myheritage.com/photo-enhancer) is the most family-photo-focused tool on this list, and it has the clearest free offer: anyone can enhance 10 photos for free, counted separately from photos you colorize. Enhancing a photo typically takes 15 to 30 seconds depending on its size and the number of faces in it. You need a free MyHeritage account, and the tool works on the web, so there is nothing to install.

The catch is the watermark. Non-subscribers will notice a MyHeritage logo watermark on the bottom right of their enhanced photos, while Complete subscribers get logo-free results and unlimited enhancements. That makes the free tier perfect for testing — run your 10 most important photos through it, see whether the faces come out right — and a poor choice for printing a whole album for free. MyHeritage also has a separate app called Reimagine, which scans album pages and improves photos; a yearly Reimagine subscription costs $49.99 (or the equivalent in local currency) and starts with a 7-day free trial, while a monthly plan costs $7.99 a month without a trial. You do not need Reimagine for the 10 free web enhancements.

Use MyHeritage first when the photo's main problem is a soft, faded, or blurry face. It is less useful for a photo that is physically torn across the middle or stained over a large area, because enhancement is mainly about faces and clarity, not about rebuilding missing pieces. If your photo has both problems, enhance the face here, then take the result to Canva or Fotor for the scratch and tear work.

## Remini: the one-tap phone fix, with a confusing price list

[*Remini*](https://remini.ai/) is the tool most people in India already know by name, because it lives mainly as a phone app on the Play Store and App Store. Upload a photo, and the result comes back in seconds with no settings to choose. That simplicity is its real strength: for a grandparent's portrait or a childhood photo that is already on your phone, Remini is the fastest route to a cleaner face.

The free tier is limited and carries ads, and video enhancement is subscriber-only. On Remini Web, watermark-free downloads are tied to a paid plan, so a free web result may carry a watermark. Prices are the confusing part. Remini says there is no fixed price list because each user sees a different offer on the paywall, and one October 2026 check of the US App Store showed Lite plans at $4.99, $5.99, or $7.99 a week and Pro at $9.99 a week, with a one-month option at the same figure. Treat any single price you read online, including the ones in this paragraph, as an example, not a promise: the figure on your own paywall is the one that counts, and subscriptions renew automatically until you cancel them in your Apple or Google account.

Use Remini when speed matters more than control, and when the photo is already on your phone. Do not use it as your only tool for a large family album, because the free tier will run out quickly and the weekly pricing adds up fast if you forget to cancel. If a free trial is offered, cancel at least 24 hours before it ends to avoid being charged.

## Canva: free web restoration for scratches, tears, and fading

[*Canva*](https://www.canva.com/features/photo-restoration/) offers a free online AI photo restoration tool that repairs old photos in one click: creases, tears, and discoloration are the damage types its own page names. After restoration, Canva's editor lets you adjust brightness, exposure, and white balance with sliders, and it also offers colorizing for black-and-white photos and upscaling for print sizes. That combination makes Canva the best free web starting point when your photo has physical damage rather than just a soft face.

The boundary to know is the Pro line. Canva's Magic Studio tools — Magic Edit, Magic Expand, and Magic Eraser for spot-cleaning unwanted details — are marked Pro on Canva's own pages. The restoration feature itself is presented as free, but if a specific cleanup step asks you to upgrade, that is the Pro boundary, not a bug. Canva works in India on the web and as an app, and a free Canva account is enough to try restoration. Because Canva is also a design tool, it is the natural next step after restoration if you want to put the restored photo into a frame, a collage, or a family post without moving to another app.

Canva is also the right place to learn the restoration habit this article keeps repeating: work on a copy. Canva keeps your uploads in your account, so download the restored result, compare it side by side with the original scan, and only then decide whether the AI's guesses — especially on faces, hands, and text — are acceptable.

## Fotor: scratch and tear repair with batch restoration

[*Fotor*](https://www.fotor.com/features/photo-restoration/) is a web and mobile photo editor whose AI restorer automatically detects and repairs scratches, tears, and blurs, and recovers colour in faded photos. Its feature page promises fast processing — upload, auto-restore, preview, and download in a few seconds — and two details that matter for families with albums: batch restoration for up to 50 old photos at once, and automatic deletion of your files within 24 hours. Its feature page also says exports carry no watermarks.

Fotor's pricing has moved to a credit model, and its pricing page no longer reads as a simple free-versus-Pro list, so this article does not quote a fixed free number for Fotor. Try it on one or two photos first and watch what it asks for at download time. If it restores your test photos without demanding payment, it is a strong free option for scratches and tears, and the 50-photo batch feature makes it the best choice in this list for working through a whole album in one sitting. If it gates high-resolution downloads behind credits, use it for the photos that matter most and do the rest elsewhere.

Fotor is the tool to pick when the damage is physical — a crease across a face, a tear at the edge, a stain over a sari border — rather than a purely soft or faded image. After restoration, Fotor's normal editing tools can handle the small manual fixes the AI misses, which is where a [photo editor](/blog/free-ai-photo-editors-india/) still beats a one-click restorer.

## Hotpot AI: restoration with a choice of finishes

[*Hotpot AI's Photo Restorer*](https://hotpot.ai/restore-picture) takes a different approach from the tools above: after you upload a photo, you choose both a kind of restoration and a finish. The finishes listed on its own page include Natural, Vintage, Polaroid, Classic color, Modern black and white, and Classic black and white, so you can keep a classic black-and-white look or try a colour finish on the same photo. You can also upload more than one image and restore each with the same treatment, which helps when an album shares the same kind of damage.

Hotpot's pricing is credit-based: its pricing page sells one-time credit packs starting at $12 for 1,000 credits, with larger packs up to $240 for 20,000 credits. Some free creations exist, but Hotpot's site no longer states a daily free limit, so do not plan around the older "75 free images a day with a watermark" figure you may see in old reviews. Free creations are also not for business use, so if you plan to sell prints or use a restored photo for a client, you need to pay and license it properly.

Use Hotpot when you want to compare finishes on the same photo — for example, whether your grandmother's portrait looks better in natural colour or kept as classic black and white. That comparison is something the one-click tools do not offer, and it matters, because a colour finish is a creative choice, not a recovery of the truth. If you only need one straightforward repair, Canva or Fotor will be faster.

## Colourise.sg: the free colour option, with one honest limit

[*Colourise.sg*](https://colourise.sg/) began as a free web app built by engineers at GovTech Singapore during an internal hackathon, trained specifically on old Singaporean photographs. It colorizes black-and-white photos in seconds, and its own team stated that the service never stores the photos you upload. The site is still reachable, and its current pages describe broader enhancement features, but its core promise for this article is unchanged: a free, no-account colour tool for black-and-white photos.

Its honest limit is the one its own team stated from the start: colourisation produces colours that are plausible, not colours that are guaranteed to be true. You cannot tell the tool what colour a sari, a wall, or a uniform actually was, so the result may look realistic while being wrong about the real scene. The tool also works best on high-resolution photos of people and natural scenery, and it can produce unrealistic colours for objects it does not recognise. Use Colourise.sg when you want to see a black-and-white memory in colour for sharing with family, and keep the original black-and-white version alongside it, clearly labelled, so nobody later mistakes the AI's guess for the real colours.

For privacy, Colourise.sg is the strongest option on this list for a single colour job, because of the no-storage statement. That still does not make it safe for identity documents: never upload an Aadhaar card, passport, or any official document to a restoration or colour tool, free or paid.

## Picsart: fast, automatic restoration with batches

[*Picsart's photo restoration tool*](https://picsart.com/ai-image-enhancer/photo-restoration/) describes itself as a free, fully automatic AI tool that restores old photos in seconds: it cleans up damage, sharpens faces, and improves clarity with no manual steps, and it offers batch processing so you can restore multiple photos at once instead of one by one. That makes it a good second opinion tool. If MyHeritage gives you a face you do not like, or Canva leaves a scratch behind, run the same photo through Picsart and compare the two results before choosing one.

Picsart's feature page does not state a fixed free number for restoration, so, as with Fotor, test it on one photo and watch the download step. Picsart is a large editing app as well as a web tool, so if you already use it for [social posts](/blog/ai-tools-for-social-media-india/) or basic edits, restoration fits into a workflow you already know. If you do not already use it, there is no reason to install the full app just for restoration when Canva and Fotor do the same job on the web.

## VanceAI: the paid fallback when free tools fail

[*VanceAI's old photo restoration*](https://vanceai.com/old-photo-restoration/) is the paid fallback on this list, included because some photos are too damaged for the free tools above. Its own page says you can start free: preview a restored result without a watermark and without a credit card, using free credits, and each completed restoration then uses credits under your plan. Paid plans start at $15 a month for 260 credits, with a 30 percent bonus of credits in every monthly allocation on monthly and annual plans. Subscription credits roll over while the subscription stays active.

Use VanceAI only after a free tool has failed on a specific photo, and only after the free preview looks right. Paying for a month to restore one photo is poor value unless you have an album to work through, and the preview step exists precisely so you do not pay for a result you have not seen. If the preview still invents a face badly — a common failure on heavily damaged prints — no paid tool will fix it either, and the honest answer is to accept a softer result or to have a human retoucher work from the scan.

## Using photo restoration tools in India: language, payment, and privacy

All eight tools work in India without a VPN. The web tools run in any browser on a phone or laptop, and Remini, Canva, Fotor, and Picsart are available through the Indian Play Store and App Store. Language is rarely a barrier: restoration tools need almost no reading, because you upload a photo and the AI does the work, and Canva, Fotor, and Picsart all have Hindi-friendly interfaces or at least simple English controls that are easy to follow. If a tool's help page is only in English, the steps in this article — upload, restore, preview, download — are the same in every tool.

Payment, when you do pay, follows the usual Indian routes. App subscriptions through the Play Store or App Store can be paid with UPI, cards, or net banking through your store account, and they renew automatically until cancelled. Web tools usually take international cards, and some accept PayPal. Before paying for any web plan, check whether the price is shown in dollars and whether your bank will add a currency conversion charge, because the dollar figure on the pricing page is rarely the final rupee figure on your statement.

Privacy deserves a separate paragraph, because restoration tools receive your most personal images. Upload only photos you own or have permission to restore, and never upload identity documents, children's school records, or anyone else's private photos without their consent. Prefer tools that state a deletion policy — Fotor auto-deletes files within 24 hours, and Colourise.sg's team stated that uploads are never stored — and delete your own uploads from a tool's account storage when you are done. A restored photo of a family member is also personal data: think before posting it publicly, especially if the person in the photo is no longer alive to consent. For general guidance on keeping personal images safe online, see the [privacy and safety tools](/blog/ai-privacy-safety-free-tools-india/) article.

## What to avoid

Avoid any tool that promises to recover the exact original colours, the exact missing half of a face, or detail that was never in the photo. Restoration is educated guessing, and a tool that hides that fact will eventually invent something embarrassing — a wrong uniform colour, a changed facial feature, or text that looks plausible but says the wrong thing. Avoid paying for a bulk plan before testing one photo, because free tiers differ wildly in quality on your specific damage type, and a tool that is excellent on scratches may be poor on faded colour.

Avoid uploading your only copy anywhere. Scan first, keep the scan, and restore copies. Avoid tools that demand full access to your phone's photo library when a simple upload button would do, and avoid "unlimited free restoration" apps that turn out to be watermarked, low-resolution, or subscription traps after the first photo. Finally, avoid using a restored photo as evidence. A restorer can make a number plate, a signature, or a document detail look sharper while changing it, and that makes it unfit for any official purpose. The original, damaged as it is, remains the only trustworthy version.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can AI restore a photo that is torn in half or missing a face?** Partly. AI is good at scratches, creases, stains, and fading, and fair at soft faces. It cannot reliably rebuild a face or a large area that is completely gone, because there is nothing to reconstruct from. Expect a plausible guess, not a recovery, and check the result against what your family remembers.

**Is a colorized photo the real colour of the original scene?** No. Colour tools like Colourise.sg produce colours that look plausible, and their own team says the result is not guaranteed to match the real scene. Keep the black-and-white original next to the colour version so the guess is never mistaken for the truth.

**Why does my free restored photo have a watermark?** Because the free tier says so. MyHeritage puts its logo on free downloads for non-subscribers, and Remini ties watermark-free web downloads to a paid plan. Canva and Fotor present their restoration exports as watermark-free, but always check the downloaded file before sharing or printing it.

**Will restoration work on a WhatsApp-forwarded photo?** Poorly. WhatsApp compression throws away the fine detail restoration needs, so the AI has less to work with and invents more. Use the original scan or the original camera file whenever you can, and only fall back to a forwarded copy if nothing else exists.

**Is it safe to upload old family photos to these tools?** Reasonably safe if you choose carefully. Prefer tools with a stated deletion policy, such as Fotor's 24-hour auto-deletion, or a no-storage statement, such as Colourise.sg's. Never upload identity documents, and delete your uploads from the tool's account when you are finished.

**What is the difference between restoration and upscaling?** Restoration repairs damage — scratches, tears, stains, fading, and colour loss. Upscaling increases resolution — it makes a small image larger. A torn photo needs restoration first; a small but undamaged photo needs only upscaling. The two jobs are covered separately because they solve different problems.

**Can I restore a photo and then turn it into a video?** Yes. Once a photo is restored, it can be used like any other image, including in [photo-to-video avatar tools](/blog/ai-photo-to-video-avatar-tools-india/) that animate a still portrait. Restore first, because a damaged input makes a worse video, and check the restored face carefully before animating it.

## The final verdict, and a checklist before you pay

Start with MyHeritage for 10 free face fixes and Colourise.sg for colour. Use Canva or Fotor for scratches, and pay only after a test looks right. Use Canva or Fotor for scratches, tears, and faded colour, and Hotpot when you want to compare finishes on the same photo. Use Remini when the photo is already on your phone and you want the fastest result, and Picsart as a second opinion when the first result disappoints. Keep VanceAI in reserve for badly damaged photos, and only after its free preview looks right.

Before you pay for anything, run this checklist. First, test the free tier on your hardest photo, not your easiest one, because free quality varies by damage type. Second, download the result and check it at full size for invented details on faces, hands, jewellery, and text. Third, confirm whether the free download carries a watermark, because that alone decides whether a tool is usable for printing. Fourth, check the renewal terms and the currency of any subscription, and cancel any trial at least a day before it ends. Fifth, keep your original scan untouched, and store the restored copy separately, labelled as restored, so the AI's guesses are never confused with the original memory.
