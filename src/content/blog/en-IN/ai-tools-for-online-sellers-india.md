## The short answer

If you sell on Meesho, Amazon or Flipkart in India, start with Meesho, because Meesho charges 0% commission across all categories and charges no registration fee. For product photos, use the free trial of Claid.ai before you pay for any photography tool, and for product descriptions use the free tier of ChatGPT in Hindi, English or Hinglish. Do not build your costs around tools that are called "free" in old blog posts: PhotoRoom's free plan is for personal, non-commercial use only, so it is not the right free option for commercial listings, and Pebblely has no free plan at all on its current pricing page. Prices, commissions and free limits change often, so re-check the official pricing or help page of every tool below before you pay for anything or promise a buyer a deadline based on a tool.

Selling online in India is mostly a phone-first, low-margin business. A saree seller in Surat, a mobile-cover seller in Delhi or a snacks seller in Indore does not need an expensive studio. What they need is a clean first photo, a title written in the words buyers actually type, an honest description that answers size, material and delivery questions, and a clear idea of what the platform keeps from every sale. The tools below cover exactly those four jobs. None of them replaces good stock, honest pricing or fast dispatch, but they remove the two most common starting blocks: "photo achhi nahi hai" and "description kya likhun".

## Meesho Supplier Panel: the cheapest place to start

Meesho's [official pricing page](https://supplier.meesho.com/pricing) states 0% commission across all categories, no registration fee for creating your account or getting products listed, and no collection fee, which means you keep 100% of the sale price with no charges on payment gateway or cash-on-delivery orders. The payment cycle is 7 days from order delivery, including cash-on-delivery orders, and the settlement goes directly to your bank account. Meesho also charges 0 penalties for supplier cancellations and auto cancellations, and no return shipping fee for return-to-origin orders. The cost you must still plan for is shipping: Meesho's shipping service charges shipping costs with 18% GST on the shipping charges, so a "0% commission" sale is not a zero-cost sale.

Meesho is also the most forgiving platform for a seller without full paperwork. Its pricing page itself asks sellers who do not have a GSTIN, or who hold a composition GSTIN, to click through for how they can still sell on Meesho. That does not mean tax rules disappear; it means Meesho has a documented route for such sellers, and you should read that page for your exact case instead of assuming you are covered. For a first-time seller, the practical order is: list your best 10 products on Meesho first, learn which photos and titles get clicks, and only then spend money on tools or on other platforms.

One honest limit: Meesho buyers are extremely price-sensitive, and returns are a real cost in this business even when return shipping itself is not charged. A listing that hides size, fabric or colour details will sell once and return twice. AI descriptions help you write faster, but they cannot fix a product that does not match its photo.

## Amazon Seller Central India: reach, but read the fee table first

Amazon.in does not have one flat fee. Its [official fees page](https://sell.amazon.in/fees-and-pricing) says referral fees start at 0% and vary by product category, closing fees start at ₹2 and vary by price range and fulfilment channel, and shipping or delivery fees start at ₹37 per item shipped, varying by volume and distance. It also advertises zero referral fee on over 12.5 crore products, which is a catalogue claim, not a promise about your product: the fee on your product depends on its category and price. Two examples from Amazon's own page make this concrete: grocery dried fruits at ₹750 carry a 0% referral fee, while baby apparel at ₹1,450 carries 7%. Before listing, use Amazon's own fee calculator logic on its pricing page: profit equals selling price minus referral fee, closing fee, weight-handling fee, other applicable fees and product cost.

Two more facts from Amazon's official pages matter for planning. First, closing fees were updated on September 7, 2026 because of logistics costs: for products priced up to ₹500 the closing fee rose by ₹1, and above ₹500 it rose by ₹3, across its fulfilment channels. If you read an older blog post quoting old closing fees, it is out of date. Second, [registration](https://sell.amazon.in/sell-online/seller-registration-guide) asks for your GST number, with an option if you only sell tax-exempt products such as books, and GST verification can take up to 72 hours. You must also complete at least one product listing to launch your store. Amazon is worth it when your product has brand value, a higher price or repeat buyers; for a ₹199 unbranded item, Meesho's 0% commission usually leaves more money in your hand.

## Flipkart Seller Hub: 0% commission where it matters most

Flipkart's [official fees page](https://seller.flipkart.com/fees-and-commission) states 0% commission on all products priced below ₹1,000, regardless of category, and a flat 0% commission across the entire Fashion category, even above ₹1,000. For other categories the commission depends on the category, and the exact rate for your product sits on the rate card inside your seller dashboard, so any article quoting one fixed Flipkart commission for everything is oversimplifying. On top of commission, Flipkart charges a fixed fee, also called the closing fee, set by seller tier: the page shows Platinum at base rate minus ₹15 up to ₹30, Gold and Silver at base rate, and Bronze at base rate plus ₹10. Payments are released after dispatch and can arrive in as fast as 3 days, depending on seller tier, with Flipkart fees deducted before transfer.

For a seller, the practical reading is simple. If your catalogue is mostly fashion, or mostly priced below ₹1,000, Flipkart's commission terms are as friendly as Meesho's, and you should compare the two on shipping cost, returns and payment speed rather than on commission alone. If your products are above ₹1,000 and outside fashion, open the rate card in your own dashboard before fixing your selling price, because the commission there can change your margin completely. Flipkart offers two fulfilment routes, Fulfilment by Flipkart, where Flipkart handles packaging, shipping and delivery, and non-fulfilment, where you handle packaging and shipping, and the fees differ between them.

## Claid.ai: the free trial to spend on your bestsellers

Claid.ai is an AI product photography suite: it removes backgrounds, upscales images, and generates AI photoshoot scenes for a product photo. Its [official pricing page](https://claid.ai/pricing) labels the free tier as a free trial, and the trial counts are specific: up to 25 upscale operations, 50 background removals, 12 AI photoshoot generations and 12 AI fashion generations. That is a trial, not a permanent free plan, and this distinction matters. Many listicles call Claid.ai "free" without saying the trial ends; when the trial counts run out, you move to a paid plan.

Use the trial the way a careful seller would. Pick your 10 to 12 bestselling or most-photographed products first, generate one clean hero image for each, and keep the original phone photos safe. Do not burn trial generations on every variant of the same product. A good AI photoshoot image still needs a real product behind it: if the AI scene changes the product's colour, shape or size, buyers will return it, and a return costs more than a photo ever saved. After the trial, judge by sales: if the new photos clearly raised clicks or orders on those 12 products, a paid plan has a business case; if not, your problem is price, title or product, not photography.

## PhotoRoom Free: free, but not for commercial listings

PhotoRoom is one of the most recommended product-photo tools, and its [help centre](https://help.photoroom.com/en/articles/6976012-what-are-photoroom-s-plans) confirms a free plan exists. But the same help page states the free plan's limits plainly: limited features, lower AI credit and export limits, no API access, and it is for personal, non-commercial use only. For a seller, that last condition is the deciding factor. A free PhotoRoom export used on a commercial Meesho, Amazon or Flipkart listing is outside the free plan's stated use. Use PhotoRoom Free only to test how a background or template looks, and use a paid plan, or a tool whose free terms allow commercial use, for the images you actually publish.

PhotoRoom also has a monthly or weekly export limit that counts every download, save to gallery, share as an image file or publish to an external service, with a batch of 10 images counting as 10 exports. That limit is per Space, and a subscription applies only to the Space it was bought for. For a seller with 50 products, a small export limit runs out fast, so check the current export limit for your plan on PhotoRoom's help pages before you depend on it for a full catalogue upload.

## ChatGPT free: the description writer for every listing

ChatGPT's free tier is free to use, and its [official help page](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/9275245-chatgpt-free-tier-faq) says free users get unlimited everyday text chats, subject to abuse-prevention safeguards, while file uploads, image generation, voice and data analysis have separate usage limits. Free users can also create images in ChatGPT and upload images or files in prompts. The default model and limits change over time; the help page checked for this article showed GPT-5.6 Luna as the free tier model, but do not treat any model name as permanent, because OpenAI changes it. What stays useful regardless of model is the workflow: give the product, its real specifications, its material, size and what's in the box, and ask for a title and description in the buyer's language, then edit every line yourself.

A description that works on Indian marketplaces is short, specific and honest. Start with what the product is, then size and material, then what's included, then who it suits, then care or use notes. Write the title in the words buyers type in search, not in clever English. For a Meesho buyer, a Hindi or Hinglish description often converts better than formal English, and ChatGPT free handles both; just read the Hindi back yourself, because AI Hindi sometimes invents a feature or a size you never gave it. Never let any AI tool invent specifications, warranty, or "original brand" claims for your product. If the description promises something the product cannot do, the return and the bad rating are yours, not the tool's.

## Adobe Express Free: banners and creatives without a designer

Adobe Express Free gives sellers a design tool without a subscription: its [official free-plan page](https://helpx.adobe.com/express/web/adobe-express-subscription/free.html) lists access to more than 100,000 static and video templates, more than 4,000 fonts from the Adobe Fonts collection, standard photo, video and document editing tools, and 5 GB of storage, on web, mobile and iPad. That is enough to make festival sale banners, "new stock" announcements, price posters for WhatsApp status, and simple listing creatives. It is not presented here as a background remover, because the free features listed on Adobe's own page do not promise background removal on the free plan; if you need background removal, use Claid.ai's trial counts or a paid tool instead of assuming Adobe Express Free includes it.

The practical use is seasonal. Indian selling runs on festivals: Diwali, Eid, Raksha Bandhan, wedding season and end-of-season sales. A seller who posts one clean banner per festival, with the real price and a real product photo, sells more than a seller who only uploads catalogue images. Keep the banner honest: same product, same price, same offer as the listing. A banner advertising a discount that the listing does not carry will bring angry messages, not orders.

## Tools that are not free, even when old lists say they are

Two tools deserve a clear warning because older articles still call them free. Pebblely, an AI product photography tool, shows no free plan on its current pricing page; its plans start at US$9 per month for 30 images, with higher plans at US$19 and US$39 per month. It may be a good paid tool, but it is not a free starting option. Copy.ai is similar: older posts describe a free plan, but its current pricing page shows paid plans starting at US$29 per month for its Chat plan, with no free tier visible. Sources disagree here because the tools changed, not because one source is lying; the current official pricing page is the one that matters for your money. If a tool's free plan has disappeared, do not waste a week trying to find it. Use ChatGPT free for words and Claid.ai's trial for photos, and pay only after sales repeat.

## Using these tools in India: language, payment and the phone in your hand

Language comes first. Marketplace search in India is a mix of English and Hinglish, and many Meesho buyers browse in Hindi. Write your title in the words a buyer would type, and if your buyers are Hindi-speaking, give them a Hindi or Hinglish description too. ChatGPT free can draft both, but you must check the Hindi yourself for invented details. Do not publish a listing in a language you cannot answer questions in; buyers message sellers, and a seller who cannot reply in the buyer's language loses the sale.

Payments and paperwork come second. Meesho settles in a 7-day cycle from delivery, Flipkart can pay in as fast as 3 days after pickup depending on tier, and Amazon's fees depend on category and fulfilment channel, so your cash flow plan must differ by platform. Keep GST documents ready: Amazon asks for your GST number during registration and verification can take up to 72 hours, with an option for sellers of tax-exempt products only. Meesho documents a route for sellers without a GSTIN or with a composition GSTIN, but read its own page for your case. All of the tools in this article work in India without a VPN, and PhotoRoom, Adobe Express and ChatGPT all work on mobile as well as web, which matters because most small sellers run the whole business from one phone.

Returns are the third India reality. A clean photo and an honest description reduce returns more than any AI tool. Measure the product, photograph it in daylight against a plain background, show the size with a common object for scale, and write the material honestly. If a kurti is polyester, write polyester. A buyer who knows what they are getting rarely returns it; a buyer who was surprised always does.

## A simple weekly workflow that uses only the free limits

On day one, list or refresh your best 10 products: one Claid.ai AI photoshoot image each, one ChatGPT description each, and one Adobe Express banner for your store or social page. On day two, compare: which listings got views and which did not, and rewrite only the titles of the weak ones. On day three, answer every buyer message and fix every description that a buyer questioned. Repeat weekly. This workflow fits inside Claid.ai's trial counts for the first batch and inside ChatGPT free and Adobe Express Free for ongoing work. The moment you pay for a tool, attach it to a number: this tool costs this much per month, and it must bring at least that much extra profit from the products it touches. If it does not, cancel it. That single rule protects a small seller more than any "best tools" list.

## What to avoid

Avoid using PhotoRoom's free plan for commercial listings, because its own help page limits the free plan to personal, non-commercial use. Avoid paying for Pebblely or any photography subscription before your first repeat sales, because a paid tool cannot fix a bad price or a weak product. Avoid copying a competitor's listing word for word; marketplaces penalise copied content, and a copied description usually carries the competitor's specifications, not yours. Avoid AI descriptions that invent features, materials, warranties or brand names. Avoid free background-removal previews that are too small for a main image: remove.bg's free downloads, for example, are previews of up to 0.25 megapixels, about 625 by 400 pixels, which is too small for a marketplace hero image, so do not build your catalogue on them. Avoid ignoring shipping and return costs when you set a price, because Meesho charges shipping plus 18% GST on shipping, and Amazon and Flipkart fees vary by category, price and fulfilment. And avoid promising a buyer a delivery date or a feature based on what an AI tool told you; check the platform's own page first.

## Frequently asked questions

**Which platform should a new seller start on, Meesho, Amazon or Flipkart?** Start on Meesho if your products are low-priced and you are new, because Meesho charges 0% commission across categories and no registration fee. Add Flipkart if your products are fashion or priced below ₹1,000, where its commission is also 0%. Add Amazon when your product has brand value or a higher price that can absorb category referral fees, closing fees from ₹2 and shipping from ₹37 per item.

**Is PhotoRoom really free for sellers?** PhotoRoom has a free plan, but its own help page says the free plan is for personal, non-commercial use only, with limited features and export limits. So it is free to test, but not the right free option for images you publish on a commercial listing. Use a paid plan or a tool whose free terms allow commercial use for published images.

**How many free product photos can I get from Claid.ai?** Claid.ai's free tier is a trial, not a permanent free plan. Its pricing page lists 12 AI photoshoot generations, 50 background removals and 25 upscales in the trial. Spend them on your bestsellers first, and keep your original photos safe.

**Can ChatGPT free write my product descriptions in Hindi?** Yes. ChatGPT's free tier offers unlimited everyday text chats with safeguards, and it drafts in Hindi, English and Hinglish. Always read the Hindi draft yourself before publishing, because AI Hindi can invent a feature or size you never gave it. Never publish specifications you did not provide.

**Do I need GST to sell on Amazon, Flipkart or Meesho?** Amazon's registration asks for your GST number, with an option if you only sell tax-exempt products such as books, and verification can take up to 72 hours. Flipkart's seller pages also expect valid tax registration for most categories. Meesho's own pricing page documents a route for sellers without a GSTIN or with a composition GSTIN, so read Meesho's page for your exact case rather than assuming one rule fits all three platforms.

**How long does payment take on each platform?** Meesho settles in a 7-day payment cycle from order delivery, including cash-on-delivery orders. Flipkart says payments can arrive in as fast as 3 days after pickup, depending on seller tier. Amazon's timing depends on its settlement cycle and fulfilment channel, so check your seller dashboard rather than relying on a single number from any article.

**Should I pay for Pebblely or Copy.ai as a beginner?** Not at the start. Pebblely's current pricing starts at US$9 per month with no free plan shown, and Copy.ai's current pricing page shows paid plans from US$29 per month with no free tier visible. Start with Claid.ai's trial for photos, ChatGPT free for descriptions and Adobe Express Free for creatives, and pay only when repeat sales prove the tool earns its cost.
