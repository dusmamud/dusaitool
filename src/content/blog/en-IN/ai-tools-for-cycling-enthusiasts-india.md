If you cycle in India and want help with routes, training and group rides, start with three free tools: Strava for recording rides and joining groups, Komoot for planning safe routes, and Intervals.icu for understanding your fitness. All three work in India without a VPN, and none makes you pay to record a ride or plan a route. Free tiers suit most riders, but offline navigation and leaderboards are paid, and prices change often — re-check the official pricing page before paying.

## The short answer, in one table

| Tool | Best for | Free | Paywall |
|---|---|---|---|
| Strava | Ride tracking, groups | GPS recording, feed | Route builder, offline routes |
| Komoot | Route planning | Planning, one free region | Extra regions, Premium |
| RideWithGPS | Club rides, cue sheets | Web planner, ride recording | Mobile planner, offline maps |
| Intervals.icu | Performance analysis | Fitness chart, power curve | Supporter: plans, zones |

## Strava: the social home of Indian cycling

Strava is the default app for most Indian cyclists. The free tier records GPS rides, shows distance, speed and elevation, and puts your ride in a social feed. Routes around Cubbon Park in Bengaluru, Marine Drive in Mumbai and Lodhi Garden in Delhi have segments with thousands of local riders. Free clubs and challenges are how most Indian groups organise weekend rides.

Route builder, offline downloads and full leaderboards need a subscription. Offline recording and GPX export are free. Indian prices disagree — about ₹2,499 a year per one source, ₹450 a month per another — so check strava.com first. A 30-day trial is usually offered.

## Komoot: the best route planner for new roads

Komoot is built for planning, not social feeds. Pick road, gravel or mountain biking and it builds a route for your tyres, showing surface, elevation and difficulty. That matters in India, where a good-looking road can turn into broken tarmac or dirt.

A free account plans routes anywhere and gives one free region for offline navigation. Voice navigation, offline maps and GPX downloads come with an unlocked region, bought once. Premium adds worldwide maps, multi-day tours, weather and live sharing. The catch is coverage: OpenStreetMap data is excellent in cities and patchy on rural roads, so cross-check routes with local riders.

## RideWithGPS: built for clubs and organised rides

RideWithGPS is the most practical tool if you run or join a cycling club. Its free web planner shows elevation, surface types and bike-lane priority, steering you toward quieter roads. Custom cue sheets — notes warning riders about a bad descent or a water stop — are why audax organisers rely on it. The free Starter account covers web planning and recording, with over a million shared routes. Paid plans unlock the mobile planner, voice navigation, offline maps and live logging.

## Intervals.icu: free analysis that rivals paid coaching apps

Intervals.icu is a web-based analysis platform started by a cyclist-developer in 2018, and the best free deal in this list. Connect it to Strava or Garmin and it analyses every ride: a fitness, fatigue and form chart, a power curve, automatic interval detection and an estimated FTP. The core platform is genuinely free — no trial, time limit or card needed. The optional Supporter tier, about $4 a month, adds a plan builder, history import and custom zones. It is web-first and maintained largely by one developer, so keep your own exported ride files.

## Using these tools in India

Mobile data is cheap, so offline maps matter less in a city than on a highway or hill ride where signal drops. Heat and traffic matter more than elevation: start early, carry extra water, and use the heatmap to find roads cyclists trust. Strava clubs and WhatsApp groups are how rides are organised, and most clubs welcome beginners on a no-drop ride. Garmin owners get free adaptive Garmin Coach cycling plans in Garmin Connect, though the full benefit needs a compatible Edge, heart-rate monitor and power meter. ## What to avoid

- Do not pay on day one. Try the free tier for two or three weekends first.
- Do not trust a planned route blindly; check it with local riders first.
- AI training numbers are estimates, not medical advice. Stop and see a doctor if you feel pain or unusual fatigue.
- Do not share live location with strangers; hide your home with a privacy zone.
- Do not believe a price in an article; re-check the official pricing page first.

## Frequently asked questions

**Which AI cycling app is best for beginners in India?**
Strava. Its free tier records rides, connects you with local clubs and shows popular routes; add Komoot for planning and Intervals.icu for analysis later.

**Is Strava free in India?**
Yes. GPS recording, the feed, route search, clubs and challenges are free. The subscription adds the route builder, offline routes, leaderboards and Beacon; prices differ, so check strava.com first.

**Can I plan cycling routes offline in India?**
Yes, but offline maps are paid almost everywhere. Komoot gives one free region; Strava and RideWithGPS need paid plans. Download on Wi-Fi before leaving home.

**Do these apps work without a power meter or smartwatch?**
Yes. A phone's GPS records distance, speed and elevation, and Intervals.icu analyses heart-rate and effort data too. A power meter only adds precision.

**Which tool is best for a cycling club or group ride?**
RideWithGPS for planning and cue sheets; Strava for the social side — clubs, challenges and finding riders. Most Indian clubs use both.

## Final verdict

Start free with Strava for tracking and groups, Komoot for routes, and Intervals.icu for deep analysis; pay only for offline maps or coaching. That combination covers nearly everything an Indian cyclist needs.
