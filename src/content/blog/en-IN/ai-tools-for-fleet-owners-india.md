If you own a fleet in India — five trucks running between cities, a dozen tempos serving local markets, a school-bus fleet, a taxi fleet, or a mix of vehicles that keeps growing — your profit hides in four places: vehicles that are where you think they are, drivers who drive safely, fuel that actually reaches the tank, and routes that do not waste kilometres. Lose control of any one of these and the cost shows up every month: a vehicle idle for hours, a driver speeding on a highway at night, a fuel bill that does not match the distance covered, or a route that sends two vehicles to the same area on the same day.

AI fleet tools exist to bring those four things onto one screen. GPS trackers report each vehicle's live location. Software turns that location data into alerts for overspeeding, idling and route deviation. Fuel sensors and fuel-card records flag pilferage and bad mileage. Route-planning tools order the day's stops so drivers cover less distance. Camera systems with AI watch the road and the driver, and score driving behaviour. None of this replaces a good fleet manager, but it gives that manager facts instead of phone calls and guesswork.

The direct answer is this: if you run a small fleet in India and your first problem is simply knowing where your vehicles are, start with an Indian GPS fleet platform such as LocoNav or Fleetx and ask for a written quote before you buy any hardware. If your fleet already has tracking and your next problem is planning the day's routes, add Routific, which is free for up to 100 orders a month, or OptimoRoute if you have several drivers and hundreds of stops. If accidents and risky driving are your biggest cost, look at an AI camera system such as Netradyne's Driver•i, but expect a custom quote, not a published price. Samsara is a powerful enterprise platform, but its published prices come from a United States public-sector contract list, not an Indian price list, so treat it as a benchmark for large fleets, not a small-fleet purchase. Google Maps and Waze stay useful as free navigation aids for individual drivers, but they are not fleet management tools: they do not track your vehicles, score your drivers, or reconcile your fuel. Every tool in this article works in India without a VPN, because all of them are ordinary web and mobile apps. Prices and free limits change often, so re-check the official pricing or help page before you pay, sign a contract, or promise a customer a delivery deadline based on a tool's claims.

## LocoNav: GPS tracking built for Indian fleets

LocoNav is one of the best-known Indian fleet management companies, and it is the natural first stop for a fleet owner who wants live visibility without importing hardware or software from abroad. Its platform offers real-time GPS fleet tracking, live alerts for overspeeding, entry and exit from set areas, and idling, plus vehicle service and maintenance alerts. It also offers a fleet lock feature that lets the owner stop a vehicle from starting without consent from the app, and reports on fleet health, diagnostics and mileage. For fuel, LocoNav says its tracking can help reduce fuel expenses by tracking driving behaviour and cutting idling time, which matters because idling and harsh driving quietly burn diesel every day.

The practical case for LocoNav is simple: it is built for Indian conditions, its app works on both Android and iPhone, and it covers the basics — location, alerts, maintenance reminders and fuel behaviour — in one place. The honest limitation is price transparency. LocoNav's own site says it offers GPS fleet tracking with no monthly fees, but on the same page it tells visitors to contact its team to learn the cost of its GPS tracking system. Those two statements do not sit comfortably together, so do not budget on the basis of "no monthly fees" alone. Ask for a written quote that states the device cost, any subscription or SIM charge, the contract length, and what happens to your tracking data if you stop paying. Also confirm that the GPS device offered is AIS-140 compliant where your vehicle category requires it, because compliance is a legal question, not just a feature checkbox.

LocoNav suits owners of roughly five to fifty vehicles who want one dashboard for location, alerts and maintenance, and who are willing to talk to sales before buying. It is not the right first tool if your only problem is ordering a day's delivery stops, because that is a route-planning job, and LocoNav's strength is tracking and control, not stop optimisation.

## Fleetx: end-to-end fleet and transport management from India

Fleetx describes itself as an AI-powered fleet and transport management platform built on AI and the Internet of Things, and it is aimed squarely at fleet and logistics operators in India and nearby markets. Its products cover five areas: a transport management system for planning, dispatching and settling freight end to end; fleet visibility for tracking every vehicle and route in real time; fuel monitoring that promises to show fuel loss before it damages operations; video and camera products for risk detection; and a transport ERP that runs the business from a single screen. For a fleet owner who also runs a transport business — booking loads, billing customers, paying drivers and settling freight — that breadth matters, because tracking alone does not close the month's accounts.

The honest caveat is pricing. Fleetx's official site does not publish a price list. A third-party software listing in India shows a starting price of ₹50 per user per year, but that figure comes from a listing site, not from Fleetx itself, and it is far too low to be taken at face value for a full fleet platform with hardware, SIM connectivity and support. Where sources disagree like this, trust the official site over the listing, and treat the listing figure as unconfirmed until Fleetx itself quotes it in writing. Ask for a quote that separates software charges, device charges, installation and support, and ask which products are included: a fleet-visibility subscription and a full transport ERP subscription may be priced very differently.

Fleetx suits transporters and mid-size fleet owners who want tracking, fuel monitoring, dispatch and billing in one system rather than four separate tools. It is probably more than a five-vehicle owner needs on day one, but it is the kind of platform a growing fleet can grow into without changing systems every year.

## FlowSense: fleet tracking, fuel and compliance for Indian roads

FlowSense is a newer Indian fleet platform that puts compliance and fuel control at the centre of its pitch. Its fleet product offers GPS and live tracking with geofencing, route replay and live location updates, AI route planning with traffic-aware re-routing, driver management with licence tracking and behaviour scoring, fuel monitoring with consumption tracking, pilferage detection and fuel-card integration, and maintenance scheduling with automated service alerts. It also claims dashboards for fleet utilisation and cost per kilometre, and compliance management with VAHAN and SARATHI integration plus permit, fitness-certificate and insurance tracking across regions.

Two points deserve honest handling. First, FlowSense's headline numbers — 200-plus fleet operators, 50,000-plus vehicles tracked, and 20 percent average fuel savings — are vendor claims from its own marketing pages, not independently verified results, and your savings will depend on your routes, vehicles and drivers. Second, FlowSense says its platform is hardware-agnostic and supports AIS-140-compliant devices in India, which is useful if you already have trackers fitted, because you may not need to replace hardware to switch software. No public price was found, so this is another quote-first purchase.

FlowSense suits fleet owners who care about fuel pilferage and compliance paperwork as much as live location — for example, owners running interstate trucks where permits, fitness certificates and insurance dates are a constant headache. If you only need to see dots on a map, it may be more platform than you need, but if fuel theft is your biggest leak, its fuel sensors and card reconciliation are the features to test first, on one or two vehicles, before rolling out across the fleet.

## Routific: free route planning for a small fleet

Routific is a route-optimisation tool, not a GPS tracker, and that distinction matters. You give it a list of stops with time windows, vehicle capacities and driver shifts, and it works out the best order, produces turn-by-turn routes that drivers open in Google Maps or Waze, and gives customers a live tracking link with email updates and accurate ETAs. It also collects proof of delivery with photos, signatures, barcode scanning and notes. For a fleet owner whose vehicles make many stops a day — milk delivery, LPG distribution, pharmacy supply, field service — this is the tool that cuts wasted kilometres.

The pricing is unusually honest and simple. Routific's own pricing page says the free plan covers up to 100 orders a month with every feature except SMS notifications, then a flat $150 a month up to 1,000 orders, with per-order charges above that at falling rates. It does not charge per driver or per vehicle, so a fleet with many part-time drivers and few orders gets a better deal than on per-driver tools. One caution: the pricing page shows prices with a dollar sign but does not name the currency on the page itself, so confirm the currency at checkout before comparing it with a rupee budget. Routific also says its customers have seen 15 to 40 percent less mileage and drive time compared with manual planning, but that is a vendor range, not a promise for your routes.

Routific suits small fleets and delivery operations that plan routes daily and want a free starting point. Pair it with a GPS tracker rather than treating it as one: Routific plans the route, while LocoNav or Fleetx tells you whether the driver actually followed it.

## OptimoRoute: multi-driver route planning at scale

When a fleet grows past what Routific's free plan comfortably covers, or when several drivers need planned routes every day with live tracking, OptimoRoute is the most practical paid step up. Its Lite plan costs $35.10 per driver per month and includes the driver mobile app, route history, live tracking, live ETAs and breadcrumbs, and planning for up to 700 orders at once, with no setup fees and no contract, plus a 10 percent discount if you prepay for a full year. Higher plans raise the order limit and add features, but Lite already covers the core job of a growing fleet: plan routes, send them to drivers, and watch progress live.

The trade-off against Routific is the pricing model. OptimoRoute charges per driver, so a fleet with many drivers pays for each one, while Routific charges per order and includes every driver free. A fleet with ten drivers and few orders may find Routific cheaper; a fleet with two drivers and thousands of orders may find OptimoRoute cheaper. Work out both totals for your actual driver count and order volume before choosing, and use the trial period to test with real stops, not demo data. Real Indian addresses, with their lanes, landmarks and incomplete pin codes, are the true test of any route tool, and a tool that plans perfect routes for a demo city may struggle with your actual delivery areas.

OptimoRoute suits fleets with several drivers, hundreds of stops a week, and a dispatcher who plans routes centrally. It does not replace GPS fleet hardware, fuel sensors or driver cameras; it plans and monitors the day's work.

## Netradyne Driver•i: AI cameras for driver safety

Netradyne's Driver•i is a different kind of fleet tool: instead of tracking where the vehicle is, it watches how it is driven. Its AI-enabled cameras analyse driving continuously, detect risky behaviour, coach drivers in the cab with audio alerts, and score drivers through its GreenZone score. Netradyne claims drivers with a GreenZone score of 950 or more are twice as less likely to have a collision, and that its AI analyses 100 percent of driving time with up to 99 percent accuracy. Those are vendor claims, and the "up to" in the accuracy figure matters: no camera system is perfect, and a fleet owner should ask what happens on false alerts, how driver-facing video is stored, and who can view it.

Netradyne operates in India through its Indian site, so this is not an import-only product, and driver-facing cameras raise a real people question alongside the safety case. Drivers may see a cab camera as surveillance, so the honest way to introduce it is to explain what is recorded, who sees it, how long footage is kept, and whether good driving is rewarded as well as bad driving punished. Netradyne itself frames its system around positive driving and recognising good behaviour, which is the right starting point for a fleet owner who wants fewer accidents without losing drivers.

No public price was found for Netradyne, so this is a quote-only purchase, and the total cost will include cameras, installation, connectivity and software. It suits fleets where accidents, insurance claims or risky night driving are already a visible cost — typically larger or highway-running fleets — rather than a small local fleet whose first problem is simply knowing where its vehicles are.

## Samsara: the enterprise benchmark, priced for large fleets

Samsara is a well-known enterprise fleet platform offering vehicle gateways for GPS tracking and diagnostics, AI dash cameras, asset trackers and fleet software, and it is worth understanding even if you never buy it, because it sets the standard for what a full platform can do. The honest pricing picture is that Samsara publishes no commercial price list of its own. The most detailed public numbers come from a United States cooperative purchasing contract, which lists a vehicle gateway licence at $39 per vehicle per month and a dual-facing AI dash camera licence at $55 per month, plus one-time hardware charges, with contracts typically running three to five years. Those are American public-sector list prices, not an Indian quote, and they exclude installation, taxes and any India-specific charges, so do not convert them into a rupee budget and call it a plan.

Samsara suits large fleets and enterprises that want one vendor for tracking, cameras, safety, maintenance and compliance reporting, and that can sign a multi-year contract. For a small or mid-size Indian fleet, the practical lesson is different: if a vendor quotes you a per-vehicle monthly price, compare it with Samsara's public contract figures as a sanity check, but buy from a vendor that supports your vehicles, your devices and your routes in India, with local installation and support.

## Google Maps and Waze: free navigation, not fleet management

Google Maps and Waze remain the free tools every Indian driver already knows, and they are genuinely useful: turn-by-turn navigation, live traffic, and route options that adapt to congestion. For a one-vehicle owner or a driver running a handful of stops, they cost nothing and need no training. Their limits are the reason this article exists: Google Maps allows only 10 places per route and does not optimise stop order automatically, it does not track your fleet, score your drivers, reconcile fuel, or prove delivery. Waze adds community-reported traffic and hazard alerts, which helps an individual driver, but it is also a single-driver tool.

Use them as the navigation layer underneath a real fleet system — Routific and OptimoRoute both send routes to Google Maps or Waze for driving — and not as the fleet system itself. The moment you need to answer "where are all my vehicles right now" or "which driver is speeding" from one screen, you have outgrown free navigation.

## Using these tools in India: vehicles, drivers, fuel and routes

Start with the problem that costs you the most money, not the tool with the longest feature list. If vehicles go missing from your view for hours, buy tracking first. If fuel bills do not match distance, add fuel monitoring on two vehicles and compare the sensor or card data with actual fill records for a month before trusting it. If drivers complain about routes, run your real stop list through Routific's free plan for two weeks and measure kilometres before and after. If accidents are the cost, trial cameras on your highest-risk route first, and share the safety scores with drivers rather than using them only for punishment.

Indian conditions shape every choice. Mobile coverage drops on highways and in rural areas, so ask vendors how tracking behaves offline and how much data is lost. Addresses are often landmarks rather than house numbers, so test route tools with your real stop lists, including the awkward ones. Drivers may share phones or use low-cost Android devices, so check that the driver app runs on the phones your team actually owns. Toll, FASTag and fuel-card records are useful cross-checks against software claims: if the tool says a vehicle covered 300 kilometres and the toll and fuel records say otherwise, believe the records and ask the vendor why.

Compliance is the other India-specific layer. India has an AIS-140 standard for vehicle location tracking devices, and the legal duty to fit such a device depends on the vehicle category: public service vehicles, national-permit vehicles, and certain goods vehicles carrying dangerous or hazardous goods fall under different rules and dates. This article is general information only, not legal advice, and the safest step is to confirm your exact duty with your State Transport Authority or a transport professional before buying hardware, because fitting a non-compliant device, or missing a required one, is a legal problem that no software dashboard can fix. Keep permits, fitness certificates, insurance and pollution certificates in the same system as your tracking, so expiry alerts reach you before a vehicle is stopped on the road.

## What to avoid

First, avoid buying hardware before you have a written quote that covers device cost, subscription, SIM or connectivity charges, installation, contract length and exit terms. A cheap tracker with an expensive monthly fee, or a platform that locks your history behind a contract, costs far more than a slightly dearer device with clear terms.

Second, avoid promising customers or drivers a result the tool has not proven on your routes. Vendor claims such as "20 percent fuel savings" or "15 to 40 percent less mileage" are averages and ranges from other customers, not guarantees for your fleet. Run a small trial, measure before and after, and only then scale.

Third, avoid tracking without telling drivers what is collected. Hidden GPS trackers and cab cameras destroy trust and can create legal and labour problems. Explain what is tracked, why, who sees it, and how long data is kept, and put that in writing for drivers.

Fourth, avoid paying in dollars without checking the currency, taxes and conversion charges. Several tools price in dollars, and the rupee total on your card statement may differ from the number on the pricing page. Confirm the final billed amount before committing, and re-check prices at renewal, because free limits and per-driver rates change.

Fifth, avoid buying an enterprise platform for a small-fleet problem. Samsara, Netradyne and full transport ERPs are strong products, but a five-vehicle owner who buys them first is paying for scale they do not yet have. Start with tracking and route planning, prove the savings, and let the fleet's growth justify the bigger purchase.

Finally, avoid treating any of this article's prices as current. Prices, free limits and feature lists change without notice, and third-party listings can be wrong. Always re-check the official pricing or help page before paying or promising a deadline, and keep a copy of the quote you were given.

## Frequently asked questions

**Which AI tool should a small fleet owner in India start with?**
Start with GPS tracking from an Indian provider such as LocoNav or Fleetx, because knowing where every vehicle is, and getting overspeeding and idling alerts, solves the most expensive daily unknown. Add Routific for route planning once stops, rather than location, become the bottleneck.

**Do these fleet tools work in India without a VPN?**
Yes. Every tool in this article is an ordinary web or mobile app available in India, and the Indian providers among them are built for Indian roads, devices and regulations. No VPN is needed for any of them.

**What is the cheapest way to plan routes for a small fleet?**
Routific's free plan, which covers up to 100 orders a month with every feature except SMS notifications, is the cheapest honest starting point. Google Maps is free but limited to 10 places per route and does not optimise stop order, so it stops being enough once stops grow.

**How do fleet tools detect fuel theft?**
They compare fuel data from tank sensors or fuel-card records with distance covered and expected consumption, and flag sudden drops, fills that do not match receipts, and consumption far above the fleet average. Treat the first month's flags as leads to investigate, not proof, and cross-check with fuel bills before accusing any driver.

**Is an AIS-140 GPS device legally required for my vehicles?**
It depends on the vehicle category. Public service vehicles, national-permit vehicles, and certain goods vehicles carrying dangerous or hazardous goods fall under different rules, so the answer differs by vehicle type and registration date. This is general information only, not legal advice: confirm your exact duty with your State Transport Authority or a transport professional.

**Are AI dash cameras worth it for a small fleet?**
Usually not as the first purchase. Cameras such as Netradyne's Driver•i make sense when accidents, insurance claims or risky driving already cost real money, because they carry camera, installation and subscription costs and need a clear driver policy. Small fleets should fix tracking and routes first, then add cameras on the highest-risk vehicles.

**Should I choose Routific or OptimoRoute for route planning?**
Choose Routific if you have many drivers but few orders, because it charges per order and includes every driver free, and its free plan covers 100 orders a month. Choose OptimoRoute if you have a few drivers and hundreds of stops, because its per-driver price of $35.10 a month on Lite buys planning for up to 700 orders at once with live tracking.

**Can one tool handle tracking, fuel, drivers and routes together?**
Indian platforms such as Fleetx and FlowSense come closest, combining tracking, fuel monitoring, driver management and route planning, and LocoNav covers tracking, fuel behaviour and maintenance. In practice most fleets still pair a tracker platform with a dedicated route tool such as Routific or OptimoRoute, because route optimisation is a specialised job.

**What should I check before signing a fleet software contract?**
Check the device cost, monthly fee, SIM charges, contract length, what happens to your data if you leave, whether the price is in rupees or dollars, and whether support and installation are included. Get it in writing, and test the system on two or three of your own vehicles before rolling it out to the whole fleet.

## Final verdict

Start with LocoNav or Fleetx for tracking, add Routific for routes, and buy Netradyne or Samsara only when scale justifies a quote.
