A resume in India in 2026 is read twice: first by software, then by a human. Applicant Tracking Software, or **ATS**, parses your resume, searches it for **keywords**, and decides whether a human ever sees it. A beautiful resume that ATS cannot read will never reach a human, and a plain resume with the right keywords will. AI tools help with both parts, but the tool that writes best does not always lay out best, and the tool that lays out best does not always check ATS.

The short answer for India is this: write and tailor your content with [*ChatGPT*](https://chatgpt.com/) free, lay it out in [*FlowCV*](https://flowcv.com/) free or [*Reactive Resume*](https://rxresu.me/) free, and check it with [*CareerCtrl*](https://careerctrl.com/) free, which gives two AI Tuner ATS scans a month with unlimited PDF downloads and no watermark. Browse examples in [*Kickresume*](https://www.kickresume.com/en/) free, which gives unlimited downloads with four basic templates and more than 1,500 examples, but keeps AI writing and the ATS checker for Premium. Try [*Rezi*](https://www.rezi.ai/) free only for one resume, because it allows one resume and three PDF downloads with limited AI. Avoid builders that let you write a resume free but charge for the PDF, like [*Zety*](https://zety.com/), [*MyPerfectResume*](https://www.myperfectresume.com/), and [*Resume.io*](https://resume.io/), which give TXT only on free.

This article is written for India on purpose: freshers, students, and professionals applying to Indian and multinational companies, Hindi, Hinglish, and English resumes, a normal phone and laptop, and no assumption that you have a paid plan or a US account. It also carries a warning that matters for job seekers more than for any other group in this series: strip your address and phone number before pasting a resume into a consumer chatbot, since consumer chats can be used for model training unless you opt out. Your resume is [personal data](/blog/ai-privacy-safety-free-tools-india/), and a free chatbot is not a private document editor.

If you read nothing else, remember three rules.

1. **One column, standard headings, real text** — an ATS-friendly resume uses one column, standard headings like Experience, Education, and Skills, and real text instead of images, and any free builder above can produce one if you pick a simple template.
2. **ATS scores are estimates, not guarantees** — ATS scores are each vendor's own estimate: employer systems parse and search your resume, they do not publish a score you can match, so a score of 80 does not guarantee an interview, and a score of 60 does not guarantee rejection.
3. **Avoid sameness** — the biggest risk with AI resume writing is sameness: replace generic verbs with numbers, tools, and outcomes only you can claim, because a resume that could belong to anyone will be ignored by everyone.

## Writing a resume that passes ATS and humans

An ATS-friendly resume uses one column, standard headings such as Experience, Education, and Skills, and real text instead of images. Use standard section names, because ATS looks for Experience, Education, and Skills, not for creative names like My Journey or What I Bring. Use real text for your name, your job titles, and your skills, not text inside an image, because ATS cannot read text inside an image. Use a simple font and a simple layout, because a fancy layout with columns, tables, and graphics will confuse ATS parsing, and a confused parse means your keywords are lost.

Tailor your resume for each job, but tailor smartly. Read the job post, note the exact skills, tools, and keywords it asks for, and include the ones you genuinely have, in your Skills section and in your experience bullets, using the job post's exact wording where it matches your experience. Do not stuff keywords you do not have, because a human will ask about them in an interview, and a keyword you cannot defend will cost you the job. The cheapest strong setup is to write and tailor your content with ChatGPT or [*Claude*](https://claude.com/), lay it out in a free builder, and check it against the job post with [*Jobscan*](https://www.jobscan.co/)'s free scans, or with CareerCtrl's free ATS scans.

For Indian freshers, the experience section is the hardest part, because you may not have work experience. Use internships, projects, freelance work, and college projects, with numbers and outcomes: what you built, what tool you used, and what changed. A bullet that says "Worked on a website" is weak. A bullet that says "Built a college event website with HTML, CSS, and JavaScript, used by 500 students for registration" is strong, because it gives a tool, a scale, and an outcome. AI can help you turn a weak bullet into a strong bullet, but only you can supply the number, the tool, and the outcome, because AI will invent them if you do not.

## Privacy, photos, and Indian resume norms

Strip your address and phone number before pasting a resume into a consumer chatbot, since consumer chats can be used for model training unless you opt out. Paste only the content you need help with, like a bullet or a job description, and add your personal details back in your builder, after AI has helped with wording. Do not paste your full resume, with your address, phone, and email, into a free chatbot, because that personal data becomes training data you cannot control, and it can resurface in ways you cannot predict.

For Indian resumes, a photo is common but not required, and it is not required for ATS. If you include a photo, use a professional photo, and keep it out of the ATS-parsed text, because ATS does not need a photo to parse your skills. For multinational companies, a photo is often not expected, and a photo-free resume is safer. For Indian companies that expect a photo, include one, but keep the resume single-column and ATS-friendly otherwise. Do not include your full address, your date of birth, or your parents' names, because modern Indian resumes do not need them, and they are personal data that should not be in a document you send to dozens of companies.

For Hindi and Hinglish job seekers, write your resume in English, because most Indian company portals and multinational recruiters expect English, and ATS keywords are in English. Use AI to [check your English grammar](/blog/ai-tools-for-learning-english-india/) and to tailor bullets, but write the content yourself first, because AI English can sound generic, and a generic resume is ignored. For government jobs, follow the exact format in the notification, because government applications often have their own form and their own rules, and an AI resume format will not match them.

## What to avoid

- **Do not use a builder that charges for the PDF** — CareerCtrl, FlowCV, and Reactive Resume all give unlimited PDF downloads free.
- **Do not use a two-column Canva design for a company portal** — ATS will misread it, so use a single-column ATS-friendly layout instead.
- **Do not copy Kickresume example bullets word for word** — recruiters recognise copied bullets, and you cannot defend them in an interview.
- **Do not paste your full resume with personal details into a consumer chatbot** — consumer chats can be used for training unless you opt out.
- **Do not trust an ATS score as a guarantee** — employer systems do not publish a score, and a vendor score is only an estimate.
- **Do not pay for Rezi, Jobscan, or Resume Worded for a whole year during a short search** — if you pay, pay for one month during an active search, because quarterly and yearly plans only save money if you actually search for that long.

A final trap is applying to hundreds of jobs with the same generic resume. Beginners often use AI to generate one resume, then apply to every job with it, and wonder why they get no interviews. Tailor your resume for each role, even if tailoring means changing only the skills order and two bullets, because a tailored resume shows the recruiter you read the job post, and a generic resume shows you did not. Use AI to tailor faster, not to avoid tailoring, because the tailor is you, and AI is only the assistant.
