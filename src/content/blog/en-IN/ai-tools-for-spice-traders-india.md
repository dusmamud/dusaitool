## The short answer

Spice trading runs on four numbers: what you paid, what the mandi pays today, what sits in your godown, and what the buyer will pay. Vyapar does billing and stock, Zoho Inventory scales with orders, ChatGPT drafts quotes, IndiaMART brings bulk enquiries, WhatsApp Business is your catalogue. ## 1. Vyapar — billing and stock for a small spice shop

Vyapar is made in India for small traders: GST invoicing, party ledgers, batch and expiry tracking, and barcode billing are built in. Batch tracking matters because chilli, turmeric and cumin lose value with age and moisture, and an old lot sold as fresh is a lost customer. The Android app is free for life, with GST invoicing, inventory, ledgers and offline working. Paid desktop editions start from around ₹3,399 a year plus GST.

## 2. Zoho Inventory — when orders outgrow a phone app

Zoho Inventory is a full inventory and order system from Zoho, the Chennai company. It tracks stock across warehouses, handles composite items such as blended masala packs, and supports dropshipping and backorders. Its free plan covers 50 orders a month for one user and one location. The Standard plan starts at ₹999 a month when billed annually, excluding GST, for 500 orders a month. 

## 3. ChatGPT — quotes, buyer messages and quick research

ChatGPT is the most flexible tool here: a general AI assistant you point at trading jobs. Ask it to draft a quotation, rewrite a buyer message in Hindi and English, or summarise an export enquiry. The free plan costs nothing and, according to OpenAI's own pages, gives unlimited everyday text chats, with limits on uploads, images and voice instead — sources disagree on exact limits. ChatGPT Go costs ₹399 a month in India and Plus costs ₹1,999 a month. Never let it invent a price: check its numbers against Agmarknet.

## 4. IndiaMART — bulk buyers looking for suppliers

IndiaMART is India's largest B2B marketplace, and for a spice trader it is where bulk buyers come looking. Seller registration is free: verify your number, add company details, list three products, and buyers can send enquiries. There is no commission on orders. Paid memberships exist for more visibility — published prices for the lower tiers start around ₹35,000 a year, so get any price in writing first. Test free first: if enquiries convert, a paid plan may pay for itself. See also our guide to [AI tools for Amazon sellers in India](/blog/ai-tools-for-amazon-sellers-india/).

## 5. WhatsApp Business — your catalogue and order book

Most spice buyers in India already talk on WhatsApp, so the WhatsApp Business app meets them where they are. It is free, and adds a business profile, a product catalogue with photos and prices, quick replies, greeting and away messages, and labels to tag chats as new order, payment pending or delivered. The catalogue reportedly holds up to 500 products; broadcasts are limited to 256 recipients.

## Using these tools in India

Agmarknet publishes daily mandi prices for dry chilli, turmeric, cumin, coriander seed and black pepper, per quintal — always check the date, because a two-day-old rate can be badly wrong. The Spices Board's e-Spice Bazaar links farmers with buyers, but it began with chilli and turmeric farmers in Andhra Pradesh and Telangana, so coverage is limited. Enter each lot once and let every bill and quote come from that one record.

## What to avoid

Do not pay for a premium plan before the free listing brings real enquiries. Do not trust any AI tool's spice price — only the mandi, your supplier and the buyer set the real rate. Do not share bank details, OTPs or GST login credentials with anyone from a marketplace — genuine buyers never ask for them.

## Frequently asked questions

**Which free tool should a spice trader start with?** Vyapar's free app for billing and stock, plus free WhatsApp Business for buyers — the daily work at zero cost.

**How do I check today's spice prices before quoting a buyer?** Check Agmarknet for the latest mandi price, note the date and market, then add grading, packing, transport and margin. Never quote a week-old price or an unchecked chatbot figure.

**Is IndiaMART really free for spice sellers?** Registration and basic listing are free, and IndiaMART takes no commission. Paid plans exist for extra visibility, with lower tiers starting around ₹35,000 a year — try free first.

**Can ChatGPT help with export buyers?** Yes, for drafting replies, translating messages and summarising requirements. It cannot verify a buyer or set a price — check the Spices Board and your bank instead.

**What is the cheapest way to manage spice inventory with expiry dates?** Vyapar's free app covers batch and expiry tracking, low-stock alerts and barcode scanning. If volume outgrows it, Zoho Inventory's free plan (50 orders a month) is next.

**Do I need GST registration to sell spices online?** It depends on how and where you sell. GST is not always required for a free listing, but it is needed for GST invoicing and most serious buyers. Confirm with your accountant.
