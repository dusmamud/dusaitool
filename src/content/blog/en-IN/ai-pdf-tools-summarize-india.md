A long PDF is a different problem from a long article. A 200-page textbook chapter, a 50-page contract, a research paper, or a government circular cannot be read line by line when you have an hour, and searching for one clause inside it is slow. AI PDF tools solve that problem in three ways: they **summarize** the document, they **answer questions** about it, and they let you **compare several documents** at once. The tools are not equal, and the best tool depends on whether you need citations, whether the document is private, and how many documents you have.

The short answer for India is this: for study and research, use *NotebookLM* free, because its answers are grounded in your uploaded sources and include inline citations that lead back to source passages. For one quick PDF in a browser, use *ChatPDF* free, because it needs no account and allows two document analyses a day. For a short document that also needs compression or page deletion, use *Smallpdf* AI free, knowing its AI features are limited on free and unlimited on Pro. For desktop editing plus AI, use *PDFgear*, whose AI editor is described as free with OCR, conversion, and summarization. Never upload a private document, like a contract with personal details, a medical report, or a company paper, without checking the tool's **retention terms** first.

This article is written for India on purpose: students, job seekers, [teachers](/blog/ai-tools-for-teachers-india/), and office workers, Hindi, Hinglish, and English documents, a normal phone and laptop, and no assumption that you have a paid plan or an institutional account. It also carries a warning that matters for PDF tools more than for any other tool in this series: a no-account trial does not mean the document is never retained. Check retention before you upload, especially for documents with personal, financial, or company information.

## NotebookLM: the citation tool for study and research

*NotebookLM* is the strongest choice for source-grounded research because its answers can include **inline citations** that lead back to uploaded source passages. That single feature changes how you study: when NotebookLM says a chapter defines a concept a certain way, you can click the citation and read the exact passage, instead of trusting a summary that might have paraphrased it wrong. Standard access allows up to 100 notebooks, 50 sources per notebook, 500,000 words per source, and 50 chat queries per day. Those limits are generous for a student: 50 sources per notebook covers a whole semester's reading, and 50 queries a day covers a full study session, as long as you do not waste queries on the same question rephrased five times.

Use NotebookLM in a specific way:

1. Create one notebook per subject or per project, upload only the sources for that subject, and ask questions that the sources can answer.
2. Ask for a summary, then ask for the key definitions, then ask for a comparison between two chapters, and check every important answer against its citation.
3. For Hindi documents, test one chapter first, because AI summarization quality varies by language and by document formatting, and a Hindi textbook with tables and diagrams may summarize less cleanly than an English textbook with plain text. NotebookLM is strongest when answers are grounded in supplied sources, and it is weakest when a PDF does not import cleanly, so if a PDF fails to import, try a cleaner copy or a text-based version before assuming the tool is broken.

NotebookLM is also the best tool for comparing documents. Upload a contract and its amendment, or two research papers on the same topic, and ask what changed, what disagrees, and what each document says about one question. That comparison is slow by hand and fast with source-grounded citations, and it is the job where NotebookLM beats every general chatbot, because a general chatbot may blend the two documents and invent a middle answer, while NotebookLM shows you which document said what.

## ChatGPT and Gemini: general chatbots that also read PDFs

*ChatGPT* is the most flexible PDF tool, because it does not just summarize: it extracts tables, synthesizes across documents, and turns a PDF into writing, like a report, an [email](/blog/ai-tools-for-email-writing-india/), or a [study note](/blog/ai-tools-for-study-notes-india/). OpenAI documents **three file uploads per day** for free users, with each file up to 512 MB and 2 million tokens, and limited data analysis on the free plan. That is enough for one or two long documents a day, but not for a whole archive. Non-Enterprise PDF processing is text-based, so embedded images may be discarded, which matters for PDFs where the key information is in a chart, a diagram, or a scanned image. If your PDF is mostly images, use an OCR tool first, or use PDFgear, before asking ChatGPT to summarize it.

*Gemini* is the best PDF tool for Google-account users, because it accepts up to 10 files in one prompt, with a 100 MB limit for many file types, and it handles multi-file prompts well. Users without Google AI Pro or Ultra have more limited file-analysis access, so free access is not equally available for every file-upload and reference capability. For a student who already uses Google Drive, Gemini is convenient: upload a PDF from Drive, ask for a summary, and ask follow-up questions without leaving Google tools. For citation-sensitive research, NotebookLM is still better, because its citation workflow is more central to the product, while Gemini is better for flexible questions and for comparing several files at once.

Use a general chatbot for PDFs in a specific way. Upload the PDF, ask for a summary with page references or section names, then ask specific questions, and verify any number, date, or legal clause against the PDF itself before you use it. Chatbots can misread tables, confuse two similar numbers, and invent a confident answer when the PDF does not contain the information. For exam answers, contract clauses, and financial figures, verification is not optional: open the PDF, find the passage, and check the number yourself.

## ChatPDF and Smallpdf: the quick browser tools

*ChatPDF* is the fastest way to try chat-with-PDF: no signup for two documents a day, cited answers, and support for summaries, questions, translations, and research-paper workflows. Its side-by-side UI, chat on one side and PDF on the other, with clickable citations that jump straight back to the source page, turns a summary from a wall of text into an actual reading tool. Multi-file conversations let you compare or cross-reference several PDFs in a single thread, which is useful for research or contract comparison. Supported formats go beyond PDF, including Word, PowerPoint, Markdown, and text files, and it is fully multilingual, so you can upload a document in one language and chat in another. Under the hood it uses dynamic routing between GPT-4o and GPT-4o-mini depending on question complexity.

*ChatPDF*'s free limit is clear: **two documents per day**. That makes it best for occasional reading rather than a large research archive. Anything beyond that needs the paid ChatPDF Plus tier, which offers unlimited documents, advanced features, and API access. Before uploading a confidential paper, review the service's current terms and data practices, and do not assume that a no-account trial means the document is never retained. For a public research paper, a textbook chapter, or a government circular, ChatPDF free is the quickest way to get a summary and ask questions. For a private contract, a medical report, or a company document, check retention first, or use a desktop tool like PDFgear that keeps the file on your machine.

*Smallpdf* AI PDF Assistant is the right choice for quick browser-based PDF actions when analysis and ordinary PDF manipulation belong in the same workflow. Smallpdf says its AI Assistant is free with no signup required and supports uploads, summaries, questions, generated questions, translations, conversion, compression, and page deletion. Its summarizer covers PDFs, Word, Excel, PowerPoint, and image files, including scanned documents, with summary styles like bullet points, key insights, or a fuller overview, and one source says uploaded files are deleted within an hour. The word free needs qualification: Smallpdf's pricing page lists Chat with PDF, AI PDF Summarizer, Translation, and Question Generator as limited features on the Free plan and describes them as unlimited on Pro. Smallpdf is therefore a useful free entry point, not an unlimited free AI PDF service. It is a sensible first stop for a short document that needs a summary followed by a routine operation such as compression or page deletion.

## PDFgear, AskYourPDF, and Acrobat: desktop, extension, and integrated options

*PDFgear* is the best fit when PDF analysis must be combined with editing, conversion, annotation, page management, or OCR. Its official pages describe the AI editor as free and include OCR, conversion, summarization, questions, and editing, although the reviewed product pages do not publish a stable, detailed quota table. For Indian users, PDFgear's desktop nature is an advantage for privacy: the file stays on your computer for editing, and you are not uploading every document to a browser service. For scanned PDFs, like old government forms, handwritten notes scanned to PDF, or Hindi documents scanned as images, PDFgear's OCR matters, because it turns the image into text that AI can actually read. Test OCR on one scanned page first, because OCR quality for Hindi and for handwriting varies, and a bad OCR result will produce a bad summary no matter how good the AI is.

*AskYourPDF* is the right choice for users who want browser, mobile, extension, and Zotero-connected access, with free access to start, supporting PDF and text uploads, questions, summaries, and key-insight extraction. Its reviewed official page does not expose a complete current quota table, so treat its free plan as a trial, not a measured allowance, and check the current limits on its pricing page before you rely on it for a large project. For [researchers](/blog/ai-tools-for-research-students-india/) who already use Zotero, AskYourPDF's Zotero connection saves the step of downloading and re-uploading papers, which matters when you have dozens of papers.

*Adobe Acrobat AI Assistant* is for users who already work in Acrobat. Adobe advertises a free online PDF AI entry point, while full Acrobat plans and features have paid restrictions, so free online access should not be confused with the complete Acrobat subscription feature set. If you already pay for Acrobat, its AI Assistant is convenient because it lives inside the document workflow you already use. If you do not pay for Acrobat, NotebookLM, ChatPDF, or Smallpdf free cover the same summary and question jobs without a subscription.

## Using PDF AI in India: language, exams, and privacy

Indian documents are often bilingual or multilingual, and PDF AI quality varies by language. English PDFs with clean text summarize best. Hindi PDFs with clean text usually summarize well, but Hindi PDFs with tables, mixed Hindi and English, or scanned images need testing. For a Hindi textbook, upload one chapter, ask for a summary in Hindi, and check it against the chapter before you upload the whole book. For a Hinglish document, ask the tool to answer in the language you will write in, because a summary in Hinglish is useful for understanding, but an exam answer must be in the exam language, with the exact technical terms your board uses.

For exams, PDF AI is a study aid, not a substitute for reading. Use NotebookLM to summarize a chapter, to generate questions from it, and to compare two chapters, then read the key passages yourself and write answers in your own words. A student who only reads AI summaries will miss the exact definitions, the exact examples, and the exact diagrams that examiners ask about. For contracts and government circulars, use PDF AI to find the relevant clause and to explain it in plain language, then read the clause itself before you act on it, because a plain-language explanation is not a legal document, and an AI summary can miss a condition, a deadline, or an exception that changes the meaning.

Privacy for PDF tools is simple to state and hard to follow: **do not upload a document you would not email to a stranger**. Personal details, financial information, medical reports, company papers, and legal documents should not go into a free browser tool unless you have checked retention, deletion, and data training terms, and you are comfortable with them. For [private documents](/blog/ai-privacy-safety-free-tools-india/), prefer a desktop tool like PDFgear, or a tool your institution has approved, or read the document yourself. For public documents, like textbooks, research papers, and government circulars, free browser tools are safe and useful, and their speed is a genuine advantage.

## What to avoid

- Do not trust an AI summary of a contract, a circular, or a financial document without reading the relevant passage yourself, because AI can miss conditions, deadlines, and exceptions.
- Do not upload a private document to a free browser tool without checking retention, and do not assume that no-account means no retention.
- Do not use a text-only chatbot on a scanned or image-heavy PDF without OCR first, because the tool will miss the information in the images.
- Do not rely on ChatPDF free for a large archive, because it allows only two documents a day, and do not rely on Smallpdf free as unlimited, because its AI features are limited on free.
- Do not use NotebookLM's 50 queries a day on the same question rephrased five times, because you will run out of queries before your study session ends.
- Do not pay for five PDF tools at once, and do not use an AI summary as your only exam preparation, because examiners ask for exact definitions and examples that summaries often drop.

A final trap is uploading everything and reading nothing. Beginners often upload a whole textbook, ask for a summary, and then feel they have studied, when they have only read a summary. AI PDF tools save time, but they do not replace reading the key passages, writing answers in your own words, and checking numbers and clauses yourself. Use AI to find what matters, to summarize what you have already understood, and to generate questions, and use your own reading for what actually counts.

## The final verdict, and a checklist before you pay

Start free, and start with citations. For study and research, use NotebookLM free, with one notebook per subject, only the sources for that subject, and every important answer checked against its citation. For one quick PDF, use ChatPDF free, with two documents a day and no account. For a short document that also needs compression or page deletion, use Smallpdf AI free, knowing its AI features are limited on free. For desktop editing plus AI, especially for scanned PDFs, use PDFgear, whose AI editor is described as free with OCR. For multi-file prompts inside Google tools, use Gemini, knowing free users have more limited file-analysis access.

Before you pay for any PDF tool, answer these questions in writing:

1. Which exact limit stopped you, and on which date?
2. Did it stop document count, query count, export, OCR, or retention, because each has a different fix?
3. Did you check retention and deletion for the documents you upload?
4. Did you check the official pricing page today?
5. Does the paid plan fix that exact limit, or does it only give you more of something you did not need?

If you cannot answer those questions, you are not ready to pay yet, and another month of free use will give you a clearer answer than any review article can.

AI PDF tools in India in 2026 are genuinely useful, but only when you treat them as reading assistants with citation, OCR, and privacy limits, not as replacements for reading. Use free tools to summarize, to find clauses, to generate questions, and to compare documents, and use your own reading for numbers, clauses, and exam answers that actually count. Pay later, **for one tool, for one proven reason**, after checking retention first. That is how a long document becomes manageable without becoming a risk.
