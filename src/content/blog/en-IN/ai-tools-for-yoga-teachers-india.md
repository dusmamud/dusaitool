A yoga teacher's hardest work is often not the teaching. It is the work around the teaching: answering the same booking question ten times on WhatsApp, chasing fees, remembering who paid for which batch, finding a link five minutes before class, and trying to remember which student is still struggling with balance poses and which one is ready for a harder sequence. The right tools do not replace your judgement as a teacher. They remove the admin work so that your judgement gets more time.

The short answer for India is this: use [Klippme](https://klippme.com/for/yoga-teachers) for class bookings and payments if you teach in India, because it is built for Indian teachers, supports UPI and Razorpay payments, and has a free plan. Use [Momoyoga](https://www.momoyoga.com/) if you run a yoga studio and want a yoga-specific system with classes, memberships, video on demand and a student app. Use [Calendly](https://calendly.com/) only for simple one-to-one bookings, such as private sessions and trial calls, because its free plan covers just one event type. Use Google Meet for online group classes up to 60 minutes on a free personal account, and Zoom when you need its recording and breakout features, remembering that free Zoom group meetings end after 40 minutes. Use Google Classroom to organise course material and track assignments and progress in a structured way, and WhatsApp Business to send reminders and answer students quickly. Prices and free limits change often, so re-check the official pricing and help page of any tool before you pay or promise a student a deadline.

This article is written for yoga teachers in India: solo teachers with morning and evening batches, teachers who run a small studio, and teachers who teach online to students in different cities. It assumes a normal phone and laptop, patchy internet on some days, students who prefer WhatsApp and UPI, and no assumption that you have a large studio budget or an international payment account. Every tool named here works in India without a VPN. Where a price is given, it is the price found during research for this article, and it must be re-checked before you spend money, because vendors change prices, free limits and fees without warning.

## Klippme: bookings and payments built for India

Klippme is the most India-first option in this list, and for a solo teacher or a small studio it is the easiest place to start. You create a booking page, set up your batches, and students book their own spot and pay at the same time. Each batch can have its own capacity, price and schedule, so a morning batch, an evening batch, a weekend workshop and a one-to-one alignment session can all live on one page without you managing four different WhatsApp threads.

The features that matter most for yoga teaching are the practical ones. You can set a seat limit for each group session, so a class closes itself when the last mat is taken. You can set a class to repeat every week instead of recreating it again and again. You can run multi-day courses, such as a 21-day or 30-day programme, that a student pays for once. Online classes can generate a Google Meet or Zoom link automatically, and in-person classes can carry a studio address or map link. Trial classes can be offered free or at a discount, and you can see exactly who converted to a full batch afterwards. Students get reminders before class, which is the single biggest fix for empty mats, because most no-shows are forgotten classes, not cancelled classes.

The free plan is genuinely usable for a teacher who is just starting out, but its limits are tight, and you should know them before you depend on it. The free plan covers 10 bookings a month, one one-to-one session and one group session with up to 10 seats, with email notifications and reminders, a shareable booking link, and manual and online payments through Razorpay. The paid Growth plan was listed at 499 rupees a month plus GST, with unlimited one-to-one and group sessions, unlimited bookings, coupon codes, session bundles and packages, and email and WhatsApp reminders. Klippme states that it takes no commission on any plan, and that only the payment gateway's standard fee applies on top. Re-check the pricing page before you upgrade, because the free trial of the Growth plan was listed as seven days with no credit card, and trial terms change.

Klippme is best when your main problem is bookings and fees. It is less suited if you need a full studio system with a branded student app, membership tiers and video on demand, which is where Momoyoga fits better. It is also not a teaching platform on its own: it books the class and collects the payment, but the class itself still happens on Meet, on Zoom, or in your studio.

## Momoyoga: a yoga-specific studio system

Momoyoga is built specifically for yoga teachers and studios, and that focus shows in its features. It covers classes, events, memberships and class passes, online teaching, video on demand, a mobile app for students, waitlists, and booking and cancellation windows. In practice, that means a student can book a class, join a waitlist when a class is full, buy a class pass, and watch a recorded session later, all inside one system, instead of you stitching together a booking form, a payment link and a video folder.

The free plan includes offering classes and events, receiving payments, online teaching, video on demand and the student app, which is generous for a teacher testing whether a studio system suits them. The catch is in the fees: on the free plan, Momoyoga charges a 5 percent platform fee on every online payment, on top of the payment processor's standard transaction fee. That matters, because a free plan that takes a cut of every payment can cost more than a cheap paid plan once your student numbers grow. The paid Standard plan was listed at 34 euros a month billed annually, and Plus at 69 euros a month billed annually, with the 5 percent fee removed on paid plans and features such as unlimited memberships, waitlists, exports and branding added as you go up. Prices are listed in euros and exclude VAT for EU customers, so an Indian teacher should check the final amount in rupees, including any conversion and bank charges, before committing. That currency point alone makes Klippme a simpler choice for many Indian teachers, even though Momoyoga is the more yoga-specific product.

Momoyoga is best when you run a studio with regular classes, memberships and repeat students, and you want one system rather than five tools. It is probably more than a solo teacher with one batch needs, and its euro pricing means you should compare the total monthly cost carefully against an Indian-priced tool before you choose.

## Calendly: simple booking links for private sessions

Calendly is not a yoga platform, and it does not try to be one. It is a scheduling link: you set your availability, share a link, and a student books a slot without the back-and-forth of messages. For a yoga teacher, its best use is narrow and clear: private one-to-one sessions, trial calls, consultation calls, and assessment sessions where one student books one slot with you.

The free plan covers one event type, one calendar connection, one-to-one scheduling and a customisable booking page. That is enough for a single kind of private session, such as a 45-minute one-to-one class. It is not enough for a teacher who runs several batch types, because the free plan allows only one event type, and group scheduling, multiple calendars, automated reminders and payment connections sit on paid plans. The Standard plan was listed at 10 US dollars per seat per month billed yearly, with unlimited event types, multiple calendars, automations and reminders, and Stripe and PayPal payment connections. As with Momoyoga, check the final rupee cost before paying, and check whether Stripe or PayPal suits your students, because many Indian students will still prefer UPI, which is where Klippme has a clear advantage.

Use Calendly when your problem is simply "stop the scheduling messages for private sessions". Do not try to force it into a full batch booking system with seat limits, class passes and waitlists, because that is not what it is built for, and you will end up managing the missing pieces on WhatsApp anyway.

## Google Meet and Zoom: the online classroom itself

Most online yoga classes in India run on Google Meet or Zoom, and the choice between them usually comes down to one number: how long your class lasts. On a free personal Google account, Google Meet group calls run up to 60 minutes. On Zoom's free Basic plan, group meetings with three or more participants end after 40 minutes, while one-to-one meetings have no such time cap. A 45-minute or 50-minute group class therefore fits inside free Meet but gets cut off mid-class on free Zoom, unless you restart the meeting, which breaks the flow of a yoga session in a way it might not break a lecture. A 30-minute class fits both. A 60-minute class fits free Meet exactly, with no margin for a late start, and a 75-minute or 90-minute class needs a paid plan on either platform.

Zoom's strengths are real, and they matter for teaching. It offers breakout rooms for smaller practice groups, local recording on the free plan, screen sharing, in-meeting chat and a whiteboard. Those features help when you want students to practise in pairs, or when you want to record a session for students to review later. Cloud recording, detailed attendance reporting and longer group meetings need a paid plan, and the Pro plan was reported at around 1,330 rupees a month per host, though you must verify the current India price on Zoom's official site before buying, because third-party reports and official prices can differ.

Google Meet's strengths are simplicity and reach. Most students already have a Google account, the link opens in a browser without a separate app install in many cases, and it connects naturally with Google Calendar, Classroom and Drive. Its free limit is the thing to plan around: keep free classes at 60 minutes or under, start on time, and tell students in advance what happens if the call ends, so nobody is confused mid-pose. For paid teaching, the honest rule is simple: if your regular class is longer than the free limit, budget for a paid plan from the start, and build that cost into your class fee, rather than restarting meetings and hoping students stay.

Whichever platform you choose, a few teaching habits matter more than the platform itself. Share the link in the booking confirmation, not only in a chat message that gets buried. Ask students to join five minutes early so camera angles and audio can be fixed before the class starts. Teach students how to frame their mat so you can see their posture, and keep your own camera framed so they can see your full body, not just your face. Record only with students' clear permission, and tell them where the recording will be stored and who can see it.

## Google Classroom: organising material and tracking progress

Google Classroom is free for a teacher with a personal Google account, and it solves a different problem from Meet or Zoom: it organises everything around the class. You can create a class for each batch or course, post announcements, share reading material and videos, set assignments and questions, organise work by topic, and give private feedback to individual students. It has a gradebook view, so you can see who has submitted what, and you can return work with comments. That structure is exactly what student progress tracking needs, because progress in yoga is not one test score: it is attendance over time, consistency of practice, flexibility and strength gains, and the gradual ability to hold poses and sequences that were hard a month ago.

A practical way to use Classroom for yoga is to keep it simple and regular. Create one class per batch, not one class per session, or you will drown in admin. Post a weekly practice plan as material, not as a graded assignment, so students can follow it without pressure. Set a short weekly check-in as an assignment: two or three questions, such as which poses felt easier this week, where they felt strain, and how many days they practised. Use private comments for personal feedback, and never post a student's health details or personal progress notes where the whole class can see them. Use the gradebook only as a record of completion and participation, not as a judgement of a student's body or worth, because yoga progress is personal and uneven, and a grading system that feels like school will push students away.

Classroom has limits worth knowing. It is not a booking system, it does not collect fees, and it does not manage seat limits or waitlists, so it works best alongside Klippme or Momoyoga, not instead of them. Live video through Meet on a personal account still follows the 60-minute group limit described above, and school accounts through Google Workspace for Education have different limits and controls, so a teacher working with a school should follow the school's account rules rather than a personal account setup. Do not paste students' personal or health information into any free AI tool connected to Classroom, and do not share a class code publicly, because anyone with the code and a Google account can try to join.

## WhatsApp Business, Canva and payments: the supporting tools

WhatsApp Business is free to download on Android and iPhone, and for most Indian yoga teachers it is the tool students actually read. Use it for what it does best: a business profile with your class timings and location, greeting messages for new students, quick replies for common questions about fees and timings, and reminders the day before class. Broadcast lists can send a private one-to-one message to many students at once, which is useful for announcements, but broadcast rules and spam limits apply, and students must have saved your number in many cases, so do not rely on broadcasts as your only reminder system. Your booking tool's email and WhatsApp reminders should carry the class link and timing, and WhatsApp should be the friendly nudge, not the only record.

Canva's free plan is useful for the visible side of teaching: class posters, weekly schedules, workshop announcements and social media posts. The free plan includes a large template library and 5 GB of cloud storage, and the Pro plan was reported at around 4,000 rupees a year for one person in India, with the full AI toolkit, background removal and 1 TB of storage. For a yoga teacher, the free plan is usually enough for posters and schedules, and the paid plan is worth it only if you make a lot of promotional material every week. Keep posters honest: show the real class time, level and price, and do not use a stock photo that promises a result your class cannot deliver.

Payments deserve a separate note, because they are where small teachers lose the most money to confusion. Klippme connects to Razorpay, and Razorpay's domestic pricing page lists a 2 percent platform fee per successful transaction across domestic instruments, with 18 percent GST on that fee, no setup fee and no annual maintenance charge, while UPI QR payments were listed at 0.99 percent per transaction. Those figures are from the pricing page found during research, and they can change, so check Razorpay's official pricing page before you set your class fee, and remember that the gateway fee is separate from any platform fee your booking tool charges. Set your fee with those costs included, tell students clearly whether the listed price includes GST and gateway charges, and keep a simple record of every payment, because a fee dispute is much harder to settle from memory than from a booking record.

## Student progress: what to track, and what not to track

Student progress in yoga is easy to overcomplicate and easy to get wrong. The useful record is small: attendance, practice frequency, the poses or sequences a student is working on, any injury or limitation they have told you about, and a short note on what changed since last month. A simple spreadsheet, a Classroom check-in, or the attendance record in Momoyoga or Klippme can hold all of that. You do not need a complex analytics dashboard, and you should not buy one until your student numbers make a spreadsheet genuinely unmanageable.

What you must not track casually is health data. A student's injury, pregnancy, medical condition or medication is sensitive personal information. Keep it only where it is needed for safe teaching, keep it private, do not share it in a group chat or a shared sheet, and do not enter it into a free chatbot or AI tool. If a student tells you about a condition that affects safe practice, encourage them to check with their doctor or physiotherapist, and adapt the class within your competence as a yoga teacher, rather than offering medical advice. A good monthly rhythm for progress is simple: at the start of the month, note each student's current focus in one line. During the month, record attendance and practice check-ins. At the end of the month, write one line on what improved and one line on the next focus, and share that privately with the student. That is enough to show a student that you are paying attention, and it is small enough that you will actually keep doing it. Tools help only if the habit is small; a tool that demands twenty fields per student will be abandoned by the second week.

## Using these tools together in India

A workable setup for a solo teacher in India looks like this. Klippme holds the bookings, seat limits and payments, and sends the booking confirmation with the class link. Google Meet or Zoom hosts the class. WhatsApp Business sends the reminder and answers quick questions. Google Classroom holds the course material and weekly check-ins for a structured course, or a simple spreadsheet holds progress notes for a casual batch. Canva makes the posters and announcements. That is five tools, but each one does one job, and no job is done twice in two places, which is the rule that keeps admin time down.

A small studio setup looks slightly different. Momoyoga or a comparable studio system holds classes, memberships, waitlists and the student app in one place, with Meet or Zoom for live classes and Classroom or a spreadsheet for structured courses. Internet and device realities in India should shape your choice more than feature lists. Choose tools that work well on a phone, because many students will book and join from a phone, not a laptop. Test your class link on mobile data, not only on home Wi-Fi, because a tool that works beautifully on broadband and stutters on mobile data will lose students. Keep a backup plan for a failed link: a second meeting link, a WhatsApp message ready to send, and a short recorded practice students can follow if the live class cannot run. Students forgive a technical failure once if you communicate quickly; they do not forgive silence.

## What to avoid

Avoid the free-plan trap: a free plan that ends your class at 40 minutes, takes a percentage of every payment, or allows only one kind of booking is not free if it costs you students or income. Check the limit that will actually affect you, not the headline word "free". Avoid collecting fees only on WhatsApp with no booking record, because you will lose track of who paid, who is owed a class, and who is on the waitlist, and every dispute will become a memory contest. Avoid posting students' photos, videos or progress notes publicly without their clear permission, and never use a student's image in advertising without written consent. Avoid promising health results, weight loss, or cure of any condition in your class marketing, because that is both misleading and outside a yoga teacher's professional scope. Avoid buying a large studio platform before you have the student numbers to justify it, and avoid signing an annual plan before a monthly trial has proven that your students will actually book and pay through it.

Avoid one more subtle mistake: letting tools decide your teaching. A booking system that pushes you to add more batches, a social media tool that pushes you to post every day, and a progress dashboard that pushes you to grade everything can quietly turn a yoga practice into a content business. Use the tools to protect your teaching time, then protect the teaching itself: time for your own practice, time to plan sequences properly, and time to rest. A teacher who is exhausted by admin will teach worse classes, no matter how good the software is.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the best free tool for yoga class bookings in India?**
For most solo teachers in India, Klippme's free plan is the best starting point, because it supports UPI and Razorpay payments, seat limits and booking links, but it is limited to 10 bookings a month and one group session, so a busy teacher will need the paid plan. Momoyoga's free plan is a strong alternative for studio-style teaching, but its 5 percent platform fee on online payments must be weighed against a paid plan.

**Can I teach a full 60-minute yoga class for free online?**
Yes, on Google Meet with a free personal account, group calls run up to 60 minutes, so a 60-minute class fits, but with no margin for a late start. On Zoom's free plan, group meetings end after 40 minutes, so a 60-minute class needs a paid plan or a restart that will break the flow of the session.

**How do I track student progress without expensive software?**
Use a simple record: attendance, practice frequency, current focus poses, and a one-line monthly note per student. Google Classroom can hold weekly check-ins and private feedback for structured courses, and a spreadsheet is enough for casual batches. Keep health details private, and do not enter them into free AI tools.

**Should a solo teacher buy a studio platform like Mindbody?**
Usually not at first. A solo teacher should start with a booking tool and a video platform, and move to a studio platform only when memberships, waitlists, class passes and multiple teachers make a simple setup unmanageable. If you do consider Mindbody, confirm the full price, payment methods and India support with the vendor first, because several tiers are quote-only and no India pricing page was found.

**Do I need WhatsApp Business if I already have a booking tool?**
It helps, because most Indian students read WhatsApp faster than email, but it should not be your only system. Let the booking tool hold the official record of bookings and payments, and use WhatsApp Business for reminders, quick replies and announcements, so a lost chat thread never becomes a lost booking.

**Is it safe to share recorded yoga classes online?**
Only with students' clear permission, and only when you control who can see the recording. Tell students before you record, tell them where the recording will be stored, and do not post a recording publicly if any student in it has not agreed. Avoid recording classes where students discuss personal health matters.

**What should I check before paying for any tool?**
Re-check the official pricing and help page on the day you pay, confirm the free limit that affects you, the total cost in rupees including taxes and gateway fees, the cancellation terms, and whether your students can pay by UPI. Prices, limits and fees change, and a comparison article cannot replace the vendor's own page.

## Final verdict

Start with Klippme for bookings and Google Meet for online classes; add Classroom only when you need structured student progress. That combination covers bookings, payments, live teaching and progress tracking for most yoga teachers in India without a large monthly bill, and it leaves room to grow into a studio system like Momoyoga when your student numbers justify it. Keep your progress records small and private, check official pricing before you pay, and let the tools handle the admin so your teaching gets your best energy.
