Writing in India in 2026 is not one job. Drafting an [email](/blog/ai-tools-for-email-writing-india/), rewriting a sentence, fixing grammar, checking plagiarism, and handling a long document are different jobs, and no single free tool does all of them well. The tool that drafts unlimited text does not check your grammar as you type, and the tool that checks your grammar does not draft a long document. The free versus paid question is therefore not which tool is best, but which job you need, and whether free covers it.

The short answer for India is this: **draft free in ChatGPT**, whose free plan includes unlimited everyday text chats, subject to abuse safeguards, with separate unpublished caps on uploads, images, and voice, and ads in the US, with Plus at 20 dollars a month. **fix free in Grammarly**, whose free plan corrects spelling and grammar, shows your writing tone, and includes 100 generative AI prompts a month, with Pro at 12 dollars a month billed yearly or 30 dollars billed monthly. **reword short text free in QuillBot**, whose free plan paraphrases up to 100 words at a time in two modes and summarizes up to 1,200 words, with Premium at 8.33 dollars a month billed yearly on one source, or 120 dollars a year on another, so check current pricing before you pay.

This article is written for India on purpose: students, freelancers, professionals, and small businesses, Hindi, Hinglish, and English writing, a normal phone and laptop, and no assumption that you have a paid plan, a US account, or a writing coach. It also carries a warning that matters for writing tools more than for any other tool in this series: free AI writing tools can draft emails, social posts, web copy, and proposals well enough to save real time, as long as you edit the result, because an unedited AI draft is generic, may contain errors, and will sound like AI, while an edited draft, with your facts, your voice, and your corrections, will sound like you.

## Using writing AI in India: free versus paid, and the editing rule

Free versus paid for writing assistants is a simple question with a simple answer: free covers drafting and fixing, while paid covers rewriting, plagiarism, and long documents, and you should pay only when you need the paid job. *Grammarly* Pro unlocks full-sentence rewrites, tone adjustments, plagiarism checks, AI text detection, 2,000 prompts a month, and brand voice tools, for 12 dollars a month billed yearly or 30 dollars monthly, which is worth it for a professional who writes daily, needs rewrites, and needs plagiarism checks, but not for a student who only needs spelling and grammar, because free Grammarly already covers those. *ChatGPT* Plus, at 20 dollars a month, unlocks faster responses, higher usage ceilings, and priority access, which is worth it for a writer who hits free caps daily, but not for a writer who drafts occasionally, because free ChatGPT already gives unlimited everyday chats.

*Google Gemini* free is the Google user's pick: standard limits refreshing every five hours to a weekly cap, with Canvas and Deep Research, and AI Plus at 4.99 dollars a month, which is the cheapest paid tier in this article, and best for research and Google users, because Canvas organises writing and Deep Research gathers sources, while Gemini lives inside Google apps. For an Indian Google Workspace user, Gemini free is the natural drafting tool, because it is already in your Google apps, and its five-hour refresh means a cap that runs out in the morning returns by afternoon, unlike a monthly cap that stops you for weeks. *Jasper*, with no free plan and only a seven-day trial, at 59 dollars a month yearly or 69 dollars monthly, is for marketing teams, not beginners, and a beginner who pays for Jasper before trying ChatGPT free and Grammarly free is paying for a team tool they do not need.

The **editing rule** is the most important rule in this article, and it is simple: free AI writing tools can draft well enough to save real time, as long as you edit the result, because an AI draft is a starting point, not a finished piece. Edit every AI draft for **facts**, because AI can invent dates, names, and numbers, and a draft with a wrong fact is worse than no draft. Edit for **voice**, because an AI draft sounds generic, and your voice, your examples, and your phrasing are what make it yours. Edit for **audience**, because an AI draft written for everyone is written for no one, and a draft for an Indian customer, student, or employer needs Indian context, not generic advice. A writer who edits every draft will sound better than a writer who sends AI drafts unedited, even when the unedited writer uses a paid tool.

## What to avoid

- Do not assume free Grammarly checks plagiarism, because full sentence rewrites, tone adjustments, plagiarism checks, and AI text detection are all unavailable on free, and only Pro adds them. - Do not use QuillBot free for a full document, because paraphrase is capped at 100 words at a time in two modes, and a document reworded 100 words at a time will lose coherence. - Do not pay for Jasper as a beginner, because it has no free plan, only a seven-day trial, and at 59 dollars a month yearly, it is a marketing team tool, not a beginner tool. - Do not send an AI draft unedited, because an unedited draft is generic, may contain invented facts, and will sound like AI, while an edited draft with your facts and voice will sound like you. - Do not trust a single source on QuillBot Premium pricing, because sources disagree, 8.33 dollars a month billed yearly versus 120 dollars a year, so check current pricing before you pay.

A final trap is using AI to write everything, and learning nothing. Beginners often generate every email, every [assignment](/blog/ai-tools-for-study-notes-india/), and every post with AI, feel productive, and then freeze when they must write without AI, because they never learned to write, they only learned to prompt. Use AI to draft, but **write the final version yourself**, because writing is a skill, and a skill you do not practise disappears, while a skill you practise daily, even with AI help, grows. For students, this matters most: an assignment written entirely by AI may pass a checker, but it will not teach you the subject, and an exam without AI will expose what you did not learn.
