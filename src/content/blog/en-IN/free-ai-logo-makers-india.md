A shop board, a WhatsApp profile photo, an Instagram page, a visiting card, a bill book: a small business in India needs its logo in many places, and it usually needs the logo today, not after a designer delivers it next week. Free AI logo makers promise exactly that, but most of them hide the real catch behind the word "free". Some let you design for free and then charge to download anything usable. Some give you a free download, but only a small PNG with a coloured background that looks bad on a shop board. Some add a watermark unless you pay. This article separates the tools that hand you a real, usable logo for free from the ones that only let you preview one.

The short answer for India is this: start with [Shopify Logo Maker](https://www.shopify.com/tools/logo-maker/) if you want a completely free logo with no hidden fee, because Shopify's own page says the tool is 100% free and the logo you design is yours to own. Use [LOGO.com](https://logo.com/) free if you want unlimited logo ideas and a free PNG download, including a transparent PNG, but only for one logo. Use [Wixel](https://www.wix.com/logo/maker/) (Wix's new logo maker) if you need a free SVG vector file, because Wixel lets you download PNG, JPG or SVG at no cost once the logo is made, while its AI credits are spent on generating and editing. Use [Namecheap Logo Maker](https://www.namecheap.com/logo-maker/) if you want unlimited free AI designs with no watermark. Use [Canva](https://www.canva.com/) free only if a PNG or JPG on a solid background is enough, because transparent backgrounds and SVG downloads need Canva Pro. Avoid planning around Looka and Brandmark if your budget is zero, because both let you design for free but charge before you can download usable files. Prices and free limits change, so re-check the official pricing or help page of any tool before you pay or promise a customer a deadline.

## Shopify Logo Maker: the genuinely free option

Shopify Logo Maker, earlier known as Hatchful, is the simplest recommendation in this list because its own page removes the usual doubt: it says the tool is 100% free, with no hidden fees or upfront costs, and the logo you design is yours to own. You pick a category for your business, choose a visual style, type your business name and slogan, and then customise the font, colours, symbol and layout from the designs it shows. You do not need design experience, and you do not need a Shopify store to use it.

The honest limitation is that this is a template-based maker, not a tool that paints an original logo from a written prompt. Your logo will come from a set of curated designs, so two shops in the same category can end up with similar-looking marks. For a kirana store, a coaching centre, a boutique or a food stall, that is usually fine: customers recognise the shop name and the colours, not whether the icon is one of a kind. The bigger practical point is to pick a design with a clean, readable name, because a logo that looks good on a laptop screen can turn into a blur on a small shop board. Download the file, print one test copy at the size you will actually use, and only then put it on boards and packaging.

## LOGO.com: unlimited ideas, one free PNG logo

LOGO.com's free plan is generous in one way and strict in another, and both halves matter. According to its own [plans page](https://logo.com/logo-plans), the free plan allows unlimited logo idea generations and unlimited logo editing and saving, and it gives you one logo with full-colour, greyscale, transparent and transparent-greyscale files, all in PNG only. That means you can try as many ideas as you want, change colours and fonts freely, and still walk away with a transparent PNG that sits cleanly on a coloured shop board, a visiting card or a social media profile.

The strict half is the format and the count. There is no free SVG vector file, and the free plan covers one logo. If you later want a second logo, more saved logos, or a full brand kit with social covers, you move to a paid Brand or Business plan. For a single shop or startup, one good logo is usually enough, so this limit rarely hurts. Where it does hurt is when a business wants separate marks for the shop, a product line and a YouTube channel. In that case, make your one free LOGO.com logo the main brand mark, and use a second free tool for the variations rather than paying for a second logo on day one.

## Wixel (Wix): free SVG, but watch the AI credits

Wixel is Wix's newer AI design platform, and it is the best free route to an SVG file in this list. Wix's own support pages say that AI credits are used when you generate or edit a logo with AI, but once you have the logo you want, downloading it as a high-quality file is completely free, in PNG, JPG or SVG, in the size you choose, and downloads do not use AI credits. Comparison roundups report about 120 free AI credits a month, which is enough for several rounds of generation and editing if you do not regenerate endlessly.

Do not confuse Wixel with the older Wix Logo Maker. Wix itself says the older Logo Maker is still available but has limited AI support, and that new logo projects are better created in Wixel. With the older tool, you buy a Logo Maker plan to unlock high-quality files and brand kit assets for each logo. So if an old guide tells you that Wix logos cost money to download, it is describing the older tool, not Wixel. For a new shop logo in India, start in Wixel, spend your credits carefully by shortlisting two or three directions before you start fine editing, and download the SVG as your master file. An SVG scales from a visiting card to a large flex board without going blurry, which is exactly why it matters for print.

## Namecheap Logo Maker: unlimited free designs, no watermark

Namecheap's Logo Maker promises three things on its own page: a free AI generator that creates an unlimited number of designs, no watermarked images, and full customisation of colours, fonts and icons. For a business owner who wants to try twenty directions before choosing one, the unlimited part is genuinely useful. Most free tools ration your tries; this one does not ration the designing itself.

The part to verify at the download screen is the file format. Namecheap's own page stresses free and no watermark, while an independent 2026 test of logo makers reported a free zip containing PNG and SVG files. Those two statements are not in conflict, but they are not the same claim either, so treat the format as "check before you commit" rather than guaranteed. If the download gives you PNG and SVG, this tool jumps straight into the top tier for free logos. If it gives PNG only, it is still a strong free option, and you can pair it with Wixel for the vector file. Either way, do not pay for anything here until you have seen the actual download screen.

## Canva free: good for social media, limited for print

Canva is the tool most Indian shop owners already know, and its free plan is genuinely useful for a logo, with one hard limit. You can design with free elements and download PNG, JPG or PDF as many times as you need. Canva's own help pages confirm that downloading with a transparent background is only available on Pro, Teams, Business, Education or Nonprofits plans, and that downloading as SVG is likewise a paid-plan option. If your design uses premium elements, Canva asks you to buy a Pro content licence, upgrade, or download a watermarked draft.

There is also a monthly AI allowance to keep in mind. Comparison roundups report about 20 free AI uses a month, shared across every Canva AI tool, so a few rounds of regenerating a logo can use most of it. That figure varies by source and by Canva's current plan, so treat it as approximate and check your own account's remaining uses before a big session. The practical rule for Canva free is simple: use it when your logo will live on Instagram, WhatsApp and a website, where a PNG on a solid background looks fine. Do not plan a printed board, a transparent shop shutter sticker or a large banner around a Canva free download, because the transparent background and the vector file are both behind Pro.

## Adobe Express: free transparent PNG, no SVG

[Adobe Express](https://www.adobe.com/express/create/logo) is the middle path between Canva free and a paid vector tool. Its logo maker asks for your brand name, industry and style, and generates designs you can edit. Comparison roundups report that the free plan gives a transparent PNG download, which solves the biggest Canva free problem, but no SVG on any plan, and that AI generations are limited each day. One roundup also notes a 500 x 500 px size for some free downloads, so the free file is good for social profiles and small print, but it is not a large-format print master.

For an Indian startup, Adobe Express makes most sense as a second tool, not the only tool. Make your main logo in Wixel or LOGO.com, and use Adobe Express when you need a quick transparent PNG version for a social post or a visiting card. Because the daily generation limit is not published as a single fixed number in the sources checked, do your generations in one focused sitting instead of spreading them across the day, and save the version you like before the limit resets. As with every tool here, re-check Adobe's own page before paying for Premium, because the credit allowance and the price change over time.

## DesignEvo: free, but only if you give credit and accept small files

[DesignEvo](https://www.designevo.com/pricing.html) is honest about its free plan in a way that helps you decide quickly. Its own pricing page lists a free plan with the condition "Give Credit", and low-resolution files limited to 300 px. The Basic plan, listed at $24.99 one-time (shown against $49.99), adds high-resolution JPG and PNG files up to 5000 x 5000 px, a transparent PNG, and edit-and-re-download for that one logo, with no need to give credit.

For most shops, the free plan is a preview tool, not a production tool. A 300 px logo looks acceptable as a small WhatsApp profile photo, but it will look soft on a printed board or a large banner. The credit requirement also matters: if you use the free file on your shop's Facebook page or packaging, you are expected to credit DesignEvo, which most businesses do not want on customer-facing material. So treat DesignEvo free as a way to test an idea, and treat the $24.99 one-time Basic plan as the real price of a usable DesignEvo logo. If your budget is truly zero, pick Shopify, LOGO.com or Wixel instead.

## Looka and Brandmark: free to design, paid to download

Looka and Brandmark belong in this article as a warning, not as free recommendations. Both let you design a logo for free, which feels like progress, and both charge before you can download anything usable. [Looka's own pricing page](https://looka.com/pricing/) shows a Basic logo package as a one-time purchase giving one low-resolution PNG file at 1000 x 1000 pixels with a coloured background, while the Premium package adds high-resolution and vector files, and the Brand Kit subscription adds social assets, business cards and more with full commercial rights even after the subscription ends. [Brandmark's pricing page](https://brandmark.io/pricing/) lists a Basic package at $35 one-time for logo files in PNG and vector format, with Designer at $95 and Enterprise at $195 adding source files, brand guidelines and more.

These tools are not bad; they are simply not free tools. Looka's designs often look more polished than template makers, and Brandmark's one-time pricing can be cheaper over time than a monthly subscription. But if you have already spent an hour perfecting a Looka logo and only then discover the download price, the tool has wasted your time. The rule is: before you invest time in any logo maker, scroll to its pricing page and find the download price for the file you actually need. If the pricing page does not show a free download, treat the tool as a paid tool, however free the designing feels.

## Using logo makers in India: language, payment and printing

All the tools in this article are websites that work in India in a normal browser without a VPN, and you sign up with an email address or a Google account. That part is easy. The parts that need care are language, payment and printing, because those are where an Indian shop owner usually gets stuck after the logo looks good on screen.

Language first. Most of these makers handle English brand names well, and they handle Devanagari names poorly or not at all. If your shop name is in Hindi, do not expect an AI generator to draw clean Devanagari lettering. The practical workaround is to design the icon and the English name in the logo maker, and then add the Hindi name yourself in Canva or Adobe Express using a Hindi font, or ask a local designer to set the Hindi version once. A logo with broken Hindi letters hurts your shop more than a simple English logo does.

Payment second. The paid plans in this article are mostly priced in US dollars: Brandmark at $35 one-time, DesignEvo Basic at $24.99 one-time, and Looka's packages in dollars. Before paying, check the checkout page for whether your card works for international payments, because that is the step where Indian debit cards most often fail. Do not promise a printer or a board maker a delivery date that depends on a paid download until the payment has actually gone through and the file is in your downloads folder.

Printing third. A logo is not finished when it looks good on your phone. Print one copy at the real size first: a visiting card, a small sticker, and, if you can, a small board sample. Check that the shop name is readable from a few steps away, that thin lines have not disappeared, and that the transparent PNG really is transparent on a coloured background. A free PNG from LOGO.com or Adobe Express passes this test for small print; a large flex board needs the SVG from Wixel or a paid high-resolution file. If you are unsure, take both the PNG and the SVG to your printer and ask which one prints cleaner at your board size.

## What to avoid

Avoid downloading a logo with a watermark and then trying to crop or cover it. The watermark is there because the file is not licensed for use, and a cropped watermark still leaves a logo that looks unprofessional and can create a dispute if the business grows. Avoid using a logo you have not checked for uniqueness. Logo makers reuse icons, fonts and layouts across thousands of businesses, so before you print boards, search your shop name and check whether a similar logo already exists in your city or trade, and consider a trademark search before you spend on large signage. Avoid paying for a brand kit on day one. A new shop needs one good logo file, a visiting card and a social profile photo; the 300-plus branded assets in a subscription can wait until the business has steady income. Avoid the trap of regenerating forever. Pick three directions, show them to two or three real customers, and choose the one they remember the next day, because a logo that customers remember beats a logo that only you think is beautiful.

One more honest disagreement to carry with you: different roundups disagree about which tools give a free SVG. Some claim free vectors from Design.com or BrandCrowd, others say those free downloads cover only a free template collection, and LOGO.com's own page says PNG only on free. The lesson is not that one roundup is lying; it is that free plans change and that roundups test at different times. The only reliable check is the download screen in front of you, on the day you download.

## Frequently asked questions

**Which free AI logo maker is best for a small shop in India?** Shopify Logo Maker if you want zero cost and zero doubt, LOGO.com free if you want a transparent PNG for one logo, and Wixel if you need a free SVG for printing. Most shops should try Shopify and Wixel first and compare the two results side by side.

**Can I use a free logo on my shop board and visiting cards?** Yes, if the free download is actually yours to use. Shopify says the logo is yours to own, LOGO.com's free PNG is a real download, and Wixel's free downloads carry no watermark. What you cannot do is use a watermarked preview, a low-resolution file meant for preview, or a file whose plan requires credit, without following that plan's condition.

**Why do some logo makers charge for SVG files?** Because an SVG is a vector file that scales to any size without losing quality, and it is the file printers and board makers actually want. Free plans usually give PNG, which is a fixed-size image. That is why the free-versus-paid line in this market is almost always drawn at the vector file, not at the design step.

**Is a logo from an AI maker really mine?** It depends on the tool's terms. Shopify says the logo is yours to own, and Looka says you keep full commercial rights even after a subscription ends. Other tools give you a licence to use the logo without exclusive ownership, which means a similar logo can exist elsewhere. Read the terms on the tool's own page before you spend on large signage, and do not assume that paying once gives you a trademark.

**Can I make a logo with a Hindi shop name?** You can, but not reliably inside these generators. Design the icon and the English name in the maker, then add the Hindi name in Canva or Adobe Express with a proper Hindi font, and check the spelling with someone who reads Hindi well. A logo with a spelling mistake in the shop's own name is worse than a plain logo.

**Should I pay for a logo maker or hire a designer?** If your budget is zero and you need a logo this week, use the free tools in this article and spend your money on printing instead. If your business already earns steadily and the logo will go on packaging, uniforms and large boards, a local designer who understands your market is worth paying for, because a designer gives you original work, proper files and a trademark check that no free generator promises.

## The final verdict, and a checklist before you print

Start with Shopify Logo Maker or LOGO.com free for a usable PNG logo, use Wixel free for the SVG master file, and pay only when you need a full vector brand kit or a polished design from Looka or Brandmark. Before you print, run this checklist: the shop name is readable from a few steps away, the transparent PNG really is transparent on a coloured background, you have both a PNG for social media and an SVG or high-resolution file for print, the file has no watermark and no credit requirement you have ignored, and you have checked that no identical logo already exists in your trade. If all five boxes are ticked, your free logo is ready for the board. If any box fails, fix that one problem before you spend on printing, because reprinting a board costs far more than the logo ever did.
