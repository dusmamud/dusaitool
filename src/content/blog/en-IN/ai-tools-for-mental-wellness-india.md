Mental wellness apps can help you build small daily habits: ten minutes of meditation, a short journal entry, a breathing exercise before bed, or a conversation that helps you name what you are feeling. They cannot diagnose you, treat you, or replace a professional. That line matters more in this article than in any other tool roundup, so here it is first: if you are in crisis, or if what you feel is getting worse, do not rely on an app. Call Tele MANAS, India's free national mental health helpline, on **14416**, any time of day or night, or go to a doctor or counsellor near you. These tools are for everyday support, not for emergencies.

The short answer for India is this: start free with [Wysa](https://www.wysa.com/faq) for AI chat support, [Insight Timer](https://insighttimer.com/) or [Medito](https://meditofoundation.org/medito-app/) for meditation, and a mood journal for tracking how you feel over time. [Calm](https://support.calm.com/hc/en-us/articles/360044707294-What-Free-Content-is-Available-on-the-Calm-App) gives you hundreds of free sessions but locks most of its library behind Premium, and [Headspace](https://www.headspace.com/app) is mainly a 14-day trial with some content to try before you pay. For India-specific help, [Amaha](https://www.amahahealth.com/services/self-care/) offers self-care activities and assessments, and [MindPeers](https://www.mindpeers.co/) offers short pre-screener tests, though its website now speaks mostly to employers and insurers. For sleep, a basic sleep tracker is free; the advanced sleep analysis is paid.

This article is written for India on purpose: a normal Android phone or iPhone, a regular internet connection, no VPN, and no assumption that you have a paid plan or a US account. Prices and free limits change, so before you pay for anything, re-check the official pricing or help page linked in each section. Nothing here is medical advice, and no app in this list claims to diagnose or cure anything.

## Wysa: AI chat support that is free to talk to

[Wysa](https://www.wysa.com/faq) is an AI chatbot for emotional wellbeing, made by a team with deep India roots, and it is the most genuinely free starting point in this list. Talking to Wysa AI is free, including the tools offered inside conversations, such as breathing exercises, thought-reframing activities and sleep tools. A premium version exists, with a much wider selection of tools available directly through a tool library as well as through conversation, and some users who get Wysa through an employer, school or insurer also get human coaching.

Wysa is unusually honest about its limits, and that honesty is a reason to trust it more than apps that promise too much. Its own FAQ says the app is intended as emotional wellbeing support, not as a substitute for professional medical advice, that it does not provide medical advice or diagnoses, that it is not recommended in crisis situations, and that it is not suitable for people with severe and enduring mental health problems. It also says clearly that you are interacting with AI software, not a person, and that AI does not replace human judgement. Use it the way it asks to be used: a daily check-in, a way to practise coping tools, a place to write down what you feel at 1 am when nobody else is awake. Do not use it as a therapist, and do not wait on it if you need real help.

## Insight Timer: the largest free meditation library

If meditation is your main goal, [Insight Timer](https://insighttimer.com/) is the best free deal in this list by a wide margin. Its free tier includes more than 300,000 guided practices, covering meditation, sleep, breathing and mindfulness, plus sleep music and ambient soundscapes, live meditation and yoga classes from teachers around the world, hundreds of free courses on stress, anxiety, sleep and emotional wellbeing, and a meditation timer with custom settings. That is not a trial: it is the free account, and it stays free.

The paid option is called MemberPlus, at $9.99 a month or $59.99 a year, and it adds ad-free listening, offline downloads, premium content from select teachers, exclusive advanced courses and all new releases. For most people starting out, none of that is necessary. The free library is so large that the real problem is choosing, not paying. Start with a short 5-minute or 10-minute guided session, use the timer for silent practice, and try a sleep track at night before deciding whether you want anything more. One honest warning: with 300,000 tracks, quality varies, because anyone who teaches can publish. Follow teachers whose voice and pace suit you, and ignore the rest.

## Medito: meditation that is free-forever, with no paywall at all

[Medito](https://meditofoundation.org/medito-app/) is a meditation app run by a non-profit foundation, and its promise is simple: free-forever meditation, with hundreds of guided meditations, sleep stories, courses and breathing exercises, and no ads, no paywalls and no account needed. There is no premium tier waiting behind the free content, because there is no premium tier. Courses run from beginner to advanced, including short and 30-day challenges, there are sleep stories and sounds to help you drift off, and fresh guided sessions appear every day.

Medito is the right pick if you dislike the trial-and-paywall pattern of most wellness apps, or if you want something you can recommend to a parent or a student without worrying that they will hit a payment screen halfway through a course. Its library is smaller than Insight Timer's, and it is less polished than Calm or Headspace, but everything it has is actually free, and that makes it the most honest free option here. If you are new to meditation, start with its beginner course rather than picking random tracks.

## Calm: hundreds of free sessions, most of the library paid

[Calm](https://support.calm.com/hc/en-us/articles/360044707294-What-Free-Content-is-Available-on-the-Calm-App) is the most famous meditation and sleep app in the world, and its free tier is better than many people expect, though it is nowhere near complete. You do not need a Premium subscription to use it. The free content includes 230+ meditations, guided and timed sessions for anxiety, focus, stress and sleep, 8 free sleep sessions including Sleep Stories and sleep audio, Day 1 of every multi-day meditation program such as 7 Days of Focus or 21 Days of Calm, a free Daily Calm preview, the Breathe Bubble guided breathing tool, and background scenes with nature sounds and ambient music.

The catch is the padlock. As you browse, a small padlock icon next to content means you need a Premium subscription to open it, and most of the full library, including most Sleep Stories and full programs, carries that padlock. Calm is worth trying free if you like its narrators and its calm, polished style, and the free sessions are enough for a daily 10-minute habit. But do not install it expecting the whole app to be free. If you want the full Sleep Story library, you will pay, and you should compare that price with Insight Timer and Medito, which give you sleep content free, before subscribing.

## Headspace: a trial, not a free plan

[Headspace](https://www.headspace.com/app) is a well-made meditation app with more than 1,000 guided meditations, plus sleep sounds, yoga and fitness content and focus music, and it is best understood as a trial product rather than a free product. The app is free to download, and you can start with a free 14-day trial, plus some content available to try before you sign up. After the trial, the full library needs a subscription, with annual and monthly plans, as well as student and family plans.

Headspace's strength is structure. Its courses are carefully sequenced, its animations explain meditation clearly, and its daily meditation gives beginners something new every morning without decision fatigue. If you have never meditated and you want guidance that holds your hand for the first month, the 14-day trial is a good test. Just set a reminder before the trial ends, so the subscription decision is yours and not an accident, and remember that when the trial is over, Medito and Insight Timer will still be free.

## Amaha: India-made self-care, assessments and daily plans

[Amaha](https://www.amahahealth.com/services/self-care/) is an Indian mental health platform, and its self-care app is built around a simple flow: pick an area of concern you relate to, take a short expert-made assessment, understand your concerns better, get a personalised plan with new activities each day, and monitor your progress as you work on yourself. The activities are based on evidence-based approaches such as cognitive behavioural therapy, and the app is made by self-care experts rather than by a generic content team. On Google Play India, Amaha reports more than 6 million downloads, which makes it one of the most used Indian wellness apps.

One honest gap: Amaha's website describes the self-care app clearly, but it does not spell out exactly which parts are free and which are paid in one place, the way Insight Timer or Calm do. The self-care activities and assessments are the app's core, while one-on-one therapy with a professional is a separate, paid service on the platform. Before you commit to anything paid, check inside the app itself, and use the free self-care plan first to see whether the style suits you. Amaha is worth trying because it is made for India, with Indian therapists and Indian contexts, but do not assume everything in it is free.

## MindPeers: quick check-ins, now aimed more at employers

[MindPeers](https://www.mindpeers.co/) started as a consumer mental health app and still offers something useful for free: clinically validated pre-screener tests that take under 3 minutes, with a report to guide your next steps, covering concerns such as anxiety, burnout, resilience, focus, sleep and social health. A short, structured check-in like that can be a good first step if you want to understand what you are feeling before deciding what to do about it.

But there is a change you should know about. MindPeers' website today describes its product mainly as a wellbeing intelligence layer for HR teams, insurers and health platforms, connecting therapy data, validated assessments and wearable signals so organisations can detect risk earlier and measure outcomes. That does not make the free tests useless, but it does mean the consumer app is no longer the company's main focus, and the free experience may be thinner than it once was. Use MindPeers for a quick, free check-in, then take the result to a professional or to one of the daily-habit apps above, rather than expecting a full free self-care programme from it.

## Daylio: mood journaling that shows patterns over weeks

[Daylio](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/daylio-journal-mood-tracker/id1194023242) is a private diary and mood tracker, and it solves a different problem from meditation apps: it shows you patterns. Each day you tap your mood and note what you did, and over weeks the app reveals connections you would miss on your own, such as low moods on days without sleep, or better days when you walked in the morning. It is a journal, not a therapist, and it does not analyse your feelings for you. The value is in the record itself.

Daylio is free to download, with in-app purchases for extra features. The free version covers daily mood entries and basic tracking, which is enough for the habit that matters: a 30-second entry every evening. Keep entries short and honest, and do not turn journaling into another chore. If you notice a pattern that worries you, such as low mood for two weeks or more, that is information to take to a professional, not something the app will fix on its own.

## Sleep Cycle: basic sleep tracking free, analysis paid

Sleep is part of mental wellness, and [Sleep Cycle](https://support.sleepcycle.com/hc/en-us/articles/206704909-Sleep-Cycle-Freemium-vs-Premium-Features) is the best-known sleep tracking app. The free version covers basic sleep tracking and a smart alarm that tries to wake you in a light sleep phase. The Premium version adds online backup, full access to all statistics, Sleep Aid sounds, snore detection and sound detection, sleep programs, sleep notes and Apple Watch features.

For most people, the free version answers the only question that matters at first: how long and how regularly are you actually sleeping? If your sleep is short or irregular, that alone explains a lot of low mood and poor focus, and fixing bedtime is more useful than any premium chart. Do not pay for Premium until you have used the free tracking for at least two weeks and you know which advanced feature you actually want. Also remember that a phone app measures sleep through sound and movement, not through medical sensors, so treat its numbers as a rough guide, not a diagnosis.

## Using wellness AI in India: language, payment, privacy and no VPN

All the apps in this list work in India on a normal connection, with no VPN, and all of them are available through the Google Play Store or Apple App Store in India. Payments, where they exist, go through the app stores, so UPI, cards and net banking work the way they do for any other app purchase. But three India-specific points deserve attention.

First, language. Most guided meditation and AI chat content in these apps is in English, and Hindi or regional-language content is limited and varies by app. If you want Hindi content, check inside the app before paying, and do not assume a global app will speak your language. Wysa and Amaha, with their India connection, are the most likely to understand Indian contexts, but even they are mainly English-first.

Second, privacy. Wellness apps hold some of the most sensitive data you will ever type into a phone: your moods, your sleep, your anxieties, your journal entries. Before you pour your life into any of them, read the privacy policy, check whether your entries are stored on your device or on a server, and avoid apps that sell data or show ads inside a journal. Our guide to [privacy and safety tools](/blog/ai-privacy-safety-free-tools-india/) explains how to check what an app actually collects, and the same caution applies here twice over.

Third, audio. Meditation, sleep stories and calming sounds overlap with other kinds of audio you may already use. If you like falling asleep to audio, our guides to [podcast and audio tools](/blog/ai-tools-for-podcast-audio-india/) and [music generation tools](/blog/free-ai-music-generators-india/) cover free audio options that pair well with a meditation habit, though they are not wellness apps themselves.

## What to avoid

Avoid any app that claims to diagnose depression, anxiety or any other condition from a chat or a quiz. Screening tests can suggest that you talk to a professional; they cannot tell you what you have. Avoid apps that pressure you with countdown timers, guilt messages or endless premium upsells inside a journal or a breathing exercise. A wellness app that makes you anxious about paying is doing the opposite of its job.

Avoid sharing highly sensitive personal details with an AI chatbot, including your full name, address, workplace details or anything you would not want stored on a server. AI chats feel private because they feel like a conversation, but they are software running on someone else's servers. Keep conversations general, and keep real names and identifying details out of them.

Avoid using any of these tools as your only support if things are getting worse. Two weeks of low mood, loss of interest in things you used to enjoy, major changes in sleep or appetite, or thoughts of harming yourself are all reasons to talk to a professional promptly, not to try another app. In an emergency, call Tele MANAS on 14416, which is free, confidential and available 24 hours a day, 7 days a week across India, or go to the nearest hospital. If someone near you is in immediate danger, stay with them and get emergency help right away.

Avoid the idea that one app will fix everything. Meditation helps attention and stress, journaling helps self-awareness, sleep tracking helps sleep, and AI chat helps you practise coping tools. None of them replaces sleep itself, exercise, real conversations with people you trust, or professional care when you need it. The best wellness routine is boring: a short daily habit, done most days, plus real life.

## Frequently asked questions

**Are these mental wellness apps free in India?** Yes, all of them can be started free in India, but free means different things. Wysa chat, Insight Timer's library and Medito are free in a lasting way. Calm gives hundreds of free sessions with the rest locked, Headspace is mainly a 14-day trial, and Amaha, MindPeers, Daylio and Sleep Cycle mix free parts with paid parts. Check the table above before you install.

**Can an AI chatbot replace a therapist?** No. Wysa itself says it is not a substitute for professional medical advice, does not provide diagnoses, and is not suitable in crisis situations or for severe and enduring mental health problems. AI chat is useful for daily coping practice and for naming feelings. Therapy is a human relationship with a trained professional, and nothing in this list replaces it.

**Which app is best for meditation if I never want to pay?** Medito, because it is free-forever with no paywall and no account needed. Insight Timer is the backup choice if you want a much larger library and do not mind choosing among 300,000 tracks. Both are genuinely free, unlike apps that are free only for a trial period.

**Is my journal and mood data private in these apps?** It depends on the app, and you should check before trusting it. Some apps store entries on your device, others sync them to servers, and some share aggregated data with partners. Read the privacy policy, avoid apps with ads inside a journal, and never type identifying details into an AI chat. Our [privacy guide](/blog/ai-privacy-safety-free-tools-india/) explains what to check.

**What should I do if I feel worse while using one of these apps?** Stop relying on the app and talk to a professional. Call Tele MANAS on 14416 for free, confidential counselling any time, or see a doctor or counsellor near you. If you feel you may act on thoughts of harming yourself, treat it as an emergency and get help immediately rather than opening another app.

**Do these apps work in Hindi?** Mostly they are English-first, and Hindi content is limited and varies by app. Check inside the app before paying for a subscription, and prefer apps with an India connection, such as Wysa and Amaha, if Indian context matters to you.

## The final verdict, and a checklist before you pay

Start free with Wysa, Insight Timer and Medito, and track mood in Daylio. These tools support wellbeing; they never replace professional help. That is the whole article in one line, and it is deliberately modest. A ten-minute meditation habit, a nightly mood entry and an AI chat you can open at midnight will not transform your life, but done most days, they can make ordinary stress easier to carry, and they cost nothing to try.

Before you pay for anything, run this checklist. Have you used the free version for at least two weeks? Do you know exactly which paid feature you want, and is it actually locked behind the paywall? Have you compared it with the free alternatives in this article? Is the subscription easy to cancel in the app store? And most importantly, if what you feel is more than everyday stress, have you talked to a professional instead of buying another app? If the answer to that last question is no, make the call first. Tele MANAS on 14416 is free, confidential and open all night, and a real conversation with a trained counsellor is worth more than any premium subscription.
