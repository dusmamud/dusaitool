If you need a Diwali sale poster, a shop banner, or a coaching-class admission creative this week, you do not need to hire a designer first. A free AI poster maker can give you a usable poster in ten minutes — but only if you pick the right tool for the right job. The mistake most people make is generating the whole poster, text included, from one prompt. The text usually comes out misspelled or warped, and then the poster is unusable for a real shop board or WhatsApp forward.

The direct answer is this: use an AI tool for the artwork and a template editor for the words. [Canva](https://www.canva.com/) free is the best starting point for most Indians because your headline, date, price and phone number stay as editable text, not as pixels baked into the image. [PosterMyWall](https://www.postermywall.com/) is the best choice when you want a ready festival or sale template and do not want to design anything from scratch. [Ideogram](https://docs.ideogram.ai/plans-and-billing/available-plans) is the best free tool for generating the background artwork itself, especially when you need readable words inside an illustration — but its free images are public, so do not use it for anything private. [Adobe Express](https://www.adobe.com/in/express/pricing) is the strongest free option when the poster will actually be printed, because it exports proper print sizes and PDF. [Microsoft Designer](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/designer/frequently-asked-questions-about-microsoft-designer) is a decent free backup, but it is licensed for personal, non-commercial use only, so a shop should not rely on it for business posters. [Sivi](https://sivi.ai/generate-banner) is the specialist for banners in many sizes at once, and [Piktochart](https://support.piktochart.com/article/39-free-account) is useful for posters that are mostly information, like a notice or a price list — with one serious catch on downloads that you must read before you start.

Every tool below works in India through a normal browser, without a VPN. Prices and free limits change often, so before you pay for anything, or before you promise a client or a relative a poster by a deadline, re-check the official pricing or help page linked in each section. Nothing in this article is a promise that a free limit will stay the same next month.

## Canva: the default free poster maker, and the one most shops should start with

Canva is not really a poster generator; it is a design editor with AI inside it. That distinction is exactly why it wins for posters. You start from a poster template, or from a blank poster size, generate a background with Magic Media (its text-to-image tool), and then type your headline, offer, date and phone number as normal text boxes. If the date changes, you edit the text. If a customer points out a spelling mistake, you fix one word. On a fully AI-generated poster, one wrong letter means regenerating the whole image and hoping the next attempt is better.

The free plan costs nothing and includes a large template library, including Indian festival templates for Diwali, Holi, Eid and Independence Day, plus sale, admission and shop-opening layouts. Canva's own poster page says Canva Free comes with "a number of poster generator credits" but does not print the number. Here the sources disagree, and you should know that: one independent comparison states Canva Free gives up to 20 AI uses a month, while Canva's own page publishes no number at all. Treat the lower, unpublished figure as the honest expectation — plan on testing a handful of prompts, not running a whole campaign on the free plan.

For a shop poster, the practical workflow is: pick a poster size (A4 for print, or the WhatsApp and Instagram sizes for sharing), choose a festival template, replace the photo background with a Magic Media image if you want something original, and keep all words as text. Export as PNG for WhatsApp and Instagram, and as PDF if a print shop will print it. Canva Print exists, but a local print shop with your PDF is usually cheaper in India. Canva Pro is listed at US$144 a year for one person on Canva's India pricing page, so even the paid upgrade is shown in dollars, not rupees, on that page. Do not pay for Pro just for posters unless you specifically need Magic Resize (one design into many sizes) or premium photos — the free plan covers ordinary festival and shop posters.

Canva also has one India-relevant strength: Hindi text works in its editor, and you can mix Hindi headlines with English details, which is exactly how most small-shop posters in India actually look. Check the font before downloading, because some decorative fonts render Hindi poorly. Stick to the clean Devanagari fonts and preview at full size before you print.

## Adobe Express: the best free option when the poster will be printed

Adobe Express is the template-first route with generative AI added. You start from one of its thousands of free poster templates, or type a prompt and let its text-to-image feature fill the artwork, and every headline stays a live text layer you can edit. Where it beats Canva for posters is the print pipeline: it lists standard poster sizes such as 11 × 17 inches and 18 × 24 inches with custom sizing, and it exports PDF "for professional printing" alongside PNG and JPG. If your poster is going on a shop shutter, a school notice board or a flex banner, that matters more than any AI feature.

The free plan gives basic access to templates and design tools plus 5GB of cloud storage. For AI generation, Adobe's own generative credits FAQ says free users get "a limited number of free daily generations across all generative AI features, available on a curated set of models", and that the limits refresh each day. The number itself is not published, so again, do not plan a bulk poster run on the free tier. The good news is that templates, photo tools and effects stay free regardless of the AI credit limit, so a poster built from a template with your own words costs nothing.

Commercial use is allowed for generative outputs, except features marked beta — that is friendlier than Microsoft Designer for a shop. One India-specific point worth knowing: Adobe's India pricing page notes that Airtel customers could claim 12 months of Adobe Express Premium free, with redemption open from 28 January 2026 until 27 January 2027 through the Airtel Thanks app. That offer is time-limited and may not apply to you, so check the Adobe India page before counting on it. Even without any offer, the free plan is enough for ordinary printed posters.

## PosterMyWall: ready templates when you do not want to design anything

PosterMyWall is built for exactly this job: thousands of ready poster, flyer and social templates for sales, festivals, restaurants, gyms, coaching classes and events. You pick a template, change the words, and download. For a shopkeeper who needs a "Diwali Dhamaka Sale — 20% off" poster in five minutes, this is faster than Canva, because the design decisions are already made.

The free basic plan is genuinely usable, with two honest catches. First, free downloads are standard quality, not high resolution — fine for WhatsApp, Instagram and a small print, but not ideal for a large flex banner. Second, the monthly allowance is small, and here the sources disagree within PosterMyWall's own help page: one part of the page lists 5 free downloads a month on the basic plan, while another part lists 10. Treat 5 as the safe assumption and treat anything above that as a bonus. Limits reset on the first of every month, and you need a free account to download at all.

Another real improvement to note: PosterMyWall's help page, updated in October 2026, says free downloads on the basic plan no longer contain watermarks. The one exception is stock photos by Getty Images used inside a design — those can still carry a Getty watermark. So if your poster uses a stock photo, check the download before you forward it. Free video downloads, if you ever make an animated poster, are limited to 20 seconds and are not HD. For still posters, the practical rule is simple: use PosterMyWall when a ready template fits your need, and move to Canva or Adobe Express when you need full control or a large print.

## Microsoft Designer: a good free backup, but not for business posters

Microsoft Designer is free to use with a free Microsoft account, and it generates posters, social posts and images from a prompt or a template. For a personal poster — a birthday, a family event, a school project — it is perfectly fine and genuinely free. The catch, stated plainly on Microsoft's own FAQ page, is that Designer is licensed for personal, non-commercial use with a Microsoft account. A shop, coaching class or restaurant should not build its business posters on it, because the licence does not cover commercial use.

Microsoft also says a subscription may be required for those who want to create more frequently, which means heavy free use can hit a limit even though no exact number is published. Some AI features also have age requirements that vary by region, so if a feature shows as unavailable on your account, that may be the reason rather than a bug. Use Designer as your backup: if Canva's AI credits run out mid-week, Designer can cover a personal poster, and its templates are clean and modern. But for anything you will put a shop name, price or offer on, use Canva, Adobe Express or PosterMyWall instead.

## Ideogram: the best free artwork generator, with one rule — nothing private

Ideogram is different from the editors above: it generates images from a prompt, and it is unusually good at putting readable words inside the image. For a poster background, a festival illustration, or a decorative title image, it is the strongest free generator in this list. The free plan gives 30 credits a week, but only if you sign in with Google, Apple or Microsoft — accounts created with an email and password do not get free credits at all, according to Ideogram's own plans page. That is a small detail that decides whether "free" actually works for you, so sign in the right way.

The rule you must not break: on the free plan, everything you generate is public. Anyone can see it. Do not generate posters with a personal phone number, a child's photo, a private address or an unpublished offer on the free plan. Use Ideogram for the artwork — a Diwali diya illustration, a cricket-themed background, a floral border — and then place your real words, prices and contact details in Canva or Adobe Express as editable text. That combination gives you the best of both: Ideogram's artwork quality and an editor's safe, editable text.

Ideogram's paid Plus plan is listed at US$20 a month, but for occasional festival posters the free weekly credits are usually enough, as long as you do not burn them on ten variations of the same idea. Generate two or three options, pick one, and stop. Also note that Ideogram's free generations are public by design, so if you want a truly original brand illustration that nobody else can reuse, the free plan is the wrong place for it.

## Sivi: one banner brief, many sizes at once

Sivi is the specialist in this list. Instead of designing one poster, you give it a brief or structured copy, and it composes editable, on-brand banner sets — the same message in many sizes for a shop board, a WhatsApp status, an Instagram post and a website banner. For a small business that needs the same sale creative everywhere, that saves real time.

The free plan gives 12 credits a month, one brand, 100MB of storage, and limited dimensions and models. Twelve banners a month is enough for one sale campaign, but not for a shop that posts daily. Sivi's pricing page also shows Indian pricing in rupees — its Pro plan is listed around ₹4,560 a month on yearly billing (₹5,700 monthly) — which is useful to know, because most tools in this list price in dollars. The free plan's banners are editable, so you can fix the Hindi or English text after generation instead of regenerating. Use Sivi when the job is "the same offer in five sizes", and use Canva when the job is "one beautiful poster".

## Piktochart: good for information posters, with a download trap

Piktochart is best known for infographics, and that is exactly what makes it useful for a certain kind of poster: a notice, a price list, a coaching timetable, a clinic's services board, or a "rules and timings" poster where the information matters more than the illustration. You can create unlimited visual projects on the free plan, and the free plan includes AI features for generating visuals from prompts or text.

But Piktochart has the sharpest catch in this whole list, and you must know it before you build anything there. Its knowledge base says free users can download visuals as PNG, but with a download limit of 2 PNG downloads per account — and that limit does not refresh once used. Two downloads, forever, on the free plan. That means you can design freely but you can only take two posters out of the tool. There is also a disagreement about AI credits: the official knowledge base says the free plan includes 60 AI credits, while one independent test of a new account found only 2 credits. Do not promise yourself 60; assume the lower number until your own account shows otherwise.

So the honest way to use Piktochart free is: design your information poster there only if you are sure it is final, and keep your two downloads for the two posters you actually need. For anything else — festival posters, sale posters, anything you will revise — use Canva or PosterMyWall, where downloads are not capped this way.

## Using poster AI in India: language, payment, and printing

Language comes first. Most poster tools handle English perfectly and Hindi acceptably, but decorative fonts are where Hindi breaks. Before you download, zoom in and read every Hindi word yourself. A misspelled festival greeting on a printed poster cannot be fixed after printing, and AI-generated text inside an image cannot be edited at all — which is why the "artwork from AI, words from the editor" rule matters so much. For mixed Hindi-English posters, Canva and Adobe Express are the safest because the text stays editable.

Payment is the second practical point. Free plans need no card, which is good — never enter card details for a "free" poster tool unless you have deliberately decided to pay. Where paid plans exist, prices are mostly shown in US dollars (Canva, Adobe Express, Ideogram), while Sivi shows rupee pricing. Indian debit cards and UPI do not work on every international checkout, so if a tool only accepts international cards, that is a real barrier for a small shop. The free plans in this article avoid that problem entirely.

Printing is the third point. A poster that looks good on a phone can look blurry on a flex banner. For WhatsApp and Instagram, a PNG export is fine. For print, use PDF export from Adobe Express or Canva, and tell the print shop the final size before you design, not after. PosterMyWall's free standard-quality download is fine for small prints but not for large banners. If a banner is the goal, design at the banner size from the start, or pay for one high-resolution export rather than printing a small image large.

All seven tools work in India without a VPN through a normal browser. None of them is blocked in India as far as any official page states. If a tool ever fails to load, it is far more likely to be a network or account issue than a country block — try another browser or network before assuming the tool is unavailable in India.

## What to avoid

Avoid generating the entire poster, words included, from a single AI prompt and then printing it. AI text rendering has improved, but a wrong letter in a price, date or phone number is still common, and you cannot edit baked-in text. Avoid putting personal details — your mobile number, home address, or a child's photo — into Ideogram's free plan, because free generations are public. Avoid building business posters in Microsoft Designer, because its licence is personal, non-commercial use only. Avoid designing a poster in Piktochart free unless you are sure it is final, because you only get two PNG downloads per account and they never refresh. Avoid paying for a Pro plan in a hurry: every tool here does ordinary festival and shop posters on its free plan, and the paid features (Magic Resize, premium photos, more credits) only matter once you are making posters every week. And avoid trusting any free limit in this article blindly — limits change, and the official pricing or help page is the only source that matters before you pay or promise a deadline.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I make a Diwali poster for free in India without a designer?** Yes. Start with a Canva free festival template or a PosterMyWall sale template, keep your offer and phone number as editable text, and export as PNG for WhatsApp. You do not need to pay for a single festival poster.

**Which free tool is best for a shop banner that will be printed large?** Adobe Express free, because it exports PDF in real poster sizes like 11 × 17 inches and 18 × 24 inches with custom sizing. PosterMyWall's free download is standard quality and is better for phone sharing than for large flex printing.

**Why does the text on my AI poster come out misspelled?** Because when text is generated as part of the image, the AI is painting letter shapes, not typing. The fix is to generate only the artwork with AI and add all words as editable text in Canva or Adobe Express, where you control every letter.

**Is Microsoft Designer really free for my shop's posters?** It is free to use, but its licence is for personal, non-commercial use only, according to Microsoft's own FAQ. For a shop, coaching class or restaurant poster, use Canva, Adobe Express or PosterMyWall instead.

**Can I use Ideogram free for my business posters?** You can use it for artwork, but remember two things: free generations are public, so never put private details in them, and free credits are only 30 a week and only for Google, Apple or Microsoft sign-in. Put your real offer text in an editor afterwards.

**How many free posters can I actually download from Piktochart?** Only 2 PNG downloads per account on the free plan, and that limit never refreshes. Design there only when the poster is final, and use Canva or PosterMyWall for posters you will revise.

**Do these tools work in India without a VPN?** Yes. All seven tools work through a normal browser in India, and none of their official pages states an India block. Free plans need no card, which avoids most payment problems for small shops.

## The final verdict, and a checklist before you print

For most people in India, the winning combination is simple: Canva free for the poster itself, PosterMyWall when a ready template is faster, and Ideogram only for background artwork. Adobe Express takes over when the poster will be printed large. Microsoft Designer stays a personal-use backup, Sivi handles one message in many sizes, and Piktochart is only for information posters you are sure about, because of its two-download limit.

Before you print or forward, run this checklist: read every Hindi and English word at full size; confirm the date, price and phone number are correct; check that no watermark appears on the download (especially Getty stock photos in PosterMyWall); confirm the export size matches where the poster will appear (phone or print); and re-check the tool's free limit on its official page if you are making more than a couple of posters this month. A poster is a public promise — a wrong price or a wrong date costs more than the ten minutes it takes to check.
