Cricket in India does not need another app that shouts predictions at you. What a fan actually needs is simple: a fast live score, a way to check a stat without arguing about it, a short highlight when the match is over, and a plain-language explanation of what just happened. This article covers the tools that do those jobs in India, including the one genuinely useful AI feature for fans — AI-generated highlights of your own local matches — and it deliberately leaves out betting and fantasy-money apps entirely.

The honest starting point is this: scores, commentary and statistics are free on the tools below. You pay only when you want live video streaming or a polished highlight reel. Nothing here will tell you who will win the next match, and any app that promises that is selling you something else.

## Cricbuzz: the default live score app

Cricbuzz is the score app most Indian fans already have, and for good reason. Its own app listing promises superfast scores and ball-by-ball commentary, plus notifications for live matches and breaking news, schedules, rankings, stats and records, and coverage of international cricket, the IPL, the Big Bash, the CPL and domestic cricket too. For a fan, that means one app answers "what is the score right now" and "what happened in that over" without paying anything.

The free part is the whole score experience: live scores, commentary, news and editorials, and stats. The app contains ads, which is how the free tier is paid for. Live video streaming is a different product — Cricbuzz offers it through Willow, covering the IPL, ICC tournaments and home tours — and that streaming is a paid add-on, not part of the free score app. Do not buy a streaming add-on expecting it to change the scores; the scores were already free.

Use Cricbuzz when you want speed during a live match, especially on a slow connection, because its text commentary uses far less data than video. Keep notifications on only for the teams you follow, or your phone will buzz through every match of every tournament.

## ESPNcricinfo Statsguru: the stat argument settler

Statsguru is ESPNcricinfo's searchable cricket statistics database, and its own description calls it the most comprehensive query tool for cricket statistics available online. It covers all international matches, and men's T20 stats are included as well. You can search by player, team, ground, or umpire, and filter by batting, bowling, fielding, all-round, partnership or team records.

This is the tool to open when a debate starts: who averages more in successful chases, who has the best economy in the death overs, or how a player performs at a particular ground. The answers are free, and they come from the recorded matches, not from an opinion. The limitation is real too: Statsguru is a database, not an explanation. It will not tell you why a number matters, and it will not predict the next match. Pair it with a chatbot explainer (below) when you want the number turned into a sentence.

One practical note: Statsguru works best on a bigger screen. On a phone it works, but building a filtered query with several conditions is much easier on a laptop or tablet.

## CricHeroes: AI highlights for your own matches

CricHeroes is the one tool in this list with a genuine AI feature built for fans rather than broadcasters. It is a scoring app for local, school, weekend-league and friendly matches: a scorer records the match ball by ball, and followers get live scores. Then comes the AI part. According to CricHeroes' own blog, its AI-powered highlights automatically capture key moments from a live-streamed match and stitch them into a reel — your match-winning six, a diving catch, or key wickets — without anyone editing video by hand.

The pricing is refreshingly specific, and that is why it is worth quoting exactly: the first player highlight is free for every user, a player highlight reel costs ₹29, and a full match highlight reel costs ₹49. That is a small, one-time payment for a finished video you can download and share, not a monthly subscription. If your child plays school cricket or your office team plays weekend matches, this is the closest thing to a professional broadcast package that an ordinary team can get.

The catch is also clear: AI highlights need a live-streamed match to work from. If nobody streams the match, there is no footage for the AI to cut. And the AI picks moments by match events, so a quiet defensive innings will not produce a dramatic reel. Use it for matches that are actually streamed, and treat the ₹29 or ₹49 as the price of a finished keepsake, not as a prediction or analysis tool.

## FanCode: streaming for the tournaments TV skips

FanCode is a sports streaming subscription in India. Its own subscription pages say a subscription gives access to live matches, highlights and live moments across cricket and other sports, and that you can buy a specific match or tour pass instead of a full subscription, or a yearly pass for premium live streaming. You can cancel anytime and keep access until the end of your billing cycle.

For a cricket fan, FanCode matters for the matches that are not on the big TV channels: smaller leagues and tours whose rights FanCode holds. Before paying, check two things on the official subscription page: whether the specific tournament you want is actually included in the pass you are buying, and whether you want a single tour pass or the yearly pass. Buying the yearly pass for one tournament is the most common waste of money here. Prices and included events change between seasons, so re-check the official page before paying or promising anyone that a match will be available.

## ICC app: official highlights and the archive

The official ICC app is the free, official route to ICC event highlights. Its app listing says it is available for free, that signing up for ICC Family gives immediate and unlimited access to all content, and that it offers match highlights from all ICC events plus an archive of on-demand content. Live matches are offered across selected territories, which means live availability depends on where you are and which tournament it is.

Use the ICC app when you missed an ICC match and want the official highlight rather than a random clip, or when you want to revisit an old tournament from the archive. It will not replace a score app for ball-by-ball coverage of a bilateral series, and it will not show you every domestic league. Its strength is exactly what its name says: official ICC content, free to access, in one place.

## ChatGPT and Gemini: the AI explainer for any match

The most flexible AI tool for a cricket fan is not a cricket app at all — it is a general chatbot. Ask ChatGPT or Gemini a question like "why did India lose wickets in a cluster in that chase" or "explain the new ball rule in simple words" and you get a plain-language answer that a stats page will never give you. Both have free tiers that handle this kind of question easily, and both work in India without a VPN.

The rule for using them honestly is simple: chatbots are good at explaining, bad at remembering. They can misremember a score, a date, or a record, especially for a match played yesterday. So use the chatbot for the explanation, and verify any number it gives you against Cricbuzz or Statsguru before you repeat it. Never use a chatbot, or any app, for match predictions that involve money — that is not analysis, it is gambling with extra steps, and this article does not recommend any tool for it.

## Cricsheet: free ball-by-ball data for stat nerds

Cricsheet is the quiet, nerdy end of cricket fandom, and it is completely free. Its downloads page offers ball-by-ball data for 23,193 matches, including 921 Tests, 3,226 one-day internationals, 5,845 T20 internationals and 1,243 IPL matches, in JSON, YAML, CSV and XML formats. The data goes back to 2001 for men and 2003 for women, and covers 110 countries.

Who is this for? Anyone who wants to answer their own question with real data: build a chart of a player's scoring areas, compare two bowlers over a season, or check a claim a TV pundit just made. You will need a spreadsheet or a little patience — the files are data, not an app. If you track your own analyses in a sheet, our guide to [data and Excel tools](/blog/ai-tools-for-data-excel-india/) pairs naturally with this one. Note one honest gap in the data itself: 383 matches are currently withheld from the downloads, mostly involving Afghanistan or the Afghanistan Premier League, and Cricsheet says so on its own site. Where sources disagree or data is missing, say so rather than filling the gap with a guess — the same rule this article follows throughout.

## JioHotstar: watching the IPL in India

For watching the IPL and other major tournaments in India, JioHotstar is the streaming home. Its model for IPL 2026, as reported, is freemium: viewers can watch the first few minutes of every match free, but watching the full match needs a subscription, with a base mobile plan reported at ₹79 per month and a Super plan at ₹149. Telecom recharges from Jio and Airtel have also bundled JioHotstar access with cricket-specific plans.

Treat those prices as a snapshot, not a promise: streaming prices change every season, and the plan that includes your tournament this year may not include it next year. Before paying, check the official JioHotstar plans page for the current season, and check whether a telecom recharge you were already going to buy includes the same access for free. If you only want scores and highlights, you do not need JioHotstar at all — Cricbuzz and the ICC app cover that for free.

## Using cricket tools in India: language, payment and data

All the tools above work in India without a VPN: they are Indian or global apps with Indian availability, and none of them needs a workaround to open. That matters because a tool that needs a VPN will fail exactly when you need it — during a live match on mobile data.

For payments, the Indian pattern holds everywhere: UPI works for app purchases and subscriptions, and the small CricHeroes highlight payments (₹29 and ₹49) are exactly the kind of purchase UPI handles easily. For subscriptions, prefer a monthly or tour pass over a yearly pass unless you are sure you will watch through the year.

For data, remember the split: text scores and commentary sip data, video streaming drinks it. On a limited mobile plan, follow the match on Cricbuzz and watch only the official highlight afterwards — that is often the better experience anyway, because a highlight shows you the six without the buffering.

Language is the one place to check before you assume. Commentary and interface language options differ by app and by tournament, and they change between seasons. Open the app's language or commentary settings before the match starts, not during the first over.

## What to avoid

First, avoid prediction and betting apps completely. Any app that sells "100% sure match predictions", "fixed tips" or paid betting signals is not an AI tool — it is a shop selling certainty about something nobody can know. The same goes for paid WhatsApp or Telegram tip groups. This article covers none of them on purpose.

Second, avoid paying for scores. Scores, commentary and basic stats are free on Cricbuzz and Statsguru. If an app charges you a subscription just to see a live score, close it.

Third, avoid highlight clips from unofficial accounts when the official highlight exists. Unofficial clips get taken down, carry wrong context, and sometimes carry betting links. The ICC app and official tournament channels are the safer source, and they are free.

Fourth, avoid trusting a single chatbot answer for a record or a score. Chatbots explain well and misremember often. Cross-check every number against Statsguru or Cricbuzz before you forward it to your cricket group — a wrong stat forwarded with confidence is how arguments start.

Finally, avoid yearly passes bought in excitement. Tournaments end, rights move, and a yearly pass bought for one series is money spent on months you will not watch. Buy the tour pass for the tournament in front of you.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the best free app for live cricket scores in India?** Cricbuzz, because its scores, ball-by-ball commentary, news and stats are free, it covers international cricket, the IPL and domestic cricket, and its text commentary works well on slow mobile data.

**Can I watch IPL matches free in India?** Not fully. Under the freemium model reported for IPL 2026, JioHotstar lets you watch the first few minutes of a match free, but the full match needs a subscription, so check the current official plans page before the season starts.

**What is CricHeroes and is it really free?** CricHeroes is a scoring app for local, school and weekend matches, free to use for scoring and live scores. Its AI highlights are the paid part: the first player highlight is free, then ₹29 per player reel and ₹49 per match reel, as stated on CricHeroes' own blog.

**How do I settle a cricket stat argument quickly?** Open ESPNcricinfo Statsguru and search the player, team or ground with the filters you need. It is free, covers all internationals, and gives you the recorded number instead of an opinion.

**Are AI cricket predictions reliable?** No. No app can reliably predict a match result, and any app selling guaranteed predictions or paid betting tips should be avoided. Use AI tools for explanations, highlights and stats — not for money decisions.

**Where can I download free ball-by-ball cricket data?** Cricsheet offers free downloads of ball-by-ball data for 23,193 matches in JSON, YAML, CSV and XML formats, including 1,243 IPL matches, though 383 matches are currently withheld and the site says so openly.

**Which tool is best for official ICC highlights?** The official ICC app, which is free to download and offers highlights from all ICC events plus an archive of on-demand content after a free ICC Family sign-up, though live match availability depends on territory rights.

**Do I need a VPN for any of these cricket tools in India?** No. All the tools in this article work in India without a VPN. If a cricket app asks you to use a VPN to watch a match, it is almost certainly not an official source for that tournament.

## The final verdict, and a checklist before you pay

Start with the free stack: Cricbuzz for the live score, Statsguru when a stat is disputed, and the ICC app for official highlights. Add CricHeroes if you play or follow local matches, because its AI highlights are the one AI feature here that does something no human in your team would do by hand. Use a chatbot only to explain a match, and verify its numbers before you repeat them.

Before you pay for anything, run this checklist: is the tournament actually included in the pass, is a tour pass cheaper than the yearly pass, does a telecom recharge you already buy include the same streaming, and do you actually need video when free scores and highlights already tell the story? If the answer to the last question is no, keep your money — the free tools already cover the match.

Prices and limits change every season, so re-check the official pricing and help page of any tool before paying or promising someone that a match will be available. That single habit will save you more money than any subscription bundle.
