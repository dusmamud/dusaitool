The short answer is this: if you are a small business in India with one to three people handling support, start with Zoho Desk's free plan for tickets or Crisp's free plan for live chat. Both are genuinely free, not just trials, and both work in India without a VPN. Add a paid AI chatbot only when your ticket volume or your need for automatic answers genuinely grows past what the free plans cover, because the AI features in almost every tool on this list sit behind a paid plan, even when the basic ticketing or chat is free.

That distinction matters more than any feature list. Many roundups call a tool "free" when it only offers a 7-day or 14-day trial, and many call a time-limited free programme a "free plan". This article separates the two honestly. Every free limit, price and India-availability claim below was checked against the vendor's own pricing or support pages, and where sources disagree, the article says so. Prices and limits change, so re-check the official pricing or help page before paying or before promising a customer a deadline based on a tool.

## Zoho Desk: the strongest free ticketing tool for India

Zoho Desk is the most practical starting point for an Indian small business that gets support requests by email, because its free edition is a real, ongoing free plan, not a trial. Zoho's own pricing-comparison page lists a Free edition with a user limit of 3 users, alongside paid editions. That means a founder plus two support staff can run a shared ticket queue without paying anything.

What the free plan covers is the core of support work: tickets coming in from email and other channels, assignment to agents, and a shared view of who is handling what. Zoho Desk also has a social media module that lets a team respond to brand mentions and tickets received from Instagram, Facebook and X, which matters in India where many small businesses get their first complaints in Instagram DMs, not by email. Check the edition comparison before assuming a particular social channel is in the free edition, because edition features differ, but the ticketing core is the free plan's strength.

Zoho is an Indian company, and that shows up in practical ways. Its pricing page shows prices in rupees per agent per month, and local taxes such as GST are charged in addition to the mentioned prices. Payment in rupees through Indian cards is the normal route, and support documentation and onboarding material are written with Indian businesses in mind. The product works in India without a VPN, as a normal web application, and Zoho operates data centres in India for customers who want their data stored closer to home.

The honest limit is AI. Zoho Desk's AI assistant, Zia, and its deeper automation and workflow features are edition-dependent, which in practice means they sit in paid editions rather than the free one. If your goal is only to stop losing customer emails in personal inboxes and to answer tickets as a team, the free plan does that. If your goal is an AI bot that drafts replies or answers customers automatically, expect to pay for a higher edition, and confirm exactly which edition includes the AI feature you want on Zoho's pricing-comparison page before upgrading.

## Freshdesk: a free programme for six months, not a free plan forever

Freshdesk is the tool most often misdescribed in "free tools" lists, so read this section carefully. Freshdesk's own support article describes a Free programme that is available free for 6 months, designed for small businesses and startups. Within the Free programme you can add up to two agents. It includes integrated ticketing across email and social channels, automatic ticket dispatch to the right agents, a knowledge base for a self-service portal, ticket trend reports, out-of-the-box analytics and reporting, team collaboration on tickets, and a choice of data centre location.

That is a genuinely useful six months. A new business can set up a proper ticket system, build a small help centre, and learn its real ticket volume before paying. But it is not a forever-free plan, and articles that call it one are wrong or out of date. After the free period, you move to a paid plan or lose the programme's access, so plan your budget around month seven, not around "free forever". Existing and new free programme users can also try higher plans for 14 days, which is a trial, not a free tier.

Freshdesk's AI features, sold under the Freddy name, are not part of the Free programme's listed features. The programme's feature list covers ticketing, dispatch, knowledge base and reporting, not AI answers. If you want AI-suggested replies or an AI agent, that is a paid conversation, and the price depends on the plan and add-ons you choose. Freshdesk works in India without a VPN, and the ability to choose a data centre location matters for businesses that prefer their customer data stored in a particular region.

Use Freshdesk when you are starting out and want the most complete free ticketing setup for a short runway, and when you are confident you can either pay after six months or move your tickets elsewhere. Do not use it if your whole plan depends on it staying free forever, because it will not.

## Crisp: the best free live chat for a website

Crisp is the simplest answer to a different question: how do visitors on your website talk to you right now, without email delays? Crisp's free plan is free forever, aimed at solopreneurs and entrepreneurs, and it includes a website chat widget and 2 seats. That is enough for a founder and one teammate to answer live chat from a website, keep conversations in one shared inbox, and stop juggling personal WhatsApp chats and email threads.

The free plan's honest limit is stated plainly on Crisp's pricing page: no AI credits are included. That means the free plan gives you human chat, not an AI bot that answers on its own. Crisp's AI features are paid, and every new account gets 14 days of full access to all features as a trial, which is useful for testing but is not the same as a free AI plan. After the trial, you fall back to what the free plan includes unless you pay.

Crisp works in India without a VPN, and its chat widget drops into a normal website, including sites built with no-code builders. For a small shop, coaching business or services site that gets questions like "what are your timings" or "do you deliver to my city", a live chat widget with two seats covers most of the real workload. Pair it with a short FAQ page and a few saved replies, and you will answer faster than most competitors without buying any AI at all.

## JivoChat: free basic chat after the trial ends

JivoChat is another live chat option with a free route, but its shape is different from Crisp. JivoChat's pricing page is headed as free live chat for websites, and its own FAQ answers the important question directly: when the 14-day demo expires, you can buy a licence to keep the professional features, or you can continue to use the chat in a basic free mode with less functionality. You can also downgrade to the free version at any time, with no contracts.

That makes JivoChat a reasonable choice if you want to try the full professional feature set for two weeks and then settle into a basic free chat if you do not need the extras. The paid Professional tier is listed at 42 dollars per agent per month when billed annually on the pricing page, with a higher tier at 56 dollars per agent per month annually, so the paid route is priced per agent in dollars, not rupees. For a one-person business, the free basic mode may be enough; for a team that needs chat routing, mobile SDK or video call modules, those sit in the paid tiers.

JivoChat also supports chats from more than a website, including social and messaging channels depending on the plan, which is useful for Indian businesses whose customers message on Facebook or Instagram. As with every tool here, confirm which channels are in the free basic mode before promising customers that every channel is covered, because the free mode has less functionality than the professional version.

## HubSpot Service Hub: free ticketing plus a CRM record

HubSpot Service Hub's free tools are worth considering when you want support tickets connected to customer records, not just a chat box. HubSpot's own pricing guide lists Service Hub Free at 0 dollars per month for up to 2 users, including a shared inbox with team email, contact management in HubSpot's Smart CRM, live chat and conversational bots with limited features, a central ticketing system, and basic reporting and dashboards.

The strength here is context. When a customer writes in, the team can see that customer's record, past tickets and conversations in the same system, instead of searching through email. For a business that already uses HubSpot or plans to grow into sales and marketing tools on the same platform, starting on the free service tools keeps everything in one place.

The limits are equally clear. The free tier allows up to two users, the conversational bots have limited features, and the AI customer agent, called Breeze Customer Agent, is a Professional feature, not a free one. HubSpot's paid plans also use HubSpot Credits for some usage-based features, including the customer agent, and Professional carries a 1,500 dollar setup fee, which is a real cost to know about before you plan an upgrade. Starter begins at 7 dollars per month per seat billed annually, and Professional at 90 dollars per seat per month billed annually, so the jump from free to AI-powered is large. Use the free tools for human support with good records; budget properly if you want HubSpot's AI agent.

## Tidio: good chatbot, but trial only, not free

Tidio appears in many "free chatbot" lists, and its own pricing page tells a different story, which is exactly why this article checks official pages. Tidio's pricing page is built around billable conversations, with sliders from 50 up to thousands of conversations, and its plans carry a 7-day free trial. The Starter plan is built around 100 billable conversations, and its AI chatbot, Lyro, is sold as AI conversations starting from 50 Lyro AI conversations on the AI plan. There is no free-forever tier shown on the pricing page.

That does not make Tidio bad. Its chatbot and live chat are well regarded, and a 7-day trial lets you test whether automated answers actually reduce your workload. But if your budget is zero, Tidio is not your tool, because after the trial you pay for conversations. Billable conversations are also a cost model to understand before you commit: every live conversation and ticket counts toward your monthly allowance, so a sudden spike in customer messages can push you into a higher tier. For a business with steady, predictable chat volume, that model is manageable; for a business with festival-season spikes, like Diwali sale queries, it needs a buffer in the budget.

Use Tidio when you are ready to pay for a chatbot and want to test it for a week first. Do not pick it on the assumption that it is free, because the official pricing page does not offer a free plan.

## Intercom: powerful, but built for paying teams

Intercom is the tool many growing startups graduate to, and its pricing explains why it is not a starting point for a zero budget. Intercom's pricing page offers its Fin AI Agent plus Intercom plans, with the Essential plan from 29 dollars per seat per month, and a new-customer offer showing 19 dollars per seat per month. It also offers a free trial, but there is no free plan. Fin, its AI agent, is charged per Fin outcome, with the page showing from 0.99 dollars per Fin outcome, which means AI answers are metered, not unlimited.

For a small Indian business, Intercom makes sense only when support volume and revenue justify per-seat plus per-outcome pricing. Its strength is a genuinely capable AI agent combined with a polished shared inbox, and teams that use it well report large drops in repetitive tickets. But a founder answering twenty tickets a day does not need per-outcome AI billing, and the dollar pricing adds up fast when converted to rupees. Start with a free tool, and consider Intercom when your ticket volume, team size and support budget all point the same way.

## Zendesk: the enterprise standard, trial only

Zendesk is the best-known name in helpdesk software, and its pricing is straightforward. Its pricing page lists a Support Team plan at 19 dollars per agent per month paid yearly, with a free trial offered. There is no free plan. The Support Team plan covers email and a ticketing system, ticket routing, prebuilt analytics dashboards, pre-written responses, customer context, and automations and triggers.

Zendesk suits businesses that already have a support team and want a standard, well-documented system with a large app ecosystem. For a one-to-three person Indian business, it is usually more than needed at the start, and the per-agent dollar pricing is a real monthly commitment. Its AI features sit in higher tiers, so the entry plan is a human ticketing system, not an AI agent. Try the trial if you are comparing enterprise tools, but do not plan a zero-budget setup around Zendesk.

## Using support AI in India: language, payment, and where customers actually are

India changes how support tools should be set up, and the biggest mistake is copying a US setup without adjusting for it. First, language. Most chatbots and AI agents on this list are strongest in English, and Hindi or Hinglish answers need deliberate work: translated help articles, saved replies in Hindi, and bot answers written for how Indian customers actually phrase questions. Machine translation can help draft those answers, but do not let an unreviewed translation answer a paying customer about refunds, delivery dates or warranties. Tools that support multiple languages still need a human to check the Hindi wording, especially for money and deadline promises.

Second, payment and billing. Zoho Desk shows rupee pricing for Indian customers, which makes budgeting simple. Most other tools on this list price in dollars per agent or per seat, so convert the monthly cost to rupees, add GST where applicable, and check whether your card supports recurring international payments. A tool that looks cheap at 19 dollars per agent per month is a different commitment once you multiply by agents, months and exchange rates.

Third, channels. Many Indian customers do not email support at all; they message on WhatsApp, Instagram or Facebook. A website chat widget alone will miss them. When you pick a tool, check which channels it actually covers on the plan you will use, not on the top plan. The free WhatsApp Business app is a separate, useful starting point for very small businesses, but WhatsApp's business platform messaging through providers is a paid service, so do not confuse the free app with a free AI WhatsApp bot. Email ticketing plus one messaging channel, answered by humans, beats an AI bot on a channel your customers never use.

Fourth, data and trust. Customers share order details, addresses and phone numbers with support. Use the tool's data centre choice where offered, set who on your team can see tickets, and never paste customer phone numbers or order IDs into a public AI demo or an unapproved tool. A support tool is a system that holds customer data, so pick one you are willing to keep, not just one that is free for a week.

Finally, no tool on this list needs a VPN in India. They are ordinary web applications, and Zoho Desk and Freshdesk both offer data centre choices that include options suited to Indian businesses. If a tool ever asks you to use a VPN to reach it from India, treat that as a warning sign, not a normal setup step.

## What to avoid

Avoid buying an AI chatbot before you know your top ten questions. If you cannot list the ten questions customers ask most, an AI bot will guess, and wrong guesses about price, delivery or refunds cost more than slow human answers. Write those ten answers first, put them in a help page, and then automate.

Avoid choosing a tool only because a roundup called it free. As this article shows, Freshdesk's free offer lasts six months, Tidio's is a seven-day trial, and Intercom and Zendesk have trials, not free plans. Always open the vendor's pricing page and look for the words "free forever", "free plan" or "free trial", because those three phrases mean very different things.

Avoid per-conversation and per-outcome billing without a monthly cap in your head. Tidio bills by conversations and Intercom's Fin bills by outcome, so a viral post or a sale can multiply your bill in the same month your team is busiest. Set an internal budget limit and a rule for when a human takes over from the bot.

Avoid letting a bot promise deadlines, refunds or replacements it cannot approve. AI answers should handle information questions, like timings, delivery areas and product details, while money decisions should go to a human with a clear approval rule. A bot that promises a refund you cannot give will create a worse ticket than the one it tried to close.

Avoid spreading support across five inboxes. One shared ticket queue or one shared chat inbox, answered by the same small team, is better than email, Instagram, WhatsApp, phone and website chat all handled separately. Pick one system as the home for tickets, and route other channels into it where the plan allows.

## Frequently asked questions

**Which customer support tool is actually free in India?** Zoho Desk's free plan for up to 3 users and Crisp's free plan for 2 seats are genuinely free, and HubSpot's free service tools cover up to 2 users. JivoChat offers a free basic mode after its trial. Freshdesk's free offer lasts six months, while Tidio, Intercom and Zendesk offer trials, not free plans.

**Do I need an AI chatbot, or is human chat enough?** For most small businesses, human chat plus a good FAQ page is enough at the start. An AI chatbot earns its cost only when the same questions repeat so often that answering them by hand takes hours every week. Count your repeated questions first, then decide.

**Can these tools answer customers in Hindi?** They can, but only with work from you. You need Hindi help articles, saved replies and reviewed bot answers, because most bots are English-first. Do not rely on raw machine translation for refund, delivery or warranty answers without a human check.

**What is the cheapest way to start support for a one-person business?** Start with Crisp free for website chat or Zoho Desk free for email tickets, depending on where your customers contact you. Both cost nothing and need no team. Add a paid plan only when volume grows.

**Is Freshdesk really free?** Freshdesk's Free programme is free for six months for up to two agents, according to its own support article. It is not a forever-free plan, so budget for a paid plan after six months or plan a move before the period ends.

**Does Tidio have a free plan?** No free-forever tier was found on Tidio's official pricing page. Its plans carry a 7-day free trial, and its AI chatbot Lyro is a paid feature. Roundups that call Tidio free are describing the trial, not a free plan.

**Will these tools work in India without a VPN?** Yes. All eight tools are normal web applications that work in India without a VPN. Zoho Desk and Freshdesk also offer data centre choices, which is useful if you prefer customer data stored in a particular region.

**Should a small shop use Intercom or Zendesk?** Only when the budget and volume justify it. Both are strong, paid tools with trials and no free plans, priced per agent in dollars. A small shop usually gets more value from a free tool until ticket volume and team size grow.

## The final verdict, and a checklist before you pay

Start with Zoho Desk free or Crisp free for tickets and chat; pay only when ticket volume or AI answers genuinely outgrow the free limits. That is the whole strategy in one line, and it protects both your budget and your customers.

Before you pay for anything, run this checklist. First, list your top ten customer questions and check whether a free tool plus a help page already answers them. Second, count your real monthly tickets and conversations, and compare that number with the free limit or the paid plan's allowance, not with a marketing claim. Third, open the vendor's pricing page and confirm whether the offer is free forever, a time-limited programme or a trial, and what happens when it ends. Fourth, convert dollar pricing to rupees, add GST where it applies, and confirm your payment method works for recurring billing. Fifth, decide which channels your customers actually use, and confirm those channels are included on the plan you will buy. Sixth, write the rule for what the bot may answer and what must go to a human, especially for refunds and deadlines. If you can tick all six boxes, the paid plan is a decision, not a gamble. If you cannot, stay on the free plan and let your real ticket numbers make the case for you.

Prices and limits change, so re-check the official pricing and help pages linked in this article before paying or before promising a customer a deadline based on a tool. A free plan that fits today may change tomorrow, and a trial that looks generous may end before your team has learned the product. The tools above are all real, all work in India, and all have a place, but the right one is the one whose free limit matches your actual workload, not the one with the longest feature list.
