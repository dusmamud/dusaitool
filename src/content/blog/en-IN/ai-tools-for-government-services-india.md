A government form is rarely hard because of one question. It is hard because of everything around it: which scheme you qualify for, which document proves it, which portal actually accepts the application, and whether the message on your phone asking for a fee is real or a scam. AI tools help with the first two problems — finding the right scheme and understanding the form — but they cannot solve the third problem for you. Only the official portal can accept your application, and no AI tool, free or paid, can promise you an approval, a fee waiver, or a deadline.

The short answer for India is this: use [myScheme](https://www.myscheme.gov.in/) free to find schemes you may be eligible for, because it is the national platform that matches your details against Central and State schemes. Use [UMANG](https://web.umang.gov.in/) free as the single app for government services, because it lists more than 2,100 services from over 200 departments. Keep [DigiLocker](https://www.digilocker.gov.in/) ready, because it holds your issued documents — Aadhaar, driving licence, marksheets — so you are not hunting for paper when a form asks for proof. Use [Bhashini](https://bhashini.gov.in/) free when a government page or document is in a language you do not read comfortably. Use [JanSamarth](https://www.jansamarth.in/) free for credit-linked government schemes, because it states plainly that it asks applicants for no payment at all. Use the [MyGov Helpdesk on WhatsApp](https://www.mygov.in/overview) when you need a DigiLocker document on your phone quickly. And use [ChatGPT](https://chatgpt.com/) free and [Gemini](https://gemini.google.com/) free only to draft a letter or explain a form in simple words — never to submit anything, and never with your Aadhaar number, OTP, or bank details in the chat.

One honest fact comes first, because it changes what you should expect. As of late 2026, there is no official "government AI assistant" that files applications for you. The Ministry of Electronics and IT issued a tender in August 2026 to build AI agents for DigiLocker, UMANG, and other Digital India platforms, including multilingual chatbots, voice-based form filling, and document summarisation. That work is at the tender and building stage, not a citizen tool you can open today. UMANG's own pages say an AI-based voice assistant is being planned to reach people beyond smartphone users. So if any app or agent today claims to be the official UMANG or DigiLocker AI that approves schemes for a fee, that claim is false, and the fee is a scam. The tools below are what actually works today.

## myScheme: the scheme finder that starts with your details

[myScheme](https://www.myscheme.gov.in/) is the national platform for searching and discovering government schemes, run through the National e-Governance Division. It is a one-stop platform: instead of visiting dozens of department websites, you enter basic details — age, gender, income level, social category, and similar demographic facts — and it shows schemes from Central, State, and Union Territory governments that match your profile. Each scheme page then gives eligibility criteria, benefits, the application procedure, and the documents required, plus its own FAQ section and a link to the actual application page.

The three-step process is deliberately simple. Step one, you enter your details in the "Find Schemes for You" feature. Step two, the platform shows relevant schemes from hundreds of listed schemes. Step three, you open a scheme, read its details, and follow its own application link to apply. That last step matters: myScheme finds and explains, but the application itself happens on the scheme's own portal. If a scheme page says you are eligible, that is still not an approval — the department decides when you apply, and the final eligibility check happens there.

myScheme is free to use as a government portal, and it lists a support email and a helpline that works 9:00 AM to 5:30 PM. Use it at the start of any search, before you trust a forwarded message about a "new scheme." If a scheme is real and current, it will usually appear here with its official details. If it appears only in a WhatsApp forward and nowhere on myScheme or the department site, treat it as unverified until you check.

## UMANG: one app for more than 2,100 government services

[UMANG](https://web.umang.gov.in/) is the government's mobile governance app, launched in November 2017, and it is the closest thing India has to a single window for services. It aggregates services from the Centre, States, and local bodies: certificates such as birth, death, domicile, income, and caste certificates; ration card services; municipality services such as new water and sewer connections; police services in several states, including lost reports and traffic complaints; and utility services. Its own pages list more than 2,100 citizen services from over 200 departments across 26 States and 7 Union Territories, available 24x7.

Three facts make UMANG practical for ordinary use. First, it supports 23 languages, so you are not forced into English to use a government service. Second, it is built to use less phone memory than carrying a separate app for every department, and it integrates with Aadhaar and DigiLocker, so documents you already hold are reachable from the same place. Third, it has an assisted mode: through delivery partners including Common Service Centres, people without a smartphone or without confidence using apps can still get services delivered with help. That matters for parents and grandparents, and for anyone whose phone is old or shared.

UMANG is free to use as a government app. Its limits are not about price but about coverage: not every service of every department is on it yet, and its own way forward says major remaining services are still being added. If a service is not on UMANG, that does not mean it does not exist — it means you go to that department's own portal, which myScheme's scheme pages will usually link to.

## DigiLocker: the document wallet that saves the second trip

[DigiLocker](https://www.digilocker.gov.in/) is the government's digital document wallet, and its job is simple: issued documents live here, verified by the issuing authority, so you can access, share, and verify them without carrying paper. Identity documents, education documents, government and public sector documents, and skill and vocational training documents are all covered, and the list of issuers is large — Maharashtra has more than 300 issuers, Karnataka more than 200, Delhi and Gujarat more than 110 each, and most other states dozens more.

For government services, DigiLocker is the tool that prevents the most common failure: reaching the end of a form and discovering you do not have the document it asks for. Before you start any application, open DigiLocker and check that the documents the scheme page lists — Aadhaar, marksheet, income certificate, caste certificate, driving licence — are actually issued and visible in your account. If a document is missing, get it issued first, on the official portal, rather than uploading a photo of paper and hoping it passes verification. An issued document from DigiLocker carries the issuer's verification; a scanned photo does not.

DigiLocker is free to use. Its warning is the same as every other tool here: DigiLocker never asks for your OTP over a call or a message link. Anyone asking for your DigiLocker OTP is trying to take your documents, not help you.

## Bhashini: the government translation mission for Indian languages

[Bhashini](https://bhashini.gov.in/) is the National Language Translation Mission, and it exists for a problem every Indian knows: the scheme page is in English, the form is in another language, and the help you need is in your own. Bhashini offers AI-powered, real-time, multilingual translation, transcription, and summarisation across Indian languages, with a mobile app, and it also runs the Sansad Bhashini initiative, which translates, transcribes, and summarises Parliament proceedings with AI.

For government services, Bhashini is most useful in two places. First, reading: when a scheme guideline, circular, or notice is in a language you read slowly, translation gives you the meaning before you decide anything. Second, speaking and listening: transcription helps when a spoken instruction, a helpline message, or a recorded announcement needs to become text you can re-read. Bhashini is a government mission and free to use, and it is the right first choice for translation precisely because it is built for Indian languages rather than adapted from English-first tools. For the wider set of Hindi–English translation tools beyond government pages, see our guide to [Hindi–English translation tools](/blog/ai-translation-tools-hindi-english-india/).

Translation is still translation, not legal advice. If a translated rule and the original rule seem to disagree, the original government document decides. Use Bhashini to understand, then confirm the key condition — age limit, income limit, last date, document list — on the official scheme page itself.

## JanSamarth: credit-linked schemes with a no-payment promise

[JanSamarth](https://www.jansamarth.in/) is the national portal for credit-linked government schemes, and it is the clearest example of how a government portal should talk about money. It links beneficiaries directly with lenders, lets you check eligibility, apply online, and get digital approval, and it covers 17 schemes across 9 loan categories with more than 300 lenders on the platform. Its own pages state, in plain words, that the JanSamarth Portal does not ask applicants for any kind of payment.

That sentence is worth remembering, because it is the test for every loan-related message you receive. If someone asks for a processing fee, a registration charge, or a "guarantee deposit" to release a government scheme loan, that demand contradicts the portal's own rule. Real government scheme loans may still involve the bank's normal process and, where the scheme allows it, its own official charges collected through official channels — but no agent on WhatsApp, no phone call, and no link in a forwarded message is the channel for that. Check eligibility on JanSamarth, apply through JanSamarth or the lender it shows you, and keep every receipt inside the official portal.

JanSamarth is free to use. Its limit is scope: it covers credit-linked schemes only, which is 17 schemes, not every government scheme. For non-loan schemes — scholarships, pensions, subsidies, certificates — myScheme and UMANG are the right starting points, not JanSamarth.

## MyGov Helpdesk on WhatsApp: DigiLocker documents without a portal visit

The [MyGov Helpdesk](https://www.mygov.in/overview) is the government's citizen engagement platform, and its WhatsApp chatbot at 9013151515 is the fastest way many people will actually use DigiLocker. You save the number, send "Hi" or "Namaste," choose DigiLocker services, authenticate your DigiLocker account, enter your Aadhaar number, verify with the OTP sent to your Aadhaar-linked mobile number, and download the document as a PDF. The downloaded Aadhaar PDF is password-protected and digitally signed by UIDAI, so it has the same legal validity as a printed Aadhaar, and the service runs 24x7 without a portal visit or a CAPTCHA.

MyGov itself is large — more than 64 million registered users as of September 2026 — and it is also where the government runs discussions, polls, quizzes, and consultations on draft policies. For this article, though, the Helpdesk is the tool that matters, because it turns a document download into a two-minute chat. It is free to use.

The safety rule for WhatsApp helpdesks is strict and simple: only the official number counts. Save 9013151515 yourself from the MyGov site, and never trust a "MyGov" number that messages you first, sends you a different number, or asks you to forward the OTP to it. The OTP goes into the official chatbot only, and only when you started the conversation.

## Chitti Government: a voice-first scheme guide for people who prefer speaking

[Chitti Government](https://sahayai.in/chitti_government.html) is a private, voice-first guide to Indian government schemes, and it is included because it solves a different problem from the portals above: reading long scheme pages. You speak or type a scheme name — PM Kisan, Ujjwala, Ayushman — and it checks your saved profile against the scheme rules and tells you yes, no, or partial, out loud and on screen. It lists more than 100 schemes, shows which documents you already hold and which are missing, tracks government deadlines with reminders 90, 30, and 7 days before, and even checks a suspicious government SMS or call against known scam patterns and tells you where to report it.

Two honest limits matter here. First, Chitti's profile and readiness score are computed on your own device — its pages say nothing is sent to a server — which is good for privacy, but it also means its eligibility answer is only as good as the profile you entered. It is a guide, not a department decision. Second, its public page states no price. Do not assume it is free forever, and do not pay through it or share bank details with it; use its public guide features, and do the actual applying on the official portal it hands you off to. Its own pages are honest about one more limit: the government offers no public API to check application status across schemes, so for tracking an application it hands you to the official scheme portal, where you sign in with the credentials you used while applying. That honesty is a good sign, and it is also the rule for every tool in this article.

## ChatGPT free: drafting and explaining, never submitting

[ChatGPT](https://chatgpt.com/) free is useful for government work in exactly two ways: it drafts, and it explains. It can draft an application letter, a request to a department, or a complaint in clear language from your rough points. It can explain a form field, a scheme condition, or a notice in simple Hindi or English when the official wording is dense. What it cannot do is submit anything, check your real eligibility, or know the current rule for a scheme — its answer is generated text, not a department record, and it can be confidently wrong about dates, limits, and documents.

Its free limits need an honest paragraph, because sources disagree. As reported in October 2026 from the companies' official help pages, ChatGPT free offers unlimited everyday text chats, with separate caps on file uploads, image generation, voice, and data analysis that reset on their own schedules; one independent check put uploads as tight as three files per day. The safe way to read this is: everyday drafting and explaining is free, but heavy use of uploads, images, or voice will hit caps, and the caps change often. Check the usage screen inside the app when something stops working, and do not build a deadline around a free tier.

The safety rule is absolute: never paste your Aadhaar number, OTP, bank account details, full address with documents, or a photo of your ID into ChatGPT or any general chatbot. For general chatbot limits beyond government work, see [AI chatbots beyond ChatGPT and Gemini](/blog/ai-chatbots-beyond-chatgpt-gemini-india/), and for drafting official emails and letters, see [AI tools for email writing](/blog/ai-tools-for-email-writing-india/). Draft with placeholders — "my name," "my district," "my income slab" — and fill in real details only on the official portal. A chatbot that has your OTP or your documents is a risk you cannot undo.

## Gemini free: the same job inside Google

[Gemini](https://gemini.google.com/) free does the same drafting and explaining job, with a different limit system. Instead of counting messages, Gemini free uses a compute budget: each prompt uses part of the budget depending on the model, features, and chat length, the budget refreshes every five hours, and there is a weekly cap on top. Its free context window is 32,000 tokens — enough for a long article or a scheme guideline, but not for a large PDF. Google's own pages offer a free tier at zero rupees per month with a Google account, and paid tiers that raise the limits.

For government work, Gemini's practical advantage is narrow: if you already live in Google apps, it is already there, and it can search the web for the official page while it drafts. Its limits are the same warning as ChatGPT's, in a different shape: long conversations eat the budget faster, image generation eats it faster still, and busy-time features like Deep Research may pause for free users. The disagreement between sources on exact models — one report says free accounts mostly get the smallest model with harder prompts routed upward without telling you — is another reason not to trust a free chatbot's answer about a scheme rule without checking the official page.

The same safety rule applies exactly: placeholders in the chat, real details only on the official portal, and no Aadhaar number, OTP, or bank details anywhere near a general chatbot.

## Using these tools in India: language, payment, and no VPN

Everything in this article works in India on a normal phone and a normal mobile connection, with no VPN. The official portals and apps — myScheme, UMANG, DigiLocker, Bhashini, JanSamarth, MyGov Helpdesk — are Indian government services built for Indian users, and the general chatbots work in India on their free tiers. If any tool asks you to install a VPN to reach a government service, that is a warning sign, not a solution: the real service does not need one.

Language is the second India question, and the answer is mixed in a useful way. UMANG supports 23 languages, which covers most daily service use. Bhashini covers translation and transcription across Indian languages, which covers reading and understanding. MyGov and the scheme pages themselves are strongest in English and Hindi, and some state scheme pages are strongest in the state language. The practical method is: find the scheme on myScheme, read its conditions in the language you understand best, use Bhashini or the chatbot to explain anything unclear, and confirm the final condition — age, income, document, last date — on the official page before you apply.

Payment is the third India question, and the rule is simple. The tools in this article are free to use. Government services themselves sometimes have official fees — a certificate fee, for example — but those fees are paid on the official portal or app, through its own payment page, with a receipt. No fee is ever paid to an agent, a WhatsApp number, a link in a forwarded message, or an "AI approval" service. JanSamarth's no-payment statement is the clearest version of this rule, and it applies in spirit to every scheme: if a payment demand arrives outside the official portal, stop and verify on the official portal first.

Low-end phones deserve a direct note. UMANG is built to use less memory, DigiLocker works through the MyGov WhatsApp chatbot without installing anything new, and myScheme, JanSamarth, and Bhashini all work in a normal mobile browser. If your phone is shared, sign out of DigiLocker and the portals after use, and never save an OTP or a downloaded Aadhaar PDF in a shared gallery without a password.

## What to avoid

Avoid any service that promises an approval, a guaranteed benefit, or a faster result for a fee. No AI tool in this article, and no legitimate tool anywhere, can promise that, because approvals are decided by departments, not by apps. Avoid any "agent" that asks for your OTP, your Aadhaar number over a call, or a photo of your bank card — those are the three details that let someone take over your accounts, and no official process collects them that way.

Avoid pasting full personal documents into a general chatbot to "check" them. A chatbot can read a form and explain it, but once your document is in a chat, you cannot control where that text goes. Use placeholders for drafting, and upload real documents only inside the official portal or app that will process them.

Avoid trusting a forwarded scheme message on its own. Forwarded messages are how fake schemes spread: a real-sounding name, a tight deadline, a link that is not the official domain, and a fee. The check takes two minutes — search the scheme name on myScheme, open its official page, and compare the conditions and the link. Our guide to [AI privacy and safety free tools](/blog/ai-privacy-safety-free-tools-india/) covers scam patterns and OTP safety in more detail. If the scheme does not appear on myScheme or the department site, do not apply through the forwarded link, and do not pay anything.

Avoid assuming a free tier will last until your deadline. Free limits on ChatGPT and Gemini change often, and a feature that works today can pause at busy times or hit a cap tomorrow. Do the drafting early, keep your drafts in your own documents, and never let a chatbot's availability decide whether you meet a government last date.

Avoid treating an AI answer as the rule. Scheme rules, income limits, age limits, and document lists change, and an AI answer may come from older data. The official scheme page is the rule. The AI is the explanation. When they disagree, the official page wins, and you should re-check it on the day you apply.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can an AI tool apply for a government scheme on my behalf?** No. As of late 2026, no citizen-facing official AI agent files applications for you — the MeitY tender to build such agents for DigiLocker and UMANG is still at the building stage. The tools in this article find schemes, explain forms, and draft letters. The application itself is always yours, on the official portal.

**Which tool should I open first if I do not know which scheme fits me?** Open myScheme first. Enter your basic details, look at the matched schemes, and read the eligibility and documents on the scheme page. Only then decide whether to use UMANG, JanSamarth, or a department portal to apply.

**Is myScheme really free, or is there a hidden charge later?** myScheme itself is a free government portal for finding and understanding schemes. It does not charge for search or discovery. If a scheme itself has an official fee, that fee appears on the scheme's own official application page, not on myScheme, and you pay it only there.

**Can I trust a chatbot's answer about my eligibility?** Treat it as a draft, not a decision. A chatbot can explain the conditions and help you compare them with your situation, but eligibility is decided by the department when you apply, using the current rules and your real documents. Always confirm the conditions on the official scheme page.

**What should I do if someone asks for a fee to approve my scheme application?** Stop, do not pay, and do not share your OTP or documents. Check the scheme on myScheme or its official page, and remember JanSamarth's own rule as the general test: official portals do not collect approval fees through agents or forwarded links. Report the message through the official reporting channel the scheme page or Chitti's scam check points to.

**Do I need a VPN or a paid plan to use these tools in India?** No. Every official tool in this article works in India on a normal connection, and the free tiers of ChatGPT and Gemini work in India too. A VPN request or a paid plan is never a requirement for reaching a government service, and either demand from a third party is a reason to stop and verify.

**My parents do not use smartphones well. How can they use these tools?** UMANG's assisted mode works through delivery partners including Common Service Centres, where staff help with the service. DigiLocker documents can also come through the MyGov WhatsApp chatbot, which needs only WhatsApp, not a new app. For scheme finding, a family member can run myScheme with the parent's details and read out the matched schemes and documents.

## The final verdict, and a checklist before you apply

Start with myScheme for schemes and UMANG for services, keep DigiLocker ready, and verify each detail on the official portal before you pay or apply. That is the whole method in one line, and it is deliberately boring: find on the official finder, read on the official page, prepare documents in the official wallet, and apply on the official portal. AI helps with the finding, reading, and drafting in between, and it helps a lot — but it never replaces the official page, and it never deserves your OTP, your Aadhaar number, or your money.

Before you apply, run this checklist. One, the scheme appears on myScheme or the department site with the same name and conditions. Two, the documents it asks for are issued and visible in your DigiLocker. Three, you are applying on the official portal or app, reached by typing its address or from the scheme page, not from a forwarded link. Four, no one has asked you for a fee, an OTP, or a document photo outside that portal. Five, you have saved your drafts and your application receipt in your own documents, so a free tier's changing limits cannot strand you. If all five are true, apply with confidence. If any one is false, stop and verify first — the scheme will still be there after you check, and a scam will not.

Prices, limits, and free tiers in this article come from official pages and October–November 2026 sources, and they change. Re-check the official pricing, help, and scheme pages before paying anything or promising anyone a deadline, because a limit that is true today may not be true next month.
