A bakery in India runs on four things at once: orders coming in on WhatsApp, recipes that must scale correctly from 10 cakes to 200, ingredients that run out at the worst moment, and deliveries that must reach customers fresh and on time. Most small bakers manage all of this in their head, in a diary, or in scattered WhatsApp chats, and that works until one busy festival week breaks it. A forgotten order, a wrongly scaled recipe, or a missing box of butter can cost more in one weekend than a year of software. AI tools do not bake the cake, but they can take over the remembering, the writing, the maths, and the tracking, so the baker stays in the kitchen instead of drowning in admin.

Use [WhatsApp Business](https://whatsappbusiness.com/products/business-app-features/) free for your catalogue and for taking orders, because that is where Indian customers already message you. ## ChatGPT: writing, recipe scaling drafts, and customer replies

[ChatGPT](https://chatgpt.com/) is the first tool because it covers the most bakery jobs for no money. Ask it to write a Diwali hamper description, a polite reply to a customer who wants a last-minute eggless cake, an Instagram caption for a new sourdough, or a festival offer message in Hindi, Hinglish, or English, and it will produce a usable draft in seconds. It is also genuinely useful for recipe maths: tell it a recipe that serves 10 and ask for quantities for 60, and it will scale every ingredient, including the awkward ones like baking powder, where a straight multiplication can go wrong. Treat that output as a draft, not a final recipe. Always check the scaled quantities yourself, and always test a scaled recipe once before selling it, because an AI does not know how your oven behaves, how humid your kitchen is, or how your flour absorbs water.

ChatGPT is also the best tool for the messages bakers hate writing: a polite no to an impossible deadline, a delivery delay message, a price increase note, or a reply to a one-star review. Paste the customer's message, describe the facts, and ask for a short, polite reply in the customer's language. Do not paste a customer's phone number, address, or payment details into any AI chat, and do not let it invent your prices, your delivery areas, or your ingredients. The free tier has usage limits that change over time, and the paid plans raise those limits, so if you hit the free limit during a festival week, that is the moment to consider paying, not before. For a home baker doing a handful of orders a week, the free tier is usually enough.

## Canva: menus, price lists, and festival posters without a designer

[Canva](https://www.canva.com/en_in/pricing/) is where a bakery's menu stops looking like a photocopied price list and starts looking like a shop customers trust. Its free plan, listed at US$0 on its India pricing page, includes templates for menus, price lists, posters, and social media posts, plus one Brand Kit with three colours. That is enough for a small bakery: pick your colours once, use the same template for every festival, and your Diwali poster, your cake menu, and your Instagram post will all look like they came from the same shop. The Pro plan is listed at US$144 a year for one person and adds more premium photos, more Brand Kits, and higher AI usage, but a bakery should only pay for it when the free templates genuinely run out, which for most small bakeries takes a long time.

The practical rule for bakery design is restraint. One clear menu with real prices, real flavours, and real photos of your own products will sell more cakes than a flashy poster with stock photos of cakes you never baked. Use Canva to lay out your own photos neatly, to add your shop name, phone number, and delivery areas, and to make a price list that a customer can read on a phone screen without zooming. If you sell packaged items such as cookies or dry cakes, remember that labelling rules apply: the official FSSAI labelling requirements say pre-packaged food must carry the name of the food, the ingredients list, and the FSSAI logo and licence number, among other declarations. Canva can design the label, but it cannot tell you whether your label is legally complete, so check the official FSSAI requirements or a food consultant before printing labels at scale. This article is general information only, not professional legal or food-safety advice.

## WhatsApp Business: the order book your customers already use

[WhatsApp Business](https://whatsappbusiness.com/products/business-app-features/) is the single most important free tool for most Indian bakers, because it is where the orders already arrive. The free business app lets you build a catalogue with photos, prices, and descriptions, so a customer can browse your cakes without you retyping the menu for every enquiry. Quick replies handle the questions you answer fifty times a week, such as delivery charges, eggless options, and advance-notice rules. Labels let you sort chats into new orders, advance payments, ready for pickup, and delivered, which turns a chaotic inbox into a visible order pipeline. The app also lets you link up to 10 devices, so a family member or helper can answer customers while you are baking.

The discipline that makes WhatsApp Business work is a fixed order format. Ask every customer for the same five details: item and flavour, quantity, date, pickup or delivery, and advance payment. Put that format in your catalogue description and in a quick reply, and half your order confusion disappears. Take advance payment through UPI for custom cakes, because a custom cake cannot be resold easily if the customer cancels. Keep a backup of your order chats, because a phone lost or reset during festival season can wipe your entire order book. WhatsApp Business is free, but it is not a full bakery system: it will not cost your recipes, track your flour stock, or warn you that two large orders clash on the same morning. That is what the next tools are for, and that is why bakers who grow eventually pair WhatsApp with a tracker or a dedicated system.

## Google Sheets: the free order tracker, costing sheet, and stock list

Google Sheets is free with a Google account, works on a phone and a laptop, and is the tool that quietly saves the most money for a growing bakery. Build three sheets and keep them updated, and you have a system that many paid tools charge for. The first sheet is an order tracker: date, customer name, item, quantity, amount, advance received, balance, pickup or delivery, and status. Filter it by date and you can see tomorrow's entire production at a glance. The second sheet is recipe costing: every ingredient, its quantity per batch, its market price per kilogram, and the total cost per cake. Once that sheet exists, pricing stops being a guess, and you can see immediately when a rise in chocolate or butter prices eats your margin. The third sheet is inventory: opening stock, purchases, usage, and closing stock for flour, sugar, butter, chocolate, boxes, and packaging.

The honest limit of spreadsheets is that they only work if someone updates them, and during a busy week that someone is usually the baker, who is already exhausted. If your tracker is three days out of date, it is worse than useless, because it gives you false confidence. Update it at a fixed time each evening, even if it takes only ten minutes, and keep the sheets simple enough that a helper can update them too. Ask ChatGPT to help you build the sheet formulas, such as scaling a recipe cost or totalling the day's orders, but check every formula against a hand calculation once before trusting it. A bakery that runs cleanly on three honest sheets is in a stronger position than a bakery that pays for software nobody updates.

## BakeOnyx: orders, recipes, costing, and inventory in one bakery system

[BakeOnyx](https://www.bakeonyx.ai/pricing) is the dedicated bakery tool most small and growing bakers should look at first when spreadsheets stop being enough. It is built specifically for bakeries, so its vocabulary matches a baker's day: orders, recipes, recipe costing, inventory, invoicing, a customer list, a calendar view, and an AI assistant called Bake Buddy that answers questions about your own orders, recipes, and inventory. Its help pages also describe AI pricing recommendations based on true cost analysis, AI inventory predictions that suggest when to reorder, and, on higher plans, AI agents that draft production plans and purchase orders for your approval, with nothing ordered until you say yes. That approval step matters: an AI should draft, and a human should decide, especially when money or ingredients are involved.

The price to weigh is real. Its Essentials plan is listed at US$24 a month billed annually, which is US$290 a year, for unlimited orders, up to 500 recipes, one staff account, order management and invoicing, recipe costing and inventory, a customer list, and 20 reports. Its Growth plan is listed at US$66 a month billed annually. The pricing page also says "start free", but the free terms were not spelled out in the pages checked for this article, so confirm what the free start actually includes, and for how long, before building your business around it. For an Indian baker, there is also a currency question: prices are listed in US dollars, so check the final charge in rupees, including any bank or card conversion charges, before subscribing. BakeOnyx is worth paying for when you regularly lose track of orders, misprice cakes, or run out of a key ingredient, because one saved wedding-cake order or one avoided stockout can cover a month of the subscription. It is not worth paying for when you have five orders a week and a sheet that you actually update.

## BakeIQ: production and inventory for larger bakeries

[BakeIQ](https://www.bakeiq.ai/pricing) is a step up in both capability and price, and it is aimed at bakeries with real production complexity: multiple products, recurring orders, wholesale accounts, and more than one outlet or market stall. Its own description presents it as one operating system for production planning, inventory management, recipe scaling, nutrition labels, staff scheduling, and distribution across stores and wholesale accounts. Recipes can be scaled by portions, weight, or ingredient, ingredients and recipes can be imported from photos, text, or a spreadsheet, and stock can be tracked in real time with minimum thresholds. That is genuinely useful for a bakery that produces the same items every day across several destinations and needs a production schedule the whole team can see.

The price is the reason most Indian home bakers should not start here. Its month-to-month plan is listed at US$149 a month, its six-month launch plan at US$99 a month with a six-month minimum, and its annual prepaid plan at US$1,300 a year, and its pricing page notes that listed numbers are charged in Canadian dollars for Canada and US dollars for the United States, with other currencies shown before purchase rather than converted. An Indian bakery should therefore confirm the exact rupee charge, and whether the nutrition labels it generates match Indian labelling rules rather than the US-style labels its pages describe, before paying. BakeIQ makes sense when a bakery has outgrown BakeOnyx-style tools and needs production schedules, distribution sheets, and staff scheduling in one place. For a single home bakery, it is far more system than the business needs, and the money is better spent on ingredients, packaging, or a better oven.

## Petpooja: billing and kitchen management for a bakery shop counter

[Petpooja](https://www.petpooja.com/poss/pricing) is the Indian option for a bakery that has a shop counter, not just a home kitchen. It is a restaurant and food-business point-of-sale system with cloud billing, a central kitchen module, multi-terminal billing, an in-built customer list, and dozens of reports, and it is built for Indian realities such as busy counters and online ordering. For a bakery shop, that means a bill that is correct at the counter, a kitchen that sees the order clearly, and a record of every sale that a diary cannot match. It is also the tool to consider when a bakery wants to take online orders alongside walk-in customers without juggling five different apps.

No free plan was found for Petpooja in the pages checked for this article, and its pricing page lists plan names and features without showing a single simple price in the results seen, so treat its cost as something to confirm directly from its official pricing page or sales team before budgeting for it. That is also the right moment to ask whether it supports the payment methods your customers use, how its billing handles GST, and whether its reports give you the daily sales and bestseller picture you actually need. Petpooja is overkill for a home baker taking orders on WhatsApp, and it is the sensible choice for a shop whose counter is busy enough that billing mistakes and lost orders are already costing money. If your bakery is somewhere in between, start with WhatsApp Business and Sheets, and move to Petpooja when the counter, not the kitchen, becomes the bottleneck.

## Google Business Profile: the free listing customers check first

Google Business Profile is free, and for a bakery with a shop, it is the listing customers check before they ever taste your cake. A complete profile shows your shop on Google Search and Maps, with opening hours, photos, a menu showcase, and reviews, and it is often the first thing a new customer in your neighbourhood sees. Keep the hours accurate, especially around festivals and holidays, upload real photos of your own products and your shopfront, and reply to every review, including the unhappy ones, politely and briefly. A bakery that answers its reviews looks alive; a bakery with unanswered one-star reviews looks like it does not care, even when the cake was fine.

For home bakers without a shopfront, the same tool needs care: think carefully before publishing a home address, and check the profile settings for how your location is shown. A home baker can often serve a local area through WhatsApp and Instagram without a public shop listing, and that is a legitimate choice. For a shop bakery, though, there is no excuse for an empty or outdated profile, because it costs nothing and it is where discovery happens. Pair it with your WhatsApp catalogue link, so a customer who finds you on Google can order in one tap. ## Using these tools in India: language, payment, and delivery

India is where the generic advice about bakery software meets reality, and three things decide whether a tool actually works here. The first is language. Your customers may write in Hindi, Hinglish, or English, sometimes in the same message, and your tools must cope. ChatGPT can draft in all three, Canva templates can carry Hindi text, and WhatsApp is already multilingual, but a dedicated bakery system built for another country may not handle Hindi product names or Indian date formats gracefully, so test it with your real menu before paying. The second is payment. Indian customers pay by UPI, and custom cakes should be booked with an advance, because the cost of a cancelled custom order falls entirely on the baker. No tool in this article replaces that business rule: it only helps you state it clearly, in your catalogue, your order message, and your bill.

The third is delivery, and it is where small bakeries lose the most money and goodwill. A cake is fragile, heat-sensitive, and time-critical, and a delivery partner who is perfect for parcels may be wrong for cakes. Use your order tracker to record the delivery slot, the delivery person's name, and the handover time, and use WhatsApp to send the customer a photo of the packed cake and a live location message when the rider leaves. For festival weeks, cap the number of delivery orders per slot instead of accepting everything and disappointing half your customers. If you also sell through food delivery platforms, remember that their commission changes your margin, so cost every platform item in your recipe sheet before listing it, not after. ## What to avoid

Avoid the trap of buying software before you have a process. A bakery that cannot describe its order format, its pricing, and its delivery slots will not be fixed by a subscription; the software will simply record the chaos more expensively. Fix the format first, on paper or in Sheets, and only then move it into a paid system. Avoid letting AI invent your food. Never publish an AI-written description that claims an ingredient, a flavour, a weight, or a "sugar-free" or "eggless" status that you have not verified, because allergy and dietary claims are safety claims, not marketing copy. Avoid using a customer's photos, or photos of cakes you did not bake, in your catalogue or on Canva posters, because customers will recognise the difference when the box opens, and a platform can remove misleading listings.

Avoid putting customer phone numbers, home addresses, or payment details into AI chats, and avoid sharing your full order sheet or recipe costs with any tool you do not trust, because recipes and customer lists are your business assets. Avoid promising a delivery time that your tracker cannot support, especially for custom cakes during festival weeks, and avoid taking an order without an advance for a custom design that cannot be resold. Avoid ignoring the legal side of packaged food: if you sell packed cookies, dry cakes, or mixes, check the official FSSAI labelling and licensing requirements for your situation, and remember that this article is general information only, not professional legal, food-safety, or financial advice. Official sources and qualified professionals must be checked before you rely on any rule described here. Finally, avoid the quietest mistake of all: a system nobody updates. Whether it is a diary, a sheet, or a paid tool, the best bakery system is the one that is actually updated every evening.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the best free AI tool for a home baker in India?**
For most home bakers, the best free starting set is ChatGPT for writing and recipe maths, Canva for menus and posters, WhatsApp Business for orders, and Google Sheets for tracking and costing. Together they cost nothing, they all work in India without a VPN, and they cover every job a small bakery has before it needs paid software. Pay only when one specific job, such as order volume or recipe costing, clearly outgrows the free tool doing it.

**Can AI scale a cake recipe safely?**
AI can scale the arithmetic, and it is good at that, but it cannot taste your result or know your oven. Scale the recipe with AI, check every quantity by hand, and test the scaled recipe once before selling it. Baking powder, yeast, salt, and flavourings do not always scale in a straight line, and a recipe that works for 10 servings can fail at 100 if the tin size, baking time, and temperature are not adjusted too.

**Should a small bakery pay for BakeOnyx or BakeIQ?**
Pay for BakeOnyx when orders, costing, and inventory regularly overwhelm your spreadsheets, and when its Essentials plan at US$24 a month billed annually is less than the cost of the orders or ingredients you are currently losing track of. Consider BakeIQ only for a larger bakery with production schedules, multiple outlets, or wholesale distribution, because it starts at US$149 a month. For a home bakery with a handful of weekly orders, both are premature, and the free tools will serve you better.

**How should a baker take orders on WhatsApp without chaos?**
Fix one order format and use it for every customer: item and flavour, quantity, date, pickup or delivery, and advance payment. Use WhatsApp Business labels to sort chats into new, paid, ready, and delivered, and use quick replies for the questions you answer every day. Keep a separate order tracker in Sheets, because WhatsApp alone will not warn you about two large orders clashing on the same morning.

**Does a home baker need Petpooja?**
Usually not. Petpooja is built for a shop counter with billing, a kitchen module, and online ordering in one system, and no free plan was found for it in these checks. A home baker taking orders on WhatsApp, tracking them in Sheets, and accepting UPI advances already has the core of the same workflow for free. Move to Petpooja when you open a shop counter and billing mistakes at the counter start costing real money.

**How do I price a cake so I actually make a profit?**
Cost every ingredient in a sheet, add packaging, delivery, and your time, and then add a margin for waste, failed bakes, and overheads such as gas, electricity, and rent. Many home bakers underprice because they forget packaging and their own time, and a recipe costing sheet is what makes that visible. Re-cost your bestsellers whenever butter, chocolate, or packaging prices rise, because a price set six months ago may already be losing money.

**What should I check before paying for any bakery software?**
Re-check the official pricing page for the final price in rupees, including taxes and conversion charges, and confirm what the free trial or free start actually includes and for how long. Test it with your real menu, including Hindi or Hinglish product names, and confirm it handles your order volume, your delivery slots, and your reports before you move your order book into it. Prices and free limits change often, so never budget or promise a customer a deadline from an old price you remember.

**Is it safe to use AI photos for my bakery menu?**
Use real photos of your own products for your menu and catalogue, and use AI or design tools only to lay those photos out neatly. AI-generated cake photos can look perfect and still be a different cake from the one you sell, and that gap is what produces angry reviews and refund requests. If you ever use a styled or illustrative image, label it honestly, and never use it to imply a flavour, ingredient, or portion size you do not offer.

## The final verdict

Start free with ChatGPT, Canva, WhatsApp Business and Google Sheets. Pay for BakeOnyx only when orders and costing outgrow spreadsheets. That is the whole strategy in one sentence, and it is deliberately boring: the free tools cover writing, design, orders, and tracking, and the paid tools earn their money only when volume makes the free tools slow. Keep your order format fixed, your recipe costs honest, your photos real, and your tracker updated every evening, and your bakery will survive the festival weeks that break bakeries running on memory alone. Re-check every price on the official page before you pay, check the official FSSAI requirements before you print packaged labels at scale, and let the software serve the baking, never the other way round.
