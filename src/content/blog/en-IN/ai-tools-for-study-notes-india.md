AI can turn a 50-page chapter into notes in two minutes. That does not mean you have learned the chapter. The difference between a student who uses AI notes well and a student who fails after using them is simple: the first student uses AI to make **practice material**, then practises **recall**. The second student only reads the AI summary and calls that studying.

The short answer for India is this: use [NotebookLM](https://notebooklm.google/), now called Gemini Notebook, to make cited study guides, flashcards, and quizzes from your own files, and use [Anki](https://apps.ankiweb.net/), which is free and open source on computers, for spaced repetition. [Quizlet](https://quizlet.com/) and [Knowt](https://knowt.com/) are useful for flashcards and practice tests, especially if you already use them. Everything else is a bonus. Do not pay in month one, and do not submit AI notes as your own work without checking your course rules.

This article is written for India on purpose: NCERT, CBSE, state board, and college syllabi, Hindi and English notes, a normal phone and laptop, and no assumption that you have a paid plan or a US student offer. It also carries a warning that matters for study tools more than for image tools: an AI summary can sound confident and still miss a topic, invent a fact, or follow the wrong syllabus. Always check AI notes against your textbook and your teacher's notes, especially for dates, formulas, definitions, and exam-specific wording.

If you read nothing else, remember three rules:

1. **Your own files first** — notes from your own files beat notes from the open internet, because your exam follows your syllabus, not the whole internet.
2. **Spaced repetition, not just reading** — flashcards without spaced repetition are just reading in disguise.
3. **Citations matter** — NotebookLM cites the passage behind every claim, which lets you check it. A tool that gives you a summary with no source is asking you to trust it blindly, and you should not.

## NotebookLM: the strongest free tool for your own material

[*NotebookLM*](https://notebooklm.google/), which Google said in July 2026 it is renaming Gemini Notebook, builds study guides, flashcards, quizzes, and Audio Overviews from the files in a notebook, and it **cites the passage behind every claim**. You can add [PDFs](/blog/ai-pdf-tools-summarize-india/), websites, YouTube videos, audio, Google Docs, and Slides as sources. For a student who wants notes across [several readings](/blog/ai-tools-for-research-students-india/) at once and wants to see where each line came from, it is the strongest free option in the October 2026 checks.

The free tier is generous for working from uploads: 100 notebooks with 50 sources each, with usage limits that reset every 5 hours, up to a weekly cap. Paid Google AI plans raise those limits, with AI Plus listed at 4.99 dollars a month and AI Pro at 19.99 dollars a month on Google's US page in October 2026. Eligible students outside the US can claim a free year of AI Plus in some offers, while US college students get a free year of AI Pro in others. Indian students should not assume a US-only offer applies to them. Check the offer for your country and account before you plan around it.

NotebookLM is weaker in two places that matter for Indian exams. It has no board or curriculum setting, so the notes only know what your sources know. If a topic is missing from your files, it is missing from the notes. It also has no fill-in-the-blank mode and no marking of written answers in the help page reviewed. That means NotebookLM is excellent for making a first draft and for quizzing yourself, but it does not replace your textbook, your teacher's syllabus, or a practice test that follows your board's pattern.

Use NotebookLM in a specific way:

1. Upload only trusted sources: your textbook chapter, your class slides, and your [teacher's notes](/blog/ai-tools-for-teachers-india/).
2. Ask for a study guide, then ask for flashcards and a quiz from the same notebook.
3. Read the citations for any claim you are unsure about, and delete any flashcard that is vague, wrong, or off-syllabus.
4. If NotebookLM gives you an Audio Overview, listen to it once for revision, not as a replacement for reading the chapter the first time. Audio revision works after you have understood the material, not before.

## Quizlet and Knowt: flashcards, Learn mode, and practice tests

[*Quizlet*](https://quizlet.com/) is a flashcard platform before it is a notes generator, but its AI tools build sets and study guides from notes and documents, and its study modes are the most familiar in this list: Learn, Test, written, and fill-in-the-blank questions. The free tier gives unlimited own sets and core study modes with ads, while AI generation from uploads varies by plan. Quizlet Plus is listed at 7.99 dollars a month or 35.99 dollars a year in the US offer reviewed in September 2026, with different prices on app stores. Shared sets are found by topic search, but a shared set may follow a different syllabus, so do not revise from a stranger's set without checking it against your own syllabus.

[*Knowt*](https://knowt.com/) takes lecture and document uploads and turns them into notes and flashcards, then adds practice tests, mock exams, and an AI assistant called Kai. Its Basic free plan gives unlimited sets, notes, and study modes on your own sets, with defined AI and exam-prep limits and ads. Its Ultra plan is listed at 24.99 dollars a month or 149.99 dollars a year billed upfront in the checks, which is expensive compared to Quizlet. Knowt also keeps ready-made hubs organised by board for GCSE and by programme for AP, IB, and SAT or ACT. That board context helps students in those systems, but Knowt does not apply a board setting to your own uploads, so notes from your own file are still generic. For Indian students, the useful part of Knowt is not the GCSE hub. It is the free unlimited sets, the notes from uploads, and the mock exam practice, if you check the content against your own syllabus first.

The practical difference between Quizlet and Knowt is habit, not quality. If you already revise in Quizlet, generate a first draft there and edit it. If you want mock exams and lecture uploads in one place, try Knowt free. In both cases, edit every generated card:

- Delete cards that are too easy, too vague, or wrong.
- Split cards that ask two things at once into two cards.
- Add a card for every mistake you make in a practice test, because your own mistakes are the best study material you have.

## Anki: free, open source, and the best spaced repetition

[*Anki*](https://apps.ankiweb.net/) is not an AI tool, and that is exactly why it belongs in this article. Anki is a spaced repetition flashcard system: it shows you a card just before you are likely to forget it, and it schedules harder cards more often and easier cards less often. That scheduling is what turns short-term reading into long-term memory. AI tools can generate cards, but Anki is what makes you remember them.

Anki is free and open source on computers, and its Android app is free. Its iPhone app is paid, listed at 24.99 dollars in the US App Store checks. That price surprises many students, so check it before you assume Anki is free everywhere. You can still use Anki free on a computer and on Android, and you can sync decks across devices with a free AnkiWeb account.

A workflow that works is: generate a first draft of flashcards in NotebookLM or Quizlet, export or copy the good cards into Anki, and review them **daily for 15 to 20 minutes**. Do not review 200 new cards in one day and then skip a week. Spaced repetition works on **daily consistency, not on cramming**. If you miss a day, Anki will show you more cards the next day, and that backlog is normal. Clear it in small batches, and do not reset your progress out of frustration. A deck you review for 20 minutes a day for a month beats a deck you cram for five hours the night before an exam.

## ChatGPT Study Mode, StudyFetch, and Turbo AI: tutors, not note machines

[ChatGPT Study Mode](https://chatgpt.com/) is different from normal ChatGPT, because it is designed to guide you step by step instead of giving you the answer immediately. In the checks, Study Mode is available on the free plan. That makes it useful for a problem you are stuck on: it can ask you what you already know, give you a hint, and check your next step, rather than solving the whole problem for you. Use it for understanding, not for copying. If Study Mode gives you a full solution, ask it to explain each step in your own words, then close it and solve a similar problem without it. That second problem is the real test.

[StudyFetch](https://www.studyfetch.com/) and [Turbo AI](https://www.turbo.ai/) are tutor-style tools around course files. StudyFetch offers a tutor chat around course files, lecture recording, and practice, with a free start and a Premium plan listed at 19.99 dollars a month or 96 dollars a year in US App Store checks. Turbo AI offers notes, flashcards, and quizzes on a free tier, with paid plans from 7.49 to 19.99 dollars a month depending on the offer. Both publish no free quota in the checks, which means you must check the allowance inside the app before you plan a full course around them. For Indian students, these tools are worth a test for one chapter, but NotebookLM free, with its published 100 notebooks and citations, is easier to plan around for a whole subject.

## Using study AI in India: syllabus, language, phone, and data

Indian exams follow a syllabus, and AI tools do not automatically know yours. NCERT, CBSE, state board, university, and entrance exam syllabi differ in wording, depth, and question pattern. An AI summary that is correct in general can still be wrong for your exam, because it uses a different definition, a different formula form, or a different example. Always compare AI notes with your textbook's exact definitions and your teacher's notes. For formulas, write them by hand at least once, because handwriting a formula helps memory in a way that reading an AI summary does not.

Language matters for study notes. You can ask NotebookLM and ChatGPT to explain in Hindi, Hinglish, or English, and for many students, learning in Hindi first and then writing the answer in English technical terms works well. For Hindi notes, check spelling and technical terms carefully, because AI Hindi can mix formal and informal terms in the same paragraph. For English answers, learn the exact technical terms your board uses, because an examiner marks the term, not just the idea. A summary that says the right idea in the wrong term can still lose marks.

Phone and data use is realistic for study AI, but with limits. NotebookLM recommends the desktop version for full use, though it works on web, iOS, and Android. Quizlet offline on phone apps is limited to your most recent sets. Anki generated decks work offline on mobile in some tools, while podcasts and audio overviews need a connection. If you have limited data, download audio overviews and decks on Wi-Fi, review flashcards offline, and use AI generation only when you have a connection. Do not waste data regenerating the same notes five times. Generate once, edit once, and revise many times.

## A study method that actually works with AI

A method that works for most subjects has five steps, and AI helps with only two of them:

1. Read the chapter yourself once, even if slowly, and mark what you do not understand.
2. Use NotebookLM to make a study guide and flashcards from your trusted sources.
3. Edit those cards and delete anything wrong or off-syllabus.
4. Practise recall with Anki or Quizlet Learn mode, without looking at the notes.
5. Write answers from memory, under time, and compare them with the textbook.

Steps four and five are where marks come from. Steps two and three only prepare you for them.

**Active recall** means closing the book and trying to remember, not re-reading. **Spaced repetition** means reviewing at increasing intervals, not cramming. **Interleaving** means mixing topics in one session, not studying one chapter for five hours straight. AI tools make it easy to generate material for all three methods, but they cannot do the recall for you. If you spend an hour generating perfect notes and zero minutes practising recall, you have done admin work, not studying, and your exam result will show it.

For [maths](/blog/ai-tools-for-maths-homework-india/), science, and other problem subjects, AI notes are even less sufficient on their own. You must solve problems yourself, with pen and paper, and check each step. Use AI to explain a step you are stuck on, then solve a similar problem without AI. For [language subjects](/blog/ai-tools-for-learning-english-india/), write answers yourself and use AI to check grammar and structure, not to write the whole essay. For history and social science, check dates, names, and causes against your textbook, because AI summaries can merge similar events or invent a plausible-sounding date.

## What to avoid

- Do not trust an AI summary without checking it against your textbook, especially for definitions, formulas, dates, and exam wording.
- Do not revise from a shared flashcard set that follows a different syllabus.
- Do not submit AI notes or AI answers as your own work without checking your course rules, and do not use AI to write an assignment you are supposed to write yourself.
- Do not pay for a study tool in month one, especially for StudyFetch or Turbo AI, where the free quota is not published.
- Do not generate 500 flashcards in one day and then never review them. Spaced repetition needs daily review, not a large deck.
- Do not use AI audio overviews as a replacement for reading a chapter the first time. Audio revision works after understanding, not before.

A final trap is using AI to avoid difficult topics. Students often generate notes for easy chapters and skip hard chapters, because AI makes easy chapters feel finished. Do the opposite: spend AI time on hard chapters, ask for step-by-step explanations, and practise those topics more, not less. Exams test hard topics more than easy ones, and AI is most useful exactly where you struggle, if you use it to understand, not to avoid.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I use these tools in India without a VPN?** Yes, for the major tools in this article. NotebookLM, Quizlet, Knowt, Anki, and ChatGPT all work in India through a normal browser with a standard account. If a smaller tool does not load, check whether it is down or region-limited before assuming you need a VPN, and do not enter payment details on a site you cannot verify.

**Which free tool is best for NCERT or CBSE revision?** NotebookLM for cited notes from your own NCERT chapter and class slides, plus Anki for spaced repetition. No AI tool automatically follows NCERT wording, so always compare AI notes with the NCERT textbook's exact definitions.

**Are AI notes enough for an exam?** No. AI notes are a draft for revision. Marks come from recall practice, writing answers from memory under time, and solving problems yourself. A student who only reads AI notes will forget most of them by exam day.

**Can I use AI for homework?** Check your course rules first. Many courses allow AI for learning and debugging, but forbid submitting AI work as your own without disclosure. Use AI to understand a problem, then solve it yourself and write the answer in your own words.

**What should I do when NotebookLM's weekly cap runs out?** Review the cards you already have in Anki or Quizlet, practise writing answers from memory, and wait for the cap to reset. Do not pay for a plan in a panic because one tool hit a weekly limit. The cap resets, and your old cards are still useful.

**Should a student pay for Quizlet Plus or Knowt Ultra?** Not to start, and not for flashcards alone. Free sets, Learn mode, and Anki cover most revision. Pay only when a specific paid feature, like unlimited AI generation or mock exams, actually blocks your revision repeatedly, and then pay for one tool, not both.

## The final verdict, and a checklist before you pay

Start free, and start with your own files. Use NotebookLM to make cited guides, flashcards, and quizzes from your textbook, slides, and teacher notes. Use Anki for daily spaced repetition, and Quizlet or Knowt for Learn mode and practice tests if you already use them. Use ChatGPT Study Mode for step-by-step understanding, not for full answers. Edit every generated card, delete anything wrong or off-syllabus, and practise recall every day.

Before you pay for any study tool, answer these questions in writing:

- Which exact limit stopped you, and on which date?
- Did it stop notebooks, AI generations, mock exams, or offline access, because each has a different fix?
- Did you check the official pricing page today?
- Does the paid plan fix that exact limit, or does it only give you more of something you did not need?

If you cannot answer those questions, you are not ready to pay yet, and another two weeks on free tools will give you a clearer answer than any review article can.

AI study tools in India in 2026 are genuinely useful, but only when you treat them as draft makers and practice makers, not as memory machines. Use free tools to make good material from trusted sources, edit that material carefully, and then do the hard work yourself: recall, write, solve, and repeat. That is how a student saves money, learns faster, and actually remembers on exam day.
