## The direct answer

If you are just starting SEO in India, you do not need an expensive toolkit. Start with Google Search Console and Google Keyword Planner, both free, and add one audit tool such as Screaming Frog's free version or a free Ahrefs account for a site you own. Use an AI assistant such as ChatGPT, Gemini or Claude only to turn real data into titles, outlines and fixes — never as a replacement for that data. AI can suggest keywords, but it cannot tell you what people actually typed into Google to reach your site. Only Search Console can do that, because it shows your own site's real clicks, impressions and positions.

That distinction matters more than any single tool. Most beginners fail at SEO not because they picked the wrong software, but because they trusted invented numbers. An AI chatbot will happily invent a search volume, a ranking position or a competitor's traffic figure if you ask it confidently enough. A real SEO tool, by contrast, either shows measured data or clearly labels its estimate. The whole method in this article is built around that rule: real data first, AI second, and a human check before anything is published or paid for.

This guide covers keyword research, ranking and site audits for beginners, with honest free limits for each tool. Every tool listed here works in India without a VPN, through an ordinary browser and internet connection. Prices and free limits change often, so before you pay for any plan or promise a client a deadline that depends on a tool, re-check the tool's official pricing and help pages. Where sources disagreed during research for this article, that disagreement is stated plainly rather than smoothed over.

If you only have one hour, spend it in Search Console, not in a new AI tool. Find the queries where your pages already sit at positions 8 to 20 with decent impressions, improve those pages first, and you will usually see faster results than chasing brand-new keywords.

## How SEO breaks into three jobs

Beginners often treat SEO as one mysterious task. It is easier to think of it as three separate jobs, each with its own tool.

First, keyword research: finding the words and questions real people search for, and judging which ones a new or small site can realistically rank for. Second, ranking work: creating or improving pages so they deserve to rank, with the right title, headings, content and internal links, and then tracking whether positions move. Third, site audits: checking the technical health of the site — broken links, missing titles, slow pages, pages Google cannot index, and mobile problems. AI helps most with the second job, drafting and improving content. It helps least with the first and third, where measured data matters most. Keep that split in mind and you will avoid most beginner traps.

## Google Search Console: the one tool every site owner needs

Google Search Console is a free service from Google that shows how your site performs in Google Search. Its own documentation describes it as a tool that helps anyone with a website understand their performance and improve their appearance in search. You can see which queries bring visitors, submit sitemaps and individual URLs for crawling, review index coverage, and receive email alerts when Google finds issues on your site.

For a beginner, three reports matter most. The Performance report shows clicks, impressions, click-through rate and average position, broken down by query, page and country. Filter it to India if your audience is Indian, and you will see exactly which phrases already bring you traffic. The Indexing report shows which pages Google indexed and which it could not, with reasons. The Experience and Core Web Vitals reports show how real visitors experience your pages on mobile and desktop.

There is one important limit: Search Console only starts collecting data after you verify ownership of your site, and it shows data only for sites you own or manage. It will not show you a competitor's traffic. That is not a flaw; it is the reason its numbers are trustworthy. Set it up on day one, even before your site has traffic, so data is waiting when you need it.

Official starting point: [Google Search Console](https://search.google.com/search-console/about). Its documentation is at [Google Search Central](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/monitor-debug/search-console-start).

## Bing Webmaster Tools: the free second opinion

Bing Webmaster Tools is Microsoft's free equivalent, and it is worth adding for two reasons. First, it gives you a second source of search data, so you are not judging everything from Google alone. Second, Microsoft has added AI-related reporting, including an AI Performance report showing how often Copilot and Bing's AI answers cite your pages. For a beginner, the practical value is simple: if a page is invisible in both Google and Bing, the problem is probably on your site, not in one search engine's mood.

Setup is similar to Search Console: verify your site, submit a sitemap, and check its site scan for errors. It costs nothing, and the extra ten minutes of setup buys you a genuinely independent check. Do not expect Bing traffic to match Google traffic in India, where Google dominates; treat Bing as a diagnostic tool and an early window into AI-answer visibility, not as your main traffic source.

## Google Keyword Planner: ideas plus honest volume ranges

Google Keyword Planner is built for advertisers inside Google Ads, but its keyword data is usable for SEO research. You need a Google Ads account to use it, though you do not need to run ads to open the tool. Enter a seed word such as "seo tools" and it returns related ideas, including phrases that do not contain your seed word at all but match the same topic.

The honest catch, confirmed across independent guides, is that volumes are shown as broad ranges — for example, 10,000 to 100,000 searches per month — unless you have active advertising spend, in which case the ranges narrow. For a beginner, ranges are still useful: they separate a phrase searched by thousands from one searched by dozens. They are not useful as exact promises. Never write "this keyword gets 40,000 searches" based on Planner alone, and never quote a range to a client as if it were a guaranteed visitor count.

Use Planner in this order: generate ideas, note the range, then check the same phrase in Search Console (do you already rank for it?) and in Trends (is interest rising or falling?). A keyword that passes all three checks is worth writing about. A keyword that only an AI chatbot suggested, with no Planner range and no Trends signal, is a guess.

## Google Trends: timing beats volume

Google Trends is free and shows how interest in a topic changes over time and across regions. Its most important limitation is also its most misunderstood feature: it shows relative interest on a scale, not absolute search volume. A score of 100 means peak interest for that term in that period, not 100 searches.

For Indian beginners, Trends answers questions Planner cannot. Is "AI tools for teachers" rising before the school year starts? Does interest in a festival product spike only in one month? Should you publish now or wait? Compare two or three related phrases, filter by India, and look at the last twelve months plus the last five years. A phrase with steady interest is usually a safer investment than a phrase that spiked once and collapsed. Seasonal content planned around Trends data is one of the few genuinely free advantages a small site has over a bigger competitor that publishes on instinct.

## Ahrefs Free: a real audit for sites you own

Ahrefs is one of the best-known SEO suites, and its paid plans are expensive for a beginner. What many beginners miss is that Ahrefs offers a free tier — previously called Ahrefs Webmaster Tools, now described as Ahrefs Free — that gives you a site audit, backlink data and SEO metrics for sites you verify as your own. That makes it one of the best free audit tools available, with one hard boundary: it will not show you competitor sites. For competitor research you need a paid plan.

The free audit is genuinely useful. It finds broken links, missing or duplicate titles, slow pages and other technical problems, and it explains each issue in plain language with a suggested fix. Run it once a month on your own site, fix the errors it lists as most severe first, and re-run it to confirm the fixes worked. Do not chase a perfect score for its own sake; a small site with a few warnings and good content will outrank a technically perfect site with thin content.

A note on prices: Ahrefs' paid plans are listed on its official pricing page and change over time, so this article deliberately does not quote a paid price as permanent. If a blog post or video quotes you a price, open the pricing page yourself before paying. The same warning applies to every paid tool in this article.

## Semrush free account: a small taste of a large suite

Semrush is the other major all-in-one SEO suite, and its free account is a good way to learn what a full suite does before committing money. According to Semrush's own free-account guide, the free tier includes 10 queries per day for Domain Overview, keyword research, competitor research and backlinks, a site audit of up to 100 pages, and position tracking for 10 keywords, with no credit card required. Its help documentation describes the free tier in similar terms — one demo project and 10 reports per day across its main tools — though the exact wording has changed as Semrush reorganised its plans, so treat the numbers above as current at the time of writing and re-check before relying on them for paid work.

For a beginner, the smart way to use those 10 daily queries is to spend them on one site, not scattered across ten domains. Audit your own 100 pages, track your 10 most important keywords, and research one keyword cluster per day. You will outgrow the free tier quickly if you manage several client sites — and that is the point. The free tier is for learning and early optimisation, not for agency-scale work. If you need more, Semrush also offers a short free trial of its paid plans, which you can cancel before the trial ends; set a reminder the day you start the trial, because trial expiry is the most common way beginners accidentally pay for SEO software.

Learn more from Semrush's own guide: [What can you do with a free Semrush account?](https://www.semrush.com/blog/what-can-i-do-with-a-free-account-from-semrush/)

## Screaming Frog SEO Spider: the desktop crawler for small sites

Screaming Frog SEO Spider is a desktop application that crawls your website the way a search engine would, then lists every page, title, meta description, heading, link and error in one table. Its free version crawls up to 500 URLs, which is more than enough for most beginner sites, blogs and small business websites. Its own pricing page states plainly that the free version finds broken links, errors and redirects, analyses page titles and metadata, reviews meta robots directives, audits hreflang attributes, discovers duplicate pages and generates XML sitemaps, with the 500-URL crawl limit as the main boundary.

Screaming Frog is the right tool when you want to see your whole site at once. Export the crawl, sort by missing titles or by 404 errors, and you have a concrete fix list. It is also the right tool to learn technical SEO vocabulary, because every column teaches you something: status codes, canonical tags, redirect chains and response times. The trade-off is that it runs on your computer rather than in a browser, and large sites over 500 pages need a paid licence. For a beginner with a small site, neither limitation matters much.

Official pricing and free-version details: [Screaming Frog SEO Spider pricing](https://www.screamingfrog.co.uk/seo-spider/pricing/).

## PageSpeed Insights: speed, measured honestly

PageSpeed Insights is Google's free tool for checking how fast a page loads on mobile and desktop. It combines lab data from Lighthouse with real-world field data from the Chrome User Experience Report, and it reports Core Web Vitals — Largest Contentful Paint, Interaction to Next Paint and Cumulative Layout Shift — classifying each as Good, Needs Improvement or Poor. You paste a URL, and it returns a score plus specific diagnostics, such as oversized images or render-blocking scripts.

Speed matters for beginners for a practical reason: most Indian visitors arrive on mobile connections, and a page that takes eight seconds to load loses readers before its content is even judged. Run your homepage and your five most important pages through PageSpeed Insights, fix the top diagnostic on each, and re-test. Do not obsess over a perfect 100; a page in the Good range on mobile with genuinely useful content beats a lightning-fast page with nothing to say. And remember that PageSpeed Insights diagnoses problems — it does not fix them. The fix still happens in your website builder, theme or hosting settings.

Test any page here: [PageSpeed Insights](https://pagespeed.web.dev/).

## AI assistants: ChatGPT, Gemini and Claude as helpers, not oracles

ChatGPT, Google Gemini and Claude all offer free plans, and all three are useful for SEO work — with the same warning attached to each. They are writing and analysis assistants, not search-data sources. Their free plans have usage caps, and their answers can include confident-sounding numbers that were never measured anywhere. Use them for tasks where their strength is real: turning a Search Console export into a prioritised fix list, drafting ten title options under 60 characters, expanding a keyword into an article outline, rewriting a dull meta description, or grouping a messy keyword list into topics.

A practical workflow looks like this. Export your Search Console queries, paste them into your assistant, and ask it to find queries at positions 8 to 20 with high impressions, grouped by the page they belong to, with a suggested new title for each page. Then check every suggestion yourself: does the title fit the page, is it under 60 characters, and does it avoid inventing a claim the page does not support? That human check is not optional polish; it is the step that separates SEO from spam. Google's own guidance has consistently been that content should be helpful and written for people, and mass-produced AI text with no checking is the fastest way to produce pages nobody wants to read.

Also, be careful with what you paste into an AI assistant. Do not paste passwords, private analytics for a client who has not agreed, or unpublished business data. Use exports with only the columns you need, and prefer the assistant's paid or business tier if a client requires stronger data handling — after checking that tier's actual terms, not a marketing summary.

## Rank Math: on-page SEO inside WordPress

If your site runs on WordPress, Rank Math's free plugin covers the on-page basics well: editable titles and meta descriptions, sitemaps, schema markup, internal-link suggestions and a content score for each post. It puts SEO controls inside the editor where you actually write, which is why many Indian bloggers and small businesses prefer it to juggling separate tools.

Rank Math's AI features, marketed as Rank Math AI, are a separate, paid offering covering AI visibility tracking, AI-assisted internal linking and content AI. That distinction matters: installing the free plugin does not automatically give you the AI suite, and older tutorials that describe a standalone Content AI product may no longer match the current packaging. Before paying, open Rank Math's own AI page and pricing page and confirm exactly which features are included in the plan you are buying.

Rank Math's AI overview: [Rank Math AI](https://rankmath.com/ai/). If you do not use WordPress, skip this section entirely — none of this applies to a static site, a Shopify store or a custom-built site, and you should not install WordPress just to get one plugin.

## A beginner workflow for your first month

Week one is setup, not writing. Verify your site in Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools, submit your sitemap, and run one audit with Ahrefs Free or Screaming Frog. Fix anything marked as an error, especially pages that return 404 or cannot be indexed at all.

Week two is keyword research. Take ten seed topics from your business, expand them in Keyword Planner, check timing in Trends, and pick twenty phrases where you can honestly say your page would be useful. Group them into clusters of three to five phrases per page, not one page per phrase.

Week three is improving what already exists. In Search Console, find your pages at positions 8 to 20, rewrite their titles and introductions, add the missing subtopic the top-ranking pages cover, and add internal links from your stronger pages. One improved page that moves from position 12 to position 5 is worth more than five new thin pages.

Week four is measuring. Re-check Search Console for movement, re-run PageSpeed Insights on the pages you changed, and re-run your audit to confirm old errors are gone. Then repeat. SEO is a loop, not a launch: research, improve, measure, and research again. Beginners who follow this loop for three months learn more about their audience than beginners who spend the same three months comparing paid tool dashboards.

## Using these tools in India: language, mobile and local intent

India changes SEO in three practical ways. First, language. Many Indians search in Hindi, Hinglish or a mix — typing "best seo tools" in one search and "SEO kaise kare" in the next. Keyword Planner and Search Console both show you the actual phrases people used, including Hindi and Hinglish queries, so build your keyword lists from that real data rather than translating English phrases in your head. If you run a Hindi site, track Hindi queries separately; they are a different audience, not a translation of your English audience.

Second, mobile comes first. Most Indian search traffic arrives on phones, often on slower connections. That makes PageSpeed Insights and mobile usability reports more important here than on a desktop-first audience, and it means long, heavy pages with uncompressed images cost you readers directly. Test on a real phone, not only on your laptop.

Third, local intent is strong. A query such as "seo services near me" or "best digital marketing course in Delhi" behaves differently from a general informational query, and Google's local results, business profiles and location pages matter for it. If you serve a city or region, make sure your location is clear on your pages and your business profile, and track location-specific queries in Search Console by filtering for India and, where data allows, by region.

One reassurance: all the tools in this article are usable in India through a normal internet connection, with no VPN required. If a tool ever asks you to use a VPN to sign up, treat that as a warning sign and check whether you are on the tool's real official site. Pricing in rupees is not always offered; some tools bill in dollars, so check the currency and any bank charges before subscribing, and prefer monthly billing until you are sure the tool earns its keep.

## What to avoid

Avoid buying a paid SEO suite before you have used the free tools for a month. The paid dashboard will look impressive, but without a habit of checking Search Console and fixing audit errors, you are paying for numbers you will not act on.

Avoid AI keyword tools that show exact search volumes with no source. If a tool claims a phrase gets exactly 18,432 searches per month but cannot explain where that number came from, it is a guess dressed as data. Planner ranges, Search Console measurements and Trends patterns are honest about their uncertainty; prefer them.

Avoid publishing AI-written articles without reading and editing them. Check every fact, every number and every claim about a tool's pricing or limits. An article that confidently states a wrong free limit will mislead your readers and damage your own credibility when they check the official page.

Avoid chasing a single metric. Domain authority, a speed score of 100, or a keyword difficulty number are all proxies, not goals. The goal is visitors who find what they needed on your page. If a metric improves while your clicks fall, the metric was the wrong one to chase.

Avoid link-buying schemes and "guaranteed first page in seven days" offers. No tool in this article can guarantee a ranking, and anyone selling a guarantee is selling something Google explicitly warns against. Earn links by publishing something worth linking to, and treat any shortcut that hides links or buys them at scale as a risk to your whole site.

Avoid copying a competitor's content with an AI paraphraser and calling it original. Search engines and readers both recognise thin rewrites, and a site built on them rarely survives a core update. Use competitors to learn which topics matter, then write a better, more specific page from your own experience.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the best free AI SEO tool for a beginner in India?**

There is no single best tool, because the job splits three ways. Google Search Console is the best free source of real traffic data, Google Keyword Planner is the best free source of keyword ideas with volume ranges, and Screaming Frog's free version or Ahrefs Free is the best free audit starting point. An AI assistant such as ChatGPT or Gemini sits on top of those tools to draft titles and outlines. Start with Search Console, and add the others one at a time.

**Can AI tools alone do keyword research?**

No. AI assistants can brainstorm keyword ideas, group them into topics and draft outlines, but they cannot measure how many people actually searched a phrase. They sometimes invent search volumes that sound precise but are not real. Always validate an AI-suggested keyword in Keyword Planner for a volume range, in Trends for timing, and in Search Console to see whether you already rank for it.

**Do I need a paid tool like Semrush or Ahrefs to rank?**

No, not at the start. Many small sites rank well using only Search Console, Keyword Planner, Trends and a free audit tool. Paid tools become worth it when free limits block real work — for example, when your site grows past 500 pages for Screaming Frog's free crawl, past 100 pages for Semrush's free audit, or when you genuinely need competitor data at scale. Pay when a limit blocks you, not when a sales page impresses you.

**How long does SEO take to show results?**

For most new or small sites, expect weeks for technical fixes to be re-crawled and indexed, and typically two to six months before content improvements show stable movement — longer for competitive phrases. Anyone promising first-page rankings in a week is either targeting a phrase nobody searches for or selling something risky. Track progress in Search Console weekly, but judge it monthly, because daily positions bounce around naturally.

**Is Google Search Console really free?**

Yes. Search Console is a free service from Google for verified site owners, with no paid tier required for its core reports. The same is true of Bing Webmaster Tools from Microsoft and PageSpeed Insights from Google. If a site charges you to "activate" Search Console, you are on the wrong site.

**What is the difference between Google Keyword Planner and Google Trends?**

Keyword Planner gives keyword ideas with search-volume ranges, which help you judge how big a topic is. Trends shows how interest in a topic rises and falls over time, on a relative scale, which helps you judge when to publish. Use Planner to choose topics and Trends to time them; neither gives an exact visitor count.

**Can I do SEO for a Hindi website with these tools?**

Yes. Search Console, Keyword Planner and Trends all show Hindi and Hinglish queries as users actually typed them, and Bing Webmaster Tools and the audit tools work on Hindi pages the same way they work on English pages. Track Hindi queries separately from English ones, write titles and descriptions in the language your readers search in, and test your pages on a mobile phone, since most Hindi search traffic is mobile.

**Should I use Rank Math AI or another AI plugin?**

Only if you run WordPress and the paid AI features solve a specific problem you already have, such as internal linking at scale. The free Rank Math plugin already covers titles, descriptions, sitemaps and schema. Check the current AI pricing page before paying, and remember that no plugin writes a good article for you — it can only help optimise an article you have already written well.

**What should I check before paying for any SEO tool?**

Four things: the current price and currency on the official pricing page, the exact free or trial limits and what happens when the trial ends, whether the tool covers your site size (pages, keywords, projects), and whether you can cancel easily. Take a screenshot of the pricing page on the day you subscribe, set a trial reminder, and start on monthly billing. If any of those four checks is unclear, do not pay yet.

## Final verdict

Start free with Search Console and Keyword Planner; pay only when a free limit blocks real work. AI assists SEO, it never replaces real data.
