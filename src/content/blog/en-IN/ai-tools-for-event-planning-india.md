If you are planning a wedding, an engagement, a birthday, or a family party in India, you do not need to hire an event planner on day one, and you do not need to pay for five different apps before you have even fixed a date. Most of the planning work — finding vendors, comparing prices, sending invites, tracking who is actually coming, and keeping the budget from running away — can be done with free tools that work in India on normal broadband and mobile data, without a VPN.

The direct answer is this: start with three free tools. Use [WedMeGood](https://www.wedmegood.com/) to find and shortlist vendors in your city. Use [Joy](https://withjoy.com/) for a free wedding website with a guest list and RSVP tracking. Use [Canva](https://www.canva.com/) to design your invites. Those three cover the biggest jobs for a wedding. For a birthday or a smaller party, swap the wedding website for [Evite](https://www.evite.com/) and its free invitations with RSVP tracking. Everything else in this article is for a specific job: a venue search, a seating plan, a ticketed event, or a budget that needs proper tracking. Add a tool only when you reach that job, not before.

One honest point first, because this article is called an AI tools article. Not everything here is truly AI. WedMeGood, WeddingBazaar, and [VenueLook](https://www.venuelook.com/) are mainly vendor marketplaces and planning tools — their value is local listings, reviews, and checklists, not artificial intelligence. The genuinely AI parts are narrower: Canva AI can turn a sentence into a design, [ChatGPT](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/9275245-chatgpt-free-tier-faq) can draft your invite text, vendor messages, and a budget sheet, and [weddly.ai](https://weddly.ai/) tries to run budget, guests, invites, and timeline in one AI-assisted dashboard. That distinction matters, because a marketplace with 150,000 vendors helps you more than a chatbot that only sounds confident. Judge each tool below by the job it does, not by whether it has AI in its name.

This article is written for India on purpose: normal internet, no VPN, Indian cities and prices in rupees where the tool shows them, and Hindi and English use where it matters. It also carries the warning that applies to almost every tool here: free limits and prices change. A number that was correct when this article was written can change without an email to you. Before you plan a deadline around a free limit, and certainly before you pay, open the official pricing or help page of that tool again and check the number yourself.

## WedMeGood: the vendor search to start with

WedMeGood is the most useful starting point for an Indian wedding because it is built around Indian vendors. Its own site describes it as India's favourite wedding planning app, with more than 150,000 verified vendors across more than 20 categories, and an app that lets you shortlist vendors, save ideas, message vendors through an inbox, collaborate with family on decisions, and follow a checklist so nothing is missed.

In practice, that means your first week looks like this: pick your city, pick a category such as photographers, makeup artists, venues, or decorators, filter by budget and style, and shortlist five to eight options per category. Read the reviews on the platform, but treat them as a starting filter, not a final verdict — a vendor with many reviews in your city is usually safer than a vendor with a beautiful Instagram page and no traceable reviews. Then message vendors from inside the app, so your conversation history stays in one place when you compare quotes later.

The free part is the search, shortlisting, checklist, and messaging. WedMeGood itself does not charge you for browsing or enquiring; you pay the vendor you finally book, and that price is a quote you negotiate, not a fixed rate on the platform. That is the single most important thing to understand about every vendor marketplace in this article: the platform is free to search, but the wedding itself is not free, and the final price always comes from the vendor. Get every quote in writing, with the date, the number of hours, the number of people, and what is included, before you pay an advance.

## WeddingBazaar: the second opinion across cities

WeddingBazaar, from the Matrimony.com group, is the natural second opinion. Its site calls itself India's largest wedding planning platform, with more than 2.8 lakh trusted vendors across more than 40 cities, more than 2.3 million connections with more than 60,000 vendors, and more than 20 lakh customers. Its own category pages list, for example, more than 27,000 bridal makeup artists and more than 24,000 wedding photographers.

Use it the way you would use a second market: search the same categories you searched on WedMeGood and compare. If a vendor appears on both platforms with consistent reviews and prices, that is a good sign. If a vendor appears on only one, that is not a bad sign by itself — it only means you should check them a little more carefully. One practical difference: WeddingBazaar's flow leans on its executives calling you to understand your requirements and suggest vendors. That can save time if you are overwhelmed, but if you dislike sales calls, be ready to say clearly that you want to browse first and talk later. Its planning pitch is the same honest deal as WedMeGood's: checking prices, verified reviews, and booking vendors is the free part; the vendor's fee is separate.

A note on the numbers in this section and the last one: vendor counts are self-reported by the platforms, and they count different things — verified vendors, trusted vendors, cities covered — so do not treat 150,000 on one platform and 2.8 lakh on another as if they were measured the same way. What matters for you is not the total, but how many good options exist in your city, in your budget, for your date.

## VenueLook: venues with prices in rupees

VenueLook is for the venue hunt: banquet halls, wedding venues, and party places for marriages, birthdays, and corporate events. Its useful difference is that it shows prices in rupees on venue listings — the examples on its own site include per-plate style figures such as ₹749, ₹849, ₹1,400, ₹1,600, and ₹1,800 — so you can filter out venues that are obviously out of budget before you visit any of them.

Use it early, because the venue usually decides everything else: the date, the guest count, the catering, and the decor. Shortlist three venues in your budget, visit them in person, and ask the same five questions at each one: what is included in the per-plate price, what is the minimum guarantee on guests, what are the decoration and DJ rules, what is the cancellation policy, and what does the final bill usually add beyond the quoted price. No website can answer those for you, and the answers are where Indian wedding budgets most often break. VenueLook gets you to the shortlist; the visit gets you to the truth.

## Canva: invites that look designed, without a designer

Canva is where your invites actually get made. Its template library includes free, customisable Indian wedding templates — traditional invitations, mehndi invitations, South Indian designs, floral royal designs, and invitation videos — so you do not have to start from a blank page or explain your taste to a designer in words. Pick a template, change the names, date, venue, and colours, and you have a shareable invite for WhatsApp, Instagram, or print.

Canva AI 2.0 adds the AI part: describe what you want in text or voice, generate elements such as an image, a chart, or a headline, and refine the design by chatting with it. For an event, the practical use is simple: generate a matching set — one main invite, one Instagram story version, and one small thank-you card — instead of designing each one separately. The honest limit is that Canva is freemium: many templates are free, but some templates and elements are marked Pro and need a paid plan. Before you fall in love with a design, check whether its key elements are free, or you will rebuild the invite at the last minute. For more on designing without a designer, see the companion article on [graphic design for beginners](/blog/ai-tools-for-graphic-design-beginners-india/), and for artwork beyond templates, the article on [free AI image generators](/blog/free-ai-image-generators-india/).

## [Paperless Post](https://www.paperlesspost.com/): digital invites when you want something formal

Paperless Post is the formal digital invite: card and flyer designs that look like real stationery, sent by email or text, with RSVP handling built in. It offers both free and paid-for cards and flyers. A card is paid-for if the design itself carries a starting price — its help centre gives the example of a design-partner card starting at 2 Coins — or if you add a paid-for design element to it. You pay either with Coins, its virtual credits, or through a Paperless Pro subscription, and the cost per recipient updates as you design, so there is no surprise at the end.

Two numbers matter for planning. First, the sending limit for a single event sent directly through the Paperless Post site is 5,000 email addresses or phone numbers — far above any normal wedding, but worth knowing for a very large community event. Second, its pricing system is changing: it is rolling out feature-set pricing in beta, which is not available on all events yet, so the exact price you see for your event is the price that counts, not a number quoted in an article. Paperless Post suits weddings and formal parties where a WhatsApp image feels too casual but printed cards feel wasteful. For a casual birthday, Evite below is simpler and fully free at the basic level.

## Evite: free invites and RSVP for parties

Evite is the simplest free option for birthdays, housewarmings, and casual parties. Its own description is plain: a free online invitation and event planning platform. You choose a free design, customise the text and event details, add guests, and send by email, text, or link. Guests RSVP from any device, you track RSVPs in real time, follow up with guests who have not replied, and send automated reminders. Free invitations also come with a shared photo album for guests to upload photos after the event, and you can update the time, location, or guest list even after sending.

Evite also offers premium options with exclusive designs and more customisation, and an option to upload your own design — useful if you already made an invite in Canva, since Evite has a Canva app that lets you bring a Canva design straight in. The rule for Evite is the same as for every freemium tool here: start free, and pay only if a premium design is genuinely worth it to you. For most parties, the free design plus real-time RSVP tracking is the whole job.

## Joy: the free wedding website and guest list

Joy is the strongest free wedding website option in this list. Its pricing page says it plainly: Joy is free to use, at $0 a month. Wedding websites, planning tools, and registry are free, online save-the-dates and invitations have hundreds of free designs alongside premium paid designs, and the guest list manager lets you collect addresses, import guest lists, send messages, and track RSVPs in one connected place.

Joy makes its money when guests buy registry items it sells, and from optional upgrades such as custom domain names, text messaging, and premium designs for digital and printed stationery. That business model is why the core can stay free, and it also tells you where the upsell will appear: if you want your own domain name or SMS messages to guests, that costs extra. For most Indian weddings, the free core is enough — one link to share on WhatsApp that has the story, the schedule for each ceremony, the venue map, and the RSVP form. It replaces the twentieth "venue kahan hai, time kya hai" message in the family group, and that alone is worth setting it up the week you fix the date.

## Zola: free website, registry, and a budget tool

Zola is the other free wedding website option, and its strength is breadth: free wedding websites, a registry, venues and vendors, invites and paper, a guest list that helps you get addresses and track RSVPs, and a budget tool to stay on top of spending. If Joy feels too simple for you, Zola's budget tool is the reason to look at it — a wedding budget that lives next to the guest list is much harder to ignore than a spreadsheet you open once a month.

The honest framing for India is this: Zola's free website, guest list, and budget tool work from anywhere, but its vendor search is built around its own marketplace, not Indian cities. For finding a photographer, makeup artist, or venue in Jaipur, Indore, or Kochi, WedMeGood, WeddingBazaar, and VenueLook above have the local listings. Use Zola for the website, the registry, and the budget; use the Indian platforms for the vendors. Mixing tools this way is not a weakness — it is how you get the best free part of each one.

## weddly.ai: an AI planner, still in early access

weddly.ai is the closest thing in this list to a single AI wedding planner. Its pitch is to plan a wedding in one place, with budget, guests, invitations, a wedding website, timeline, and vendors in one dashboard, and weddly AI helping at every step. Its budget planner lets you set a total budget, split it by category, and track paid versus remaining in real time, flagging overspending early and suggesting where to adjust — its own example shows a $48,500 budget with photography flagged as over and a suggested package switch saving $1,200. Its guest management tracks RSVPs, meal choices, plus-ones, and side lists in one view, and reminds you to follow up with guests who have not responded before the RSVP deadline.

The catch is stated on its own site: it is early access only, with a signup for early access. Its pricing is not published on the pages checked for this article. That means you should treat weddly.ai as a promising tool to try alongside your main plan, not as the only place your plan lives. Keep your guest list and budget in a second place — Joy for guests, or a simple spreadsheet — until weddly.ai has public pricing and a track record you can check. An early-access tool that holds your only copy of the guest list is a risk, not a plan.

## Social Tables: seating plans without graph paper

Social Tables solves one specific, painful job: who sits where, and how the room is laid out. Its free plan costs $0 and includes 1 user account, 3 events, 2 diagrams per event, and 150 attendees per attendee list. That is enough for one wedding: one diagram for the main ceremony hall, one for the reception or sangeet, with the guest list attached to each seat. Its paid plans start at $49 a month for Standard (15 events) and $150 a month for Pro, and all paid plans carry a 12-month commitment, so do not start a paid plan for a single wedding unless you plan events all year.

The free plan's real limit is 150 attendees per list. For a 150-guest wedding it fits; for a 500-guest Indian wedding it does not, and you will need to split the list or upgrade. Check your real guest count before you build the diagram, because rebuilding a seating plan under time pressure is exactly the stress this tool is supposed to remove. Its own site notes that planners and properties have created more than 8.1 million diagrams and that it is trusted by more than 96,000 planner users, which is reassuring for reliability, but your decision should still rest on the free plan's limits, not on its popularity.

## Eventbrite: ticketed parties, free events, and a fee warning

Eventbrite is for events with tickets: a paid workshop, a ticketed party, a charity show, or a large community event where you need real ticket control. Its pricing is simple and genuinely free at the bottom: you can publish unlimited free events at no cost, and free events carry no ticketing fees for you or your ticket buyers. For paid tickets, its published fees are a 3.7% plus $1.79 service fee per ticket and a 2.9% payment processing fee per order, and you can choose whether you or the buyer absorbs them.

Here is the warning that matters for India. Eventbrite's own pricing page says its pricing plans are available in the United States, Canada, Australia, New Zealand, the United Kingdom, and Ireland — India is not named there — and its help page says ticketing fees vary by country and currency and should be checked in your organisation settings. That does not mean Eventbrite cannot be used from India, but it does mean you must check, inside your own organiser account, what fees and payout options apply to you before you sell a single paid ticket. For a free community event, none of this matters and Eventbrite is simply free. For a paid event, verify first, sell second.

## ChatGPT free: the drafting assistant for everything else

ChatGPT's free tier is the drafting assistant behind all the tools above. According to OpenAI's own help centre, the free tier gives access to a range of chat capabilities, tools, and GPTs, with the default model GPT-5.6 Luna at the time of checking, plus web search, file and image upload, use of GPTs, image creation, and a library with 500 MB of storage. Plus users get higher rate limits, and the help centre warns that the default model and available limits can change over time — which is exactly why this article keeps telling you to re-check before a deadline.

For event planning, use it for the writing jobs, not the deciding jobs. Ask it to draft five versions of an invite in Hindi and English, to turn a vendor's long quote into a comparison table, to write a polite follow-up message to a vendor who has not replied, to build a budget sheet from your category list, or to turn a 400-guest list into table groups of eight with family kept together. Then check its work: verify every price, date, and vendor name yourself, because a confident draft with one wrong date is worse than a plain draft with the right date. For vendor emails specifically, the companion article on [email writing](/blog/ai-tools-for-email-writing-india/) covers tone and follow-ups in more depth, and for announcing the event, the article on [social media tools](/blog/ai-tools-for-social-media-india/) covers scheduling and captions.

## Using these tools in India: language, payment, and no VPN

Language first. WedMeGood, WeddingBazaar, and VenueLook are built for Indian users and work in English with Indian city and vendor data; Canva's templates include Indian wedding designs; Joy, Zola, Evite, and Paperless Post are English-first tools that work fine from India but will not give you Hindi invite wording by themselves — write your Hindi text in ChatGPT or Canva and paste it in, and always proofread names and dates in Devanagari yourself, because a wrong matra in a family elder's name is the kind of mistake nobody forgives. For photo edits on invite pictures, the article on [free photo editors](/blog/free-ai-photo-editors-india/) is a useful companion.

Payment second. The Indian platforms are free to search, and you pay vendors directly, usually by UPI, bank transfer, or cash advance — never pay a vendor's full amount in advance through an unverified link, and always get a written receipt. The international tools are freemium: their free tiers work from India, but their paid upgrades usually need an international card, and some features, such as SMS to guests on Joy, may be priced or limited differently outside their home market. If a paid feature matters, check its price in your account before you promise it to anyone.

VPN third. None of the tools in this article needs a VPN to work in India on normal broadband or mobile data. If a tool ever asks you to use a VPN to sign up, treat that as a warning sign and pick a different tool — a planning tool that cannot be reached normally on the wedding week is a liability.

## What to avoid

Avoid paying for a planning tool before you have a date, a city, and a rough guest count. Without those three, every premium feature is a guess, and you will pay for capacity you never use. Avoid keeping your only guest list inside a single new or early-access tool; keep a second copy as a spreadsheet or in Joy or Zola, and export it after every big change. Avoid booking a vendor from a marketplace listing alone — always call, always visit the venue or see recent full work, and always get the quote in writing with inclusions and cancellation terms. Avoid sharing your full guest list, with phone numbers, on a public wedding website; keep phone numbers and addresses in the private guest-list tool and share only the public schedule link. Avoid believing any AI-generated budget, seating plan, or vendor shortlist without checking it: AI drafts are fast, but your money, your family, and your date are real, and the final check is always yours.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I plan a full Indian wedding using only free tools?**
Yes, for the planning itself. WedMeGood or WeddingBazaar for vendors, Joy for the website and RSVP, Canva for invites, and ChatGPT for drafting cover the planning work free. The wedding — venue, food, photography, decor — is still paid to vendors; the tools only remove the planner's fee, not the wedding's cost.

**Which tool is best for finding vendors in my city?**
WedMeGood first, WeddingBazaar second. Both are built for Indian cities with local vendor listings and reviews. Compare the same category on both, shortlist five to eight vendors per category, and then call them. A marketplace listing is a lead, not a booking.

**Are digital invites acceptable for an Indian wedding?**
For most guests, yes, especially for save-the-dates and for younger guests. Paperless Post suits formal digital invites, Evite suits casual parties, and Canva suits WhatsApp-friendly designs. For elders and for the main wedding card, many families still print a small set alongside the digital invite — plan for both, and keep the wording and date identical on each.

**How do I keep the budget from doubling?**
Fix a total first, split it by category in Zola's budget tool or a spreadsheet, and track paid versus remaining after every advance. weddly.ai's budget planner does this with overspending flags, but it is early access, so keep a second copy. The most common budget breaks are the guest count growing, the per-plate minimum guarantee, and decor extras added on the day — agree all three in writing before the event.

**What if my guest list is over 500 people?**
Joy and Zola handle large guest lists, but Social Tables' free plan covers only 150 attendees per list, so split seating diagrams or upgrade deliberately. Paperless Post's single-event sending limit is 5,000 recipients, which covers even very large weddings. The real problem at 500 guests is not software — it is the venue's capacity and the per-plate minimum, so confirm those before anything else.

**Do I need a VPN for any of these tools in India?**
No. Every tool in this article works in India on normal broadband and mobile data. If a tool asks for a VPN to sign up, skip it and use one of the alternatives here.

**Is weddly.ai free?**
Its site offers early access signup, but it does not publish pricing on the pages checked for this article. Treat it as a tool to try alongside your main plan, not as your only plan, until its pricing and track record are public.

**Can Eventbrite be used for paid events in India?**
Free events are free and unlimited. For paid tickets, its published fee structure names the United States, Canada, Australia, New Zealand, the United Kingdom, and Ireland, and its help page says fees vary by country — so check the fees and payout options inside your own organiser account before selling paid tickets in India.

## The final verdict, and a checklist before you pay

Start with WedMeGood for vendors, Joy for a free website and RSVP, and Canva for invites, and add the other tools only when you reach their job: VenueLook for the venue, Evite for a casual party, Social Tables for seating, Eventbrite for tickets, and ChatGPT for drafting. Before you pay for anything, run this checklist: is the date, city, and guest count fixed; is the quote in writing with inclusions and cancellation terms; is the guest list backed up in a second place; and have you re-checked the tool's official pricing page this week. If all four answers are yes, pay with confidence. If any answer is no, fix that first — the tool will still be there tomorrow, but a rushed advance is much harder to undo.
